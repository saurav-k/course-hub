The tenant is the boundary, and three different populations live inside it

working 12 min Capability area identity-workforce, rows iam-principals, workforce-sso and managed-directory, with ciam-user-directory from identity-customer. · Microsoft Entra ID

The one-minute version

  • The Entra tenant is the hard boundary. One tenant can own many subscriptions, and a subscription belongs to exactly one tenant - which makes subscription ownership a directory relationship rather than a billing one.
  • Groups drive role assignments transitively, so a nested group membership grants access as surely as a direct one and is far harder to see.
  • Workforce applications reach the directory through enterprise application federation, with SAML or OpenID Connect for sign-in and SCIM for provisioning accounts into the application.
  • Two fallbacks exist for applications that cannot speak those protocols: the application proxy for on-premises web applications, and Entra Domain Services for LDAP, Kerberos, NTLM and domain join.
  • Customers are a separate population in separate tenants: Microsoft Entra External ID is the strategic customer-identity platform, and Azure AD B2C is at end of sale rather than retirement.

What a directory relationship actually means

Three populations, three surfaces

The reframing Groups grant access transitively. A role assignment made to a group reaches every member, including members that are themselves groups. That makes group nesting an access-control decision rather than an organisational convenience, and it is why answering "who can do this" from a role assignment list alone is unreliable - the list names groups, and the groups name more groups.

The two fallbacks, for applications that cannot federate

Customers live somewhere else

Microsoft Entra External ID is the customer-identity platform: branded sign-up and sign-in for external users in dedicated external tenants, priced per monthly active user with a free tier that caps that count. Social and enterprise identity providers federate into those flows, self-service sign-up collects the attributes you ask for, and Azure Functions hooks extend the flow where the built-in shape is not enough.

Azure AD B2C is at end of sale, which lesson 0103 established is not a retirement: it is closed to new customers while existing ones keep support for a period the vendor states on its own frequently asked questions page. So an existing B2C estate is not in danger, and a new customer-identity design starts on External ID. Treating the two as interchangeable in either direction is the error.

Answer from memory before revealing.

How many directories can one subscription trust for its identity plane?
A subscription belongs to exactly one tenant, and changing the directory is a move rather than an addition. The first and second options invent multi-directory trust. The fourth places the relationship at the wrong scope entirely.
An application federates with Entra for sign-in but has no SCIM provisioning. What is the consequence?
Federation controls whether a sign-in succeeds; provisioning controls whether the account exists. Without the second, leavers keep accounts. The second and third options invent dependencies on provisioning that federation does not have. The fourth confuses the missing half with a different mechanism.
Which statement about Entra Domain Services is correct?
One-way synchronisation out of Entra ID is the defining property, and it is what makes it not a forest extension. The first option is the misreading the page warns about. The third inverts the relationship. The fourth contradicts the sizing model, which is by tier rather than by controller count.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A company is building a consumer product and also modernising its internal estate. Four requirements arrive: shoppers must sign up with an email address or a social account; a purchased internal tool speaks SAML and needs accounts created and removed as staff join and leave; an old internal web application authenticates with Kerberos and cannot be changed; and a supplier's two engineers need access to one internal application without being given company accounts.

Route them: name the population and the surface for each, and say which requirement is the one people most often place in the wrong population.

Hint

Ask for each requirement whose directory the person's account should live in. Two of the four belong to the workforce population, one to customers, and one is the middle case.

Show the worked solution
  1. Place the shoppers. These are customers, so their accounts belong in an external tenant on External ID, with social identity providers federated into the sign-up flow. They are not guests in the workforce tenant.
  2. Place the purchased SAML tool. Workforce, through enterprise application federation for sign-in and SCIM provisioning for the joiner-and-leaver requirement. Note that the requirement as written names both halves, and only one of them is federation.
  3. Place the Kerberos application. Workforce, and it cannot federate, so it needs a domain. Entra Domain Services supplies LDAP, Kerberos, NTLM and domain join without controllers of your own, reached over the virtual network it is deployed into.
  4. Place the supplier's engineers. This is the middle case: business-to-business collaboration invites them as guests into the workforce tenant, so they keep their own accounts in their own organisation and receive access here.
  5. Name the common error. The shoppers. Creating consumer accounts as guests or as members of the workforce tenant is the mistake, and it is expensive because the workforce tenant's licensing, controls and lifecycle are all built for employees.

Sanity check. Exactly one of your four answers should involve a second tenant, and exactly one should involve a managed domain. If the shoppers ended up in the same directory as the staff, the population question was skipped rather than answered.
