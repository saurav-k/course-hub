Hybrid means two different things, and only one of them carries packets

working 12 min Capability area hybrid-connectivity, rows site-to-site-vpn, dedicated-interconnect, client-vpn and on-prem-extension. · Azure ExpressRoute

The one-minute version

  • One kind of hybrid is transport: a tunnel or a circuit that carries packets between your estate and a virtual network.
  • The other kind is projection: Azure Arc pulls servers, Kubernetes clusters and data services into Azure Resource Manager so policy, security and monitoring reach them. It is a control plane, not a path.
  • On the transport side, a site-to-site VPN rides the internet and an ExpressRoute circuit is private BGP peering to Microsoft's edge, with consistent, service-level-backed behaviour.
  • Their bandwidth ceilings overlap. The real separation is consistency, add-ons and resilience, and maximum resiliency means two circuits in different peering locations.
  • People are a third case. Point-to-site gives a user a tunnel, and Azure Bastion gives browser-based access to machines that hold no public address at all.

The reframing There is no standalone BGP resource on Azure. The speaker is embedded inside the VPN gateway and the ExpressRoute gateway, so dynamic routing is a property of the connection you already bought rather than a component you add. A gateway also needs its own gateway subnet, sized at least a /27, which is why lesson 0700's address plan has to reserve space for one before anybody asks for a tunnel.

People are a separate case

A point-to-site connection gives an individual user a tunnel, with authentication through Entra ID, RADIUS or certificates, and the sizing question is a user count rather than a bandwidth figure. Azure Bastion answers a narrower question in a stronger way: browser-based access to machines that hold no public address at all, with the Standard tier adding native-client and address-based connections. Lesson 0200 already used it; the point here is that it belongs to this module rather than to compute.

Two shapes on this page depend on geography rather than on configuration. The direct interconnect to Oracle Cloud Infrastructure works only where an ExpressRoute peering location sits in or beside the matching provider location, so the paired-location list governs the design rather than the requirement does. Every other provider needs a partner circuit or a cross-connect you arrange in a colocation facility. A multicloud diagram drawn without checking those lists is a diagram of somewhere else.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

A company wants its on-premises servers governed by the same policy and monitoring as its Azure ones. What does it need?
Governance is a projection requirement, and Arc is the projection. The first two answer a transport question that was not asked. The third replaces the servers rather than governing them, which is a far larger change than the requirement calls for.
What separates an ExpressRoute provider circuit from a site-to-site VPN?
Both published envelopes end at ten gigabits, so the difference is behaviour rather than ceiling. The second option is the assumption the chart exists to break. The third and fourth invent categorical differences: the circuit's advantage is consistency, and dynamic routing is available on both gateway types.
Which add-on connects an organisation's own on-premises sites to each other?
Global Reach is the site-to-site job. Premium is the closest distractor and is a true statement about a different thing - it extends global connectivity to Microsoft services, not between your sites. FastPath and Local both change the character of one circuit rather than joining two.

Practice about 20 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A manufacturer has three sites and a data centre. Requirements: the data centre needs a predictable, service-level-backed path to Azure carrying roughly two gigabits; the three sites currently reach each other over an expensive carrier service the company would like to stop paying for; a hundred servers in the data centre must be covered by the same policy and security tooling as Azure workloads; support engineers must reach Azure machines that have no public addresses; and the design must survive the loss of a peering location.

Design it: name what each requirement needs, say which ones need no transport at all, and state what the resilience requirement costs.

Hint

Sort all five into transport and projection first. Then, within transport, check each requirement against the add-on that names it, and read the resilience rule literally.

Show the worked solution
  1. Place the data centre path. Predictable and service-level-backed at roughly two gigabits sits inside the provider-circuit envelope of 50 Mbps to 10 Gbps, so this is an ordinary ExpressRoute circuit. Nothing here requires Direct, whose reason to exist starts above ten gigabits or at owned ports.
  2. Place the site-to-site requirement. Connecting the company's own sites to each other over Microsoft's backbone is Global Reach, using the circuits the company already has. This is the add-on people reach for Premium to solve, and Premium is the wrong one.
  3. Place the hundred servers. Policy and security tooling parity is a projection requirement, so it is Arc. It needs no tunnel and no circuit, and it would still be the answer if the data centre had no Azure connectivity at all.
  4. Place the support engineers. Reaching machines with no public addresses is Bastion, which is browser-based and needs no address on the target. This is also not a transport requirement in the sense the first two were.
  5. Count what needs transport. Two of the five. The other three are answered without carrying a packet between the estates, which is the point of separating the two kinds of hybrid.
  6. Price the resilience requirement. A single circuit already comes with dual connections to redundant edge routers, so surviving the loss of a peering location means a second circuit in a different peering location - a second circuit's cost, and a second provider relationship, not a configuration change on the first.

Sanity check. Exactly two of your five answers should involve a circuit or a tunnel. If Arc ended up behind a VPN in your design, ask what the tunnel is carrying - the answer is nothing that Arc needs.
