Azure sells you a place in four sizes, and only one of them is a number you can compare

foundation 11 min Capability area resilience-migration, rows region-az-model and cross-region-replication. · Availability zones overview

The one-minute version

  • The physical model is geography, then region, then availability zone, then datacenter, and a region usually holds three or more zones with independent power and networking.
  • Services divide into two shapes: a zonal service is pinned to one zone you name, and a zone-redundant service spreads itself for you.
  • Zone 1 is not a place. The mapping from the number to the physical zone is assigned per subscription, so two subscriptions in one tenant can mean different buildings by the same number.
  • Cross-region protection is bought as a storage redundancy option, and region pairs replicate asynchronously.
  • The trap is a promise that is not made: ordinary geo-redundant storage carries no published recovery point objective. The fifteen-minute figure people quote belongs to a different, narrower feature.

Four nested sizes of place

The reliability documentation is the per-region authority for that last point, and it is worth checking rather than assuming: zone availability is listed region by region, and it changes. The same asymmetry reaches into compute, where some virtual-machine sizes are zonal-only in newer regions, so a size that exists in a region is not automatically a size you can place anywhere in it.

The number on a zone is local to your subscription

The reframing The zone number is a per-subscription label, not a coordinate. This is one of the few Azure behaviours that is deterministic, documented and still routinely got wrong, because everything about the interface invites you to read the number as global.

Buying survival: the redundancy ladder

Above the zone, resilience stops being a placement choice and becomes a product choice. For storage it is bought as a redundancy option on the account, and the ladder has four rungs plus two read variants.

OptionCopies keptWhat it is meant to survive
LRSWithin one location in the primary regionHardware failure inside that location
ZRSAcross the availability zones of the primary regionThe loss of one zone
GRSLocally redundant in the primary, replicated to the paired regionThe loss of the primary region
GZRSZone-redundant in the primary, replicated to the paired regionThe loss of one zone, and the loss of the region
RA-GRS, RA-GZRSAs above, with the secondary readableThe same, and adds read access before any failover

Two properties of the cross-region rungs decide how they are used. Replication to the paired region is asynchronous, and account failover is customer-triggered rather than automatic. So a geo-redundant account is not a hot standby that switches itself: it is a second copy, some unknown distance behind, that you decide to promote.

The promise that is not made

Region pairs carry one more property that is easy to over-read and one that is easy to under-read. Platform maintenance is rolled out to one member of a pair at a time, which is a genuine benefit. But some newer regions are unpaired by design, so a design that assumes "every region has a pair" will meet a region where the cross-region rungs of the ladder simply are not the answer, and the workload needs its own replication instead.

Knowing when a place is unwell

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

Two teams in two subscriptions of one tenant both deploy into "zone 2" of the same region, aiming to share a physical failure domain. What is wrong with that plan?
The mapping from the logical number to the physical zone is assigned per subscription, so identical numbers in two subscriptions may name different zones. The first option invents a rotation that does not happen. The third confuses the zonal and zone-redundant shapes. The fourth names the right mechanism at the wrong level, which is the most common version of this error.
A design states a recovery point objective of fifteen minutes and cites the account's GZRS setting as the evidence. What is the defect?
The fifteen-minute figure belongs to Geo priority replication for block blobs, not to the redundancy letter. The first option is wrong because the G in GZRS is the cross-region half. The second inverts the documented asynchronous behaviour. The fourth invents a per-container setting.
Which pairing of feed and question is correct?
Service Health is the subscription-scoped feed of platform events and it names your affected resources. The first two options swap the two feeds. The third credits the unauthenticated status page with knowledge of your estate that it does not have.

Practice about 20 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A finance application must survive the loss of one availability zone with no data loss, and must survive the loss of its whole region with a stated, defensible recovery point. It stores its documents as block blobs. The chosen region is new and has no paired region.

Design the storage placement: name the redundancy option you would choose for the in-region requirement, say what the unpaired region does to the cross-region requirement, and state what you would have to build yourself.

Hint

Split the two requirements before choosing anything. Ask which rung of the ladder answers the zone requirement, then ask which rungs are even available in a region with no pair.

Show the worked solution
  1. Answer the zone requirement. Zone-redundant storage spreads copies across the availability zones of the primary region, which is exactly the loss-of-one-zone case. ZRS is the rung that answers it.
  2. Test the cross-region requirement against the region. The geo rungs replicate to the region's pair. Some newer regions are unpaired by design, so in this region GRS and GZRS are not on the menu, and the ladder stops at ZRS.
  3. Check the recovery point claim separately. Even where a pair exists, ordinary geo-redundant replication publishes no recovery point objective, so it could not have carried a "stated, defensible" number anyway. Only a feature that states one can, and Geo priority replication for block blobs is the one that does.
  4. State what is left to build. Cross-region protection becomes your own replication: copy the blobs to an account in a second region on a schedule you control, and derive the recovery point from that schedule, because the number is now yours to measure rather than the platform's to promise.

Sanity check. The answer should contain one platform feature and one thing you own. If the whole design is platform features, the unpaired region was not taken seriously; if none of it is, the zone requirement is being solved the hard way.
