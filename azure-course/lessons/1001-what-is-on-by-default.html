Module 10 · Observability and audit · Lesson 1001

Two streams arrive without asking, and both delete themselves

working 12 min Capability area audit-telemetry, rows control-plane-audit-log and identity-provider-audit-log, with metrics-store. · Azure Activity Log

The one-minute version

  • Platform metrics are on, free and need no configuration: one-minute numeric series per resource, held for 93 days, with a single chart query capped at a 30-day window.
  • The Activity Log is on by default and immutable: control-plane operations with the caller and the time, held 90 days and then deleted.
  • It does not capture reads, and it does not capture data-plane operations at all - so "who read this file" is never answerable from it.
  • The creator identity of a resource lives only there, which makes exporting it before the ninety days elapse a genuine forensic decision.
  • The identity streams have the shortest clock in the platform: seven days on the free tier and thirty on premium, in the portal, and nothing longer exists unless export was armed beforehand.

On, off, and the clocks

How long anything survives

The reframing The Activity Log is a control-plane record, and only that. It captures creates, updates and deletes with the caller and the timestamp; it does not usually capture reads, and it does not capture data-plane operations at all. So it answers "who deleted the storage account" perfectly and "who downloaded the blob" not at all - that second question belongs to a resource log that somebody had to enable.

Two traps in the control-plane record

Latency is worth carrying too, because it decides how an alert feels. The control-plane record is three to twenty minutes end to end. Metrics reach their store in under a minute, and take about three more to appear in an exported copy. Resource logs are typically three to ten minutes. An alert built on the control-plane record is therefore a minutes-class alert whatever its evaluation frequency is set to.

The creator identity of a resource exists only in the Activity Log, and the log deletes itself after ninety days. So the answer to "who created this thing" has a hard expiry unless the stream was exported into a workspace or a storage account beforehand. This is the clearest example of a general rule in this module: the platform's defaults are enough to operate on and not enough to investigate with.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

An investigation needs to know who downloaded a particular blob four months ago. Nothing was configured. What is available?
Data-plane access needs a resource log, which is off until enabled, and even the control-plane record would have expired by then. The first option misstates what the Activity Log covers. The third confuses a numeric series with an attributable event. The fourth invents a default retention.
Why is a single management-group diagnostic setting an inadequate tenant-wide audit feed?
Resource-provider events outside Resource Manager are not included, so the feed has a documented gap. The second, third and fourth all invent mechanisms - and the duplicate behaviour is the opposite of the third: overlapping settings produce duplicates that you must remove yourself.
Which of these has the shortest default retention?
Seven days on the free tier is the shortest clock on this page, and it covers the stream an identity investigation starts from. The other three are ninety-three, ninety and thirty days respectively.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

An organisation is told by its auditor that it must be able to answer three questions about any event in the last year: who created a given resource, who signed in from an unexpected location, and who read a particular file. Nothing beyond the platform defaults has ever been configured, and the identity tenant is on the free tier.

Answer the auditor: say for each question what is available today, what would have to be configured, and which of the three has the tightest deadline for acting.

Hint

For each question, first name the stream that would carry the answer, then check whether that stream is on and how long it keeps anything. One of the three has a clock measured in days rather than months.

Show the worked solution
  1. Who created a resource. The Activity Log carries the creator identity, it is on by default, and it is the only place that identity lives. Today it answers the question for the last ninety days and not for a year.
  2. Fix that one. Export the Activity Log to a workspace or a storage account. Its ingestion into a workspace rides free, and once exported it can be held far beyond ninety days.
  3. Who signed in from an unexpected location. The identity sign-in stream carries this, viewing is free, and on the free tier the portal holds seven days. A year is not remotely available.
  4. Fix that one. Arm identity diagnostic settings so the audit and sign-in streams are exported. Note that this is the fix that also has to be paid for in ingestion, and that risky sign-in analysis has its own tier requirement.
  5. Who read a particular file. This is data-plane access, which the control-plane record never captures. It needs the storage service's own resource log category, which is off by default, so there is nothing at all for the past.
  6. Fix that one. Enable the relevant resource log category through a diagnostic setting, and accept that the pipeline is best-effort, so a completeness claim needs the durable path from lesson 1000.
  7. Rank the deadlines. The identity stream is the most urgent by a wide margin: on the free tier its portal window is a week, so every day of delay permanently loses a day of evidence. The control-plane record gives ninety days of grace, and the data-plane record has no history to lose because it was never collected.

Sanity check. Exactly one of the three should be answerable today for a useful window, and exactly one should have no history at all. If all three looked configurable at leisure, the seven-day identity clock has been missed.
