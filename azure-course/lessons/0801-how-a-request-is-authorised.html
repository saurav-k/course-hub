Authorisation is additive, and the only subtraction belongs to the platform

working 12 min Capability area identity-workforce, rows iam-roles and iam-policy-language, with the recorded absence permission-boundary. · Azure RBAC

The one-minute version

  • A deny assignment short-circuits everything. If one matches, the request is blocked before any role assignment is considered - and customers cannot create them.
  • Role assignments are purely additive. Overlapping assignments union their permissions, and no assignment ever takes a permission away from another.
  • Subtraction exists only inside one role definition: effective permissions are Actions minus NotActions, and the same for the data plane.
  • An assignment may carry conditions, and those are evaluated after the permission is found rather than before.
  • Azure has no permission-boundary object. Three narrower tools come close, and each of them is narrower in a different way.

The waterfall

effective = (Actions − NotActions), and separately (DataActions − NotDataActions) Read as: within one role definition, the permissions that actually apply are those listed in Actions with any listed in NotActions removed. The control plane and the data plane are computed the same way and kept separate, and every list is matched through wildcards.
The reframing Subtraction is inside a definition, not across assignments. A NotAction narrows the role it belongs to and says nothing about any other role the principal holds. So a principal assigned a narrowed role and a broad role has the broad role's permissions, and the narrowing achieved nothing at all - which is the most common way a carefully written custom role turns out to do nothing.

The subtraction the platform keeps for itself

What a role definition is, and where custom ones bite

There is no permission-boundary object on Azure - nothing that constrains what grants an administrator may make. Three tools come close and each falls short differently: platform-created deny assignments stop actions but you cannot author them; the DenyAction policy effect blocks specific operations but is a policy rather than a limit on delegation; and NotActions narrow one definition and nothing else. A design that assumes a boundary exists will build on one of the three and discover the gap at the wrong moment.

Two systems that answer after this one

Azure Policy evaluates independently of role-based access control, so a fully authorised create or update is still refused if the resulting resource breaks a rule, and the DenyAction effect blocks operations such as deletion regardless of what the caller was granted. Resource locks sit outside role-based access control as well. Ordering those subsystems after the waterfall above is a teaching sequence rather than a documented one: the steps inside the waterfall are documented platform behaviour, and the arrangement of the surrounding systems is this course's way of sequencing the material. Module 09 takes policy properly.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

A principal holds a broad role and a custom role whose NotActions exclude deletion. Can it delete?
Subtraction is internal to a definition, so the broad role still grants deletion and the union includes it. The first two options both treat NotActions as if they acted across assignments, which is exactly the misconception. The third is a real step, but conditions refine an assignment rather than reintroducing subtraction.
Which statement about deny assignments is correct?
They are platform-created and short-circuit the evaluation. The first option describes the permission boundary Azure does not have. The third inverts the order that gives them their power. The fourth confuses them with a role definition, which they are not.
A subscription cannot be moved out from under a management group. What is a documented cause?
A custom role's assignable scopes bind it to a place in the tree, and moving out from under that place breaks the path and errors. The other three describe real mechanisms that block other things - none of them is the documented cause of this specific failure.

Practice about 20 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A platform team is asked to guarantee three things. Subscription owners must not be able to delete a set of shared networking resources. A team of contractors must be able to do everything an Owner can except assign roles. And a delegated administrator must be prevented from granting anybody more access than the administrator holds themselves. The team proposes a custom role with NotActions for the second, and says the first and third are "the same problem".

Assess it: work each requirement through the waterfall, say which are achievable and how, and say plainly which one is not.

Hint

For each requirement, ask which step of the waterfall would have to do the work. Two of them need a step that can say no; one of them needs a step that does not exist.

Show the worked solution
  1. Work the first requirement. Stopping an Owner needs the deny check, because everything after it is additive and an Owner's assignment already covers deletion. Deny assignments are not customer-creatable, so the achievable route is a mechanism that causes Azure to create one - a deployment stack with deny settings on the managed resources.
  2. Work the second requirement. A custom role with Actions covering the Owner surface and NotActions excluding role assignment does express this, and it works only if the contractors hold no other assignment. Any second assignment unions in and can restore what the NotActions removed.
  3. State the condition the proposal missed. So the second requirement is achievable, but as a statement about the contractors' whole set of assignments rather than about the custom role. That is an operational commitment, not a definition.
  4. Work the third requirement. Limiting what grants an administrator may make is a permission boundary, and Azure has no such object. Deny assignments cannot be authored, DenyAction blocks named operations rather than constraining delegation, and NotActions narrow one definition. None of the three is a boundary.
  5. Correct the team's claim. The first and third are not the same problem. The first is about actions on resources and is reachable through a platform-created deny. The third is about the power to grant, and there is nothing on the platform that expresses it.
  6. Say what to do about the third. Since it cannot be enforced by the authorisation system, it moves to a different control: who holds User Access Administrator at all, how that is granted, and detection of new assignments through the control-plane record that module 10 covers.

Sanity check. Exactly one of the three should be impossible as stated, and your answer to the second should include a condition about other assignments. If the custom role alone was declared sufficient, the additive union step was skipped.
