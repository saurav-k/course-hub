A landing zone is a skeleton with a pipeline attached

working 11 min Capability area org-tenancy, rows landing-zone, subscription-vending and service-catalog. · Azure landing zones

The one-minute version

  • The reference skeleton splits a platform landing zone - connectivity, identity and management subscriptions - from application landing zones where workloads live.
  • Two more management groups complete it and are the ones people leave out: Sandbox and Decommissioned.
  • The Connectivity subscription holds DNS, firewalls and gateways centrally, which is why lesson 0702's ownership argument was a landing-zone argument all along.
  • Subscription vending is the pipeline: a subscription is issued with budget, policy, network and access already applied, rather than created by ticket and configured afterwards.
  • A service catalogue extends the same idea to workloads: a managed application ships an opinionated stack in a resource group the consumer cannot edit directly.

The skeleton

The reframing The reference architecture ships two connectivity topologies, hub-and-spoke and Virtual WAN, which are exactly the two shapes lesson 0702 compared. Choosing between them is a landing-zone decision made once for the estate rather than a networking decision made per workload, and it belongs to whoever owns the Connectivity subscription - which is why that subscription is a real ownership boundary rather than a folder.

How a subscription arrives

Both the skeleton and the pipeline ship as accelerators - portal experiences and Bicep or Terraform modules - so an organisation adopts a reference implementation and changes what genuinely differs, rather than authoring a hierarchy from a diagram.

The same idea, one level down

A hierarchy is not free of constraints, and two from lesson 0101 shape vending directly. The control plane caches hierarchy state for up to thirty minutes, so a freshly placed subscription is not instantly visible everywhere and a pipeline that immediately asserts something about its position can race. And a custom role defined at a management group binds subscriptions to that branch: moving one out later fails rather than degrading, so where a custom role is defined is a decision about future flexibility.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

Why does the reference skeleton split landing zones into Corp and Online?
The split exists so each population can carry rules suited to it. The first two describe other splits organisations sometimes make, neither of which is what this one is for. The third invents a capacity motivation for a governance structure.
What does subscription vending change about when governance is applied?
Placement and configuration happen before the team receives the subscription, which is what removes drift. The other three all describe applying governance after the fact, which is exactly the operating model vending replaces.
What distinguishes a managed application from a deployment environment?
The audience is the difference: managed applications are a publishing model, and deployment environments target internal application teams. The first invents a code-against-infrastructure split. The second is false - neither escapes policy. The fourth confuses both with vending.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

An organisation has forty subscriptions created over three years, all directly under the tenant root group. Networking is done per subscription with its own gateway. A new subscription is created by raising a ticket, after which an operations engineer works through a page of setup steps. Two subscriptions are for experiments and one belongs to a product that was shut down last year.

Plan the move: name the management groups you would create and what goes where, say what changes about networking, and say what replaces the ticket.

Hint

Build the skeleton first and only then decide where the existing forty go. Two of the forty have obvious homes that most plans forget to create.

Show the worked solution
  1. Create the skeleton. Platform with Connectivity, Identity and Management beneath it; Landing zones with Corp and Online beneath it; plus Sandbox and Decommissioned as siblings.
  2. Place the two experiment subscriptions. Sandbox. That is what the node is for, and it is what keeps experimental work from either being blocked by production guardrails or forcing those guardrails to be loosened.
  3. Place the shut-down product. Decommissioned. It stops being counted as part of the live estate, and it stops inheriting rules that are about running workloads.
  4. Sort the remaining thirty-seven. Corp or Online, by whether the workload faces the internet, so each branch can carry policy written for its own exposure rather than for the looser of the two.
  5. Create the platform subscriptions. A Connectivity subscription owns DNS, the firewalls and the gateways centrally, which replaces forty per-subscription gateways with one shared connectivity estate - and makes the hub-and-spoke against Virtual WAN choice a single decision for the organisation.
  6. Replace the ticket. A vending pipeline, triggered from the request system, that creates the subscription under the right node with its budget, policy assignments, network connection and role assignments already applied. The page of setup steps disappears because there is nothing left to do after handover.
  7. Watch two constraints. The hierarchy cache means the pipeline should not assume a move is instantly visible everywhere, and any custom role defined on a branch binds subscriptions to that branch, so define them where you are willing for subscriptions to stay.

Sanity check. Your plan should have found homes for all forty subscriptions and created at least one node that currently holds nothing. If Sandbox and Decommissioned are missing, the experiments and the dead product are still inheriting production rules.

How this connects

  • Next, knowing what you have is how an estate this shape is inventoried and how drift in it is detected.
  • Lesson 0702 made the case for a connectivity subscription from the networking side, and this page is where that ownership boundary comes from.
Your teacher. If the skeleton looks like bureaucracy, ask an agent which policy assignments would have to be duplicated across forty subscriptions to achieve the same effect without it. The count is the argument, and it grows every time a subscription is added.
