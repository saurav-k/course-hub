Three planes, two stores, and one object that routes almost everything

working 12 min Capability area observability, rows metrics-store and log-store, with data-plane-access-log from audit-telemetry. · Diagnostic settings

The one-minute version

  • Telemetry decomposes into three planes - metrics, platform logs, and agent and application streams - converging on two stores plus a third for Prometheus.
  • The stores are the Azure Monitor metrics time-series database and Log Analytics workspaces, queried by two different languages against two different clocks.
  • Almost every stream reaches a destination through a diagnostic setting, whose destinations are a workspace, a storage account, an event hub or a partner solution - one of each type per setting.
  • There is a hard cap of five diagnostic settings per resource, which makes multi-team log fan-out a design problem rather than a configuration task.
  • After creating a setting, expect up to ninety minutes before data flows. Investigating an empty destination inside that window is investigating nothing.

Three planes, and where each one lands

The object almost everything flows through

The reframing A diagnostic setting is coarse on purpose. Its granularity is the log category, and there is no filter for individual entries inside a category - lesson 0900 already noted that policy cannot supply one either. Trimming what is kept happens after ingestion, through workspace transformations, which means the volume decision and the retention decision are made at two different places in the pipeline.

What the pipeline does not promise

Two more traps sit around the edges. Metric dimensions are flattened on export, so per-node analysis of a multidimensional metric is impossible in the workspace copy and has to be done against the metrics store itself. And a deleted, renamed or moved resource can leave an orphaned diagnostic setting that silently reattaches to a resource recreated with the same identity - so a stream you thought you had removed can come back on its own.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

Four teams each want a resource's logs in their own workspace, and a partner tool wants them too. What is the constraint?
The cap is five settings per resource, and each setting takes at most one destination of each type - so five separate workspaces is the ceiling and fan-out beyond it happens downstream. The other three invent constraints that do not exist and would each rule out ordinary configurations.
A compliance requirement says no audit record may be lost. Which routing is defensible?
The vendor documents that small losses may occur in the store-and-forward pipeline, so durability is built downstream of it. The first and third options change the destination without changing the pipeline. The fourth assumes losses are independent per destination, which the documented behaviour does not support.
A per-node metric looks right in the metrics explorer and is unusable in the workspace. Why?
Export flattens dimensions, which is exactly what removes the per-node view. The first and second invent transformations that do not happen. The fourth confuses platform metrics with guest telemetry, which is a different plane.

Practice about 20 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A platform must satisfy four consumers of one busy service's telemetry: a security team wanting audit categories retained for years, an operations team wanting everything for a month, a data team wanting raw events for analysis, and a partner monitoring tool. Somebody proposes four diagnostic settings, one per consumer, and adds a fifth later for a compliance archive. Two weeks after the settings are created, the security team reports that a handful of records they expected are missing.

Design it properly: say what is wrong with one setting per consumer, propose a routing, and explain the missing records.

Hint

Count the settings against the cap, and count the destinations of each type inside one setting. Then ask which of the four consumers actually needs its own copy from the source, and which could be served from somebody else's copy.

Show the worked solution
  1. Count against the cap. Five settings is the maximum per resource, so the proposal reaches the ceiling exactly and leaves no room for the next consumer. That is a design with no headroom on a limit that cannot be raised.
  2. Use the destinations inside one setting. A single setting can carry one workspace, one storage account and one event hub at once. So one setting can serve the operations workspace, the archive in storage and the event hub feeding the data team and the partner tool - which collapses most of the proposal into one object.
  3. Fan out downstream. The event hub is the fan-out point: the data team consumes it, the partner tool consumes it, and anything else that appears later consumes it too, without touching the resource's settings again.
  4. Serve the security requirement. Route the audit category group to the workspace and hold it under a long retention there, or export it onward. Choosing the audit category group rather than naming categories means a category added later is picked up automatically.
  5. Explain the missing records. Resource logs are a store-and-forward pipeline in which small losses may occur by design. A handful of missing records is documented behaviour rather than a fault, and no amount of reconfiguration removes it.
  6. Fix the security team's guarantee. If completeness is a requirement rather than a preference, it cannot rest on resource logs. Build it on event-hub capture or on workspace data export, and say plainly in the design that the diagnostic-setting path is best-effort.
  7. Watch the timing. If any of this is being tested immediately after configuration, remember the ninety-minute wait before data flows - and that a setting for a recreated resource may have reattached from an earlier deployment.

Sanity check. Your final design should use fewer than five settings and should serve at least two consumers from one downstream copy. If you still have one setting per consumer, the three-destinations-per-setting property has not been used.
