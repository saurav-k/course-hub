A guardrail is shipped, not switched on

working 10 min Capability area governance-policy, rows policy-as-code and compliance-pack. · Azure Policy as Code

The one-minute version

  • Definitions, initiatives and assignments are authored in a repository, validated in continuous integration, and deployed through a pipeline like any other change.
  • The documented rollout is a ramp: audit, then auditIfNotExists, then deny once the blast radius has been measured.
  • Exemptions are auditable objects with an expiry, not a quiet edit to a rule. That is what keeps a temporary exception temporary.
  • Built-in compliance packs map controls to recognised standards, so an estate does not author a control library from scratch.
  • The security posture product layers a pass-and-fail dashboard on the same evaluations, so compliance reporting and guardrail enforcement are one system seen twice.

The ramp

The reframing An exemption is a resource, not an edit. It is a first-class object with an expiry, so a granted exception can be listed, reviewed and made to end. That is the whole difference between an estate whose rules have known exceptions and one whose rules have quietly been narrowed until they permit everything - and it is why an exclusion on the assignment is a blunter instrument than an exemption.

Where the pipeline fits

Not writing the control library yourself

A pack is a starting point and not an answer. Its initiative counts against the per-scope limits from lesson 0900, its definitions are written for a general estate rather than yours, and adopting one in deny mode without the ramp is the fastest way to stop an entire organisation deploying. Assign it as audit, read the result as a survey of your own estate, and promote selectively.

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

What decides when an audit-mode policy should be promoted to deny?
Promotion is a blast-radius decision, and the audit mode exists to produce that number. The second option is the trap: a rule can be agreed and still break hundreds of deployments. The third and fourth substitute elapsed time for a measurement.
Why is an exemption preferable to quietly narrowing a rule?
Being a first-class object with an expiry is what makes an exception reviewable and temporary. The first has the scope argument backwards. The second and fourth invent enforcement and persistence differences that are not the reason.
What is the relationship between a compliance dashboard and policy evaluations?
One mechanism, two views - which is why fixing compliance means fixing policy compliance. The first option imagines a second engine. The third and fourth both grant the dashboard powers of its own that it does not have.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

An organisation must show compliance with a published standard by the end of a quarter. A consultant proposes adopting the matching built-in pack at the root management group in deny mode on day one, on the grounds that anything less is not really compliance. The estate has around six hundred resources across thirty subscriptions, built over four years by teams that have never had a guardrail applied to them.

Argue the case: say what happens on day one under the proposal, describe the rollout you would run instead, and say what you would put in the repository alongside the assignments.

Hint

Ask what a deny effect does to an estate whose existing resources have never been measured against the rule, then ask what it does to the teams' ability to work while they fix things.

Show the worked solution
  1. Say what deny does on day one. A deny effect refuses creates and updates. Existing non-compliant resources are not deleted, but every team that touches one is blocked, and nobody knows in advance how many that is - which is precisely the number the proposal has not measured.
  2. Name the second problem. A four-year-old estate with no prior guardrails will be non-compliant in ways nobody has enumerated. The pack's definitions were written for a general estate, so some of its findings will be genuine and some will be a poor fit for this organisation.
  3. Start the rollout with audit. Assign the pack at the root management group in audit mode. Within a compliance cycle the estate has a measured non-compliance list - a survey rather than a guess - and no team has been blocked.
  4. Work the list, then promote selectively. Split the findings into rules the organisation accepts and rules it does not. Promote the accepted ones to deny once their remaining non-compliance is a worked list, and leave the rest at audit while the argument is had.
  5. Handle the genuine exceptions. Where a resource cannot comply in time, create an exemption with an expiry rather than excluding the scope or softening the rule. The exception then has a date attached to it and appears in a review.
  6. Say what goes in the repository. The definitions, the initiative, the assignments with their effects, and the exemptions with their expiry dates - so the compliance position of the estate is readable from one place and every change to it went through review.

Sanity check. Your rollout should produce a number before it produces a refusal. If your first step blocks anybody, the measurement has been skipped and the quarter is likelier to end in rollback than in compliance.
