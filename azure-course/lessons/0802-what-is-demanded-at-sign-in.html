Module 08 · Identity and access · Lesson 0802

Holding a role and being able to use it are two different things

working 11 min Capability area identity-workforce, rows conditional-access, privileged-access and os-login, with ciam-mfa beside them. · Conditional Access

The one-minute version

  • Conditional Access evaluates after the first factor, not instead of it. It reads signals - user, device, location, risk - and decides what else the sign-in must satisfy.
  • Report-only mode is how a policy is rolled out: it records what would have happened without changing any outcome.
  • It applies to users, and to service principals with the workload-identities premium tier, so machine identities can be brought under the same engine.
  • Privileged Identity Management turns a role into something you activate: eligible rather than active, with approval, a second factor and a time box, and access reviews closing the loop.
  • Even operating-system sign-in can be moved here. Entra login for virtual machines authorises through two roles and puts the same policies in front of an SSH or RDP session.

A sign-in, with the engine in the middle

The reframing Report-only mode is the deployment mechanism, not a testing nicety. A policy in report-only records the decision it would have made and changes nothing, so the blast radius of a badly scoped policy can be measured before it is real. A Conditional Access rollout that skips it is a change with no rehearsal, applied to the one system that can lock everybody out of everything.

Second factors, ordered by how well they resist an attacker

Multifactor authentication offers authenticator push with number matching, time-based one-time codes, FIDO2 passkeys, and SMS or voice as fallbacks. The vendor's guidance prioritises the phishing-resistant methods - passkeys and Windows Hello for Business - and number matching exists because a plain push approval can be answered by a tired person who did not initiate it. There are two ways to require any of this: the legacy per-user setting, and a Conditional Access policy. The second is the one that can express a condition.

A role you activate rather than hold

Around it, Entra ID Governance is the wider umbrella: lifecycle workflows for joiners and leavers, access packages that bundle what a role of person needs, and recurring attestation campaigns that ask an owner whether an access still makes sense. Access reviews are what stop an estate accumulating eligibility that nobody remembers granting.

The same engine, in front of a machine's console

Two of these capabilities are licence-gated, and a design that assumes them without checking is a design with a purchase order in it. Conditional Access requires the P1 tier; Privileged Identity Management and the governance umbrella require P2; and applying Conditional Access to service principals requires the workload-identities premium tier on top. None of this is visible in an architecture diagram, and all of it is visible on an invoice.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

When does a Conditional Access policy evaluate?
Evaluation happens after the first factor and before the token. The first option would make it a password control, which it is not. The third describes revocation, a different mechanism. The fourth confuses it with the authorisation waterfall from the previous lesson.
What does an eligible assignment grant before it is activated?
Eligibility grants nothing until activation, which is what makes the ordinary state of the account powerless. The first two options both leak some standing permission, which would defeat the model. The third describes what activation does, not what eligibility is.
Which tier is needed to apply Conditional Access to a service principal?
Service principals need the workload-identities premium tier. The second and third options assume parity with users, which the licensing does not grant. The fourth attaches the requirement to the wrong product.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

An incident review finds that an attacker used a stolen password, approved a push notification the victim tapped without reading, and then used a standing subscription Owner assignment to delete resources. The victim was a platform engineer who administers Linux machines by SSH using a key stored on their laptop. The organisation asks for four changes, and wants to know which ones can be rehearsed before they take effect.

Propose the changes: name the mechanism for each step of the attack, and say which of them can be rolled out without risking a lockout.

Hint

Walk the attack in order and stop at each step to ask which control would have broken the chain there. Two of the four are the same product configured differently, and only that product has a rehearsal mode.

Show the worked solution
  1. Break the password step. A stolen password alone should not be enough, so a Conditional Access policy requires a second factor. It evaluates after the first factor, which is exactly where this attack succeeded.
  2. Break the push approval. A push that can be approved without context is the weakness, so move the second factor towards the phishing-resistant methods the guidance prioritises - passkeys or Windows Hello for Business - and use number matching where push remains.
  3. Break the standing Owner assignment. The attacker found a role already active. Under Privileged Identity Management the assignment becomes eligible rather than active, so activation would have required approval, another factor and a justification, and would have expired on its own.
  4. Break the stored SSH key. Replace it with Entra login for virtual machines, where the two login roles decide the outcome, the credential is short-lived, and Conditional Access applies to the session itself. The key on the laptop stops being a credential worth stealing because it stops existing.
  5. Say what can be rehearsed. Only the Conditional Access changes have a rehearsal mode: report-only records what a policy would have decided and changes nothing. The eligibility change, the authentication-method change and the machine-login change all take effect when they are made.
  6. Note the licensing consequence. Two tiers appear: P1 for Conditional Access and P2 for Privileged Identity Management. That is part of the proposal, not a detail to be discovered afterwards.

Sanity check. Your four changes should break the chain at four different points rather than hardening one of them twice. If two of your changes both address the password, the standing-assignment step is still open and the attack still ends the same way.
