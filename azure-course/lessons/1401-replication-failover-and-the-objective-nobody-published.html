The recovery point objective most designs quote does not belong to the feature they are using

working 12 min Capability area resilience-migration, rows cross-region-replication and dr-orchestration. · Storage redundancy

The one-minute version

  • Region pairs replicate asynchronously, so the secondary is normally behind the primary by some amount.
  • Ordinary geo-redundant storage carries no published recovery point objective. The often-quoted fifteen-minute figure belongs to a narrower priority replication feature for block blobs.
  • Pairs receive platform maintenance one member at a time, and some newer regions are unpaired by design - so pairing is not something to assume.
  • Account failover is customer-triggered: the platform does not decide for you, and read access from the secondary is a separate, extra-cost option.
  • Site Recovery is the orchestration layer: continuous replication plus recovery plans that sequence a multi-machine start-up, with a test failover that proves it.

The number, and where it actually comes from

The reframing Pairing is a property of a region, not a guarantee of the platform. Paired regions receive platform maintenance one member at a time, which is a real availability benefit, and some newer regions are unpaired by design. So "we will fail over to the paired region" is a sentence to verify against the specific regions in the design rather than a pattern that always applies.

What happens when a region goes

Lesson 0102 introduced the redundancy letters and lesson 0501 met their database equivalents. The addition here is what happens at the moment of failure, and the answer is that in-region redundancy is handled by the platform while cross-region failover is a decision somebody makes.

Orchestrating more than a copy

Two objectives are easy to conflate and this module keeps them apart. A recovery point objective is how much data you can afford to lose, and on this page it is bounded by replication - which is why the unpublished figure matters. A recovery time objective is how long you can afford to be down, and it is bounded by the orchestration and by how long the sequence takes to run. Backup, replication and orchestration each move one of the two, and no single mechanism moves both.

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

What recovery point objective does ordinary geo-redundant storage publish?
There is no published figure for ordinary geo-redundancy. The first is the number that belongs to a narrower block-blob priority feature and is the specific error this page exists to correct. The second describes synchronous replication. The fourth invents an interval.
A region becomes unavailable. What triggers failover of a storage account?
Failover is a customer decision, so a plan needs a decision-maker and a threshold. The second and third hand the decision to the platform. The fourth describes an active-active model this mechanism does not provide.
Why is a recovery plan more than replication?
Ordering and scripting are what turn replicated machines into a working application. The first confuses coverage with orchestration. The third changes the replication model, which a plan does not. The fourth invents automatic failover.

Practice about 20 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A recovery plan states: "Data is protected by geo-redundant storage with a fifteen-minute recovery point objective. On a regional outage the platform fails over to the paired region and the application is available within one hour." The application is four virtual machines - a database, two application servers and a job runner - in a region the team believes is paired. Nothing has ever been tested.

Review the plan: identify every unsupported claim, and say what would have to exist for each one to be true.

Hint

There are four claims in those two sentences. Take them one at a time and ask what the platform actually states about each, and what would have to be built for the sentence to be defensible.

Show the worked solution
  1. The fifteen-minute objective. Unsupported. Ordinary geo-redundancy replicates asynchronously with no published figure, and the fifteen-minute objective belongs to a narrower priority replication feature for block blobs. To make a bounded claim, the design must name that feature and confirm it covers the data in question - otherwise the plan says "asynchronous, unbounded".
  2. The pairing assumption. Unverified. Some newer regions are unpaired by design, so "the paired region" is a fact to check against these specific regions rather than a property every region has.
  3. The platform failing over. Wrong. Account failover is customer-triggered, so the plan needs a named decision-maker, a threshold for making the call, and a rehearsed procedure. As written, nobody is responsible for the single action the whole plan depends on.
  4. Reading the secondary. Worth checking even though the plan does not mention it: read access from the secondary before a failover is a separate option with an extra cost. If the incident response assumes anybody can read the secondary copy while deciding, that has to have been bought in advance.
  5. The one-hour availability claim. Unsupported for a different reason. Replication brings back disks; four machines becoming a working application requires ordering - the database before the application servers, the job runner after both - which is what a recovery plan in the orchestration product expresses, with scripts between the steps.
  6. The untested part. An hour is an estimate until a test failover has produced a measurement. That is the single most valuable thing missing from this plan, because it is the only way the recovery time claim becomes a number rather than a hope.
  7. Rewrite the two sentences. Something closer to: data replicates asynchronously to a secondary with no published loss bound; on an outage a named role triggers account failover; the application is recovered by a tested recovery plan whose measured completion time is a stated figure, remeasured after any change to the application.

Sanity check. You should have found four unsupported claims in two sentences, and your rewrite should contain no number that has not been either published by the vendor or measured by a test. If a number survived from the original plan, check which of the two it was.
