The framework is a skin

The framework is a skin; the layers underneath are the same in every backend.

The connected route from one request to a horizontally scaled fleet, taught as a design you can reason about. Thirteen modules, one idea per page, each mechanism owned rather than waved at. Prose and diagrams are re-authored from first principles, following the arc of the open Backend from First Principles field manual.

Start with the shape of a request, keep the Glossary open in a tab, and read Inside AWS when a mechanism's deployment is the point.

HTTP is the language

6 lessons
Lesson 0200
A request is a method, a target and headers
The frame every framework fills in rather than replaces: three parts that are always present, a body that is optional, and a target that names a resource rather than a file.
foundation8 min
Lesson 0201
Methods are verbs with guarantees
Safe, idempotent and cacheable are promises the whole network reads without asking you, and they are what makes a retry free or dangerous.
foundation7 min
Lesson 0202
Status codes are three families with one job each
The first digit is the contract: 1xx to 3xx say where the request stands, 4xx blames the request, 5xx blames the server, and every client must understand the class.
foundation7 min
Lesson 0203
Headers carry what the body cannot
Four families of field settle everything a body cannot say about itself: what it is, which version you wanted, whether your copy is current, and who is asking.
working7 min
Lesson 0204
Caching is a contract between two clocks
A hit is one comparison: the lifetime the origin published against the age the cache counted. Validators are what make the re-check cost headers instead of a body.
working7 min
Lesson 0205
CORS is the browser enforcing the server's own policy
The server states a policy and enforces nothing. The preflight asks permission before the real request, and a CORS error is a reading refused rather than a request blocked.
working7 min
04

Serialization and contracts

6 lessons
Lesson 0400
A wire format is a contract you can break silently
Two independently deployed programs agree here, and the format checks only its own grammar - so the break arrives as a wrong value rather than as a compiler error.
foundation8 min
Lesson 0401
JSON is text and text has no types
Six kinds of value, and not one of them is a date, a decimal or a 64-bit integer. Every domain type you send is a convention you have to state and keep.
foundation9 min
Lesson 0402
Schema-first makes the contract the source
A .proto file compiles into code both ends import, so the contract becomes a build artefact - and the field number, never the name, is what travels.
working8 min
Lesson 0403
Validation belongs at the boundary, once
Parse untrusted input into a trusted type at the edge. A re-check deeper in the service is a symptom: it says the value arrived carrying no guarantee.
working8 min
Lesson 0404
Content negotiation picks the representation
One resource, many renderings. Accept is a wish about the response and Content-Type is a fact about this body, and the two refusals are 406 and 415.
working8 min
Lesson 0405
Compatibility is a rule, not a hope
Adding a field is safe and reusing a number is corruption, and renaming is free on the binary wire and breaking in JSON, because the encodings key on different things.
working9 min
