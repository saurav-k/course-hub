The difference is who decides where a message goes

working 7 min Capability keys message-queue, pub-sub, event-bus, scheduler-jobs, managed-integration and mqtt-broker. · Amazon SQS

The one-minute version

  • A queue is chosen by the sender, a topic fans out to whoever subscribed, and an event bus routes by matching the content of the event itself.
  • Standard queues are at-least-once with near-unlimited throughput; FIFO queues give exactly-once processing and ordering within a message group, with a high-throughput mode.
  • Queue behaviour is three settings: retention from 60 seconds to 14 days defaulting to four, a visibility timeout defaulting to thirty seconds, and a dead-letter queue driven by a receive count.
  • A message is up to 256 KB, and a client-side extension stores the payload in object storage to carry up to 2 GB.
  • What it costs: a FIFO topic cannot deliver to email, SMS, mobile push or HTTP, so ordering and those delivery targets are mutually exclusive.

1. The mental model: who routes

2. The mechanism: three settings that decide behaviour

Two constraints decide designs. A FIFO topic cannot deliver to email, SMS, mobile push or HTTP endpoints, so a requirement for ordering rules those targets out. And a bus rule reaches five targets, so a fan-out wider than five is several rules rather than one - which is fine, and is worth knowing before drawing the diagram.

3. Time, and protocols you did not choose

A scheduler is the fourth shape: one-time and recurring jobs with cron and rate expressions, an explicit time zone and an optional flexible window, invoking templated targets for common services and universal targets for most other API actions, each with a role you supply and its own retry policy and dead-letter queue. It reacts to time rather than to events, which is what distinguishes it from a bus rule (EventBridge Scheduler).

Three managed brokers exist for protocols chosen by somebody else. Amazon MQ runs ActiveMQ and RabbitMQ, which is how an application speaking those protocols moves without being rewritten. Transfer Family accepts SFTP, FTPS, FTP and AS2 and lands the files in object or file storage (Transfer Family). IoT Core brokers MQTT for devices, with a rules engine routing device data onward - and both its broker and its rules are Region-scoped, so topics are not routable between Regions.
The reframing Ask one question before choosing: does the producer know who should receive this. If yes, a queue. If it knows the list but not the recipients' details, a topic. If it knows neither - and the answer depends on what the event contains - a bus. Everything else on this page is a setting, and every setting is easier to change than a coupling you built in.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

A consumer keeps receiving the same failing message and no other work is done. What is missing?
Redrive to a dead-letter queue after a configured receive count is what removes a poison message from the path. Longer retention makes the problem last longer, ordering does not help a message that cannot be processed, and a filter policy decides what arrives rather than what happens when processing fails.
Which requirement rules out a FIFO topic?
FIFO topics cannot deliver to email, SMS, mobile push or HTTP endpoints, so those targets and ordering are mutually exclusive. Exactly-once processing and per-group ordering are what FIFO provides, and fan-out to several queues works on either topic type.
When is an event bus the right choice over a topic?
Content-based routing across many producers and many consumers is what a bus adds. A single reliable path is a queue, retry behaviour belongs to the consumer's queue, and large payloads are handled by the client-side extension rather than by choosing a bus.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

An order is placed. Four things must happen: the warehouse must be told exactly once and in order per customer, an analytics pipeline must receive every order, the customer must get an email, and a fraud service must see only orders above a threshold. A nightly job must also reconcile the day's orders at 02:00 in the company's own time zone.

Design the messaging, name the constraint that forces a decision, and place the nightly job.

Hint

Ask who decides each destination. Two of the four consumers care about content or ordering, and one of those two collides with a delivery target the requirement also names.

Show the worked solution
  1. Publish once. The producer should not know about four consumers, so it publishes one order event and something else routes. That is the decoupling decision, and everything below follows from it.
  2. Warehouse - a FIFO queue. Exactly-once processing and ordering per customer is precisely per-message-group ordering, with the customer identifier as the group. Standard queues are at-least-once and unordered, so they do not meet this as written.
  3. Name the constraint that forces a decision. The email. A FIFO topic cannot deliver to email, so a single FIFO topic cannot serve both the warehouse and the customer notification. Route the ordered path and the notification path separately - a bus rule or a standard topic for the email, and the FIFO queue fed on its own.
  4. Analytics and fraud - content-based routing. Analytics wants every order and fraud wants only those above a threshold, which is a condition on the event's content. A bus rule per consumer expresses both, and a rule reaches five targets, which is ample here.
  5. The nightly job - the scheduler, not a rule. It reacts to time rather than to an event, so it belongs on a schedule with a cron expression and an explicit time zone - which matters, because the requirement is stated in the company's local time rather than in a fixed offset. Give it its own retry policy and dead-letter queue.

Sanity check. Your design should have exactly one publish from the producer, one ordered path, and the nightly job on a schedule rather than on a bus rule. If a single FIFO topic served both the warehouse and the email, re-read what a FIFO topic may deliver to.
