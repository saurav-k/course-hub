Three kinds of control, acting at three different moments

working 7 min Capability keys landing-zone, service-catalog and policy-as-code. · AWS Control Tower

The one-minute version

  • A landing zone packages the tree, the guardrails and the accounts into something you apply rather than assemble - Control Tower is the opinionated form of it.
  • Its controls come in three kinds, and the difference is when they act: preventive guardrails at the API call, proactive hooks before a resource is provisioned, and detective rules after the fact.
  • Controls are labelled mandatory, strongly recommended or elective, so the argument about which to enable starts from a position rather than from nothing.
  • Account Factory vends accounts into the right organisational unit with the controls already applied, and drift detection reports when a landing zone diverges from what it should be.
  • What it costs: guardrails that only say no stop teams working, so Service Catalog provides approved products people may launch themselves, constrained by a launch role rather than by their own permissions.

2. The mechanism: vending, and drift

The detective side is Config rules and conformance packs: managed and custom rules, proactive evaluation, and org-wide packs that bundle rules together with their remediations, from a library of more than a hundred prebuilt templates (evaluating resources with Config rules). This is also the go-forward compliance tool as the automated evidence-collection service enters maintenance, which lesson 0903 returns to.

3. Letting teams move inside the guardrails

The reframing Governance that only subtracts produces shadow estates. The working pattern is a tight ceiling paired with a generous catalogue: the guardrails say what may never happen, the catalogue says what can happen immediately, and the gap between them is the queue of requests a platform team has to work through. Widening the catalogue is usually cheaper than loosening the ceiling.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

Which control type reports on resources that existed before the policy was written?
Detective controls evaluate existing resources, which is the only way to learn about what was created earlier. Preventive controls act on new API calls, proactive hooks act on templates being submitted, and applying controls at a unit changes their reach rather than making them retrospective.
A developer must create a compliant database without being allowed to create databases generally. What delivers that?
A launch constraint provisions the product using a role the launcher does not hold, which grants the outcome without granting the permission. A boundary can only narrow what the developer already has, a detective rule acts after creation, and a guardrail restricting the engine still requires the developer to be able to create one.
What does landing-zone drift detection actually report?
Drift detection compares the landing zone against what it is supposed to be and reports divergence. Quota use, template rejections and budget variance are all real signals that come from other services entirely.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A platform team must enforce one rule: every S3 bucket in the organisation blocks public access. The estate already has 4,000 buckets, developers create new ones through CloudFormation daily, and a few teams create them by hand in the console.

Design the enforcement using all three control types, say what each one covers that the others do not, and name the one thing none of them fixes on its own.

Hint

Take the three moments in order - before provisioning, at the call, after the fact - and ask which of the three populations of bucket each one reaches. The population that is left over is the answer to the last part.

Show the worked solution
  1. Preventive, at the API call. A guardrail denying the API calls that make a bucket public. This covers the console creators and the templates alike, because both end at the same API, and it is the only one of the three that an account administrator cannot switch off locally.
  2. Proactive, before provisioning. A CloudFormation hook rejecting a template that declares a public bucket. This one gives the developer a failure at submission, with the offending line named, rather than a denied call in the middle of a stack deployment - a difference in feedback quality rather than in coverage.
  3. Detective, after the fact. A Config rule evaluating every existing bucket. This is the only control that reaches the 4,000 buckets that already exist, and a conformance pack can carry a remediation alongside the rule so that non-compliance is corrected rather than merely reported.
  4. Say what each covers uniquely. Only the detective rule sees the existing estate. Only the proactive hook stops the work before anything is created. Only the preventive guardrail cannot be bypassed from inside the account.
  5. Name what none of them fixes. A bucket created in an account that is not in the organisation, or not in an organisational unit the controls are applied to. Enforcement is inherited, so an account outside the tree is outside all three - which is why account vending, not control design, is the real first step.

Sanity check. Your answer should assign the existing 4,000 buckets to the detective control and nothing else, and your final answer should be about where accounts come from rather than about buckets. If all three controls covered the same population in your answer, re-read when each one acts.
