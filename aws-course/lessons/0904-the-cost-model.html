Cost tooling does three different jobs, and only one of them acts

working 7 min Capability keys cost-management, commitment-discounts and cost-advisory. · Cost Explorer

The one-minute version

  • Cost Explorer explains the past and projects the future: about thirteen months of history and an eighteen-month forecast.
  • Budgets come in six types and are the only cost tool that can take an action - a budget action can attach a restrictive IAM policy when a threshold is crossed.
  • Anomaly detection runs three times a day and reports unusual spend, with one documented blind spot: it skips third-party Marketplace spend.
  • Data Exports is the successor to the legacy cost and usage report, with SQL-selectable columns, a FOCUS-format option and recurring delivery to S3.
  • What it costs: rightsizing advice needs about fourteen days of metric history before it stabilises, so a workload changed last week cannot be rightsized this week.

1. The mental model: three jobs

2. The mechanism: how far the window reaches

Data Exports is that export. It supersedes the legacy cost and usage report, lets you select columns with SQL, offers a FOCUS-format output for tooling that expects the open standard, and delivers on a recurring schedule to S3 - where lesson 0601's query engine reads it (Data Exports). The legacy report is still available as a legacy export type, so an existing pipeline does not break.

3. Reacting, and the one control that acts

Two coverage limits worth writing on the runbook. Anomaly detection does not cover third-party Marketplace spend, so a surprise from a Marketplace subscription arrives on the invoice rather than as an alert. And full Trusted Advisor check coverage requires a Business or higher support plan - the core security and service-limit checks are available on basic plans, and the cost-optimisation checks that people usually want are not (Trusted Advisor).

The reduce side has two advisers. Compute Optimizer produces rightsizing recommendations for EC2, EBS volumes, Lambda functions, ECS services on Fargate and scaling groups from observed utilisation, and needs about fourteen days of metric history before those recommendations settle; organisation-wide analysis runs through a delegated administrator account (Compute Optimizer). Trusted Advisor is broader and shallower, covering cost, performance, security, fault tolerance, service limits and operations, with an organisational view across member accounts.

The reframing Rightsizing and commitment discounts pull in opposite directions and have to be sequenced. Rightsize first, commit second: a three-year commitment sized against an over-provisioned fleet locks in the over-provisioning, and lesson 0204's discount ceiling is calculated against whatever you actually run.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

Which cost mechanism can stop spending without anyone reading an alert?
Budget actions are the only mechanism here that changes permissions when a threshold is crossed. Anomaly detection reports rather than intervenes, Cost Explorer is an analysis surface, and Trusted Advisor produces recommendations for a human to act on.
A Marketplace subscription doubles the monthly bill and no anomaly alert fires. Why?
Anomaly detection skips third-party Marketplace spend, which is a documented coverage gap rather than a delay. The fourteen-day figure belongs to rightsizing recommendations, evaluation runs three times a day, and Marketplace charges do appear on the consolidated invoice.
A team wants to compare this September with last September. What makes that possible, or not?
Thirteen months of history is exactly what makes a same-month year-on-year comparison possible, with very little margin. The window is not six months, an export is what you need for anything older, and budgets have no bearing on what history is queryable.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A company's bill has grown 40 percent in a year with no matching growth in traffic. Nobody can say which team is responsible. The finance director wants a three-year commitment signed this quarter to bring the number down, and an engineering manager wants a guarantee that no team can ever surprise them again.

Write the sequence of work, say why the commitment must not be signed first, and name what the engineering manager is really asking for and where it can safely be applied.

Hint

Nothing here can be answered until spend is attributable. Then there is an ordering rule on this page that decides where the commitment sits in the sequence, and one control that is genuinely preventive rather than informative.

Show the worked solution
  1. Attribute first. No question here is answerable while spend is unattributed, so the sequence starts with the previous lesson's work: an organisation tag policy, bulk tagging, and activation of the cost allocation tag - remembering the roughly one-day delay and that closed months will not be retagged.
  2. Explain second. With tags active, use the thirteen-month history to find which teams and services grew, and turn on the detailed export so that next year's version of this question has data older than the window.
  3. Rightsize third. Run the rightsizing recommendations, allowing about fourteen days of metric history to accumulate before trusting them, and act on the clear cases. Growth without matching traffic is exactly the shape that produces over-provisioning.
  4. Commit fourth, and say why the order matters. A three-year commitment is sized against what you actually run. Signed before rightsizing, it locks in the over-provisioned fleet for three years and turns a fixable problem into a contracted one. Cost Explorer's recommendations are also more useful once the fleet is stable.
  5. Answer the engineering manager. They are asking for a preventive control, and only a budget action is one - it attaches a restrictive policy when a threshold is crossed. Put it on sandbox and pre-production budgets, where a hard stop is proportionate, and use alerting plus anomaly detection on production, where automatically removing permissions is itself an outage. Note that Marketplace spend will not raise an anomaly, so a subscription approval process is a separate control.

Sanity check. Tagging should be first and the commitment fourth, and your answer to the manager should apply the preventive control to some environments and not others. If the commitment appeared before rightsizing, re-read what a three-year commitment does to an over-provisioned fleet.
