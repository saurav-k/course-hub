Peering does not scale transitively, so the estate becomes a hub

working 8 min Capability keys network-peering, transit-hub, site-to-site-vpn, dedicated-interconnect, client-vpn and sdwan-integration. · AWS Transit Gateway

The one-minute version

  • VPC peering is strictly pairwise and non-transitive. Two peered VPCs cannot reach a third through each other, and using a peer's internet gateway, NAT gateway or Direct Connect is blocked outright.
  • Transit Gateway is the regional hub: thousands of VPCs, VPNs, Direct Connect gateways and appliance attachments, separated by route tables, billed per attachment-hour plus per gigabyte processed.
  • Cloud WAN is the newer, policy-managed form of the same idea - a global core network whose segments and attachments are described in a policy document rather than assembled by hand.
  • The office reaches the hub two ways: Site-to-Site VPN over the internet, two tunnels per connection, or Direct Connect over a private circuit, in resiliency tiers with published SLAs.
  • What it costs: MTU differs by attachment - 8500 bytes, except 1500 on VPN - and an SD-WAN Connect attachment lowers it further, so path MTU is a real design input rather than a detail.

1. The mental model: the growth path

Peering keeps one advantage worth stating: it has no per-gigabyte processing charge, so a small number of high-volume pairs can be cheaper peered than hubbed. Inter-Region peering is encrypted across the AWS backbone in both designs.

2. The mechanism: getting the office in

People, as opposed to sites, arrive through Client VPN: managed OpenVPN with Active Directory, certificate or SAML authentication, a default quota of five endpoints per Region and a concurrent capacity that grows with the number of associated subnets (Client VPN). An existing SD-WAN reaches the hub through a Connect attachment, which runs GRE with BGP over a VPC or Direct Connect attachment beneath it - and that encapsulation is what lowers the usable MTU (Transit Gateway Connect).

Three honest absences, so a design does not go looking for them. There is no first-party circuit into another cloud provider's fabric - a cross-cloud link is built from Direct Connect plus a colocation or partner cross-connect. There is no single management plane for the whole network estate: a Cloud WAN policy document carries the wide-area topology and segmentation, and nothing manages VPC topology as a whole. And the BGP speaker is embedded in the virtual private gateway, the Direct Connect gateway and the transit gateway rather than being a resource you configure on its own.
The reframing Choosing between peering and a hub is not a networking preference, it is a prediction about how many networks you will have in two years. Peering is cheaper and simpler for a handful of high-volume pairs; a hub is the only one of the two whose cost grows linearly with the estate rather than quadratically.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

VPC A peers with B, and B peers with C. What can A reach?
Peering is strictly pairwise, so A reaches only B whatever routes exist. Edge-to-edge routing through a peer's gateways is blocked as well, and non-overlapping CIDRs are a precondition for peering at all rather than something that unlocks transitivity.
A packet crossing a Site-to-Site VPN attachment is fragmented while the same packet over a VPC attachment is not. Why?
Transit Gateway attachments carry an MTU of 8500 except VPN attachments, which carry 1500, so a large packet must fragment. Encryption, billing and the two-tunnel design are all real properties of a VPN and none of them changes the maximum transmission unit.
A team adds a second Direct Connect port in the same facility, on the same device, as a link aggregation group. What have they bought?
A link aggregation group spans a single device, so it raises throughput while leaving the device as a shared failure. The high tier requires two connections at separate locations, and the maximum tier builds on that rather than on a group in one building.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

An estate has eleven VPCs joined by peerings, added one pair at a time as teams needed them. Production and development networks can currently reach each other. The company is also opening a data centre that needs 8 Gbps of steady replication traffic into the estate, and has forty branch offices already behind an SD-WAN.

Propose the target topology, count the peerings a full mesh would need, choose the data-centre path with its resiliency tier, and say how the branches attach.

Hint

The mesh count is n times n minus one, over two. For the data centre, put 8 Gbps on the bandwidth chart before choosing anything. For the branches, ask what already exists rather than what you would build.

Show the worked solution
  1. Count the mesh. Eleven VPCs fully meshed is 11 × 10 ÷ 2 = 55 peerings, each with route-table entries at both ends. Every twelfth VPC would add eleven more. This is the quadratic growth that makes the hub argument.
  2. Choose the topology. One transit gateway per Region, with separate route tables for production and development so the current mutual reachability becomes a deliberate decision rather than an accident of who peered with whom.
  3. Place the data centre. 8 Gbps of steady traffic is above the 5 Gbps ceiling of even a large-bandwidth VPN tunnel, so this is Direct Connect. A single 10 Gbps dedicated port carries it, and a single port is the development tier.
  4. Pick the resiliency tier deliberately. Steady replication for a business is normally worth the high tier - two connections at separate locations, with a 99.9 percent SLA - or the maximum tier with a VPN backup at 99.99 percent. Note explicitly that a second port in the same building as a link aggregation group would not buy this.
  5. Attach the branches. They already have an SD-WAN, so a Connect attachment brings the appliance into the hub over GRE with BGP, on top of a VPC or Direct Connect attachment. Record that this lowers the usable MTU on those paths, and check any application that sets the do-not-fragment bit.

Sanity check. Your peering count should be 55, and your data-centre answer should rule out VPN on bandwidth alone rather than on preference. If your resiliency choice was a single port, say out loud what an outage of that one facility costs - that is the sentence the tier exists to make you write.
