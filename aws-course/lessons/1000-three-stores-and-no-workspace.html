Three stores, three retention models, and nothing above them

working 7 min Capability keys metrics-store, log-store and distributed-tracing. · CloudWatch concepts

The one-minute version

  • Telemetry on AWS is three separate stores - a metric store, a log store and a trace store - and AWS ships no single workspace that spans them.
  • Metrics live in namespaces with up to 30 dimensions, at one-minute standard or one-second high resolution, and they are rolled up as they age.
  • Log groups never expire by default. The minimum retention you can set is one day, and the default is the most expensive setting available.
  • Logs have three query languages and several exits: metric filters make a metric, subscription filters stream onward, and Live Tail follows new events at a one-second cadence.
  • What it costs: traces are sampled by rule, and the service map covers thirty days, so a trace question about last quarter has no answer.

1. The mental model: three stores, three landing places

2. The mechanism: what ageing does to a metric

A CloudWatch log group never expires by default. That is the most expensive setting the service offers and it is what you get by not choosing, so the first thing to do with a new log group is set a retention period - the minimum available is one day (What is CloudWatch Logs).

3. The exits from the log store

Traces are the third store and the one with the most moving parts. X-Ray models a request as segments and subsegments, distinguishes annotations, which are indexed and searchable, from metadata, which is not, applies sampling rules so that not every request is recorded, and keeps a thirty-day service map (X-Ray concepts). Its SDKs and daemon have entered maintenance, and OpenTelemetry instrumentation is the forward path - lesson 1004 covers what that changes.

The reframing Do not go looking for the observability product. There is not one. There are three stores with three retention models, and a per-service decision about what is delivered into which. An observability design here is therefore a list - of streams, destinations and retentions - and the teams that skip writing that list discover it one outage at a time.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

A capacity review needs minute-level data for the same week last quarter. What is available?
One-minute points survive fifteen days and one-hour points survive 455, so the older window is available at the coarser resolution. Metrics are rolled up rather than deleted, retention is not uniform across resolutions, and high-resolution ingest does not extend how long fine points are kept.
What is the retention of a new CloudWatch log group if nobody sets one?
Log groups never expire by default, so an unattended log group accumulates indefinitely. One day is the minimum you can choose rather than the default, and the thirty and ninety-day figures belong to the trace service map and the audit event history respectively.
A team wants to alarm when a specific error string appears in logs. What is the smallest mechanism?
A metric filter turns a matching pattern into a metric that an ordinary alarm can watch, with nothing exported. A subscription filter and an export task both move the data somewhere else first, and Live Tail requires a person to be looking.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A platform team inherits an account whose CloudWatch bill is dominated by log ingestion and storage. Investigation finds 300 log groups, none with a retention setting, a debug-level application log at high volume, and a compliance requirement to keep application logs for seven years. Separately, an engineer wants to be paged when a particular fatal error appears.

Write the plan, saying what changes for each finding, and name the one requirement that argues against keeping the logs in the log store at all.

Hint

Separate the three jobs a log can do: be queried during an incident, be watched for a pattern, and be kept for years. They do not all belong in the same place.

Show the worked solution
  1. Set a retention on every group. Three hundred groups with no retention is the default doing its worst. Pick a working retention that covers incident investigation - days to weeks, not years - and apply it everywhere as a baseline.
  2. Deal with the debug volume. Ingestion is charged, so a debug-level log at high volume is an ingestion decision rather than a storage one. Reduce what is emitted, or route it somewhere cheaper, before tuning retention on it.
  3. Name the requirement that changes the destination. Seven years. Holding seven years of application logs in a queryable log store is paying query-layer prices for archive-layer data. Deliver those logs to S3 instead, where storage classes and lifecycle rules apply and the query engine of lesson 0601 can read them when somebody actually asks.
  4. Keep both, deliberately. A short retention in the log store for incident work, and a durable copy in object storage for the seven-year requirement. The two are different jobs and each is cheap when it is only doing its own.
  5. Answer the paging request. A metric filter matching the fatal string, an alarm on the resulting metric, and the alarm's action pointing at whatever pages the engineer. Nothing is exported and nothing has to be watched.

Sanity check. Your plan should end with logs in two places for two reasons, and the paging answer should not export anything. If seven years of logs stayed in the log store with a seven-year retention, price the ingestion and storage of the debug log at that duration before defending it.
