A region is an independent platform, and a zone is the failure you design against

foundation 8 min Capability area region-az-model, with the four placement tiers that hang off a region. · AWS Global Infrastructure

The one-minute version

  • AWS is not one platform. It is a set of independent regional platforms joined by a few global layers, and a region is the widest blast radius a single failure is meant to reach.
  • Inside a region, an Availability Zone is a physically isolated cluster of data centres with its own power and networking. AWS guarantees a minimum of three per region.
  • Zones are close enough to each other that spanning them is the default design, not an exotic one. Spanning regions is not.
  • When the nearest region is still too far, four smaller tiers extend it: Local Zones, Wavelength, Outposts and the CloudFront edge.
  • What it costs: nothing crosses a region boundary by itself. Images, quotas and buckets are all regional, so a second region is a second copy of everything.

1. The mental model: many platforms, not one

The useful picture is not a single cloud with regions inside it. It is a set of regional platforms that happen to share an API shape, a billing account and a handful of global services. A region is a fully independent blast-radius unit: a failure inside one is not meant to reach another, and neither is your data unless you arranged for it to travel.

At the snapshot this course was written from, AWS published 39 regions and 124 Availability Zones, with a stated minimum of three zones per region (Global Infrastructure). Those counters move, so treat the vendor page as the live authority and the shape as the durable fact.

2. What a zone actually is

An Availability Zone is one or more physically isolated data centres with independent power and networking, close enough to the others in its region that traffic between them stays at metro distance. That distance is the whole design point: it is short enough that a synchronous write across two zones is normal, and long enough that one flood, fire or power event is unlikely to take both.

The reframing Multi-zone is not a high-availability upgrade you buy later. On AWS it is the default posture, because the platform assumes it: a load balancer wants subnets in several zones, a managed database offers a standby in another zone, and a scaling fleet spreads itself across the zones you gave it.

3. When the region is too far: four smaller tiers

The tiers differ in count as sharply as they differ in capability, and the shape of that difference is worth seeing rather than reading.

4. What independence costs you

The same isolation that makes a region a clean blast radius makes it a wall. Three consequences bite on the first multi-region design.

  • Quotas are per account and per region. An account that comfortably runs a fleet in one region starts from the default limits in the next one, and increases are requested per region. Quotas for genuinely global services are requested in us-east-1 (Service Quotas).
  • An image is region-bound. An Amazon Machine Image belongs to the region it was registered in and has to be copied to be used elsewhere (AMIs). Lesson 0201 works through what that does to a release process.
  • Replication is per service, not per platform. There is no one switch that makes an estate multi-region. S3 replication, Aurora Global Database, DynamoDB Global Tables and ElastiCache Global Datastore are separate mechanisms with separate semantics (disaster recovery options).
The seductive mistake is to treat a second region as a second zone. It is not: zone-spanning is a property the platform gives you almost for free, and region-spanning is a separate copy of your images, your quotas, your data and your deployment pipeline. Reach for a second zone by default and for a second region only when you can say what recovery objective it buys.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

A workload must keep serving after one data centre is lost. What is the smallest boundary it has to span on AWS?
A zone is a physically isolated cluster with independent power and networking, so spanning two of them survives losing one. Two regions would work but buys far more than the requirement and costs a full second copy. An account is a security and billing boundary, not a physical one. Two subnets inside one zone share that zone's fate entirely.
Your team wants single-digit millisecond latency to a metro that has no region nearby. Which tier answers that and still runs your own compute?
A Local Zone is a metro extension of a parent region with its own internet and Direct Connect egress, reached by adding a subnet to the parent-region VPC. A CloudFront POP caches and terminates but hosts nothing of yours. A second zone does not move you closer to the metro. Wavelength is the right shape only when the users are behind a carrier 5G network.
You extend a running service into a second region. Which statement about what travels with it is correct?
Service quotas are held per account and per region and increases are requested per region, and an AMI belongs to the region it was registered in. The other three options each let one of the two travel with the account, which is exactly the assumption that makes a second region take longer than planned.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1foundation

A trading dashboard runs in one AWS region today, in a single VPC, from an image your build pipeline registers each week. Two new requirements arrive. It must keep serving when one data centre is lost, and traders in a metro roughly 400 km from the region complain about round-trip time.

Write the placement for both requirements. Then name three things you must do that a single-region single-zone design never made you do.

Hint

Answer the two requirements with two different boundaries. Then ask, for each of images, quotas and subnets, whether the thing is scoped to the account, the region or the zone.

Show the worked solution
  1. Answer the data-centre loss first. Spread the workload across at least two Availability Zones in the region it already runs in. A zone is a physically isolated cluster with its own power and networking, so it is the smallest boundary that satisfies the requirement, and the region guarantees at least three of them.
  2. Answer the metro latency second, and separately. Add a Local Zone subnet to the same parent-region VPC and place a serving tier in it. A Local Zone is an extension of the parent region, so it does not become a second region and does not need a second copy of the estate.
  3. List what the new placement forces. First, subnets: a subnet lives in exactly one zone, so multi-zone means several subnets rather than a bigger one. Second, quotas: the Local Zone group is opt-in and quotas are held per account and per region, so capacity has to be checked rather than assumed. Third, the image: an AMI is region-bound, so any future move to a genuinely different region means copying it rather than referencing it.
  4. State what you did not do. Nothing here crossed a region boundary, so there is no second bucket, no cross-region replication and no second pipeline. That is the point of answering each requirement at the smallest boundary that satisfies it.

Sanity check. A correct answer stays inside one region and one VPC and adds subnets rather than regions. If your design ended with two regions, you bought a disaster-recovery posture nobody asked for and are now paying to keep two copies of the image in step.
