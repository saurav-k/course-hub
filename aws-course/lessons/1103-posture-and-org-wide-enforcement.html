Module 11 · Security services · Lesson 1103

A score says where you stand, and a different service makes it true

working 6 min Capability key posture-management. · Security Hub CSPM

The one-minute version

  • Posture management evaluates an estate against published standards - the AWS foundational best practices, and the CIS, PCI and NIST sets - and reports consolidated controls and a score.
  • Findings from every detector are normalised into one format, which is what lets four services feed one queue and one score.
  • Firewall Manager is the enforcement half: it pushes web ACL, DDoS, network firewall and security group policy across every account in the organisation.
  • A naming trap sits here: the posture product is now Security Hub CSPM, and a separate unified Security Hub correlates findings across the detectors of lesson 1102.
  • What it costs: an active finding is deleted after ninety days without an update, so a score is a statement about the present rather than a record.

1. The mental model: two jobs, two services

2. The mechanism: standards, controls and a score

A standard is a published set of expectations - the foundational best practices, or the CIS, PCI and NIST sets - and each one resolves into controls that are evaluated against your resources. Consolidated controls mean one control is reported once even when several standards ask for it, which is what stops a single misconfiguration appearing four times and distorting the score.

Two products now share the name Security Hub. The posture product is Security Hub CSPM, and a separate unified Security Hub exists whose job is correlating findings across the threat detector, the vulnerability scanner and the data classifier. Older material naming "Security Hub" means the first one, and a design document should say which is meant.

3. Pushing a policy into every account

The reframing Posture management is a scoreboard, not a control. It is genuinely valuable - it turns an argument into a number, and the number can be compared across accounts and over time - but nothing in it fixes anything. Pair every standard you adopt with the mechanism that will make it true: a Firewall Manager policy, a conformance pack remediation, or a guardrail.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

The count of open findings has fallen sharply and nobody remembers fixing anything. What should you check?
Active findings are deleted after ninety days without an update, so an unattended backlog disappears on its own. The other three are worth checking eventually, but only ageing out explains a fall with no action and no configuration change.
Which service applies a web ACL to resources in every account of an organisation?
Firewall Manager is the enforcement half and distributes web ACL, DDoS, network firewall and security group policy across accounts. Posture management measures rather than acts, correlation groups findings, and a guardrail caps permissions rather than creating resources.
Why are findings normalised into a common format?
One format is what lets threat, vulnerability, data and configuration findings arrive in a single view and be scored together. Normalising does not extend retention, does not itself remediate anything, and export convenience is a side effect rather than the purpose.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A security team adopts a published standard across forty accounts. Six months later the score has improved from 62 to 78 percent, and an auditor asks for evidence that three specific controls have been continuously satisfied for the whole period. The team also wants every internet-facing application load balancer in the organisation to carry the same web ACL, including in accounts created next quarter.

Say what the score can and cannot evidence, what has to change to answer the auditor, and how the load balancer requirement is met.

Hint

Ask what a score is a statement about - a moment, or a period. Then ask which of this module's services actually changes a resource rather than reporting on it.

Show the worked solution
  1. Say what the score is. A statement about the present state of the estate against a standard. It is genuinely useful for direction and comparison between accounts, and it is not a history.
  2. Say what it cannot evidence. Continuous satisfaction over six months. Findings are deleted after ninety days without an update, so the console does not hold the earlier part of the period at all, and an improvement from 62 to 78 percent says nothing about whether those three specific controls ever failed in between.
  3. Change what is needed for the auditor. Export findings on a schedule to object storage, so the record outlives the ninety-day window, and pair the three controls with the configuration recorder's per-resource timeline from lesson 0903, which is what shows a resource's state at a point in the past rather than today.
  4. Meet the load balancer requirement. A Firewall Manager policy carrying the web ACL, scoped to internet-facing application load balancers across the organisation. It applies to accounts as they come into scope, so next quarter's accounts are covered without anybody revisiting the policy.
  5. Close the loop. Then add the corresponding control to the standard being scored, so the same requirement is both enforced and measured - enforcement without measurement drifts quietly, and measurement without enforcement is the backlog the reframing warns about.

Sanity check. Your answer to the auditor should involve an export and a timeline rather than a screenshot of the score, and the load balancer answer should name a service that changes resources. If the score itself was your evidence, check how long an untouched finding survives.
