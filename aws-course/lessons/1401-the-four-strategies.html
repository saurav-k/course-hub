Four strategies on one curve, and you are choosing a point on it

working 7 min Capability key cross-region-replication. · Disaster recovery options in the cloud

The one-minute version

  • AWS names four strategies in ascending cost: backup and restore, pilot light, warm standby, and multi-site active-active.
  • They differ in how much is already running in the second Region - nothing, the data only, a small live copy, or a full one taking traffic.
  • Recovery time falls as you climb the ladder, and so does recovery point loss, and the cost rises with both.
  • There is no single cross-Region replication product. It is delivered per service - object replication, a global database cluster, global tables, a global datastore.
  • What it costs: a strategy is a claim about two numbers, and the numbers are only real if lesson 1403's experiments have tried to break them.

1. The mental model: what is already running

2. The mechanism: replication is per service

A recovery objective applies to a service, not to a database. The slowest component decides the outcome, so a design with a sub-second database replica and a machine image that exists in only one Region has the recovery time of the image, not of the database.
The reframing Start from the two numbers the business will actually accept - how long may it be down, and how much data may be lost - and read the strategy off the ladder. Doing it the other way round produces an architecture chosen for its elegance and a recovery objective written afterwards to match, which is how a real outage becomes a surprise.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

Which strategy keeps the data current in the second Region while running no serving capacity there?
Pilot light replicates the data and leaves the compute uncreated until failover. Backup and restore does not keep data continuously current, warm standby does run scaled-down serving capacity, and multi-site runs a full one.
A database replicates to a second Region with sub-second lag, but recovery takes hours. What is the likeliest reason?
A recovery objective applies to the whole service, so an image, a template, a secret or a certificate that lives in only one Region sets the recovery time regardless of the database. Promotion is quick, asynchronous replication explains data loss rather than hours of downtime, and the objective being aggressive does not by itself make recovery slow.
What does the placement of the four strategies on two axes show?
The four fall almost on a diagonal, so faster recovery costs more to hold and the two empty corners stay empty. No single strategy is best for every workload, the two axes are strongly related rather than independent, and multi-site protects against a Region failure rather than against deletion or corruption.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A retailer runs a storefront and an internal reporting tool in one Region. The business states two objectives: the storefront may be down for at most fifteen minutes and lose at most one minute of orders, and the reporting tool may be down for a day and lose a day of data. The storefront uses a relational database, an object store for images, container images from a registry, and secrets for its payment integration.

Choose a strategy for each application, list what has to be replicated for the storefront, and name the component most likely to break the fifteen-minute objective.

Hint

Read each pair of numbers against the ladder before naming anything. Then enumerate everything the storefront needs in the second Region, including the things that are not data.

Show the worked solution
  1. Read the storefront's numbers. Fifteen minutes of downtime and one minute of data loss rules out backup and restore, and rules out pilot light unless the compute can be created and warmed inside fifteen minutes - which is optimistic for a storefront. Warm standby is the honest choice, and multi-site if the fifteen minutes is a hard commercial commitment.
  2. Read the reporting tool's numbers. A day of downtime and a day of data loss is exactly what backup and restore provides, at the lowest standing cost. Spending more here would buy nothing the business asked for.
  3. List what the storefront needs replicated. The relational database through its global cluster mechanism, the object store through bucket replication, the container images copied into the second Region's registry, and the secrets replicated across Regions. Add the templates that create the environment and the certificate the front door presents.
  4. Name the likely breaker. The container images, or the secrets - whichever exists in only one Region. Both are easy to forget precisely because they are not thought of as data, and either one turns a fifteen-minute failover into however long a human takes to notice and copy them.
  5. Say how to make the claim true. Automate the replication of every item on the list, then test the failover as an experiment rather than as a document. The next two lessons cover the replication mechanism and the experiment.

Sanity check. Your two applications should land on different strategies, and your replication list should contain at least two items that are not databases. If the list was only the database and the objects, the fifteen-minute objective is not yet achievable.
