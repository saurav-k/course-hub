An engine per shape of data, and one shape that lost its engine

working 7 min Capability keys nosql-document, graph-database, timeseries-database and ledger. · Amazon Neptune

The one-minute version

  • Four more engines exist because four shapes of data are awkward in the stores you already have: documents, graphs, time series and verifiable journals.
  • DocumentDB is MongoDB-API-compatible on the same shared-storage design as Aurora, with up to fifteen replicas on one volume and elastic clusters reaching 4 PiB.
  • Neptune is a property-graph database speaking Gremlin, openCypher and SPARQL. Neptune Analytics is a separate engine, loaded from a snapshot or from S3, not a mode you switch on.
  • Timestream is two products: serverless LiveAnalytics and managed-instance Timestream for InfluxDB, and the documentation steers new work towards InfluxDB.
  • What it costs: the dedicated verifiable-ledger engine has shut down, and the service that now carries that capability key solves a different problem, so this is a real absence rather than a rename.

1. The mental model: name the shape first

2. The mechanism: Neptune, and the engine beside it

Graph clusters have no cross-Region global-database option. The pattern lesson 0500 used for Aurora - a cluster replicated to secondary Regions with sub-second lag - is not available here, so a multi-Region graph design has to be built from exports and loads rather than configured.

Time series splits differently. Timestream for LiveAnalytics is the serverless member of the family and remains generally available; Timestream for InfluxDB is the managed-instance member, and the documentation points new users towards it (What is Amazon Timestream). One family name, two products with different operating models, which is the naming habit lesson 0103 warned about.

3. The shape with no engine

The reframing A purpose-built engine is a schema decision that has escaped into the infrastructure. That is why it is worth naming the shape of the data out loud before choosing anything: shape is the one property that does not change when the traffic does, and it is the property these four services are sold against.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

A team wants graph algorithms and vector search over a graph already running on Neptune. What must they plan for?
Neptune Analytics is a separate in-memory engine that is loaded from a snapshot or from S3, so the plan involves a second copy of the graph and a load step. It is not a setting, not an instance size, and not a query-language option on the running cluster.
A design needs a cryptographically verifiable journal owned by one company. What does the platform offer today?
The dedicated ledger database has shut down and AWS points these workloads at Aurora PostgreSQL. Managed Blockchain holds the capability key but serves multi-party networks rather than one owner's journal, and neither a document store nor a graph store offers cryptographic verification of a journal.
Which statement about the Timestream family is accurate?
LiveAnalytics and Timestream for InfluxDB are two products with different operating models, both generally available, and the documentation points new users at the InfluxDB one. Nothing here is a storage class or a billing mode on a single product, and neither member has been withdrawn.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A logistics company is designing four data stores at once. Sensor readings arrive from vehicles every second and are queried by time window. Customer records have wildly varying fields because each country's onboarding form differs. A fraud team wants to ask which accounts share a device, an address or a payment instrument, two or three hops out. Regulators require a tamper-evident record of every consignment handover.

Assign an engine to each, and identify the one requirement that this module cannot satisfy with a dedicated product - and say what you would tell the regulator's engineers instead.

Hint

Three of the four map onto a shape named on this page. Work those out first, then look hard at the fourth: the honest answer is not one of the three, and it is also not "AWS cannot do this".

Show the worked solution
  1. Sensor readings - a time-series engine. Measurements stamped with a time and queried by window is exactly the shape. The remaining choice is between the serverless LiveAnalytics member and the managed-instance InfluxDB member, and new work is steered towards InfluxDB.
  2. Customer records - a document store. Fields varying per record is the definition of the document shape, and DocumentDB gives it MongoDB-compatible APIs on Aurora-style shared storage. Check the version matrix before promising serverless, because that option and the newest API compatibility are not on the same version.
  3. Shared-attribute fraud queries - a graph database. Two or three hops between entities is a traversal, not a join, and Neptune speaks Gremlin, openCypher and SPARQL. If the fraud team also wants graph algorithms and vector search, that is Neptune Analytics, a second engine with a load step rather than a switch.
  4. Tamper-evident handovers - the gap. The dedicated verifiable-ledger engine has shut down, and Managed Blockchain, which now carries that capability key, is built for multi-party permissioned networks rather than a single owner's journal.
  5. Say what you tell them. AWS's own migration guidance for journal workloads points at Aurora PostgreSQL, so the tamper-evidence becomes something the design provides on top of a relational database rather than something the database provides for you. Say that plainly to the regulator's engineers rather than presenting a blockchain product as an equivalent, because the trust model is genuinely different.

Sanity check. Three assignments should be one word each, and the fourth answer should contain the phrase "no dedicated engine" rather than either "impossible" or a product name. If you assigned Managed Blockchain to the regulator requirement, re-read what it is for: a multi-party network is not a single owner's journal.
