Softmax: one distribution over many classes

10 min the sigmoid's big sibling Two classes needed one sigmoid; many classes need a whole distribution that sums to one. Drag six raw scores around and watch the bars renormalise to exactly 100% every time - then break a naive implementation on purpose.

The one-minute version

  • Softmax turns any vector of raw scores (logits) into probabilities: exponentiate each, divide by the total. Positive, and summing to exactly 1 - always.
  • The Sampling Bench opens today: six draggable logits over one concrete completion, "The capital of France is ...". The bars are the model's entire opinion.
  • The sample button draws tokens from those bars using a seeded generator, so your sequence of sampled tokens can match a classmate's exactly.
  • Naive softmax overflows for large logits: e¹⁰⁰⁰ is Infinity in double precision, and Infinity/Infinity is NaN. Subtracting the max before exponentiating changes nothing mathematically and fixes everything numerically - real stacks do exactly this.

1. From two classes to k

The output neuron of the Glass Network emits p̂(class 1) by squashing one score with a sigmoid; class 0 silently receives 1 - p̂. That shortcut only exists when there are two classes. A language model choosing among 50,000 next-tokens has no "other" bucket to dump probability into: every candidate needs its own number, and the numbers must form one distribution. Softmax generalises the sigmoid pair to k candidates:

softmax(s)i = esi / Σj esj Exponentiate every score - which makes them all positive and exaggerates differences - then divide by the sum, which forces the total to exactly 1. With two scores s₁ and s₀, softmax gives e^s₁/(e^s₁+e^s₀) = sigmoid(s₁-s₀): the sigmoid is softmax wearing two hats.

2. What must be true, whatever the input

Pull any slider anywhere and two properties survive: every bar stays positive (exponentials never return zero or negatives for finite inputs), and the bars always total exactly 100% within rounding. Those two facts are what let downstream code treat softmax output as probabilities at all - sampling weights, expected losses, perplexities all quietly assume them. It is also why relative moves matter more than absolute ones: adding the same constant to every logit changes nothing after division, because eᵣ cancels from numerator and denominator alike. Only differences between logits carry information.

3. The trick you will copy into every implementation

Doubles overflow just past e709.8: Math.exp(709.78) still returns a finite number and Math.exp(710) returns Infinity. A logit of 1000 - absurd here, entirely ordinary inside an untrained network or a badly scaled model - exponentiates to Infinity, and the normalisation divides Infinity by Infinity: NaN across the whole board, a model that outputs no opinion at all. The fix costs one line:

softmax(s) = softmax(s - max(s)) Subtracting a constant from every logit shifts all exponents down equally and cancels out of the ratio - section 2's relativity, weaponised. After the shift the largest exponent is exactly e&sup0; = 1, so nothing can overflow.

Worked once by hand, with the report's example scores s = (1000, 1001, 999). Naively: e¹⁰⁰⁰, e¹⁰⁰¹, e⁹⁹⁹ - the first two are Infinity, the third is finite, and the ratio is NaN everywhere. Shifted by the max: s - 1001 = (-1, 0, -2), giving e⁻¹ = 0.36788, e&sup0; = 1, e⁻² = 0.13534; total 1.50322; distribution (0.24473, 0.66524, 0.09003), summing to 1.00000. Same mathematics, actual numbers. Every production stack you will ever touch ships this subtraction; now you know both why and what it costs (one max pass).

4. Check yourself

Softmax turns some scores into probabilities. What MUST be true of its outputs whatever the input scores?
Exponentiation makes every term positive; dividing by the sum makes them total exactly 1. Those are the two properties everything downstream relies on. Sigmoid symmetry is a two-class accident that does not generalise; there is no product rule for softmax terms; and the range question answers itself - probabilities cannot be negative, so option four describes tanh's range, not softmax's.
Why do real implementations subtract the maximum logit before exponentiating?
The shift cancels exactly from the ratio - softmax(s - c) = softmax(s) - so the mathematics is untouched, while the worst exponent falls from e^1000 to e^0. Sharpening would require changing differences between logits, which a uniform shift does not do; normalisation already guaranteed the sum; and nothing is pruned, every candidate still computes. You watched the un-shifted version produce NaN bars in the bench above.
