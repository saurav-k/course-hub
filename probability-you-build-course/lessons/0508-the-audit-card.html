The audit card

15 min assemble, then audit your own model Everything this week taught, pressed into one printed page that survives a sceptical reviewer: fixed headings, priced numbers, a named trade-off, and a recommendation written for a human being.

The one-minute version

  • The bench below runs the whole pipeline on embedded data or your own pasted rows - {y, logit, g} per row, exported in one line from your Week 4 suite.
  • Drag the threshold once, press Fit T, press CIs: the card fills itself with every number, bracketed, threshold-stamped and provenance-stamped - including the rescaled threshold t' at which the pre-scaling confusion matrix reappears.
  • Three fields stay yours: the calibration verdict's meaning, the fairness position and its cost, and the recommendation in plain sentences.
  • A card with an empty required field, an undeclared threshold, or a number without its interval is not finished. That is the week's exit exam.

Run the bench

Start with the embedded two-group population so you can watch the card fill with known numbers: accuracy 0.8255, AUC 0.9126, ECE 0.0843. Then bring your own model: export rows as JSON from your Week 4 test suite or Week 5 network - one array of objects carrying y (true label), logit (or p, the probability), and g if you have any grouping - paste it into the import box, and press Apply. If you have no natural grouping, split on a feature median and label it honestly as a proxy analysis; the card's group section will say what you told it.

Import your own rows

The paste box sits inside the bench above. It accepts a JSON array of {"y":1,"logit":-0.42,"g":0} or {"y":1,"p":0.396}; probabilities are converted to logits internally so temperature scaling works identically, and g may be omitted when you have no grouping. Set the dataset selector to "pasted rows", press Apply, then Fit T and CIs. A malformed array reports its error on the canvas and the panel recovers as soon as you apply valid rows.

Reading your own card like a reviewer

Before printing, run the checklist reviewers actually apply. Every required field present? The threshold declared exactly once and used by every number above it? T fitted on disjoint rows from the ones the report scores? A bracket beside each headline number, and every gap's bracket checked against zero before anyone claims a gap has a direction? If a before-and-after-scaling comparison appears, is it read at t = 0.5 or at the rescaled t', rather than at the same printed threshold on both sides - which compares two different policies and blames the difference on calibration? The fairness section naming which criterion was sacrificed rather than implying all were met? And the recommendation falsifiable - phrased so a future observation could prove it wrong? Common failures announce themselves: an after-scaling ECE far better on your report rows than on fresh ones means leakage through the fit; a group section contradicting its own criterion line means thresholds moved between computations; a missing interval means a rumour is masquerading as a result.

Then finish the week properly: point the bench at your own Week 4 export and produce the full card - confusion family with all six rates and intervals at one declared t, ROC/AUC, reliability and ECE before and after held-out temperature scaling, group gaps if any grouping exists, the aleatoric-versus-epistemic paragraph, and the recommendation. That document is the capstone's third requirement already done, and the habit that outlives every model you will ship: no claim without an interval, without a calibration statement, without an account of who pays for the errors.

Platt scaling generalises temperature scaling by:
Temperature divides by one scalar; Platt fits A*z+B with two degrees of freedom, which helps when miscalibration varies across the score range - as it does on this week's population, whose reliability curve pivots near 0.82 rather than at 0.5. Both maps are post-hoc and strictly increasing when A > 0, so ranking is untouched and AUC is bit-identical; the decision at a given threshold reappears at the correspondingly rescaled one.

Those four are the spine the week built: one threshold so numbers reproduce, brackets so claims are priced, a calibration line so probabilities mean something, and an owned value judgement about which fairness cost the organisation accepts. Quoting every metric at several thresholds is not thoroughness but threshold-shopping with the evidence attached - the card demands one declared policy and then holds you to it.
