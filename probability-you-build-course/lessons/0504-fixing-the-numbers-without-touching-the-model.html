Fixing the numbers without touching the model

12 min one scalar, fitted on held-out rows, provably harmless to accuracy Temperature scaling is the most useful twenty lines in this week: it makes probabilities honest while leaving every decision bit-identical - and this page proves both halves.

The one-minute version

  • The fix is one scalar: divide every logit by a temperature T before the sigmoid. Nothing inside the model changes.
  • T is fitted by minimising log loss on held-out rows only. On this page's population, fitting on half the rows gives T* = 0.73.
  • On the untouched test half: ECE 0.0925 → 0.0671, Brier 0.1215 → 0.1173, log loss 0.3886 → 0.3691.
  • The invariance proof: accuracy at any fixed threshold is bit-identical before and after - 0.8280 → 0.8280, same four cell counts. Scaling re-prices confidence; it cannot move a single decision.

One knob, outside the model

Retraining to fix calibration would be expensive and unnecessary. Guo et al.'s repair needs one parameter: replace p = sigmoid(z) with p = sigmoid(z / T), where z is the model's logit and T is a positive scalar fitted once on validation data. T above 1 softens overconfident outputs toward the middle; T below 1 sharpens underconfident ones outward. The direction of the cure follows the sign of the disease you saw in lesson 4.

How is T found? By making the honest ruler happy: choose the T that minimises mean log loss on rows the model never trained on. Why that target is legitimate rather than arbitrary is exactly the question lesson 6 answers with proper scoring rules - fit first, interrogate the licence second, which is the order that makes the interrogation bite.

The proof that accuracy cannot move

The invariance line is not an empirical observation that happened to hold; it is forced. Take any threshold t and any row with logit z. Before scaling, the row is flagged when sigmoid(z) ≥ t. After scaling, when sigmoid(z/T) ≥ t. Because the sigmoid is strictly increasing and T is positive, both conditions rearrange to the same inequality:

sigmoid(z)   >= t   <=>   z >= logit(t)
sigmoid(z/T) >= t   <=>   z >= T x logit(t)

Different thresholds in logit space, identical partition of the rows. Every TP stays TP, every FP stays FP; the confusion matrix at t is the same table of integers, so accuracy agrees digit for digit - which is what the build checks when it reports unchanged? true. The corollary matters just as much: if recalibration changed your AUC, your precision-recall trade-off, or your fairness gaps, something is broken in the pipeline, because none of those quantities can respond to a monotone rescaling either. Only the probability VALUES moved - the prices, not the decisions.

Two disciplines around the fit

Hold out the rows, always. Fitting T on the same rows you then report ECE on is leakage: the improvement looks spectacular on those rows and evaporates on fresh ones. The build enforces the split - the fit sees its half, the report reads the other half. If you audit your own Week 4 export in lesson 9, keep that wall up yourself; nobody else will.

Know what Platt scaling adds. Temperature scaling fits one degree of freedom. Platt scaling (Platt, 1999, for SVM outputs) generalises the map to a full affine transform A*z + B through the sigmoid, fitting two parameters where T fits one - worth reaching for when miscalibration varies across the range rather than acting like one uniform stretch. The origin paper concerns support vector machines; the mechanism transfers unchanged to any real-valued score, and both variants belong to the same family: post-hoc maps that leave ranking untouched.

Practice about 12 minutes, with paper

Practice 4working

A row has logit 2.0, so sigmoid(2.0) = 0.881. After dividing the logit by T = 2, what is the new probability? Then find the approximate T that maps logit 2.0 to p = 0.70 (one grid step is fine).

Solution

T = 2: z' = 2/2 = 1.0, and sigmoid(1.0) = 0.731. For the target: solve sigmoid(2/T) = 0.70, so 2/T = logit(0.70) = ln(0.7/0.3) = 0.8473, giving T = 2/0.8473 = 2.36.

Notice the direction: T above 1 softens an extreme logit toward the middle - the cure for overconfidence. This page's fitted value came out below 1 because its synthetic distortion ran the other way. The sign of the fix follows the sign of the disease; memorise the mechanism, not a magic number.
