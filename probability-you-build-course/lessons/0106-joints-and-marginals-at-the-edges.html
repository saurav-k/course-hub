Joints and marginals: the cloud and its shadows

11 min working Two sliders stretch the splat into an ellipse; one slider tilts it. The shadows at the edges move for exactly one of those actions - and that asymmetry is the lesson.

The one-minute version

  • A splat position is a pair (X, Y) drawn together: a joint distribution over the plate.
  • Projecting the cloud onto an axis gives the marginal of that coordinate - which is exactly what the edge histograms display.
  • The correlation slider ρ tilts the ellipse diagonally while leaving both edge histograms unchanged.
  • Punchline, worth underlining: marginals do not determine dependence. Two very different clouds can cast identical shadows.

1. Pairs are random variables too

Every splat centre so far was described by one number, its x-offset. But a stamp lives at a position, which is two numbers decided together: (X, Y). Drawing many splats therefore samples a joint distribution - probability spread over a plate rather than a line, with density read as height above the floor. Nothing new is being smuggled in: a joint is just a distribution whose outcomes happen to be pairs.

From any joint you can always recover the distribution of one coordinate alone by ignoring the other. That operation is called marginalising, and mechanically it means adding up, over every possible partner value, the probability of the pair:

f_X(x) = the density of X alone, obtained from the joint by summing out Y Read as: the marginal density of X asks only where the horizontal position lands, whatever the height does. In the discrete case it is literally a sum over y of the joint probabilities; in the continuous case, the same idea as an integral.

2. Stretch, then tilt - watch which one the shadows obey

The build manufactures correlated pairs with a two-line recipe worth reading once, in code, because it demystifies the whole subject:

the correlated-pair recipe · from this build's source, runnable anywhere
x = sx * z1;
y = sy * (rho * z1 + Math.sqrt(1 - rho * rho) * z2);
// z1, z2 independent standard normals from Box-Muller

When ρ = 0 the recipe collapses to two independent coordinates and the cloud is an upright ellipse. Raising ρ blends some of z1 into y, so tall-x splats tend to also be tall-y: the cloud leans right. The square root keeps y's own variance pinned at sy² no matter the blend - which is precisely why the edge histograms refuse to move. The tilt redistributes combinations, never either coordinate's private spread.

3. Why "the marginals don't determine the joint" matters outside art

This is Week 1's independence lesson returning with sharper teeth. There, correlated failures broke a product formula silently. Here you can see the gap itself: every per-feature summary agrees across the two clouds, while their joint behaviour differs completely. When a model report hands you per-feature statistics, you now know exactly what question to ask next - and the edge histograms have trained your eye to ask it.

Check yourself

The histograms drawn along the canvas edges of a splat cloud recover:
Each edge strip collects one coordinate across all points - a projection, which is the definition of a marginal. The joint lives in the middle of the plate where both coordinates matter at once. Cumulative curves would accumulate from one end rather than binning, and residuals presuppose a fitted line that nothing here has drawn.
You drag rho from 0 to 0.8 holding both sigmas fixed. What changes on screen?
Correlation reshuffles pairings, not private spreads, so the lean appears and the shadows stay. Widening edges would mean the sigmas moved, which the frozen sliders forbid. Swapping is projection done in the wrong direction, and invisible correlation contradicts the plainly tipping ellipse you just watched.
Your teacher. Set sigma-x = 60, sigma-y = 20, rho = 0, and screenshot the edges in your head: narrow-and-tall cloud, one wide shadow, one narrow shadow. Now predict problem 8 before opening it - it asks the same judgement with a pencil. If the phrase "the marginals stay fixed while the joint changes" feels obvious already, you have the week's hardest idea banked early.
