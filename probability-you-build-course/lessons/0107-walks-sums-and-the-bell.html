Walks, sums and the bell: watching the central limit theorem happen

13 min working No single step knows anything. Sum enough of them anyway and a specific, predictable bell assembles itself - then watch what dependence does to it.

The one-minute version

  • A walk's endpoint is a sum of N independent +/-1 steps: mean 0, variance N, so spread grows like √N, not N.
  • The CLT says more: standardise that sum and its distribution approaches the normal bell as N grows - visible here as endpoint histograms going bell-shaped.
  • The grey cone on the trail plate has half-width √t at step t: the √N law drawn as scenery.
  • Flip the momentum toggle and consecutive steps stop being independent: the bars desert the independent-step bell. Normality bends; the √N prediction, built on independence, breaks.

1. The endpoint is a sum, and sums have algebra

Write one step as S with P(S = +1) = P(S = -1) = 1/2. After N independent steps the walker stands at X = S₁ + S₂ + ... + S_N. Two facts fall straight out of the definitions on page 0105:

E[X] = N · E[S] = 0,   Var(X) = N · Var(S) = N Read as: expectations of independent sums add, and so do variances. Each step contributes mean 0 and variance 1, so N steps contribute mean 0 and variance N - which makes the typical distance from home grow like the square root of N.

Why square root and not linear? Because cancellations do real work. Ten right-steps and ten left-steps net zero; only the unbalanced surplus moves the walker. Surpluses of order N would require luck of a kind that decays factorially; surpluses of order √N are ordinary. At N = 200 the typical endpoint sits around √200 ≈ 14.1, and the cone in the build draws exactly that growth, step by step.

2. Run the emergence dial

Read the momentum result carefully, because it is subtler than "the maths broke". The central limit theorem is robust: sums of weakly dependent steps still converge to some bell. What failed is narrower and more instructive - the variance formula, whose derivation on page 0105 multiplied variances of independent pieces. Persistence adds positive correlation between steps, small agreements compound, and the true spread becomes roughly √3 times the naive prediction here. The gold curve keeps drawing yesterday's promise; the bars have moved on.

3. Why the bell, though?

Honest answer for an algebra-level course: the full central limit theorem needs a limit argument this week will not fake, and the foundations course owns the careful statement. But the mechanism has a shape you can appreciate now. Each step's idiosyncrasies - its exact position among +1 and -1 outcomes - get divided among ever more terms as N grows, while what survives summation is what all steps share: finite variance and zero tendency. The bell is the shape left when individuality cancels and only bulk remains. That is why it appears everywhere data aggregates: daily deltas into yearly returns, pixel noises into averaged images, independent judgements into crowd estimates.
