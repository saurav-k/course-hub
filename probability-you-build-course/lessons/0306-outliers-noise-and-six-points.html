Week 4 · Logistic regression · Lesson 306

Outliers, label noise, and six points

working 13 min Three built-in cases: one far outlier, 15% label noise, tiny n=6 · Piech, ch. Logistic Regression

The one-minute version

  • Logistic regression has no robustness mechanism: every point pulls the fit forever.
  • One confirmed positive planted far off-axis twists this week’s boundary about six degrees - while training accuracy stays exactly 1.000.
  • Flipped labels do their worst work on confidence, not accuracy: contradictory neighbours compress probabilities toward 0.5.
  • Six points pin almost nothing: refits on fresh draws swing P(positive at the origin) across 0.77 of range.
  • The honest report quotes the spread, not the luckiest fit.

1. The far outlier: measuring a twist against a clean refit

The generator draws the usual clean clouds, then plants one extra confirmed positive at (10, −10) - far from both clusters, well outside anything the label claims should exist there. Because the point is genuinely labelled 1, the loss treats it as truth, and its error term keeps pulling for as long as it is poorly satisfied. Points like that never expire: logistic regression’s gradient has no discount for distance, no down-weighting for rarity, no mechanism at all for saying this one is probably a data-entry error.

How does the harness turn that story into a number? For this case only, it fits twice: once with all 120 points, and once on the same draw with the planted point removed. The angle between the two fitted normal vectors is the case’s tilt observable:

  1. Fit everything. With the plant included, the normal vector lands at angle 39.6° from the x-axis.
  2. Refit clean. Without it, the same clouds give 45.4° - essentially the diagonal the clouds suggest.
  3. Take the difference. Tilt = −5.9°: the single point dragged the boundary several degrees off the clouds’ axis, shifting it by roughly half a unit across the data band.

Sanity check. Training accuracy reads exactly 1.000 in both fits - the twist cost the model nothing it measures by default. A distortion invisible to accuracy but visible in geometry is precisely the kind of fact this suite exists to catch.

Contrast is the trick The tilt observable needs a comparison fit - with versus without one point. That pattern generalises: when you suspect one datum is steering a model, remove it, refit, and measure the delta. A coefficient that moves by half itself was never a coefficient; it was two numbers wearing one label.

2. Label noise: confidence dies first

The generator now flips 15% of labels at random. Accuracy suffers - on the canonical draw it caps at 0.81 - but the subtler damage shows in probabilities. Near the boundary, genuinely contradictory neighbours now sit at nearly identical positions with opposite labels, and no straight line, however steep, can make both simultaneously confident. The model’s best play is to hedge: on the canonical run, the highest probability anywhere in the central window is 0.59. Perfect confidence is mathematically unreachable with contradictory data - which also means the separation-style saturation of lesson 05 is impossible here, and any check expecting 0.99+ confidence would be wrong by construction.

This is worth internalising because dashboards rarely show it: two models can match on accuracy while one outputs calibrated hedges and the other overconfident coin-flips. What noise does to confidence is a property of the data, not of your epochs.

3. Six points: report the spread, not the fit

The last case draws three positives against three negatives and fits. Any such fit nails its own sample - accuracy 1.000 on the canonical draw - but the boundary it implies is barely constrained. The harness quantifies that by refitting twenty times on twenty independent draws and reading the predicted probability at one fixed probe point, the origin:

  1. Twenty refits. Fresh three-v-three draws each time, same trainer, no retuning.
  2. Probe the origin. P(positive at (0,0)) across the twenty fits ranges over 0.77 end to end - from confidently negative to confidently positive territory.
  3. State the honest summary. Six points pin the boundary loosely enough that the sign of the answer at the origin is undecided. Quote the spread; treat any single fit’s confident reading as luck.

Sanity check. The spread cannot be removed by training longer - each fit already converged on its own draw. Wide spread over redraws is a statement about information content, and no optimiser creates information.

4. Run the trio

Predictions first, honour system. For the outlier: will training accuracy notice it? Will the boundary? For label noise: what accuracy ceiling do you claim, and what happens to confidence near the boundary? For six points: how much should P(positive at the origin) be allowed to wander before the word “pinned” becomes false?

One obviously-positive point sits at (10, −10), far outside both clouds. What will the fit do?
The gradient gives every poorly-explained point permanent pull, however rare - so the boundary rotates to accommodate it, costing nothing in training accuracy. Nothing down-weights outliers, convergence is unthreatened (the twisted fit still separates its own training set perfectly), and label-flipping is not a mechanism this model possesses.
With 15% of labels flipped at random, do trained probabilities near the boundary saturate toward 0 and 1?
Identical positions with opposite labels cap any linear score’s honesty: pushing toward one pushes equally hard away from the other, so the optimum hedging lives near the middle. Probabilities are bounded by construction, more epochs sharpen a contradiction rather than resolving it, and no trainer here edits labels.
Six points. Twenty refits on fresh draws put P(positive at the origin) everywhere from 0.05 to 0.82. The honest summary is...
Each refit converged on its own draw; the disagreement is about information, not effort, so averaging hides the finding and longest-run confers no wisdom. Broken data would be a different diagnosis - these draws are behaving exactly as six points must. The spread is the result.
