Week 5 · Closing bridge · Lesson 8 of 8 · milestone M5

From glass network to large model, honestly

13 min the honest accounting First watch capacity betray you: islands of memorised noise while the held-out curve climbs. Then apply your own softmax to an attention pattern, and put the toy and the transformer side by side - sharing exactly four ingredients, missing at least five things.

The one-minute version

  • A 2–16–16–1 net on noisy moons draws ever-thinner islands around individual noise points: train loss keeps falling while test loss visibly turns upward. That divergence is overfitting, seen rather than described.
  • Attention scores each token against every other and softmaxes the scores into mixing weights. You will apply your own sharpened softmax to one frozen illustrative pattern.
  • The shared skeleton with large models: learned weight matrices, non-linear activations, a final softmax distribution, and gradients by chain rule. Everything else - sequences, attention itself, scale, data - is missing from the toy, on purpose.
  • You did not build a small language model this week. You built the smallest instrument that makes its mathematics visible. Both halves of that sentence matter.

1. Capacity past need: overfitting islands

Everything this week trained cleanly because moons and blobs were the whole story. Real evaluation needs data the model never saw - week 4 built that habit into its suite, and it transfers unchanged. The trainer below adds a train/test split: a deterministic shuffle puts 70% of the points into training and holds 30% back, and the loss panel draws both curves once the split is on.

Mechanically, nothing new runs here - same forward pass, same backward pass you coded in lesson 5. What changed is the match between capacity and signal. Count it: 2–16–16–1 carries 2×16 + 16×16 + 16×1 = 304 weights plus 33 biases, so 337 free parameters chasing 140 training rows. More than two tunable numbers for every row it has ever seen is enough freedom to fit the noise as if it were structure, and the noisy-moons generator puts plenty of noise there to fit. The held-out curve is the only voice that can say so. Hold onto the visual: falling train with rising test, the divergence signature you will meet again as vocabulary in every model-audit conversation for the rest of your career.

2. One new object: attention scores through your softmax

Large language models differ from the Glass Network architecturally in one central way: instead of every position talking to every position through fixed weights, each token scores every earlier token for relevance, then softmaxes those raw scores into mixing weights and averages. Read that definition again with this week's eyes: the only new object is the score, and the transformation applied to it - exponentiate, normalise, total 100% - is the exact operation you dragged around the Sampling Bench.

Why frozen illustration instead of real weights? A trained head worth reading lives inside a checkpoint measured in gigabytes, which cannot ride in a zero-dependency page - and the payoff would collapse into watching numbers move. The design decision mirrors the one made all week: mechanism first, spectacle never. If you want the real thing, the paper that introduced the mechanism is Attention Is All You Need, covered in the papers course alongside visualisations of actual trained heads.

4. What we did and did not build

Say it plainly, because this week earns its ending. Built: a multi-layer probability machine whose internals you can read like text; your own backward pass, agreeing with finite differences to a relative error of 2.9 × 10⁻¹¹; a sampler that behaves like production decoding loops behave; and the diagnostic reflexes - plateau means geometry, diverging curves mean memorisation, front bars pinned near zero with the loss stuck at ln 2 mean vanishing signal. Not built: anything that reads or produces language. No tokens, no positions, no attention, no corpus, no emergent behaviour - and this toy's probabilities are not calibrated in any sense week 6 will honour. When someone says "neural networks are black boxes", you now own the counterexample experience: this one is glass, and looking through it taught you the four ingredients that survive scaling. Week 6 audits the outputs of models like these - calibration, thresholds, fairness - using the Audit Bench, and it starts precisely where the honesty note above points: with the gap between confident and correct.

5. Check yourself

Train loss is still falling; test loss fell, bottomed out, and has been climbing for a thousand epochs. Diagnosis?
The falling-train, rising-test divergence is overfitting's signature; section 1 let you screenshot it. Adding capacity attacks underfitting, the opposite regime, and would deepen the dive; vanishing gradients show up as uniformly slow training, not as a divergence between splits; and the split here is deterministic by construction - recheck it, then trust the curve.
Our toy maps two features to p(class 1); a production LLM maps thousands of token positions to a next-token distribution. Which ingredient does the LLM use that ours entirely lacks?
Three of the four are the shared skeleton of section 3's table - the toy computes all of them, smaller. Attention is the structural newcomer: our connectivity is fixed and input-independent, while a transformer routes information based on the content of each sequence. That, plus sequence handling and sheer scale, is exactly the boundary the honest bridge refuses to blur in either direction.
In attention, after computing raw compatibility scores between one token and every earlier token, what operation turns them into mixing weights?
It is the Sampling Bench again: exponentiate the scores, divide by their sum, and you have positive weights totalling exactly one to mix values with. Independent sigmoids would not preserve a total, dividing by count is not even monotone in relevance, and hard top-1 is just greedy attention - a special case the sharpening slider approaches but real heads rarely take. The explorer above ran this very operation under your slider.

Where this came from

Overfitting as a capacity-versus-data phenomenon follows the classical account; the divergence signature was demonstrated live above rather than cited. The attention mechanism is introduced in Vaswani et al., Attention Is All You Need (arXiv:1706.03762), treated lesson-by-lesson in the papers course; the explorer's frozen scores are hand-authored illustration, dated in the header of assets/builds/attention.js, in the style of published single-head visualisations. The shared-ingredients framing of section 3 was checked line by line against what the week's builds actually compute.
