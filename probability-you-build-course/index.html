PROBABILITY YOU BUILD Week 1 Week 6 Course Hub
Modelled on Stanford PAI1 · six weeks plus a capstone · zero dependencies

The build-first probability course.

Every week ends with a working browser artifact you built yourself, and the theory arrives exactly when the artifact needs it - never earlier. A spend planner that picks your model tier by arithmetic. Generative artwork where every layer is a named random variable. A pyramid scanner that finds a hidden chamber from counted muons. A test suite that grades your predictions about a classifier. A glass-box network. A fairness audit. Finish a week and you have something running to show a person.

This course is the hands-on twin of Statistical Foundations of Machine Learning, which owns the formal lecture treatment of this material. Where you want a derivation done carefully, that course has it; where you want to make the mathematics do work in code you wrote, start here.

Week 6: Probabilistic evaluation - the Audit Bench

10 written · lessons 0500-0509

Turn any set of predictions into a defensible probabilistic audit: confusion-matrix rates at a declared threshold, reliability against truth with expected calibration error, temperature rescaling whose effect on the decision is proved rather than assumed, bootstrap intervals on every headline number, group-wise rates exposing the fairness collision by name - ending in a recommendation a non-statistician can act on.

Lesson 0500 · the Audit Bench opens
Ninety-five percent sure and useless
The base-rate trap live: on a rare-event population, always-say-no beats the trained model on its own headline number - and accuracy is demoted from king to witness.
Lesson 0501 · the counting core
One table, six dials
The confusion matrix and its derived rates, with the threshold as a live policy knob: cells trade size and every dial swings in its fixed direction.
Lesson 0502 · ranking against detecting
Two curves, two questions
Precision-recall for what flags are worth, ROC for how well you sort, AUC read exactly once, correctly - then the degenerate scorer with a perfect 1.000 that ships useless.
Lesson 0503 · calibration against truth
Do the probabilities mean anything?
Reliability diagrams and ECE against truth; confidently wrong as the modern documented default; and the binning fragility that makes ECE a number you must qualify.
Lesson 0504 · temperature scaling
Fixing the numbers without touching the model
One scalar fitted on held-out rows makes probabilities honest without reordering a single row - what that does and does not preserve, derived and then measured at the reader's own threshold.
Lesson 0505 · proper scoring rules
Scores you cannot game
Brier with Murphy's uncertainty-resolution-reliability decomposition, log loss as the honest ruler, and why fitting temperature by accuracy is flat nonsense.
Lesson 0506 · the week's peak
Three definitions of fair, one impossible
Demographic parity, equalised odds and predictive parity computed on your numbers; then the collision - a perfectly calibrated base-rate scorer whose error rates cannot be equalised - and Chouldechova's identity derived step by step.
Lesson 0507 · intervals and honesty
Price the claim
Bootstrap intervals on every headline number with a seeded engine, aleatoric versus epistemic residual error, and the exact sentence about what an interval does not cover.
Lesson 0508 · the deliverable

The full bench plus paste-your-rows import, feeding a printable one-page audit with fixed headings - ending in your own Week 4 model audited end to end.

How this course reads

what to expect
Method
Derived, then coded, then simulated
A result is worked analytically on the page, computed by the build, and verified by Monte Carlo beside it within a stated tolerance. The agreement between the columns is the proof the reader watches happen.
Artifacts
One running build per week
Every build lives inside the lesson page as vanilla JavaScript in the shared .build wrapper: canvas, controls, readout, caption. No installs, no keys, no network - open the page off disk and it runs.
Data
Frozen snapshots or seeds
Prices enter as dated, cited vendor snapshots isolated in marked constants; datasets are generated in-page from seeded generators so two readers see identical points. Nothing calls an API at runtime.
Honesty
Predictions before results
Where behaviour is about to be observed, the reader writes their prediction first and the harness grades the prediction. Where a simulation uses a teaching constant instead of a paper's value, the page says so plainly.
