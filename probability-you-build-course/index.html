PROBABILITY YOU BUILD Week 1 Week 6 Course Hub
Modelled on Stanford PAI1 · six weeks plus a capstone · zero dependencies

The build-first probability course.

Every week ends with a working browser artifact you built yourself, and the theory arrives exactly when the artifact needs it - never earlier. A spend planner that picks your model tier by arithmetic. Generative artwork where every layer is a named random variable. A pyramid scanner that finds a hidden chamber from counted muons. A test suite that grades your predictions about a classifier. A glass-box network. A fairness audit. Finish a week and you have something running to show a person.

This course is the hands-on twin of Statistical Foundations of Machine Learning, which owns the formal lecture treatment of this material. Where you want a derivation done carefully, that course has it; where you want to make the mathematics do work in code you wrote, start here.

Week 1: Core probability - the Spend Planner

10 planned · lessons 0000-0099

An inference-time resource decision maker: given a quality bar and a dated model catalogue, compute the probability each candidate policy produces a correct answer, price each policy by total probability, pick the cheapest one that clears the bar, and revise the plan by Bayes as validation evidence arrives. Week 3: Maximum likelihood - three instruments

8+ planned · lessons 0200-0299

Point an instrument at the world, write down the probability of the data under each hypothesis, and let the data pick. Three builds share one method: find a hidden chamber in a simulated pyramid from counted muons, locate a phone from four noisy signal strengths and watch the answer degrade tower by tower, and explain why zero failures in fifty orders does not mean the failure rate is zero.

  • 0200The method that found a hidden chamber - the 2017 discovery, likelihood versus probability, the prosecutor's fallacy warning
  • 0201Counting muons - Poisson counts from traced rays, sky and stone, two hypotheses compared by eye
  • 0202Sliding the hypothesis - parameters become sliders, log-likelihood updates live, argmax named
  • 0203Why the log - products into sums, underflow, and the monotone argument that keeps the peak
  • 0204When algebra finds the peak - Bernoulli p-hat = k/n derived line by line; the zero-failure paradox opens
  • 0205When only a computer will do - grid search first, then hand-coded gradient ascent with step-size pathology live
  • 0206How wrong can the answer be - removing towers, widening blobs, bias and consistency made visible
  • 0207Where MLE breaks - boundary maxima, likelihood intervals, and unbounded likelihoods teased toward Week 4

Week 4: Logistic regression - the adversarial test suite

8+ planned · lessons 0300-0399

Train a classifier you wrote yourself, predict before running how it behaves on adversarial datasets, and be right. The artifact is not the model: it is a test suite of eight cases - perfect separation, 19:1 imbalance, correlated features, XOR, an outlier, label noise, six points, clean overlap - where the runner grades your written prediction, not the model.

  • 0300A test suite for a classifier - probabilities not bare labels, the sigmoid squash, the harness shell

  • 0302The gradient, derived then coded - five chain-rule steps to (y - sigma(z))x, then the trainer ships
  • 0303The geometry of the boundary - z = 0 as a line, theta as its normal, cases one and two run predict-then-run
  • 0304Separation and imbalance - saturated confidence, the accuracy lie, and what the lambda slider trades
  • 0305Correlated features and missing capacity - coefficients swing across refits while predictions stay put; XOR cannot be carved

9 planned · lessons 0500-0599

Turn any set of predictions into a defensible probabilistic audit: confusion-matrix rates at a declared threshold, reliability against truth with expected calibration error, temperature rescaling that provably leaves accuracy untouched, bootstrap intervals on every headline number, group-wise rates exposing the fairness collision by name - ending in a recommendation a non-statistician can act on.

  • 0500Ninety-five percent sure and useless - the base-rate trap live, accuracy demoted from king to witness
  • 0501One table, six dials - the confusion matrix, its derived rates, thresholds as policy knobs
  • 0502Two curves, two questions - PR for rare events, ROC for ranking, AUC read correctly
  • 0503Do the probabilities mean anything? - reliability diagrams, confidently wrong as the modern default, ECE and its binning fragility
  • 0504Fixing the numbers without touching the model - temperature scaling fitted on held-out data, the accuracy-invariance proof
  • 0505Scores you cannot game - proper scoring rules, Brier with its Murphy decomposition, log loss as the honest ruler
  • 0506Three definitions of fair, one impossible - demographic parity, equalised odds, predictive parity on your numbers, then the collision
  • 0507Price the claim - bootstrap intervals on every headline number, aleatoric versus epistemic uncertainty
  • 0508The audit card - assembling everything into the printable critique document, then auditing your own Week 4 model

How this course reads

what to expect
Method
Derived, then coded, then simulated
A result is worked analytically on the page, computed by the build, and verified by Monte Carlo beside it within a stated tolerance. The agreement between the columns is the proof the reader watches happen.
Artifacts
One running build per week
Every build lives inside the lesson page as vanilla JavaScript in the shared .build wrapper: canvas, controls, readout, caption. No installs, no keys, no network - open the page off disk and it runs.
Data
Frozen snapshots or seeds
Prices enter as dated, cited vendor snapshots isolated in marked constants; datasets are generated in-page from seeded generators so two readers see identical points. Nothing calls an API at runtime.
Honesty
Predictions before results
Where behaviour is about to be observed, the reader writes their prediction first and the harness grades the prediction. Where a simulation uses a teaching constant instead of a paper's value, the page says so plainly.
