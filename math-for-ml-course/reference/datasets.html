The datasets this course uses

reference grows with the course Generated by us, committed to the repository, and small enough to open in a browser tab. Nothing here is scraped and nothing depends on a third-party host.

This sheet is a skeleton and it grows with the course. Two datasets are meant to serve the whole course, and a third is added only when an existing one genuinely cannot carry a lesson, because a reader who already knows the columns can concentrate on the mathematics.
Read this first Every number in these files is invented. They are built so that the answers you compute are checkable, not so that they describe the world. Nothing here is evidence about real user behaviour or real machinery, and no page cites one as though it were.

sessions.csv

20,000 rows, 691 KB. One row per web session. Used by Module 02 for summaries and correlation, and by Module 09 for estimation. Download sessions.csv.

What was designed into it: session_seconds is lognormal, so its mean of 171.7 sits far from its median of 98.9 and the mean is visibly the wrong summary. spend correlates with it at r = 0.487 while screen_brightness correlates at r = 0.002, so one table holds a real correlation and a null one. Roughly one session in four hundred is a bot with up to 897 page views, which is what makes a trimmed summary differ from an untrimmed one.

sensors.csv

12,000 rows, 754 KB. Eight sensor readings per row. Used by Module 03 for vectors and matrices, and by Module 04 for eigenvalues, the SVD and PCA. Download sensors.csv.

What was designed into it: the eight columns are generated from three latent factors, so the standardised covariance matrix has eigenvalues 5.36, 1.40 and 0.92 and then a cliff. The scree elbow at three is built in, and the first three components carry 96.0% of the variance. Left unstandardised, the first component carries 98.9% because pressure_kpa is on a scale of hundreds, so the page on standardising has a dataset that punishes skipping it. vibration_x and vibration_y correlate at 0.987, so near rank deficiency is visible rather than theoretical.

experiment.csv

24,000 rows, 616 KB. A randomised A/B log, 12,000 users per arm. Used by Module 09 for hypothesis testing, p-values, A/B testing and the Bayesian comparison. Download experiment.csv.

What was designed into it: the true conversion rates are 0.0500 and 0.0560, a real 12 per cent relative lift, and the arms are sized so the test has only 0.546 power to find it. It duly misses, at z = 1.371 and p = 0.1703, while the 95 per cent interval on the difference still covers the true effect. The file exists so a reader can hold three true things at once: the effect is real, the test failed, and the failure was computable before the experiment ran. The device mix is the same in both arms, so a reader can check that too.

features.csv

4,000 rows, 896 KB. Thirty predictors, of which five carry signal. Used by Module 09 for MAP and regularization, the bias-variance tradeoff, and regression inference. Download features.csv.

What was designed into it: x01 to x05 have true coefficients 4.0, -2.5, 1.5, -1.0 and 0.6, and every one of x06 to x30 has true coefficient exactly zero, so a page can show a fitted model spending effort on nothing. The noise standard deviation is 3.0, which is the irreducible error: no model drives the test error below 9.0, and knowing that lets bias and variance be measured against a truth rather than estimated.

population.csv

30,000 rows, 1085 KB. A survey frame with strata and clusters, and one column of every measurement scale. Used by Module 02 for survey sampling designs and for the measurement-scale ladder. Download population.csv.

What was designed into it: four strata of unequal size and unequal spread, with means from about 240 to about 4,100, so the between-stratum share of variance is 87.4 per cent and stratified sampling measurably beats simple random. Six hundred clusters carry a cluster-level offset, so units inside a cluster resemble one another and cluster sampling measurably loses. Separately, region is nominal, satisfaction is ordinal, office_temp_c is interval and spend is ratio, which is what lets the scale ladder be demonstrated rather than described.

anscombe.csv

44 rows, under 1 KB. Anscombe's quartet, transcribed from the paper rather than generated. Used by Module 02. Download anscombe.csv.

What was designed into it, by Anscombe rather than by us: four eleven-point sets that agree on the mean of x, the mean of y, both variances, the correlation, the fitted line, the standard error of the slope and R-squared, and look nothing alike. It is the one file here that is not ours, and regenerating it would defeat the point. Source: Anscombe, F. J. (1973), Graphs in Statistical Analysis, The American Statistician 27(1), 17-21.

tickets.csv

5,000 rows, 1.9 MB. One row per support ticket, with its text as a list of tokens. Used by Module 01 throughout. Download tickets.csv.

Why a third file exists. Module 01 needs three things the other two do not carry: text, so that a product of per-token probabilities can be built and watched underflow; a repeated entity, so that a split can leak at a level above the row; and a genuinely exponential column, so that fitting a rate from a median is fitting the right family. sessions.csv is lognormal and has no text and no repeated entity, so it cannot carry lessons 02, 06 or 07. That is the bar for adding a dataset and this is the one lesson group that clears it.

What was designed into it: token counts are heavy-tailed, so short tickets survive a naive product in float64 and long ones underflow to exactly 0.0 - on the held-out split, 8 tickets where both class scores underflow and 26 where exactly one does, which is the silent failure. first_response_seconds is exponential with a median of 420.3 s, so lambda = ln(2)/median is checkable against a number the generator put there. score_urgent is deliberately imperfect, giving a held-out AUC of 0.933. row_split is assigned per row, so 510 of the 527 test customers also appear in training - that leakage is the exercise, not an oversight. The two class vocabularies barely overlap, so the classifier in lesson 07 separates them almost perfectly; that accuracy is a property of the generator and the page says so.

m06-credit.csv

20,000 rows, 1.9 MB. Twelve features of a credit application, one binary default target and one continuous credit_limit_inr target. Used by Module 06 for every page of optimization. Download m06-credit.csv.

What was designed into it: the raw feature scales span six orders of magnitude, from a ratio in [0, 1] to an income in the hundreds of thousands, so the logistic Hessian's condition number is 2.82 × 1012 raw and 26.5 once the columns are standardised, and the largest learning rate that converges moves from 1.5 × 10-9 to 3.85. That nine-order-of-magnitude gap is what makes standardising a measurable intervention rather than a habit. Four columns are pure noise, independent of both targets, so an L1 path can be tested rather than described: it drops all four before it touches any real predictor. utilisation_ratio and emi_to_income correlate at 0.921, and the module uses that pair to show what correlation does and does not do to variable selection.

documents.csv

8,000 rows, 866 KB. Word counts over a 40-word vocabulary, one row per document, in four topics. Used by Module 03 for the dot product and cosine similarity. Download documents.csv.

What was designed into it: document length is drawn from a lognormal independently of topic, so the corpus holds 52-word and 5,965-word documents on every subject, and eight shared stopwords take about a third of every document's mass. That is what makes a raw dot product rank by length rather than by subject. Over 500 random queries the top ten share the query's topic 50.3% of the time by dot product and 100.0% by cosine, and the nearest neighbour of a 93-word astronomy document is a 5,760-word football document. This is the one lesson sensors.csv cannot carry, because a sensor reading has no analogue of document length.

Reproducing them

Each file is written by a seeded generator that produces byte-identical output on every run, so the numbers quoted on a page always match the numbers you get. The generators need only NumPy and Pandas and live beside the data they write.

inference_runs.csv

40,000 rows, 2.2 MB. One row per served model request. Used by Module 08 throughout. Download inference_runs.csv.

Why Module 08 needed its own files. Three things the other datasets do not carry. A genuinely rare binary column, cache_hit at 0.012, so a nominal 95 per cent interval on it covers 0.304 at n = 30 and the Central Limit Theorem lesson can measure the failure instead of asserting it. A pair of columns that are exactly dependent and still uncorrelated, log_resid and its own square resid_energy, correlating at 0.0202 while the absolute value correlates at 0.9356. And a heavy right tail: latency_ms is lognormal with mean 201.342 ms against median 156.127 ms and skew 3.097, which is what makes the mean visibly the wrong summary and the limit theorems slow enough to watch. screen_dpi is a null column at r = -0.0006, so a real and a null relationship sit in one table.

lifecycle_states.csv

240,000 rows, 3.5 MB. One row per user per week: 6,000 users observed for 40 weeks. Used by Module 08's Markov chains lesson. Download lifecycle_states.csv.

No cross-sectional table can carry a Markov chain, because a chain needs a per-entity sequence of states. The four states run trial, active, dormant and churned, and the generating transition matrix is fixed and known, so a lesson can estimate it by counting and check the estimate against the truth: the largest gap is 0.005 over 234,000 observed transitions. churned is absorbing on purpose, which makes the chain reducible and gives the lesson a real failure to show before repairing it.
