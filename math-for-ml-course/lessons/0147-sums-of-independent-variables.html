Adding two random variables does not add their distributions

working 11 min It convolves them, and the shape that comes out is nobody's intuition until they have seen it.

The one-minute version

  • Means always add. Variances add only when the parts are independent.
  • The distribution of a sum is a convolution: for every total, add up every way of splitting it between the two parts.
  • A few families are stable, so a sum of normals is normal and a sum of Poissons is Poisson. Most families are not.
  • Two uniforms add to a triangle, which is the first step of the pile-up the Central Limit Theorem finishes.

1. The mental model: count every way to make a total

Two independent dice-like variables. To find how likely a total of 5 is, you cannot look up 5 anywhere: you have to find every pair that makes 5, work out how likely each pair is, and add those up.

That is the entire idea. The probability of a total is a sum over the ways of splitting that total, and because the parts are independent, each way costs the product of its two probabilities.

pX+Y(t) = ∑k pX(k) · pY(t - k) Read as: the probability that the total is t equals the sum, over every value k the first variable might take, of the probability it took k times the probability the second took whatever is left over. The subscripts say which variable each distribution belongs to. This operation is called a convolution.

For continuous variables the sum becomes an integral over the same idea, and the operation keeps its name.

2. The two numbers, and which one needs independence

Means come free. Linearity of expectation from lesson 142 never asked about independence, so the mean of a sum is the sum of the means whatever the parts do.

Variances are the other story. Lesson 143 gave the general law, and the cross term is exactly what independence kills.

Var(X + Y) = Var(X) + Var(Y) + 2 Cov(X, Y)  →  Var(X) + Var(Y) when independent Read as: in general the variance of a sum carries a covariance correction, and independence sets that correction to zero. It is the only place independence enters this page's arithmetic.
Variances add. Standard deviations do not. Two independent sources with standard deviation 2 and 1.5 give a sum with variance 4 + 2.25 = 6.25 and standard deviation 2.5, not 3.5. Adding standard deviations overstates the spread every time, and it is the most common arithmetic slip in this whole module.

3. Which families survive being added

Sometimes the convolution lands back in the family it started in. Those families are called stable under addition, and they are the reason so much probability is done with so few distributions.

4. Worked example: total latency across a fan-out

A request fans out to three independent backends and waits for all of them, then adds a fixed 12 ms of serialisation. Each backend's own service time has mean 60 ms and standard deviation 25 ms.

  1. Write the total. T = S1 + S2 + S3 + 12, with the three service times independent.
  2. Add the means. 60 + 60 + 60 = 180, and the constant adds directly, so E[T] = 192 ms.
  3. Add the variances. Each is 252 = 625, so the sum has variance 625 × 3 = 1,875.
  4. The constant contributes nothing. Adding 12 shifts every outcome equally, so no deviation changes and the variance is unmoved.
  5. Take the square root. √1,875 = 43.3 ms.
  6. Notice what did not happen. Adding the three standard deviations would give 75 ms, which is 73 percent too large.
  7. Sanity check. The spread grew by √3 while the mean grew by 3, so the total is relatively tighter than any one backend: 43.3 ÷ 192 = 22.6 percent against 25 ÷ 60 = 41.7 percent.
  8. What changes if the backends are correlated. If they share a database and correlate at 0.5, three covariance terms appear, each 0.5 × 625 = 312.5, adding 2 × 3 × 312.5 = 1,875 to the variance. It doubles to 3,750 and the standard deviation rises to 61.2 ms. Shared infrastructure is not a detail, it is a factor of √2 on your tail.
code/0147-sums-of-independent-variables.py · NumPy and Pandas only · download
import numpy as np

pmf_a = np.array([0.10, 0.25, 0.30, 0.20, 0.10, 0.05])
pmf_b = np.array([0.30, 0.40, 0.20, 0.10])

exact = np.convolve(pmf_a, pmf_b)          # the convolution, in one call

rng = np.random.default_rng(20260827)
a = rng.choice(len(pmf_a), 400_000, p=pmf_a)
b = rng.choice(len(pmf_b), 400_000, p=pmf_b)
simulated = np.bincount(a + b, minlength=len(exact)) / 400_000

assert np.allclose(exact, simulated, atol=0.003)
print(exact.round(5), exact.sum())

5. Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

Two independent sources have standard deviations 3 and 4. What is the sum's standard deviation?
Variances add: 9 + 16 = 25, so the standard deviation is 5. The first option is the slip this page warns about, and it is the most common one in the module. Averaging the two spreads and multiplying them are not rules for anything here; they are shapes that look like arithmetic.
You add two independent Uniform(0,1) variables. What shape is the result?
Middle totals have more ways of being made than extreme ones, so the density rises to a peak at 1 and falls away. The first option assumes uniformity survives addition, which is what "not stable" means. The third jumps to the Central Limit Theorem, which is about many terms rather than two, and two uniforms are visibly a triangle rather than a bell.

Practice about 10 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A batch of 64 training examples is drawn independently. Each example's loss has mean 2.4 and standard deviation 1.8.

Find the mean and standard deviation of the batch's average loss, then say what batch size would be needed to halve that standard deviation, and what it would cost.

Hint

The batch average is a sum divided by 64, so use the variance rules in that order: add first, then scale. Remember that scaling by a constant multiplies the variance by its square.

Show the worked solution
  1. Mean of the average. Linearity gives 64 × 2.4 ÷ 64 = 2.4, unchanged. Averaging does not move the centre.
  2. Variance of one example. 1.82 = 3.24.
  3. Variance of the sum. Independent, so they add: 64 × 3.24 = 207.36.
  4. Variance of the average. Dividing by 64 divides the variance by 642: 207.36 ÷ 4,096 = 0.050625.
  5. Standard deviation. √0.050625 = 0.225, which is 1.8 ÷ 8 and 8 is √64.
  6. Halve it. The standard error goes as one over the square root of the batch size, so halving needs four times the batch: 256.
  7. What it costs. Four times the compute per step, for two times the precision per step.
  8. What changes if the examples are not independent. Sorted or clustered batches introduce positive covariance, the variance rises above 0.050625, and the effective batch size is smaller than 64. That is why batches are shuffled.

Sanity check. The standard deviation of the average must be smaller than one example's 1.8, and by exactly the factor √64 = 8. If yours came out as 1.8 × 8 or as 1.8 ÷ 64, the scaling rule was applied to the standard deviation rather than to the variance.

Your teacher. Find a latency budget in your own system that was built by adding percentiles or standard deviations across services. Redo it by adding variances instead. The number will come out smaller, and if the services share a dependency you now know which direction the correction goes.
