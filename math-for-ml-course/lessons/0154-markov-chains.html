Module 08 · Expectation, Limits and Simulation · Lesson 154

The present screens off the past, and one matrix is the whole future

frontier 14 min Repairing a chain so it settles takes two edges, not one, and finding that out is the lesson.

The one-minute version

  • The Markov property: the next state depends on the current one and on nothing before it.
  • The transition matrix holds every one-step probability, and n steps is just that matrix to the power n.
  • A stationary distribution satisfies πP = π. It is a left eigenvector of P for eigenvalue 1.
  • It is unique and reachable only if the chain is irreducible and aperiodic, and those are conditions to check.

1. The mental model: a state machine with numbers on the arrows

A user is in one of four states each week: trial, active, dormant or churned. Where they go next depends on where they are now, and not on how they got there. That assumption is the Markov property, and it is exactly what makes the process tractable.

P(Xt = j | Xt-1 = i, Xt-2, …) = P(Xt = j | Xt-1 = i) Read as: the probability of being in state j at time t, given the entire history, equals the probability given only the immediately preceding state. Everything before t minus one is on the left and absent from the right, and that absence is the whole assumption.

2. The mechanism: one matrix, and powers of it

Collect the arrows into a matrix. Row i, column j is the probability of moving from i to j in one step, so every row sums to one.

If v is the current distribution over states, written as a row, then one step forward is a vector-matrix product, and t steps is the matrix raised to t.

vt = v0 Pt Read as: the distribution over states after t steps equals the starting distribution multiplied by the transition matrix raised to the power t. v is a row vector of probabilities summing to one, and it is not a state: it is a belief about which state you are in.

Estimating P from data is nothing more than counting. Take every observed transition, tally them into a grid, and divide each row by its own total. On 234,000 observed transitions the estimate matches the matrix that generated them to within 0.005.

3. Stationary distributions

πP = π Read as: a distribution pi that another step of the chain leaves unchanged. Read in the language of Module 04, pi is a left eigenvector of P with eigenvalue 1, normalised so its entries sum to one.
Theorem If a finite chain is irreducible and aperiodic, it has exactly one stationary distribution π, and v0Pt converges to π from every starting distribution v0.

Proof, and where it stops here. Existence is the reachable half. P has every row summing to one, so the all-ones column vector is a right eigenvector with eigenvalue 1. A matrix and its transpose share eigenvalues, so 1 is an eigenvalue of P transposed too, which means a left eigenvector with eigenvalue 1 exists. That vector is π once it is normalised, and the Perron-Frobenius theorem is what guarantees it can be chosen with all entries non-negative.

Uniqueness and convergence are where this course stops. Both need Perron-Frobenius in its full form, which says that for an irreducible aperiodic non-negative matrix the eigenvalue 1 is simple and every other eigenvalue is strictly smaller in modulus. Proving that is a linear-algebra result beyond this course. What is worth carrying is the consequence, because it is checkable: the second-largest eigenvalue modulus controls the rate, and the distance to π falls roughly like that number raised to t.

4. The two conditions, and why the repair takes two edges

Irreducible means every state can reach every other state in some number of steps. Aperiodic means the chain is not locked into a cycle, and a single self-loop anywhere in an irreducible chain is enough to guarantee it.

The lifecycle chain fails the first condition, because churned goes only to itself. Run it and the answer is that everybody churns: by week 35 the state shares are 0.0000, 0.0293, 0.0130 and 0.9577. That is a limit, but it is not a useful stationary distribution.

The obvious repair is to let churned users reactivate. It is not enough, and this is the most useful thing on the page.

The reframing This is precisely what PageRank's damping term does. A raw web-link graph has dangling pages and disconnected components, so it is reducible and the theorem does not apply. The teleport probability adds an edge from every page to every page. It is not a hack bolted on for numerical reasons; it is what makes the theorem apply at all.

5. A glance at MCMC

Turn the whole idea around. Instead of analysing a chain someone handed you, build a chain whose stationary distribution is a target you cannot sample from directly, then run it and keep the states it visits.

Metropolis-Hastings is the recipe: propose a move, compute a ratio, accept with probability equal to the smaller of one and that ratio, and otherwise stay where you are. Staying is a real outcome and the repeated state is recorded as a sample.

A rejected MCMC proposal is not like a rejected proposal in the previous lesson. There, a rejection is discarded and nothing is recorded. Here, a rejection means the chain repeats its current state and that repeat is a sample. Dropping the repeats biases you away from high-density regions, which is the opposite of what the method is for. The shared word "reject" is the whole source of the error.

The price is that MCMC samples are not independent. You pay burn-in while the chain forgets its starting point, and autocorrelation means your effective sample size is smaller than your sample count.

6. Worked example: running the chain, and finding where it settles

  1. Start everybody in trial. v0 = (1, 0, 0, 0).
  2. One step is just the first row. Starting all mass in trial, v1 = (0.55, 0.30, 0.10, 0.05).
  3. Two steps, the trial entry worked by hand. 0.55 × 0.55 + 0.30 × 0 + 0.10 × 0 + 0.05 × 0 = 0.3025.
  4. The active entry. 0.55 × 0.30 + 0.30 × 0.82 + 0.10 × 0.22 + 0.05 × 0 = 0.165 + 0.246 + 0.022 = 0.4330.
  5. Check it sums to one. The remaining two entries are 0.1540 and 0.1105, so the full vector is (0.3025, 0.4330, 0.1540, 0.1105), which totals 1.0000.
  6. Now the repaired chain's stationary distribution. Solving πP = π gives (0.0446, 0.2076, 0.0786, 0.6691).
  7. Verify it. Multiplying that by P returns the same four numbers, which is what stationary means.
  8. What changes if you make churn stickier. The second-largest eigenvalue modulus is 0.8664, so the distance to π falls about 13 percent per step. Raise the self-loop on churned and that modulus rises towards one, the chain mixes more slowly, and a simulation needs far longer before its averages mean anything.
code/0154-markov-chains.py · NumPy and Pandas only · download
import numpy as np, pandas as pd

P = np.array([[0.55, 0.30, 0.10, 0.05],
              [0.00, 0.82, 0.13, 0.05],
              [0.00, 0.22, 0.60, 0.18],
              [0.03, 0.01, 0.00, 0.96]])      # the repaired chain

values, vectors = np.linalg.eig(P.T)          # pi is a LEFT eigenvector
pi = np.real(vectors[:, np.argmin(np.abs(values - 1.0))])
pi = pi / pi.sum()

assert np.allclose(pi @ P, pi)                # another step changes nothing
print(pi.round(4), "second eigenvalue", np.sort(np.abs(values))[-2].round(4))

The full program estimates the transition matrix from the committed 234,000 transitions and compares it against the truth, computes the stationary distribution three independent ways, and checks irreducibility by walking the zero pattern rather than by multiplying probabilities, which would underflow and lie.

7. Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

A chain has reached its stationary distribution. What is true?
Stationarity is a property of the distribution, not of any trajectory: individual users keep churning and reactivating forever while the shares stay put. The first option is the most common misreading of the word. The fourth confuses stationary with uniform, and the worked example's pi of (0.04, 0.21, 0.08, 0.67) is nowhere near uniform.
Why does PageRank add a small teleport probability to the link matrix?
A raw web graph has dangling pages and disconnected components, so it is reducible and the theorem guaranteeing a unique reachable stationary distribution simply does not apply. The second option is the strongest distractor because faster convergence is a genuine side effect, but it is a benefit rather than the reason. Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1frontier

A cache entry is either fresh or stale each minute. A fresh entry stays fresh with probability 0.7. A stale entry is refreshed, becoming fresh, with probability 0.4.

Find the transition matrix, the stationary distribution, and the second-largest eigenvalue modulus. Then say how many minutes it takes for the distance to stationarity to fall below one percent, starting from all-fresh.

Hint

With two states, pi P = pi plus the requirement that the two entries sum to one gives you a small linear system you can solve by substitution. For a two-state chain the second eigenvalue has a shortcut: it is the trace minus one.

Show the worked solution
  1. Build the matrix. Rows are from-states, so P = [[0.7, 0.3], [0.4, 0.6]], and each row sums to one.
  2. Write the stationary equations. With π = (f, s): f = 0.7f + 0.4s, and f + s = 1.
  3. Substitute. f = 0.7f + 0.4(1 - f) = 0.7f + 0.4 - 0.4f = 0.3f + 0.4.
  4. Solve. 0.7f = 0.4, so f = 4/7 = 0.5714 and s = 3/7 = 0.4286.
  5. Verify. πP gives 0.5714 × 0.7 + 0.4286 × 0.4 = 0.4000 + 0.1714 = 0.5714. Unchanged, as required.
  6. Second eigenvalue. The eigenvalues sum to the trace, 0.7 + 0.6 = 1.3, and one of them is 1, so the other is 0.3.
  7. Set up the decay. The distance falls roughly like 0.3t, so solve 0.3t < 0.01, giving t > ln(0.01) / ln(0.3) = 4.605 / 1.204 = 3.83.
  8. Answer, and what changes if. Four minutes. Now make the cache stickier at 0.95 fresh-to-fresh and 0.05 stale-to-fresh: the second eigenvalue becomes 0.95 + 0.95 - 1 = 0.90, and the same calculation gives t > 43.7, so 44 minutes. A chain that mixes slowly needs an order of magnitude longer before any average taken from it is trustworthy.

Sanity check. The stationary entries must be positive and sum to exactly one, and the second eigenvalue modulus must be strictly below one or the chain would never settle. Here 4/7 plus 3/7 is 1 and 0.3 is well under 1. Refreshing more eagerly should also make the fresh share larger, and 0.5714 is indeed above one half.

Your teacher. Find a state machine you already own, a job queue or a subscription lifecycle, and estimate its transition matrix by counting last month's transitions. Then check irreducibility. If any state has no way back, your long-run answer is that everything ends up there, and that is a statement about your model rather than about your product.
