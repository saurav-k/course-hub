Module 04 · Eigenvalues, SVD and PCA · Lesson 63

A matrix is a landscape once you feed it a vector twice

working 13 min Calculus-free by design. Module 05 owns the Hessian and module 06 owns convexity.

The one-minute version

  • q(x) = xTAx takes a vector in and returns one number, so it draws a surface.
  • The awkward part is the cross term, and rotating into the eigenbasis removes it completely.
  • In that basis the surface is a sum of independent parabolas, one per eigenvalue.
  • All eigenvalues positive is a bowl, mixed signs is a saddle, a zero is a flat valley floor.
  • On the level set a big eigenvalue gives a short axis. On the data cloud it gives a long one. Two ellipses, opposite behaviour.

1. From a matrix to a number

Sandwich a vector either side of a matrix and you get a single number. For a two-by-two, writing it out gives

q(x) = xTAx = a x12 + 2b x1x2 + d x22 Read as: a and d are the diagonal entries of the matrix and b is the off-diagonal one. The first and last terms involve one coordinate each. The middle term, the cross term, mixes them, and it is the only difficult part of the whole expression.

Assuming symmetry costs nothing here. For any matrix at all, xTAx equals xT((A + AT)/2)x, because a single number equals its own transpose, so only the symmetric part of a matrix ever reaches a quadratic form. That is why lesson 62 applies to every one of them.

2. The rotation that removes the cross term

Principal axes For symmetric A, substituting x = Qy with Q the orthonormal eigenvector matrix turns xTAx into λ1y12 + … + λnyn2, with no cross terms at all.

Proof. By lesson 62, A = QΛQT with QTQ = I. Substitute x = Qy:

xTAx = (Qy)T(QΛQT)(Qy) = yT(QTQ)Λ(QTQ)y = yTΛy Read as: the transpose of a product reverses it, which puts a QTQ on each side of Λ. Each of those is the identity because Q is orthogonal, so both vanish and only the diagonal middle survives.

Since Λ is diagonal, yTΛy is just ∑λiyi2.

The step that does the real work is QTQ = I collapsing in the middle, twice. It is available only because the eigenvectors are orthonormal, which is exactly what symmetry bought on lesson 62. For a matrix that is not symmetric the change of basis leaves a P-1 that does not cancel, and the cross terms survive.

What the substitution is not. x = Qy turns the coordinate system, not the surface. The bowl does not move. The reader's axes rotate to line up with it, which is why the cross term disappears without anything about the matrix changing.

3. The three shapes

4. The trap worth slowing down for

On the level set xTAx = 1, substituting into the eigenbasis gives ∑λiyi2 = 1, so along eigenvector i the curve reaches yi = 1/√λi. A big eigenvalue gives a short axis.

But a cloud of data whose covariance is A spreads by √λi along eigenvector i, so there a big eigenvalue gives a long axis. Both statements are correct about different objects, and mixing them up is the most reliably reversed fact in this module.

5. Worked in full

  1. Take the matrix. S = [[5, 2], [2, 2]], whose eigenpairs lesson 62 already found.
  2. Write the form. q(x) = 5x12 + 4x1x2 + 2x22. The 4 is 2b with b = 2.
  3. Evaluate three points. q(1, 0) = 5. q(1, 1) = 5 + 4 + 2 = 11. q(1, -2) = 5 - 8 + 8 = 5. All positive, which is a hint and not yet a proof.
  4. Bring in the eigenvalues. 6 and 1, with q1 = (2, 1)/√5 and q2 = (1, -2)/√5.
  5. Rotate. In the eigenbasis the form is 6y12 + 1y22. Both coefficients positive, so the form is positive for every non-zero vector. Step 3's hint is now a proof.
  6. Read the level set. It reaches 1/√6 = 0.408 along q1 and 1/√1 = 1 along q2.
  7. Sanity check. Evaluate the form at those two points. Along q1 at distance 0.408: 6(0.408)2 = 0.999, which is 1 to rounding. Along q2 at distance 1: 1(1)2 = 1. Both land on the level set, so the formula is the right way up. Getting 2.449 and 1 instead means √λ was used where 1/√λ belongs.
  8. What changes if. Use [[1, 2], [2, 1]] instead. Its trace is 2 and determinant -3, so the eigenvalues are 3 and -1. Now q(1, 1) = 1 + 4 + 1 = 6 is positive while q(1, -1) = 1 - 4 + 1 = -2 is negative. One matrix, both signs: a saddle.

6. A quadratic form doing real work

Give every node of a graph a number and ask how rough that labelling is across the edges. The graph Laplacian L = D - W answers it as a quadratic form.

fTLf = ½ ∑i,j wij(fi - fj)2 Read as: f holds one number per node, wij is the weight of the edge between nodes i and j, and the sum runs over every pair. Each term is the squared disagreement across one edge, so the whole quantity measures how unsmooth the labelling is.

On a five-node path with unit weights, a smooth ramp (1, 2, 3, 4, 5) scores 4, while an alternating labelling (1, 5, 1, 5, 1) scores 64. The form has judged the second sixteen times rougher, and because it is a sum of squares it can never be negative, which is the next lesson's subject arriving early.

Answer from memory before revealing.

For a positive definite A, along the eigenvector with the LARGEST eigenvalue, the level set x transpose A x = 1 is:
In the eigenbasis the level set is a sum of lambda times y squared equal to one, so the axis reaches one over the root of lambda and a big eigenvalue gives a short axis. The first option is the trap: it is true of the data cloud, whose spread is the root of lambda, which is a different ellipse from the same matrix. The second contradicts the equation and the third invents a constraint.
A symmetric matrix has eigenvalues plus 4 and minus 1. What shape is its quadratic form?
Mixed signs mean the surface climbs along one eigendirection and falls along the other, which is a saddle. A bowl needs both positive and a dome needs both negative, so the second and third each get half the condition. The fourth would need an eigenvalue of exactly zero, and 4 and -1 do not cancel.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

Write the quadratic form of the matrix below in full, find its eigenvalues, say what shape it is, and give both semi-axes of the level set where the form equals one.

A = [[2, -1], [-1, 2]] Read as: a symmetric two-by-two matrix. Both diagonal entries are 2 and the repeated off-diagonal entry is -1.
Hint

The trace and determinant give the eigenvalues with no expansion. Once you have them, remember that the level set reaches one over the root of each, not the root.

Show the worked solution
  1. Write the form. q(x) = 2x12 - 2x1x2 + 2x22, because the cross term picks up 2b with b = -1.
  2. Read the invariants. trace = 4, det = 4 - 1 = 3.
  3. Solve. λ2 - 4λ + 3 = 0 gives 3 and 1. Both positive, so it is a bowl.
  4. Eigenvectors. For 3, A - 3I = [[-1, -1], [-1, -1]] gives (1, -1). For 1, A - I = [[1, -1], [-1, 1]] gives (1, 1).
  5. Semi-axes. 1/√3 = 0.577 along (1, -1)/√2, and 1/√1 = 1 along (1, 1)/√2.

Sanity check. Take the short axis endpoint, which is 0.577 × (0.7071, -0.7071) = (0.408, -0.408), and evaluate the form there: 2(0.1667) + 2(0.1667) + 2(0.1667) = 1.000. It lands on the level set, so the axes are the right way up. Also 3 + 1 = 4 and 3 × 1 = 3, reproducing both invariants.

Your teacher. If the two ellipses in section 4 still feel interchangeable, ask an agent to sample two hundred points from a Gaussian with covariance [[5, 2], [2, 2]], plot them, and draw the curve xTSx = 1 on the same axes. The cloud is long where the curve is short, and seeing them overlaid once fixes it permanently.
