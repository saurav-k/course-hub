Centre and spread are two questions, and the shape decides the answers

foundation 12 min The mean answers to every value. The median answers only to rank. That is the whole trade.

The one-minute version

  • Four numbers do most of the work: mean, median, mode for where the column sits, and the standard deviation for how far it spreads.
  • Deviations from the mean always sum to zero. That is a theorem, it is two lines, and it is why a spread measure has to square or take absolute values first.
  • The n-1 is there because one number was already spent working out the mean. M09 owns the word for it and the proof.
  • The mean uses every value and therefore answers to every value; the median uses only order. One far-out value moves one and not the other.
  • Two libraries disagree on the default divisor, so the same column gives two standard deviations depending on which you called.

1. Three answers to "where is it"

The mean is the total shared out equally. The median is the value in the middle once the column is sorted. The mode is the value that occurs most often. Written with lesson 01's sigma, the mean is the only one that needs notation at all:

x̄ = (1/n) Σi=1n xi Read as: add up all n values in the column and divide by how many there were. xi is the i-th value, i runs from 1 to n, and the bar over the x is the conventional mark for "the mean of this column".

On the 20,000 session lengths from lesson 21, those three come out at 171.75 seconds, 98.90 seconds and 30.0 seconds. They disagree by a factor of nearly six, and the disagreement is not an error - it is the shape of the column telling you something.

2. Deviations sum to zero, and why that forces a squaring

The obvious way to measure spread is to see how far each value sits from the mean and average those distances. It does not work, and the reason is a theorem.

Deviations from the mean sum to zero For any column at all, Σi (xi − x̄) = 0.

Proof. Split the sum: Σi (xi − x̄) = Σi xi − n·x̄, since x̄ is a constant being subtracted n times. By the definition of the mean, Σi xi = n·x̄. So the expression is n·x̄ − n·x̄ = 0.

The step that does the work Substituting the definition of the mean into its own deviation sum. Once you see that Σ xi is n·x̄, the result stops being surprising and starts being inevitable - which is the useful state to be in.

So the positive and negative deviations cancel exactly, every time, on every column. The average deviation is zero for a column of identical values and zero for a column spread across a mile. To measure spread you have to remove the signs first, and squaring is the choice that makes the algebra work later.

3. Variance, standard deviation, and the divisor

s2 = (1/(n−1)) Σi=1n (xi − x̄)2      s = √(s2) Read as: square every deviation from the mean, add them up, divide by one less than the count, and that is the variance. The standard deviation is its square root. s2 is the variance, s the standard deviation, n the number of values.

The two are the same information and they are not interchangeable in practice: the standard deviation is in the column's own units, so it can go on a chart beside the data, and the variance is in units squared, so it cannot. Variance is for the algebra; standard deviation is for the reader.

Why n−1? Because one number was already spent. The deviations are measured from x̄, and x̄ came from this same column - section 2 just showed that once you know any n−1 of the deviations, the last one is forced, because they must sum to zero. So there are only n−1 genuinely free quantities in that sum, and dividing by n would be dividing by more than you have. M09 owns the formal word for this and the proof that goes with it; this page owns the intuition and the arithmetic.

The divisor trap, as a number rather than a warning. On these 20,000 sessions, dividing by n−1 gives a variance of 58,584.37 and dividing by n gives 58,581.44. Pandas defaults to the first and NumPy to the second, so frame.var() and np.var(frame) on the same column return different numbers. At 20,000 rows the gap is invisible; at 8 rows it is not, and it is always silent.

4. Which centre answers to what

Measured on the real column: drop the top 1.5% of sessions - the bot traffic lesson 21's tail was made of - and the mean falls from 171.75 to 151.51 seconds, a move of 20.24. The median moves from 98.90 to 97.00, a move of 1.90. Same rows removed, one summary moved ten times as far as the other.

There is a sharper way to say it. The mean is the single value that minimises the total squared distance to the column, and the median is the value that minimises the total absolute distance. Squared distance punishes being far away much harder than absolute distance does, which is precisely why the mean chases a tail and the median ignores it.

5. So which one do you report?

6. Worked: ten values, then one outlier

Ten session lengths in seconds: 12, 19, 23, 31, 44, 52, 67, 89, 104, 159.

  1. Add them and divide. The total is 600, so the mean is 600 ÷ 10 = 60.0.
  2. Build the deviation column. −48, −41, −37, −29, −16, −8, 7, 29, 44, 99.
  3. Check the theorem. The negatives total −179 and the positives total 179, so the sum is 0. If it is not zero the mean is wrong, and everything after it would be too.
  4. Square and total. 2304 + 1681 + 1369 + 841 + 256 + 64 + 49 + 841 + 1936 + 9801 = 19,142.
  5. Divide by n−1 and take the root. 19,142 ÷ 9 = 2,126.9, so s = 46.1 seconds.
  6. Median by sorting. Already sorted; with ten values it is the average of the 5th and 6th, (44 + 52) ÷ 2 = 48.0.
  7. Now make the largest ten times bigger. Replace 159 with 1,590. The total becomes 2,031, so the mean jumps to 203.1 - it more than tripled. The median is still the average of the 5th and 6th values, so it is 48.0, unchanged. The standard deviation goes from 46.1 to 488.2.
Sanity check. Your deviations must sum to zero, and your standard deviation must be smaller than the range of the data - 46.1 against a range of 147 is comfortable, and a standard deviation larger than the range means an arithmetic slip. What changes if you had used the median instead of the mean to build the deviation column: the sum would not be zero, because that theorem is specifically about the mean.

7. The definition and the library, side by side

code/0022-four-numbers-that-summarise-a-column.py · NumPy and Pandas only · download · data: sessions.csv
# the variance from the definition, and the same number from the library
deviations = column - column.mean()
by_definition = (deviations ** 2).sum() / (len(column) - 1)
assert np.isclose(by_definition, column.var())          # pandas defaults to ddof=1

# and the trap, printed rather than warned about
print(np.var(column), np.var(column, ddof=1))           # 58581.44 against 58584.37

The program also checks the theorem numerically: the deviations sum to 1.26e-10, which relative to the scale of the data is 3.7e-17. That residue is floating point, not algebra - the theorem is exact and the arithmetic is not, and knowing which is which is the difference between shrugging at a tiny number and chasing a bug.

One note on scope. The program goes further than this page does: it simulates the divisor question directly, resampling the column at several sizes to show the n divisor sitting systematically low and the n−1 divisor sitting on target. That demonstration needs vocabulary M02 has not introduced, so M09 owns it. Run it when you get there; it is the same code.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

Why does the sample variance divide by n−1 rather than n?
The deviations are measured from a mean computed from this same column, and they must sum to zero, so only n-1 of them are free. The first option sounds like an explanation and explains nothing - it is true of every sample and does not say why the divisor changes. The second is a consequence, not a reason. The fourth is unrelated, and the root is applied after the division regardless.
One value in a 1,000-row column is multiplied by 100. Which summary moves most?
The deviation of that one value grows a hundredfold and is then squared, so the variance and its root move furthest by a wide margin. The median barely stirs, because the value was already the largest and stays the largest - its rank did not change. The mode does not move at all unless that value happened to be the most common one. The count is untouched.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1foundation

Ten queue depths, in order of arrival: 4, 7, 7, 9, 11, 12, 15, 18, 22, 35.

(a) Compute the mean, median, mode and sample standard deviation.
(b) A monitoring glitch turns the 35 into 350. Recompute all four.
(c) Which one would you put in a report to a stakeholder, and what would you say alongside it?

Hint

Build the deviation column before anything else. You need it for the standard deviation, and its sum is a free check on your mean.

Show the worked solution
  1. Mean. The total is 140, so the mean is 140 ÷ 10 = 14.0.
  2. Deviations, and the check. −10, −7, −7, −5, −3, −2, 1, 4, 8, 21. Negatives −34, positives 34, sum 0. The mean is right.
  3. Squares and standard deviation. 100+49+49+25+9+4+1+16+64+441 = 758. Divide by 9 to get 84.2, and the root is 9.18.
  4. Median and mode. Sorted already; the 5th and 6th are 11 and 12, so the median is 11.5. The only repeated value is 7, so the mode is 7.
  5. Now the glitch. The total becomes 455, so the mean is 45.5 - it more than tripled on one bad row. The median is still the 5th and 6th values, 11.5. The mode is still 7.
  6. The spread after the glitch. The deviation on that row is 304.5, and squaring it dominates everything else: the standard deviation goes from 9.18 to 107.1.
  7. Answer (c). Report the median, and report it as a median rather than as "the average". Then say two more things: that one row is a suspected glitch, and what the mean is with and without it. A summary that quietly excluded a row is not a summary, it is an edit.

Sanity check. Deviations sum to zero and the standard deviation is smaller than the range in both parts - 9.18 against 31, then 107.1 against 346. If your deviations do not sum to zero, stop: the mean is wrong and every number after it inherits the error.

Your teacher. The theorem in section 2 is two lines and it earns its place, because it is the reason spread measures square things rather than just averaging distances. If the n−1 still feels arbitrary, the honest position is that this page gives you the intuition and M09 gives you the proof - and you can check the intuition right now by asking an agent to "compute the variance of ten numbers with both divisors and show me the gap at n = 10, n = 100 and n = 10,000".
