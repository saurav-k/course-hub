A quantile answers only to rank, which is its strength and its blindness

foundation 12 min The vocabulary a latency budget is written in, and the reason a mean latency is nearly useless for one.

The one-minute version

  • A quantile is a position before it is a value: sort the column, step a fraction of the way along, read what is there.
  • The median is the 50th percentile under another name, and the interquartile range is the width of the middle half.
  • A box plot is the five-number summary drawn. It keeps the middle faithfully and throws the shape away.
  • A point beyond a whisker is far from the middle. It is not an error, and treating the two as the same is how real data gets deleted.
  • Quantiles are robust and blind: move the largest value a thousand times further out and the p95 does not stir.

1. From the median to any fraction

The median splits a sorted column in half. Nothing about that is special to one half. Sort the column, walk a fraction q of the way along, and read the value there: that is the q-quantile. Expressed as a percentage it is a percentile, so the 0.95-quantile and the 95th percentile are the same number.

The quartiles are the three that cut the column into quarters: Q1 at 0.25, Q2 at 0.50 (the median), and Q3 at 0.75. The distance between the outer two is the interquartile range:

IQR = Q3 − Q1 Read as: the width of the middle half of the data. Q1 is the value a quarter of the way through the sorted column and Q3 is the value three quarters of the way through, so the IQR is a spread measure that ignores everything outside those two cuts entirely.

On the 20,000 sessions: Q1 is 48.58 seconds, the median is 98.90, Q3 is 200.80, and the IQR is 152.23 seconds.

2. The awkward bit, said out loud

When the fraction lands between two data points, something has to be chosen. Take the 0.25 quantile of eleven values: a quarter of the way along is position 3.5, and there is no 3.5th value.

There are several conventions. This course uses linear interpolation between the two neighbouring values, which is what NumPy and Pandas do by default: the value at position 3.5 is the midpoint of the 3rd and 4th. Other conventions take the lower value, the higher one, or the nearest.

Libraries differ, and the differences are usually invisible and occasionally not. On this column, linear interpolation and taking the lower value give Q1 as 48.58 against 48.50 - a gap smaller than the measurement precision. On eleven values the same choice can move a quartile by a whole data point. State which convention you used when the sample is small; ignore the question when it is large.

3. The five-number summary, and the box that draws it

The five-number summary is minimum, Q1, median, Q3, maximum: on this column, 2.00, 48.58, 98.90, 200.80, 6050.40. The box plot draws it, with the box spanning the IQR and a line inside at the median.

4. The whiskers, and what a point beyond one does not mean

The usual convention puts the whiskers at Q1 − 1.5·IQR and Q3 + 1.5·IQR, and draws anything past them as an individual point. On this column those fences land at -179.76 and 429.14, and 1,628 sessions - 8.14% of them - sit beyond the upper one.

The 1.5 is a convention, not a theorem. On a symmetric bell-shaped column it flags roughly 0.7% of points, which is where the number came from. This column is not that shape, so it flags eight per cent. A point beyond a whisker has been marked as far from the middle. It has not been marked as wrong. Eight per cent of your traffic is not an error, and deleting it because a chart drew it as a dot is how real data gets destroyed.

The honest reading of a flagged point is: this is unusual relative to the middle half, go and find out why. Sometimes the answer is a broken sensor. On this column the answer is bot traffic, which is real and worth knowing about.

5. Robust, and blind, for the same reason

That flatness is exactly what you want when the far-out value is contamination, and exactly what you do not want when it is signal. A quantile cannot tell you which it is, because it never looked at the magnitude. That is the trade-off, and it is why lesson 22's advice to report the mean beside the median holds here too.

6. Why a latency budget is written in p99

The mean of this column is 171.75 seconds, and it sits at about the 70th percentile: 70.23% of sessions are shorter than "the average session". A number that most of your data falls below is a poor description of a typical case.

Worse, a mean says nothing at all about the worst experiences. Here p50 is 98.90 seconds, p95 is 557.92, and p99 is 1,122.94. A budget written as "average response under 200 ms" is satisfied by a system where one request in a hundred takes six seconds. A budget written as "p99 under 200 ms" is not, and that is the entire reason the vocabulary exists.

7. Worked: eleven values, then twelve

Eleven response times, sorted: 8, 12, 15, 19, 23, 28, 34, 41, 55, 72, 130.

  1. Find the median by position. Eleven values, so the middle is the 6th: 28. Five below, five above, no ambiguity.
  2. Find Q1. A quarter of the way along eleven values is position 1 + 0.25 × 10 = 3.5, so halfway between the 3rd and 4th: (15 + 19) ÷ 2 = 17.
  3. Find Q3. Position 1 + 0.75 × 10 = 8.5, so halfway between the 8th and 9th: (41 + 55) ÷ 2 = 48.
  4. Take the IQR. 48 − 17 = 31, the width of the middle half.
  5. Write the five-number summary. 8, 17, 28, 48, 130.
  6. Draw the fences. 17 − 1.5 × 31 = −29.5 and 48 + 1.5 × 31 = 94.5. So 130 is drawn as a separate point, and 72 is not.
  7. Now multiply the largest by 20. 130 becomes 2,600. The sorted order is unchanged, so every position is unchanged, so Q1, the median, Q3 and the IQR are all identical. Only the maximum moved, from 130 to 2,600.
Sanity check. Q1 ≤ Q2 ≤ Q3 always, and the IQR is never negative. If your Q1 comes out above your median, the sort was skipped. What changes if you use the lower-value convention instead of interpolating: Q1 becomes 15 and Q3 becomes 41, so the IQR drops from 31 to 26 - which is why the convention is worth stating on a sample this small.

8. Two ways to a quantile, checked against each other

code/0023-quantiles-and-the-box-plot.py · NumPy and Pandas only · download · data: sessions.csv
# the definition: a quantile is a position before it is a value
ordered  = np.sort(column)
position = q * (len(ordered) - 1)
low, frac = int(np.floor(position)), position - np.floor(position)
by_hand = ordered[low] + frac * (ordered[low + 1] - ordered[low])   # linear interpolation

assert np.isclose(by_hand, np.percentile(column, 100 * q))

Writing the interpolation out is what makes the convention visible: low is the data point below the cut and frac is how far past it the position falls. Swap those two lines for ordered[low] alone and you have the lower-value convention instead, which is the whole of the difference between the two columns the program prints.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

A box plot draws six points beyond the upper whisker. What have those points been shown to be?
The whisker rule is arithmetic on Q1, Q3 and the IQR, and it knows nothing about whether a value is correct. The first option is the belief this page exists to break: on this course's own column the rule flags 8.14% of sessions, and they are bot traffic - real, and worth knowing about. The second assumes a cause the chart never checked. The third has it backwards, since those points are inside the range by definition, being part of it.
Why does a latency budget quote p99 rather than the mean?
A mean averages the bad requests away, so "average under 200 ms" is satisfied by a system where one request in a hundred takes six seconds. The second is false and backwards, since a mean is the cheaper statistic to keep. The third is a true statement about quantiles in general that answers a different question - robustness is not why a budget is written this way, coverage is. The fourth is true of both.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1foundation

Eleven response times in milliseconds, sorted: 40, 45, 52, 61, 74, 88, 96, 115, 140, 190, 480.

(a) Give the five-number summary and the IQR, interpolating where the position falls between two values.
(b) Which values, if any, fall beyond the whiskers?
(c) Multiply the largest by 20 and say which of the five numbers changed.

Hint

Sort first, then find each quantile as a position before you read a value. For eleven values the position of the q-quantile is 1 + q × 10.

Show the worked solution
  1. Median. Position 1 + 0.5 × 10 = 6, so the 6th value: 88.
  2. Q1. Position 1 + 0.25 × 10 = 3.5, halfway between the 3rd and 4th: (52 + 61) ÷ 2 = 56.5.
  3. Q3. Position 1 + 0.75 × 10 = 8.5, halfway between the 8th and 9th: (115 + 140) ÷ 2 = 127.5.
  4. IQR and the summary. 127.5 − 56.5 = 71. Five-number summary: 40, 56.5, 88, 127.5, 480.
  5. Fences for (b). 56.5 − 1.5 × 71 = −50 and 127.5 + 1.5 × 71 = 234. Only 480 exceeds the upper fence; 190 is inside it.
  6. Say what that means. 480 ms has been marked as far from the middle half. It has not been marked as wrong, and on a latency column a single slow request is usually the most interesting row you have.
  7. Part (c). 480 becomes 9,600. It was already the largest, so the sorted order is unchanged and every position is unchanged. Q1, the median and Q3 are identical; only the maximum moved. Four of the five numbers did not notice.

Sanity check. Q1 ≤ median ≤ Q3, the IQR is positive, and the fences must sit outside the box - a lower fence below the minimum simply means nothing is flagged at that end, which is normal for a column that cannot go negative.

Your teacher. This page names no theorem; a quantile is a definition and the 1.5 in the whisker rule is a convention someone chose. What is worth arguing with is that convention. Ask an agent to "show me what fraction of a normal column and what fraction of a lognormal column the 1.5 IQR rule flags" - seeing 0.7% against 8% is what stops the rule feeling like a law.

Primary source to go deeper

Tukey, J. W., Exploratory Data Analysis (Addison-Wesley, 1977) is where the box plot and the 1.5 IQR convention come from, and it is worth reading for the attitude as much as the technique. For the interpolation question, numpy.percentile documents the nine methods it implements and names its default, which is the one this page uses.
