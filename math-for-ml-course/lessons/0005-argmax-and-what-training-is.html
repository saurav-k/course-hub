Training is an argmin, and prediction is an argmax

foundation 10 min Two operators, one question each: what is the best score, and where is it.

The one-minute version

  • max returns a value and argmax returns a place. They have different types and answer different questions.
  • Supervised learning fits on one line: choose the parameters that minimise the average loss. Everything else is how.
  • argmax is not differentiable, and softmax exists because of that. Its name is wrong: it is a soft argmax, not a soft max.
  • Softmax is shift invariant - this page proves it - and that fact is both the stabilisation trick and the reason temperature never changes a prediction.

1. Two operators that are not the same operator

Take one row of real ticket logits. The model has scored three classes.

So max is the model's confidence and argmax is its answer. When a monitoring dashboard shows "0.91" it is showing you a max, and when it routes the ticket it used an argmax.

argmin is the same operator with the sign flipped: argmin f = argmax(−f). That is the whole relationship, and it is why a loss to be minimised is so often a negative log-likelihood to be maximised.

Ties, which nobody warns you about. If two classes score exactly the same, argmax is a set of two answers, not one. Library code picks a convention and does not tell you: NumPy returns the first. That is a convention, not the mathematics, and one day it will be the reason two implementations disagree.

2. Supervised learning, in one line

θ* = argminθ (1/n) Σi=1n L(f(x(i); θ), y(i)) Read as: θ* is the setting of the parameters that makes the average mistake smallest. n is how many examples there are, i counts them, x(i) is the i-th input and y(i) its correct answer, f is the model with its parameters after the semicolon, L measures one mistake, and the subscript on argmin says the search runs over θ and nothing else.

Every symbol in that line came from an earlier page: the index and the sigma from lesson 01, the semicolon from lesson 03, and the operator from section 1. What it adds is the subscript. The subscript on an argmin names the search space, and "over what?" is the first question to ask whenever you meet one.

Prediction is the same operator over a different set. Once θ* is fixed, ŷ = argmaxc p(c | x) searches over classes rather than parameters. Same question, different space.

3. Why softmax exists, and why its name is wrong

argmax has a problem: it is a step. Nudge a losing logit and the answer does not move at all, until the moment it overtakes and the answer jumps. A function that is flat everywhere and then jumps carries no gradient anywhere, so nothing can be trained through it.

Softmax is the smoothed replacement, and the Deep Learning textbook is blunt about the naming: the function "is more closely related to the arg max function than the max function", the "soft" comes from being continuous and differentiable where argmax is not, and "it would perhaps be better to call the softmax function softargmax, but the current name is an entrenched convention".

softmax(z)i = ezi / Σj ezj Read as: exponentiate every score, then divide each one by the total so the results add to one. z is the vector of raw scores, i picks which output you want, and j is the index of the sum in the denominator - a bound index, running over every class.

Read it against a one-hot vector and the name explains itself. argmax returns [0, 1, 0]; softmax returns something like [0.09, 0.90, 0.01]. It is a blurred one-hot, not a blurred maximum. The soft version of the maximum is a different object entirely: softmax(z) · z.

4. Shift invariance, proved

One property of softmax does an unreasonable amount of work in the next two lessons.

Softmax shift invariance For any score vector z and any constant c, softmax(z + c) = softmax(z). Adding the same number to every score changes nothing at all.

Proof. Write out the i-th output with the constant added, and split each exponential using ea+b = eaeb:

ezi+c / Σj ezj+c  =  (ec ezi) / (ec Σj ezj)  =  ezi / Σj ezj Read as: the same factor ec appears in the top and in every term of the bottom, so it can be taken outside the sum and cancelled. What is left is the softmax of the original scores.
The step that does the work ec can be pulled out of a sum because it is a common factor of every term. Nothing in the argument needs c to be small, or positive, or anything else. That is what makes the next paragraph legal.

Now spend it, twice.

Once for arithmetic. Choose c = −maxj zj. Every argument to the exponential becomes zero or negative, the largest is exactly zero, and e0 = 1 can neither overflow nor vanish. The answer is provably unchanged, so this costs nothing. That is the stabilisation the next lesson leans on.

Once for meaning. Adding a constant preserves the ordering, so by lesson 04's theorem the argmax cannot move either. Shifting all the logits changes no prediction and no confidence.

5. Temperature, which changes confidence and never the answer

The program checks that claim rather than asserting it: on all 5,000 rows of the ticket data, at four temperatures, the argmax is identical every time, while the mean top probability falls from 0.9559 to 0.4076.

code/0005-argmax-and-what-training-is.py · runnable Python, numpy and pandas only · download · data: tickets.csv
reference = logits.argmax(axis=1)
for temperature in (0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 10.0):
    probabilities = softmax_stable(logits / temperature)
    assert np.array_equal(probabilities.argmax(axis=1), reference)

with np.errstate(over="ignore", invalid="ignore"):
    naive_big = softmax_naive(logits + 800.0)
assert np.isnan(naive_big).all()                       # every one of 5,000 rows
assert np.isfinite(softmax_stable(logits + 800.0)).all()

It also shifts every logit by 800 and watches the naive formula return nan on all 5,000 rows while the stabilised one does not move by a single bit. That is section 4's proof, cashed.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

A model reports maxc p = 0.91 and argmaxc p = 7. Which number is the prediction?
The prediction is the class, and the class is what argmax returns. The first option is the confidence, which is a genuinely useful number answering a different question, and reporting it as the prediction is the confusion this page exists for. The third is the misconception stated outright, and the two have different types. The fourth is false: the logits add nothing once you have the argmax.
The Deep Learning textbook calls softmax misnamed. What is it a soft version of?
Softmax returns a vector that blurs a one-hot, which is a softened argmax. The first option is the name itself and it is the error the textbook names: the soft version of the maximum is a different object, the dot product of softmax with the scores. The second is the denominator rather than the function. The third is unrelated.

Practice about 10 minutes, with paper

Practice 1foundation

A model emits the logits [1000, 999, 998].

(a) What does a naive ez do here, and why?
(b) Apply the stabilisation from section 4 and compute the softmax by hand.
(c) Compare your answer with the softmax of [2, 1, 0]. What does the comparison show?

Hint

For (c), subtract the largest entry from each of the two vectors and put the results side by side before computing anything.

Show the worked solution
  1. Find the ceiling. float64 stores numbers up to about 1.8 × 10308, which is e709.78. So e1000 is inf.
  2. Follow the arithmetic through. Every term is inf, the denominator is inf, and inf / inf is nan. All three outputs are nan, so the answer is not merely inaccurate, it is absent.
  3. Subtract the maximum. c = −1000 gives [0, −1, −2], and by the theorem the answer is unchanged.
  4. Exponentiate. e0 = 1, e−1 = 0.3679, e−2 = 0.1353. The largest is exactly 1, which is why nothing can overflow.
  5. Sum and divide. Total 1.5032, giving 0.665, 0.245, 0.090.
  6. Do the same to [2, 1, 0]. Subtracting its maximum also gives [0, −1, −2], so the answer is identical.
  7. Say why. The two vectors differ by the constant 998, and softmax cannot see a constant. Two score vectors that look nothing alike are the same distribution.

Sanity check. The three outputs must sum to 1 - if yours sum to nan, you stabilised after exponentiating instead of before, which is the one ordering that does not work. And the largest input must give the largest output, since exponentiating is strictly increasing.

Your teacher. The proof in section 4 is three lines and the whole of it is one exponent rule. If it went past too quickly, ask an agent to "show me softmax of [2,1,0] and softmax of [1002,1001,1000] computed both naively and with the max subtracted, printing every intermediate value in float64" - watching the naive column fill with inf and then nan is more convincing than the algebra.
