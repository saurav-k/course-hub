Survey sampling designs, and which question each one answers

foundation 11 min The one page in this module you can skip and still read the rest of the course.

The one-minute version

  • How you choose the rows decides how precise your answer is, and no arithmetic afterwards repairs a bad choice.
  • Total variance splits into within-stratum and between-stratum. Stratifying removes the second from your error entirely.
  • On this population the between part is 87.4 per cent of the total, which predicts the measured variance ratio of 0.130.
  • Stratified k-fold cross-validation is this exact idea, which turns a machine learning habit into a statistical fact.
A word collision worth flagging now. On this page, sampling means choosing rows from a real population that already exists. In Module 08 it will mean drawing values from a known probability law. Both are called sampling and they are different acts. Nothing on this page involves probability distributions; every quantity is arithmetic on a population file you could open.

1. The question every design answers

Two questions, always. Which population does this sample let me describe? And how precise is the answer? The design decides both, before any analysis begins.

The population here is population.csv: 30,000 units with a spend column whose true mean is 629.2010. Because we have the whole population we can check every design against the truth, which is exactly what you cannot do in real life and exactly why a teaching population is worth generating.

2. Five designs

Simple random gives every unit the same chance. It is the baseline, and it is unbiased for the population mean. Stratified partitions first and samples inside each part, fixing how many units each part contributes. Cluster takes whole groups, which is cheap when reaching a group is the expensive step. Systematic takes every k-th unit and fails badly in exactly one situation: when the data has a period matching k. Convenience takes whoever was easiest.

3. The two stated results

Result 1 Simple random sampling is unbiased for the population mean. Drawing n units without replacement from N, every unit's chance of appearing is n/N, identical across units. The sample mean weights every included unit equally, and since every population unit is equally likely to be included, its expectation is the population mean.

The step that does the work is that the inclusion probability is the same for every unit. That is what the estimator's equal weighting has to match: if some units were more likely to appear, equal weighting would over-represent them and the estimator would be biased.
Result 2 Proportionally allocated stratified sampling has lower variance than simple random of the same size. Write the population variance as a within-stratum part plus a between-stratum part. Simple random sampling pays for both, because the number of units it happens to draw from each stratum varies from sample to sample. Stratified sampling fixes those counts, so the between-stratum term never enters the sampling error at all.

The step that does the work is that fixing the allocation removes a source of randomness rather than averaging over it. The boundary, stated honestly: this is exactly why cluster sampling does not get the same benefit. It also fixes something, but what it fixes is which whole groups are in, and units inside a group resemble one another, so it adds a source of variation instead of removing one.
total variance = ∑h Wh σh2  +  ∑h Wh (μh − μ)2 h: the index over strata, running over the four groups in the table above. Wh: stratum h's share of the population, so the four shares add to 1. σh2: the variance inside stratum h. μh: stratum h's own mean. μ: the whole population's mean. The first sum: how spread out units are within their own group, averaged over groups. The second sum: how spread out the group means are from each other. Together: exactly the population variance, with nothing left over.

The 0.130 is the number to hold. The theory said stratification removes the between-stratum part, which is 87.4 per cent of the variance, leaving 12.6 per cent. The measurement says 13.0 per cent. The prediction and the measurement agree, which is what makes Result 2 a fact about this population rather than a slogan.

Cluster sampling comes out worse than simple random, at 1.209×. Units inside a cluster resemble one another, so the twelfth unit from a chosen cluster tells you much less than a fresh independent unit would. That is the boundary in Result 2, measured.

6. Stratified k-fold is this idea

When you split data into folds and keep each class's proportion the same in every fold, you are proportionally allocating a stratified sample, with the class label as the stratum. The reason it reduces the variance of your cross-validated score is exactly Result 2: it removes the between-class part of the variation from the fold-to-fold noise, instead of letting the shuffle decide how many rare-class rows land in each fold.

7. Worked: twelve units, by hand

A population of 12 units in three strata, small enough to enumerate.

A (6 units): 10, 12, 14, 16, 18, 20    B (4 units): 40, 44, 48, 52    C (2 units): 90, 110 Read as: twelve numbers in three named groups. Group A has six units, B has four, C has two. The group means differ a great deal and the spread inside each group is modest, which is the situation stratification is for.
  1. Stratum means. A: 90 ÷ 6 = 15. B: 184 ÷ 4 = 46. C: 200 ÷ 2 = 100.
  2. Population mean. (90 + 184 + 200) ÷ 12 = 474 ÷ 12 = 39.5.
  3. Weights. WA = 6/12 = 0.5, WB = 4/12 = 0.3333, WC = 2/12 = 0.1667.
  4. Within-stratum variances. A: deviations −5,−3,−1,1,3,5, squares summing to 70, so 70/6 = 11.67. B: deviations −6,−2,2,6, squares summing to 80, so 80/4 = 20. C: deviations −10,10, so 200/2 = 100.
  5. The within part. 0.5(11.67) + 0.3333(20) + 0.1667(100) = 5.83 + 6.67 + 16.67 = 29.17.
  6. The between part. 0.5(15 − 39.5)2 + 0.3333(46 − 39.5)2 + 0.1667(100 − 39.5)2 = 0.5(600.25) + 0.3333(42.25) + 0.1667(3660.25) = 300.13 + 14.08 + 610.04 = 924.25.
  7. Check they sum. 29.17 + 924.25 = 953.42, which is the population variance computed directly. The between share is 924.25 ÷ 953.42 = 96.9 per cent.
  8. Now the mechanism. A proportional stratified sample of 6 takes exactly 3, 2 and 1 units from A, B and C, in every single draw. A simple random sample of 6 can return 0, 1 or 2 units from C, and C's units are the ones near 100.

Sanity check. The two components must sum to the population variance exactly, and 29.17 + 924.25 = 953.42 does. If yours do not sum, a weight is wrong: they must be the stratum shares and must add to 1.

What changes if all three stratum means were equal? The between part becomes zero, stratification buys nothing, and simple random is just as good. That is the honest condition on Result 2: the benefit is proportional to how different the groups are, which is why sessions.csv could not carry this lesson and population.csv was generated for it.

What it means. With C at 16.7 per cent of the population, a simple random sample of 6 misses C entirely about 35 per cent of the time. Those samples estimate a mean near 25 instead of 39.5, and no analysis afterwards can tell you it happened.

8. The honest close

A summary describes the population your design actually sampled, which is often narrower than the one you meant. If your training data is whoever used the product last quarter, your model describes them, and no amount of careful arithmetic afterwards widens it.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

Does cluster sampling give the same information per unit as simple random?
Each extra unit from an already-chosen cluster tells you less than a fresh one would, and the measured table shows cluster sampling at 1.209 times the variance of simple random. The first two are true statements about unbiasedness answering a question about precision, and the measured table shows exactly that: cluster's bias is −0.4150, no worse than simple random's, while its spread is larger. The third names the wrong failure: cluster sampling is unbiased.
Stratification removes which part of the variance from the sampling error?
Fixing the allocation removes the randomness in how many units come from each stratum, which is exactly the between-stratum term. The first has it backwards: stratification changes nothing inside a group, and the within part is still paid in full. The third would mean zero variance, which no design achieves. The fourth is what stratification would be if the counts were not fixed, and the fixing is the entire mechanism.

Practice about 20 minutes, with paper

Practice 1foundation

A population of 1,000 units in three strata, and you may sample 100:

shares 70% / 20% / 10%     stratum means 100, 400, 900 Read as: 700 units whose mean is 100, 200 units whose mean is 400, and 100 units whose mean is 900. The shares add to 1 and every unit belongs to exactly one stratum, which is what makes this a partition.

Give the proportional allocation. Then compute the between-stratum variance component, and say what fraction of the total it would be if the within-stratum variance were 2,500 in every stratum.

Hint

Proportional means each stratum's share of the sample equals its share of the population. For the between component, it is the spread of the stratum means around the overall mean, weighted by stratum share, and the overall mean is itself a weighted average rather than a plain one.

Hint for the last part

If every stratum has the same within variance, the weighted average of those variances is that same number, because the weights add to 1. That makes the within part easier than it looks.

Show the worked solution
  1. Allocation. 70 per cent of 100 = 70, 20 per cent = 20, 10 per cent = 10. They sum to 100 and none is zero.
  2. Overall mean, weighted. 0.7(100) + 0.2(400) + 0.1(900) = 70 + 80 + 90 = 240. Note it is not 466.7: the plain average of the three means would be wrong because the strata are different sizes.
  3. Deviations of the stratum means. 100 − 240 = −140. 400 − 240 = 160. 900 − 240 = 660.
  4. Square them. 19,600, 25,600, 435,600.
  5. Weight and sum. 0.7(19,600) + 0.2(25,600) + 0.1(435,600) = 13,720 + 5,120 + 43,560 = 62,400.
  6. The within part. 0.7(2,500) + 0.2(2,500) + 0.1(2,500) = 2,500, since the weights add to 1.
  7. Total. 62,400 + 2,500 = 64,900.
  8. The share. 62,400 ÷ 64,900 = 0.9615, so about 96 per cent of the variance is between strata and stratification would remove essentially all of it.

Sanity check. Your allocation must sum to exactly 100 and no stratum may get zero, or that stratum contributes nothing and the design is not stratified over it. The share must land strictly between 0 and 1: if it exceeds 1 you have divided by the within part instead of the total. And note the smallest stratum contributes the largest single term, 43,560 of the 62,400, because its mean is furthest from the overall one - which is precisely why leaving it to chance is expensive.

Your teacher. If you want this in your hands, take any labelled dataset with an imbalanced class, score a model with plain k-fold and again with stratified k-fold, and compare the spread of the fold scores rather than their average. The averages will be close and the spreads will not, and that gap is Result 2 measured on your own data.
