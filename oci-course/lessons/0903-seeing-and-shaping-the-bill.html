Module 09 · Governance, tenancy and cost · Lesson 0903

The commitment here is a sum of money, so there is nothing to match to a running machine

working 10 min Capability area org-tenancy, matrix rows cost-management, commitment-discounts and cost-advisory.

The one-minute version

  • Three views of spend: Cost Analysis for interactive questions with forecasting, budgets for alerts, and cost and usage reports for hourly rows you can process yourself.
  • Those reports carry hourly rows, retained for a year, and can arrive up to a day late, and they can be exported in a schema common to financial operations tooling.
  • The commitment product is a promise to spend a sum over a year, drawn down by any eligible service in any region, traded for a discount.
  • Because the commitment is to money, nothing has to be matched to a running instance: services can change, and services that launch later are covered.
  • There is no first-party spend anomaly detector. The documented approach is to build alarms on spend or to use partner tooling.

The mental model: four surfaces, four questions

The mechanism: how fresh the numbers actually are

There is no first-party detector for unusual spend. The verified inventory records the absence plainly and names the alternatives: build alarms against spend using the monitoring service of module 10, or use partner tooling. Combined with the delay above, this means a runaway resource is noticed by a budget threshold or by an alarm you wrote, and not by the platform telling you something looks wrong.

The mechanism: a commitment to a sum, not to a shape

The reframing On a platform where the discount is bought against money rather than machines, cost optimisation stops being an inventory-matching exercise and becomes a forecasting one. The question is not "does this reservation still fit the fleet" but "will the year's consumption land on the number we committed to".

The trade-off: freedom in the term, a cliff at the end of it

Committing to a sum leaves every architectural decision open. A team can move from instances to containers to functions, adopt a service that did not exist when the commitment was signed, and shift work between regions, all without renegotiating anything or stranding an entitlement.

The cost is that under-consumption is not recoverable: unused credit is forfeited at the end of the term. So the forecasting in Cost Analysis is not a convenience, it is how the commitment is kept honest, and it is worth reading monthly rather than annually.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

What is the annual commitment actually committing to?
The commitment is to spend, which is why services can be switched freely inside the term. The first and third describe resource-matched models from other platforms. The fourth inverts the flexibility that is the model's main property.
A runaway resource starts costing heavily. Which source notices first?
Budgets and your own alarms are the mechanisms available. The first is hourly but can arrive up to a day late. The second does not exist on this platform. The third finds under-used resources rather than a sudden increase, which is a different question.
Which surface proposes an action rather than reporting a number?
Cloud Advisor produces recommendations with estimated savings and an apply action. The first forecasts but suggests nothing. The second notifies. The fourth hands over rows for somebody else to analyse.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

An organisation signs an annual commitment and, four months in, finds it is consuming at about seventy per cent of the rate needed to use it up. At the same time a test environment was left running over a weekend and nobody noticed until the monthly review. Finance asks for two things: a way to know sooner, and a plan for the commitment.

Answer both, naming the specific mechanisms, and state what will happen to the unused portion if nothing changes.

Hint

The two requests pull in opposite directions: one is about spending less on waste and the other is about spending more overall. Deal with the detection question using the mechanisms this platform actually has, and read the commitment question against what happens at the end of the term.

Show the worked solution
  1. Knowing sooner. There is no first-party anomaly detector, so this is a budget with alert thresholds scoped to the test compartment or its tag, plus an alarm on spend built in the monitoring service. Both are things you configure rather than enable.
  2. Why the report will not do it. The rows are hourly but can arrive up to a day late, so a weekend of waste is a Monday discovery at best from that source alone. It is the ledger, not the alarm.
  3. The commitment position. At seventy per cent of the needed rate for four months, consumption has to rise for the rest of the term or the balance goes unused. Cost Analysis forecasting is the tool for tracking whether it will.
  4. What happens otherwise. Unused credit is forfeited at the end of the term. It does not roll forward and it is not refunded.
  5. The tension worth naming to finance. Removing the waste lowers consumption and widens the shortfall against the commitment. The two requests are both correct and they are not the same project.

Sanity check. Your answer to the first request should contain two configured mechanisms and no detector, and your answer to the second should end in forfeiture rather than a rollover. If the two answers did not conflict at all, look again at what removing waste does to the consumption rate.

Your teacher. Ask an agent for the tenancy's consumption rate against the elapsed fraction of a commitment term. That single ratio is the whole financial health of the arrangement, and it is a number almost nobody looks at until the last quarter.
