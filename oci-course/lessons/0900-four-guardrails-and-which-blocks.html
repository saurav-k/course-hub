Module 09 · Governance, tenancy and cost · Lesson 0900

Four controls guard a tenancy, and the one everybody reaches for cannot stop anything

working 11 min Capability area org-tenancy, matrix rows org-guardrail-policy, quota-management and cost-management.

The one-minute version

  • Governance rules are created by a parent tenancy and locked in the child: allowed regions, quota policies and tags. The child sees them and cannot change them.
  • Security Zones attach a recipe to a compartment and block non-compliant creation at the moment it is attempted, raising the violation to Cloud Guard.
  • Compartment quotas cap resource counts declaratively, and a quota of zero is how a service is switched off for a subtree.
  • Budgets alert and never block. They are soft limits, and they are managed only in the root compartment.
  • So three of the four prevent and one informs, which is the distinction to hold when somebody asks for a spending limit.

The mental model: where each control is set, and what it does

The mechanism: two requests, two very different endings

The reframing Sort every control by when it acts: before the call, or after it. Governance rules, Security Zones and quotas act before, so they change what is possible. Budgets act after, so they change what somebody knows. A requirement phrased as "we must not be able to" needs a control from the first group.

The mechanism: a quota of zero is a switch

Two properties are easy to get wrong. Budgets are managed only in the root compartment, so a team cannot own its own budget object inside its own compartment the way it owns its resources. And a governance rule restricting regions limits which regions a child may subscribe to going forward; a subscription the child already holds outside that list persists. Neither of those is visible from the control's name.

The trade-off: prevention is precise and unforgiving

A control that blocks is the only kind that survives an incident review, because it does not depend on anybody reading a message. It is also the kind that stops a legitimate emergency change at three in the morning, and on this platform the strongest of them is held by a different tenancy whose administrators may not be awake.

The practical arrangement is layered rather than chosen: governance rules for the handful of things that must be true across every child tenancy, Security Zones for the configuration mistakes that recur, quotas for the resource classes with runaway potential, and budgets for everything else, where knowing quickly is enough.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

A team asks for a hard cap on their monthly spend. What can you actually give them?
Quotas cap resource counts before creation, which is the closest thing to a spending cap available. The first describes budgets doing something they explicitly do not do. The second names a control set by a parent tenancy, not by you. The fourth stretches recipes into a cost control they are not.
Which control cannot be removed by an administrator of the tenancy it applies to?
Governance rules are created by the parent and locked against the child. The other three are objects inside the tenancy, so whoever can manage them can also remove them, however strict they look.
What happens when a Security Zone recipe forbids the resource being created?
Security Zones prevent the creation and surface the violation. The first describes a detect-and-remediate loop, which is a different mechanism. The third describes a budget. The fourth invents an approval flow that does not exist here.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

An organisation runs a parent tenancy with three child tenancies for three business units. It requires that no workload runs outside two named regions, that publicly readable buckets can never be created in production, that the graphics-capable compute service is unavailable to the sandbox business unit entirely, and that finance is told within a day when a unit passes eighty per cent of its expected spend.

Assign a control to each requirement, say where it is configured, and identify which requirement is the only one that will not be enforced.

Hint

Read each requirement for whether it says "cannot" or "should be told". Then, for each "cannot", ask whether it must hold across all three tenancies or only inside one.

Show the worked solution
  1. The two regions. A governance rule restricting allowed regions, created in the parent and attached to all three children, where it is locked. Note that any extra region a child already subscribes to persists, so check the current subscriptions before relying on it.
  2. No public buckets in production. A Security Zone with a recipe forbidding public buckets, attached to the production compartment in each tenancy. Creation is refused at the call and the violation reaches Cloud Guard.
  3. No graphics compute for the sandbox unit. A quota of zero for that service. Set it as a compartment quota inside that tenancy, or as a governance-rule quota policy from the parent if it must be beyond that unit's own reach.
  4. Telling finance at eighty per cent. A budget with an alert threshold, defined in the root compartment. This is the requirement that is not enforced: the spend continues past the threshold and only a message is produced.
  5. What this reveals. Three of the four requirements were written as prohibitions and got preventive controls. The fourth was written as a notification, and it is worth confirming with finance that a notification is genuinely what they meant.

Sanity check. Exactly one of the four should be unenforced, and it should be the one whose wording asked to be told rather than to be stopped. If the region rule ended up as a Security Zone, ask who could remove it.

Your teacher. Ask an agent which compartments in a tenancy have a Security Zone attached and which do not. The gap between the list of production compartments and that list is the honest measure of how much of the estate is actually guarded rather than merely audited.
