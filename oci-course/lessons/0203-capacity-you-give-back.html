Cheap capacity is bought with a two-minute warning and a long exclusion list

working 10 min Capability area compute-iaas, matrix row spot-capacity.

The one-minute version

  • A preemptible instance is ordinary capacity at a discount that OCI may reclaim at any time, with no minimum runtime.
  • It is not silent about it. An instancepreemptionaction event is emitted through the Events service two minutes before termination begins.
  • The cost is packaging. Preemptible capacity is excluded from instance configurations, instance pools, capacity reservations and dedicated hosts, and from bare metal, burstable and confidential shapes.
  • One place accepts it anyway: Kubernetes node pools take preemptible worker nodes, and the platform cordons and drains the node before the reclaim.
  • The opposite end of the same axis is a capacity reservation, which holds hardware for you before you launch onto it.

The mental model: one axis, four ways to buy

Preemptible instances are reclaimed whenever the platform needs the capacity back, and the documentation offers no minimum runtime. What it does offer is notice.

The mechanism: what the two minutes are for

The trade-off: the exclusion list is the price

On a Kubernetes node pool the platform cordons and drains the node before the reclaim, so the pods are rescheduled by the cluster rather than by you. That is a materially better contract than the raw event, and it is available only through that door.

Read the exclusion list as a design constraint and not as a feature comparison. It says nothing about whether OCI can run interruptible work - it plainly can - and everything about which of the platform's own automation objects will accept it. The failure mode is a design drawn against the automation first and priced against the discount second, which then cannot have both.

At the far end of the axis, a capacity reservation holds hardware of a chosen shape before anything is launched onto it, so the capacity is there when the launch happens. It is the mirror image of the discount: you pay in advance for certainty rather than being paid in discount for uncertainty, and preemptible instances are excluded from it for exactly that reason.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

How does a workload learn that its preemptible instance is about to be reclaimed?
The warning arrives as a platform event, which means the handler is an Events rule rather than something running inside the machine. The first option describes the pattern on other clouds and is the assumption a port usually carries in. The second and third describe real OCI surfaces that carry no such notice.
Which design keeps both the preemptible discount and automatic replacement of lost workers?
Kubernetes node pools are the one place preemptible capacity is accepted with replacement handled for you, and the node is cordoned and drained before the reclaim. Each of the other three names a mechanism the documentation excludes preemptible instances from, and they are the three exclusions worth memorising together.
A batch unit of work takes eleven minutes and cannot be resumed. What does the preemption contract imply?
Two minutes is far shorter than the unit of work and there is no minimum runtime, so unfinished units are lost unless the work checkpoints. The first misreads the notice as a grace period long enough to finish, the second invents a guarantee the documentation denies, and the fourth combines two things the documentation says cannot be combined.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A nightly render farm processes 4,000 frames. Each frame takes about ninety seconds and frames are independent. The team wants preemptible capacity and asks whether the two-minute warning is enough to avoid losing work.

Reason it through: what fraction of a frame is at risk when a reclaim arrives, what the warning can actually save, and what changes if a frame took eleven minutes instead.

Hint

Compare the unit of work against the notice period rather than against the runtime of the whole job. Then ask what the handler can do inside the window that the worker could not have done on its own.

Show the worked solution
  1. Compare the unit to the window. A frame takes about 90 seconds and the notice is 120 seconds. The frame in flight fits inside the window with 30 seconds to spare, so the warning is long enough to finish it rather than only to abandon it.
  2. Work out the exposure without a handler. With no subscriber to the event, a reclaim lands mid-frame on average, so about half a frame - roughly 45 seconds of work - is lost each time. Across a job of 4,000 frames that is small, because the loss is bounded by one frame per reclaim.
  3. Say what the handler buys. The handler stops the worker taking a new frame and lets the current one complete, so the expected loss per reclaim falls from about half a frame to zero. It buys the difference between finishing and abandoning, not between running and not running.
  4. Change the unit of work. At eleven minutes a frame, the window covers less than a fifth of it, so no handler can finish the frame. The design has to checkpoint mid-frame, or split the frame into sub-units shorter than two minutes - which is the same answer expressed twice.

Sanity check. The loss per reclaim should be bounded by one unit of work and should never grow with the length of the job. If your answer scaled the loss with the 4,000 frames, the interruption was treated as fatal to the job rather than to one unit.

Your teacher. Ask an agent to write the Events rule that catches the preemption action and calls a function. Then ask what happens if the rule is never created. The second answer is the one worth having: the capacity is still discounted, still reclaimed, and now silent.
