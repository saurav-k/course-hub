OCI stacks two hierarchies, and only the inner one lives inside your tenancy

foundation 11 min Capability area org-tenancy, matrix rows org-hierarchy and org-guardrail-policy.

The one-minute version

  • The tenancy is the root container. Inside it, compartments nest up to six levels deep and every single resource belongs to exactly one of them.
  • A compartment is global. One compartment spans every region the tenancy is subscribed to, so the folder tree and the map are independent of each other.
  • Above the tenancy, Organization Management builds a tree of child tenancies sharing one funded subscription, with governance rules the child cannot change.
  • Permissions attach to groups through policy statements and inherit downwards. There is no role object to hand out and nothing to assume.
  • The limit that bites first is not the compartment count. It is 500 policy statements per compartment hierarchy, and Oracle documents it as a hard limit that cannot be raised.

The mental model: a folder tree, and a tree of accounts above it

Inside the tenancy, compartments are the isolation unit. Every resource lives in exactly one, they nest six levels deep, and they span all regions - which is the property that most often surprises someone arriving from a cloud where the equivalent container is regional. A compartment named prod-network holds its virtual networks in every subscribed region at once.

Above the tenancy, Organization Management is the account-tree construct. The parent holds the primary funded Universal Credits subscription, children can be created new or invited in and consume from that subscription without a separate order, and the parent reports cost across every linked tenancy through Cost Analysis, cost and usage reports and budgets.

The reframing A compartment is not an account and not a project. It is a folder inside one account, so it shares that account's identity store, its bill and its service limits. The thing that behaves like a separate account is a child tenancy, and reaching for it is a much larger decision than creating a folder.

The mechanism: how a grant reaches a resource

The statement shape is Allow group <name> to <verb> <resource-family> in <location>, with optional where conditions, and the verbs widen in a fixed order: inspect, then read, then use, then manage. The full grammar is on the Policy Syntax page, and module 08 is where this course works it properly. What matters here is the shape of the tree it runs on.

Human identities are held in identity domains: containers for users, federation, single sign-on applications and multi-factor settings. A tenancy has a Default domain and can add more, and Oracle publishes a ladder of domain types from Free through to External User domains billed per monthly active user. Identity resources behave globally but their master definitions sit in the home region.

The limits that shape the design

All three figures come from the Service Limits catalogue, which states 1,000 compartments per tenancy, 100 policy objects per tenancy, 50 statements per policy object, and that 500 policy statements per compartment hierarchy is a hard limit that cannot be increased.

Two Oracle pages disagree about one of these numbers. The service limits catalogue says 1,000 compartments per tenancy; the compartments guide says 6,000. Both are live vendor pages, so this course does not pick a winner: design to 1,000 and check the catalogue when the number matters, because planning against the larger figure and being wrong is the expensive direction of the error.

The trade-off: deleting is not the reverse of creating

Moving a compartment is the friendlier operation: the policy scopes attached to it are rewritten automatically, so a reorganisation does not silently strand a grant. That is worth knowing before designing a deep tree, because it means the depth of the tree is easier to change later than the number of statements written against it.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

A compartment named prod-network is created while the tenancy is subscribed to two regions. Where does it exist?
Compartments span all regions, so it exists everywhere the tenancy is subscribed. The first option describes identity mastering, which is a different property. The second imports a regional-container habit from another cloud, and the fourth confuses the container with its contents.
An administrator wants to be certain a team cannot exceed a spend or region policy, even if the team's own administrators disagree. What gives that guarantee?
Governance rules are created by a parent tenancy and locked in the child, which is the only control here the child's administrators cannot change. A compartment only groups resources. A tenancy-root policy can be rewritten by whoever administers that tenancy, and a tag namespace records ownership without preventing anything.
Which published limit is the one Oracle documents as impossible to raise?
The service limits catalogue calls 500 policy statements per compartment hierarchy a hard limit that cannot be increased. The other three are real catalogued limits and none of them carries that wording, which is exactly why the distinction is worth holding: three are budget, one is a wall.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1foundation

A platform team proposes one compartment per microservice, each with its own policy granting that service's group manage all-resources in its own compartment. The estate is planned to reach 200 microservices under a single prod compartment.

Test the proposal against the published limits, say which one it breaks first and by how much, and give a revised design that fits.

Hint

Count statements per hierarchy before counting compartments. Then ask what a single statement can cover if the compartment names follow a pattern, and whether anything other than the compartment name can select a resource.

Show the worked solution
  1. Count the compartments. 200 service compartments plus prod is 201, comfortably under the catalogued 1,000 per tenancy. The compartment count is not the problem.
  2. Count the statements in that hierarchy. One grant per service is 200 statements, and real services need more than one - a read on shared networking, a use on a vault key. At three statements each that is 600, against a hard ceiling of 500 per compartment hierarchy.
  3. Find the margin. 500 − 200 = 300 statements left for everything else in the whole prod tree, including platform and audit grants. The design breaks the hard limit at roughly 167 services if each needs three statements, so it fails before the estate is fully built.
  4. Revise it. Keep the compartments, but stop writing one statement per compartment. Grant against a group of compartments through a shared parent, and use conditions on tags to narrow, so that the statement count grows with the number of distinct access patterns rather than with the number of services.

Sanity check. The failing limit should be a statement limit and not a compartment limit, and the revised design should keep the same number of compartments. If your revision reduced the compartment count, it solved a constraint that was never binding.

Your teacher. If the two hierarchies still blur, ask an agent to describe one real tenancy twice: once as a compartment tree with the policies attached at each level, and once as a position in an organization with the governance rules attached from above. The two descriptions should share no objects, and seeing that is the point.

Primary source to go deeper

Read Managing Compartments first, then Organization Management. The first is the hierarchy you will use every day and the second is the one you will use once, and reading them in that order keeps the difference between a folder and an account sharp.
