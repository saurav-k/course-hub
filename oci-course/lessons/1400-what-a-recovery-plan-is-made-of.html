Module 14 · Resilience and migration · Lesson 1400

A recovery plan is generated from a group of members, not written by hand

working 11 min Capability area resilience-migration, matrix row dr-orchestration.

The one-minute version

  • Full Stack Disaster Recovery works from a protection group: the members of one workload in one region, paired with a group in another.
  • Members can be instances, volume groups, several database kinds, file systems, both load balancer kinds and stateful clusters, so a plan covers the whole stack rather than the data alone.
  • It generates the plans: a switchover for a planned move, a failover for an unplanned one, and a drill for a rehearsal.
  • Prechecks validate readiness before a plan runs, which is what turns a recovery document into something known to work.
  • It is billed by the protected processor hour, so the cost follows how much you protect rather than how often you test.

The mental model: a group, its members, and three plans

The mechanism: planned and unplanned are different plans

The mechanism: prechecks come before the plan runs

The reframing Recovery on this platform is a property of a group of resources, not of each resource. Every individual piece already has its own copy mechanism from module 04 and module 05. What this service adds is the ordering, the completeness and the rehearsal, and those three are what an incident actually needs.
The region model from lesson 0100 decides what a recovery plan can achieve. Many regions have a single availability domain, so spreading across fault domains protects against host-level failure while leaving one site. Genuine site redundancy means a second region, which is exactly what this service is built around. Check how many availability domains your region actually has before promising anything about surviving a site.

The trade-off: protecting more costs more, testing does not

Billing by the protected processor hour means the cost of this service follows the size of what you protect. That makes the question "which workloads are worth protecting across regions" a real one, and it usually has a shorter answer than a list of everything running.

It also means rehearsal is not the expensive part. A drill uses the standby that already exists, so the marginal cost of testing a plan is small compared with the cost of having a standby at all. That asymmetry is the argument for testing often, and the next lesson is about what a drill actually does.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

What does a protection group contain?
A protection group holds the resources of one workload and pairs with a group in another region. The first names a mechanism from module 04. The third confuses a group of resources with an on-call rota. The fourth describes the document this service exists to replace.
Why are switchover and failover separate plans rather than one?
A switchover can act on a reachable primary and a failover must assume it is not. The first invents an approval step. The second invents a scope difference. The third describes an automation difference the service does not make.
What do prechecks do?
Prechecks validate readiness on both sides before execution. The first describes a canary shift from module 12. The second invents a backup step. The fourth confuses readiness with authorisation, which is the policy language's job.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A workload runs in a single-availability-domain region: six instances behind a proxy load balancer, two block volumes each, a database, and a shared file system. The team has a recovery document written two years ago that describes restoring the database in another region. A review asks whether they can survive losing the region.

Say what the current document leaves out, describe what a protection group would contain, and name the step that turns the result into an answer rather than a claim.

Hint

List every component of the workload and mark the ones the old document mentions. Then remember that a load balancer and a file system are members too. The final step is the one that can be run today, before any incident.

Show the worked solution
  1. What the document covers. The database only. Restoring it in another region leaves an application with nowhere to run and nothing to serve traffic.
  2. What it leaves out. The six instances, their volume groups, the file system, and the load balancer that gives the workload an address at all. Every one of those is a member type this service supports.
  3. The protection group. Instances, volume groups for the block volumes, the database, the file system and the load balancer, paired with a group in a second region.
  4. Why a second region and not another availability domain. The region has one availability domain, so fault domain spreading protects against host failure and not against losing the site. Site redundancy means the second region.
  5. The step that settles it. Run the prechecks. Until they pass, the answer to the review is a claim about a document. After they pass, it is a statement about a plan, and the drill in the next lesson makes it a demonstrated one.

Sanity check. Your protection group should have at least five member kinds, and the old document should account for exactly one of them. If the answer stopped at a second availability domain, re-read how many that region has.

Your teacher. Ask an agent to list every resource a workload depends on, then compare that list against the members of its protection group. The difference is what will be missing during a failover, and it is almost always the load balancer or a file system rather than the database everyone remembers.
