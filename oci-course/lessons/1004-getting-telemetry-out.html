Module 10 · Observability and audit · Lesson 1004

One managed pipeline moves every telemetry family, and it turns itself off when it keeps failing

working 10 min Capability area integration-messaging, matrix rows telemetry-export-pipeline and dashboards.

The one-minute version

  • Connector Hub is the standard export path: managed pipelines carrying logs, metrics, queue and stream payloads between services and outward.
  • Its sources are logging, monitoring, the queue and the stream. Its targets are functions, log analytics, monitoring, notifications, object storage and streaming.
  • A connector can carry a filter task and a function task, so it selects and reshapes on the way rather than delivering everything.
  • A connector that keeps failing is deactivated automatically, which protects the platform and silently stops your export.
  • Every tenancy gets two connectors at no charge, and beyond that the service's own limits page governs how many you may run.

The mental model: four sources, six targets, one object

The mechanism: what happens when the target stops accepting

The reframing A connector is a standing instruction, not a transfer. Nothing is exported once and finished. Every export in the estate is a live object that can be deactivated, misconfigured or outgrown, and the export you rely on most is the one nobody has looked at since it was created.

The mechanism: the three exports most estates need

The Dashboards service is the console-native way to look at the result without exporting it: widgets over metrics, logs and custom markup, scoped per compartment and shareable inside the tenancy. It is a different product from the business intelligence one in lesson 0604 and from the log analytics dashboards, which is the naming collision that page was about.

One absence to state exactly: there is no correlated investigation graph on this platform. Security findings and threat intelligence lookups are joined by hand, or by whatever the destination system does once a connector has delivered the data there. Module 11 covers the findings themselves. The point here is that the joining is work you arrange rather than a view you open.

The trade-off: filtering saves money and discards evidence

A filter task in front of an expensive destination is the single most effective cost control in this module, because telemetry volume is dominated by records nobody will ever read. It is also irreversible in the direction that matters: what the filter dropped never reached the destination, and it is only recoverable if the source still holds it.

The safe arrangement is two connectors rather than one clever one. Send everything to cheap storage unfiltered, and send the filtered subset to the expensive system. The storage tiers of module 04 make the first of those nearly free, and the second is then a decision you can revise.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

A connector's target has been rejecting deliveries for days. What is its state?
Continuous failure leads to automatic deactivation. The first and second describe retry and buffering behaviours that would eventually be unbounded. The fourth confuses deactivation with deletion, and the connector is still there to re-enable.
How does a connector reshape data the target will not accept as it stands?
A function task sits in the connector and reshapes on the way. The second and third invent other places for the work. The fourth is the absence error and would make the mechanism far less useful than it is.
Why does audit history longer than a year require a connector?
Retention is fixed at a year and unchangeable, so anything longer is an export. The first contradicts the always-on property. The third understates the search window, which covers the whole retention. The fourth invents a deletion trigger.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A regulated organisation needs seven years of API history, a security team that wants firewall and flow logs in their own analysis system charged by the gigabyte, and an operations team whose fleet-wide alarms generate far more messages than a notification topic will carry. They currently have no connectors at all.

Design the set of connectors, say what filtering belongs where, and name the one thing about this design that itself needs monitoring.

Hint

Each of the three requirements maps to one connector, and one of them wants two rather than one. Then ask what happens to the whole design if a target goes down for a week.

Show the worked solution
  1. Seven years of history. A connector from the audit source into Object Storage, unfiltered, with a lifecycle policy moving the objects into a colder tier as they age. The fixed audit retention is why this exists at all.
  2. The security team, cheaply. Two connectors rather than one. Everything unfiltered into Object Storage for completeness, and a filtered subset into their system so that the per-gigabyte charge covers only what they will read.
  3. The alarm storm. Alarms already publish to a stream directly, and a connector from that stream into a function is what summarises before anything reaches a person. This is the small notification capacity from lesson 1001 being designed around rather than discovered.
  4. What needs monitoring. The connectors themselves. A continuously failing one is deactivated by the platform, and the symptom is an archive that stops growing, which nothing else will announce.
  5. Count what you built. Four connectors, two of them carrying filters, and one alarm watching the connectors. The last item is the one most designs omit.

Sanity check. The unfiltered copy of the security data should land somewhere cheap before the filtered copy is sent anywhere expensive. If the filter is the only path, ask where you would recover a record the filter dropped.

Your teacher. Ask an agent for the state and the last delivery time of every connector in a tenancy. A deactivated connector is the quietest failure in this module, and the only way it announces itself is a destination that stopped growing.
