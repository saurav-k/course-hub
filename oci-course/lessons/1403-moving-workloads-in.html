Module 14 · Resilience and migration · Lesson 1403

A migration is a wave of machines and a separate project for the data underneath them

working 10 min Capability area resilience-migration, matrix row migration-service.

The one-minute version

  • Oracle Cloud Migrations lifts virtual machines from common hypervisors into this platform, and it starts with discovery agents rather than with a move.
  • Discovery produces an inventory and an assessment, so the plan is built from what is actually running rather than from what a spreadsheet says.
  • Machines are moved in waves, which is how a large estate becomes a sequence of small, reversible decisions.
  • The data tier is a separate project: the database migration service of lesson 0504 handles it, and the fork question there is whether the source may stop.
  • The Well-Architected pillars are the structure for reviewing what you land, and every module of this course is one of the choices that review will ask about.

The mental model: discover, assess, then move

The mechanism: two projects, one cutover

The reframing A wave is not a batch of machines, it is a batch of decisions you are willing to make at once. Each wave meets shapes, compartments, network placement, identity and observability, and the reason the first wave is small is that those decisions are cheaper to change when five machines depend on them than when five hundred do.

The mechanism: what every wave decides

Landing a machine is not finishing. The Well-Architected pillars are the structure for reviewing what arrived: operational excellence, security, reliability, performance and cost. As lesson 1401 noted, this is documentation to review against rather than a tool that inspects a workload, so the review is something a person schedules. Doing it after the first wave rather than after the last is what keeps its findings affordable.

The trade-off: lifting a machine is fast and keeps its history

Moving a virtual machine as it stands is the quickest way to leave a data centre, and it preserves everything the machine accumulated, including the parts nobody documented. For an estate under a deadline that is often the correct decision.

It also arrives with all of that history, and lesson 1204 is what happens next: a long-lived machine that has to be patched in place, in stages, forever. The alternative of rebuilding from an image costs more at the moment of migration and produces a machine the platform can replace rather than maintain. Both are legitimate, and choosing per wave rather than per estate is usually what makes the deadline survivable.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

What comes before any machine is moved?
Discovery and assessment precede the move. The second and fourth name recovery mechanisms that apply after arrival. The third names a sensible preparation step that is not what this service starts with.
Which service moves the databases underneath the machines?
The data tier is handled by the database migration service from lesson 0504. The first merges two projects that have different constraints. The second misuses a recovery product. The fourth confuses file copying with a database migration.
Why are waves kept small at the start?
A wave commits decisions about placement, shape, identity and observability, and those are cheaper to revise when few machines depend on them. The other three invent limits on the tooling, which is not what constrains a wave.

Practice about 20 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

An organisation must leave a data centre. It has about two hundred virtual machines, three databases, an unknown number of scheduled jobs, and a deadline. They ask you to plan the first two waves and to say what has to be decided before wave one moves a single machine.

Write the plan, listing what wave one contains, what must be decided first, and where the databases sit in the schedule.

Hint

Nothing can be planned before the inventory exists. Then choose wave one for what it teaches rather than for what it delivers, and place the databases by their own constraint rather than by the machine schedule.

Show the worked solution
  1. Before anything. Run discovery and produce the inventory and assessment. Two hundred is the number they believe; the inventory is the number they have, and it will include machines nobody could name.
  2. Decide the landing shape. Compartments, network and subnets, the identity model for workloads, and which telemetry is enabled. Every one is from an earlier module, and every one is cheaper to change now than after fifty machines depend on it.
  3. Wave one. A small number of low-risk machines chosen for what they will teach: one of each common shape, one that talks to something on premises, and one nobody will miss overnight. Its purpose is to test the decisions above.
  4. Wave two. Sized from what wave one revealed, and only after the landing decisions have been revised. If nothing was revised, wave one was too easy.
  5. The databases. A separate project running in parallel, with the fork question from lesson 0504 asked of each one: may this source stop accepting writes. Their answer usually sets the cutover date, so ask it in week one rather than in the last month.
  6. The scheduled jobs. The unknown count is the risk. They appear in the inventory as machines and not as jobs, so somebody has to enumerate them separately, and lesson 0504's advice about listing every client that connects to a database applies here too.

Sanity check. Nothing should be scheduled before the inventory exists, and the databases should have their own timeline. If wave one was chosen to deliver value rather than to test decisions, ask what you will learn from it.

Your teacher. Ask an agent to compare the discovery inventory against whatever the organisation believed it was running. The difference between those two lists is the real scope of the migration, and it is the single most useful artefact this service produces.

Primary source to go deeper

Read the Oracle Cloud Migrations documentation. Its discovery and assessment sections are the part worth reading before a plan is written, because a plan written before the inventory exists is a plan about the wrong estate.
