Module 08 · Identity and access · Lesson 0800

Access attaches to a group, never to a role, and there is no role object to assume

working 11 min Capability area identity-workforce, matrix rows iam-principals, workforce-sso and conditional-access.

The one-minute version

  • Human identities live in identity domains: containers for users, groups, federation, application single sign-on and multi-factor settings. A tenancy has a default domain and may have more.
  • There is no role object. Access attaches to a group for people and to a dynamic group for workloads, through policy statements. Nothing is assumed and nothing is switched into.
  • A dynamic group is a rule, not a list. It matches workloads by their own attributes, such as the compartment they sit in or a tag they carry.
  • Federation is SAML first for console and API sign-in, with SCIM provisioning and group mappings, and identity domains also speak OIDC for application sign-on.
  • Sign-on policies are configured per domain, so a tenancy with several domains configures the rules more than once rather than in one place.

The mental model: principals, and where they live

The identity documentation describes users, groups and dynamic groups, and records that identity resources are global while their master definitions sit in the home region from lesson 0101. Dynamic group rules reference resource attributes such as a compartment identifier or a tag value, which is what makes them the backbone of the machine authentication in lesson 0802.

The mechanism: what an identity domain holds

Sign-on policies with Adaptive Security can allow, deny or demand a stronger authentication per sign-in, reacting to the group, the network the request came from, the client, and a computed risk score. The risk score comes from a default provider or from a third-party provider you register.

The mechanism: a federated sign-in, in order

The reframing Coming from a platform with roles, the instinct is to look for the object that bundles permissions. There is not one. The group is the bundle, and the statements that mention it are the permissions. A design that wanted three roles for one person becomes three groups and a person who is in all three, permanently, with no act of switching between them.

The trade-off: no role means no switch, in both directions

The absence of a role object removes a whole class of confusion: nobody has to remember which identity they are currently wearing, and every action in the audit trail belongs to the person or workload that took it rather than to a shared assumed identity.

What is lost is the deliberate step. On a platform with roles, an operator can hold a dangerous permission only while they have chosen to assume it. Here, if a person's group carries a permission, they carry it at all times, and the compensating controls are the sign-on policies above, the conditions in the next lesson, and access review, which on this platform is a separately licensed Oracle product rather than part of the base identity service.

The verified inventory also records that there is no permission boundary control: nothing caps the maximum permissions a principal may ever be granted, even by a delegated administrator. The deny statements of the next lesson subtract permissions, but anyone with authority to write policy can also remove a deny. Governance rules, which lesson 0900 covers, cap a child tenancy from outside rather than capping your own administrators.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

A policy statement grants a permission. What can it name as the recipient?
Statements name groups and dynamic groups. The first describes a primitive this platform does not have. The second is the design people expect and is not how the language works. The fourth confuses the scope of a grant with its recipient.
How does a new compute instance become a member of a dynamic group?
A dynamic group is a rule over attributes, so matching happens by having them. The second describes a static group. The third confuses authentication with membership. The fourth names one attribute a rule may use and states it as the only one.
A tenancy has three identity domains. Where are sign-on policies configured?
Sign-on policies are scoped to a domain, so each domain is configured on its own. The first and third both assume an inheritance that does not exist. The fourth confuses where identity definitions are mastered with where a policy applies.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A design arrives from another platform. It has a read-only role that every engineer holds, an operator role that on-call engineers assume during an incident, and a deployment role that a build system assumes to release. Engineers sign in through the company's own identity provider.

Re-model all three onto this platform, name the principal kind for each, and say plainly which property of the original design cannot be reproduced.

Hint

Sort the three by who or what holds them: two are held by people and one by a machine. Then ask, for each, whether anything in the original depended on the moment of assuming rather than on the permission itself.

Show the worked solution
  1. The read-only role. A group, with statements granting read across the relevant compartments. Engineers arrive in it through the federation group mapping, so the corporate directory decides membership.
  2. The operator role. A second group with the stronger statements. Membership comes from the same mapping, so a corporate on-call group can drive it.
  3. The deployment role. A dynamic group whose rule matches the build system's own resources, granted the deployment statements. Nothing holds a credential, which is the subject of lesson 0802.
  4. What cannot be reproduced. The moment of assumption. An on-call engineer in the operator group holds those permissions at all times, not only during an incident, because there is no role to step into and out of.
  5. What to do about it. Narrow the compensating controls instead: sign-on policies that demand a stronger factor, and the policy conditions of the next lesson, which can restrict a statement by operation, network source or region.

Sanity check. Three principals, two of them groups fed by the federation mapping and one a dynamic group matching a machine. If your answer contains anything a person assumes, look again for the object that would let them.

Your teacher. Ask an agent which corporate directory groups map onto which groups in a tenancy. That mapping is a permission model maintained by a team that usually does not know it is one, and it is rarely written down beside the policies it drives.
