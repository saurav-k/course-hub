Module 13 · AI and ML · Lesson 1303

Climb the ladder only as far as the problem forces you, because every rung costs a team

working 10 min Capability area ai-ml, matrix rows model-training-platform and speech-vision-document-ai.

The one-minute version

  • Four pretrained services answer perception problems with no model work at all: Speech, Vision, Language and Document Understanding.
  • They chain naturally. Speech produces text, Language analyses it, and the document service shares its character recognition with Vision.
  • Some of these accept a custom model trained on your own examples, which is a middle rung between calling an interface and building a model.
  • Data Science is the top rung: notebooks, jobs, pipelines, automated model selection, a model catalogue with provenance, and deployment endpoints on ordinary or accelerated shapes.
  • Its quick actions also deploy open-source models, so bringing a model of your own does not require building one from nothing.

The mental model: a ladder of how much is yours

The mechanism: four services that chain

Transcription work is submitted as jobs, and those jobs have documented shapes: a queue per tenancy, a ceiling on the tasks in one job, and a retention period after which results are gone. Read the service page for the current figures before designing a bulk pipeline around it.

The mechanism: what the top rung actually contains

The reframing Ask what would have to be true for the rung below to fail, and then test that. A pretrained interface that is wrong on your data is a fact you can establish in an afternoon. A custom model built without that test is a team's year spent on an assumption.
The top rung inherits every operational concern from earlier modules, and that is the part most often underestimated. A deployment endpoint runs on shapes from module 02, is reached across the network of module 07, authenticates with the principals of module 08 and is watched with the telemetry of module 10. None of that comes free with the model, and a team that has only ever worked in notebooks is meeting all four for the first time on the day they deploy.

The trade-off: a custom model is a thing you now own forever

A model trained on your data can do what no pretrained interface will, and that is sometimes exactly the requirement. It also becomes an asset with a lifecycle: the data drifts, the metrics move, the framework versions age, and someone has to notice and retrain.

The catalogue with provenance is what makes that survivable, and using it properly from the first model is far cheaper than reconstructing lineage later. A model whose training data, code and environment are all recorded can be rebuilt by somebody who was not there; one that is only a file cannot.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

A team needs sentiment from recorded support calls. What is the lowest rung that works?
Two pretrained services chain to answer this with no model work. The first and third climb the ladder before the bottom rung has been tested. The fourth would work and reaches for a general model where two specific interfaces already exist.
What does the model catalogue add beyond storing a model?
Provenance is what makes a model maintainable by someone who did not build it. The first and second promise automation and guarantees the service does not offer. The fourth invents a conversion path between two different rungs.
Which rung is most often skipped without being noticed?
The middle rung, training a custom model inside a perception service, is the one teams jump over. The bottom and top rungs are both highly visible. The fourth describes a discipline this page exists because teams do not have.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

Four requests arrive. Read the totals from scanned supplier invoices. Detect a specific manufacturing defect in photographs from a production line, where nothing off the shelf recognises it. Translate incoming support messages. And predict which customers will cancel, from twenty columns of their account history.

Place each on the ladder, and for the one that reaches the top rung, name the operational concerns it inherits from earlier modules.

Hint

Two of these are ordinary perception problems with a service each. One says explicitly that nothing off the shelf recognises it, which decides its rung. The last is not a perception problem at all.

Show the worked solution
  1. Invoice totals. Bottom rung, Document Understanding, which extracts fields from documents with prebuilt models. No model work.
  2. The manufacturing defect. Middle rung. Vision supports custom models trained on your own examples, so this is labelled photographs rather than a machine learning project. The requirement to have labelled examples is the real cost.
  3. Translating support messages. Bottom rung, Language. If the messages arrive as audio, chain Speech in front of it.
  4. Predicting cancellation. Top rung. This is tabular prediction rather than perception, so no pretrained interface applies and it belongs in the Data Science service, where automated model selection can do much of the first pass.
  5. What the top rung inherits. A deployment endpoint on a shape from module 02, reachable across the network of module 07, authenticating with a principal from module 08, and watched with the telemetry of module 10. Plus the catalogue entry recording how the model was produced.

Sanity check. Two requests should need no model at all, one should need labelled examples but no machine learning team, and only one should reach the top. If the invoice request climbed the ladder, ask what the prebuilt document model failed at.

Your teacher. Ask an agent to try the pretrained service on fifty real examples before anyone plans a custom model. Half of these projects end there, and the fifty examples are the evidence that settles the argument either way.
