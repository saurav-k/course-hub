Module 08 · Identity and access · Lesson 0803

Both sides write a statement, and the caller never stops being themselves

frontier 11 min Capability area identity-workload, matrix row cross-account-assumption.

The one-minute version

  • There is no assume-role step. No temporary credential is minted, and the caller's identity is the same on both sides of the boundary.
  • Access across tenancies needs statements in both tenancies: an Endorse at home and an Admit at the destination. One alone does nothing.
  • Each side must Define the other's identifier first, and the destination must also Define the foreign group so its Admit statement can resolve it.
  • The destination's location clause names its own tenancy or compartment, never the alias for the foreign one.
  • There is no general resource-sharing service. Compartments plus these statements are the mechanism, and the deny forms of lesson 0801 reach across the boundary too.

The mental model: three distances, three requirements

The mechanism: the statements, in full

cross-tenancy access · the shape both sides must write
# In the requesting tenancy A
Define tenancy B as ocid1.tenancy.oc1..bbbb
Endorse group A-Devs to read object-family in tenancy B

# In the owning tenancy B
Define tenancy A as ocid1.tenancy.oc1..aaaa
Define group A-Devs as ocid1.group.oc1..aaaa-group-ocid
Admit group A-Devs of tenancy A to read object-family in tenancy

Read the last line carefully. The location clause says in tenancy, meaning tenancy B's own, and never the alias for the foreign one. The cross-tenancy documentation also requires the second Define in tenancy B: without a definition for the foreign group, the Admit statement has nothing to resolve.

The reframing On a platform with roles, cross-account access is one side granting and the other side stepping through. Here it is two sides agreeing. That makes the grant harder to arrange and much harder to arrange by accident, and it means nobody can widen the path from one side alone.

The mechanism: what else lives at this boundary

One item is recorded as unresolved rather than settled, and it is repeated here in the same spirit. For machine identities from outside this platform, the documented paths are pod service accounts on enhanced Kubernetes clusters and applications registered in an identity domain. Whether a general exchange of an arbitrary external token exists beyond those is an open question in the research behind this course rather than a confirmed absence. Treat it as something to check on the vendor's pages for your own case rather than as a fact in either direction.

The trade-off: two signatures, and no way to be quiet about it

Requiring both sides removes the confused-deputy problem by construction. A tenancy cannot be reached because someone in another one wrote a generous statement, and revoking access needs only one of the two halves to be deleted, by whichever administrator moves first.

The cost is coordination. Every cross-tenancy grant is a change request in two places, usually owned by two teams, and it cannot be prepared in advance from one side. In an organisation with many tenancies, that is real friction, and it is exactly the friction that the organisation-level structures of the next module are designed to manage.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

A cross-tenancy call is refused although the Endorse statement exists. Why?
Both halves must exist, so a missing Admit refuses the call. The first and second describe an assume-role model that this platform does not have. The third invents an approval workflow that the policy language does not contain.
In the destination tenancy's Admit statement, what does the location clause name?
The clause names the destination's own tenancy or compartment. The first is the mistake the documentation calls out directly. The third confuses where the caller lives with where the resource lives. The fourth invents a syntax the language does not have.
Which identity appears in the audit record of a cross-tenancy action?
The caller keeps their identity throughout, which is the property this model buys. The first describes assumed credentials. The second invents a proxy identity. The fourth confuses the unit a grant is written against with the identity that acts.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1frontier

A partner organisation has its own tenancy and wants its analysts to read one bucket in yours. Their group is called PartnerAnalysts. Your bucket sits in a compartment named Shared. A colleague has written an Endorse statement in the partner's tenancy and a single Admit statement in yours, and the calls are still refused.

Write the complete set of statements for both tenancies, and name the two most likely mistakes in the colleague's version.

Hint

Count the Define statements the documentation requires on each side, then look hard at the location clause of the Admit statement. Both errors are things that are absent or wrong in one line rather than missing statements of a whole kind.

Show the worked solution
  1. The partner's tenancy. Define your tenancy as its identifier, then Endorse group PartnerAnalysts to read object-family in your tenancy alias.
  2. Your tenancy, first Define. Define the partner tenancy as its identifier, so your statements can refer to it.
  3. Your tenancy, second Define. Define group PartnerAnalysts as its own identifier from the partner tenancy. This is the line the documentation warns about and the one most often missed.
  4. Your tenancy, the Admit. Admit group PartnerAnalysts of the partner tenancy to read object-family in compartment Shared. The location is your compartment.
  5. The two likely mistakes. The missing Define for the foreign group, so the Admit had nothing to resolve, and a location clause naming the partner's alias instead of your own compartment.

Sanity check. Four statements in your tenancy and two in theirs, with three Define lines in total. If your version has only one Define on the destination side, that is exactly the failure described.

Your teacher. Ask an agent to list every Admit statement in a tenancy and the foreign tenancy each one names. That list is the complete set of outside parties who can reach inside, it is short, and it is almost never reviewed on any schedule.
