Evaluation and Quality

18 topics The chapter that separates a demo from a product.

The one-minute version

  • Without evaluation you cannot ship a change safely, because you have no way to know whether it helped. This is the deploy pipeline of AI systems.
  • The golden dataset is the asset. Models, prompts, and vendors all churn; a well-curated labelled set outlives every one of them.
  • Faithfulness and factuality are different metrics with different fixes, and conflating them is the most common quality mistake.
  • LLM-as-judge is useful and biased. Say both, name the biases, and describe how you calibrate it against humans.

1. The evaluation loop

Treat quality as a control system rather than a launch checklist. Offline evaluation gates changes before release; online evaluation measures reality; human review adjudicates the disagreements and refreshes the labelled data. Sculley et al. warned that feedback loops are where machine-learning systems accumulate their worst debt, and that warning applies directly here. Source: Sculley et al., 2015.

2. The two evaluation modes

Offline evals

Run a fixed dataset through the system and score it, in continuous integration, before release. Fast, cheap, repeatable, and blind to anything your dataset does not contain. This is the gate that lets you change a prompt on a Friday.

Online evals

Measure production traffic: implicit signals such as thumbs, copy actions, retries, abandonment, and escalation to support, plus sampled automated scoring. Online evaluation sees the distribution your offline set is missing, and it is the only place user trust actually shows up.

Golden dataset

A curated set of inputs with known-good outputs or rubrics, covering the common path, known failure modes, adversarial cases, and edge cases from real incidents. Every production bug should end its life as a row in this set. When asked what you would build first on a new AI product, this is a strong answer.

Human review

The ground truth that everything else is calibrated against. Design it deliberately: a written rubric, more than one annotator on a sample, and an inter-annotator agreement measurement. If your humans do not agree with each other, no automated judge can be validated against them.

LLM-as-judge

Use a strong model to score outputs against a rubric. Zheng et al. found judge agreement with human preference above eighty percent on their benchmark, comparable to agreement between humans, while documenting position bias, verbosity bias, and self-enhancement bias. The honest interview answer is: useful for scale, calibrated against a human-labelled subset, with position randomised and known biases controlled. Source: "Judging LLM-as-a-Judge" (Zheng et al., 2023).

Interview trap. "We use GPT to grade the outputs." Follow-up: how do you know the judge is right? If you cannot describe calibrating it against human labels and controlling for verbosity and position bias, the metric is decoration.

A/B testing

The only reliable way to attribute a quality change to a system change in production. The AI-specific complications are that outcomes are often subjective, effect sizes are small relative to variance, and users adapt their behaviour to the system over the life of the experiment.

Regression testing

Lock previously fixed failures into the evaluation suite so they cannot silently return. This matters more than in conventional software, because a prompt or model change can regress behaviour that no code change touched.

3. What to actually measure

Answer relevance

Does the response address the question that was asked? Weak relevance often indicates a query-understanding or rewriting failure rather than a generation failure.

Factual accuracy

Is the claim true of the world? TruthfulQA is the canonical demonstration that larger models can be less truthful when they have learned popular misconceptions, which is why scale is not a quality strategy. Source: TruthfulQA (Lin et al., 2021).

Faithfulness

Is every claim supported by the retrieved context? RAGAS operationalises this by decomposing an answer into claims and checking each against the supplied context, which makes it automatable at scale. Faithfulness is the metric that catches the most damaging RAG failure: a confident answer the sources do not support. Source: RAGAS (Es et al., 2023).

Groundedness

The stricter operational cousin of faithfulness: can each statement be traced to a specific cited span, such that a user could verify it? Groundedness is what makes citations honest rather than decorative.

Toxicity checks

Classifier-based screening of inputs and outputs for abusive or harmful content, run as an independent component so it can be updated, measured, and rolled back on its own.

Safety checks

Broader policy conformance: self-harm, legal and medical advice boundaries, regulated content, and brand constraints. Holistic evaluation frameworks argue for reporting several metrics side by side rather than collapsing them into one score, because accuracy and safety trade against each other. Source: HELM (Liang et al., 2022).

Your assistant produces an answer that is true in general but unsupported by any retrieved document. Which metric catches this?
A true-but-ungrounded claim passes factual accuracy and fails faithfulness. This is exactly why the two are measured separately: in a RAG system, an answer the sources do not support is a defect even when it happens to be correct, because you cannot tell the difference at serving time.

4. Keeping quality over time

Drift detection

Quality decays without any code change: user behaviour shifts, the corpus changes, and the vendor updates the model underneath you. Monitor input distribution, retrieval score distributions, refusal and abstention rates, and output length, and alert on movement rather than on absolute thresholds.

Feedback loops

User signals feed evaluation, evaluation drives changes, changes alter user behaviour. Sculley et al.'s warning about hidden feedback loops is the reason to keep an untouched holdout and to be careful about training on your own outputs.

Confidence scoring

Produce a per-response score from retrieval scores, grounding checks, judge scores, and model signals. Token probabilities alone are a poor proxy for correctness, so treat confidence as an ensemble you calibrate rather than a number the model hands you.

Escalation criteria

Written, testable rules for when the system stops answering and hands off: confidence below threshold, retrieval empty, a regulated topic, or an explicit user request. Escalation is a product decision expressed as a threshold, and interviewers reward candidates who set it explicitly rather than gesturing at it.

Quality monitoring

Dashboards and alerts on the metrics above, sliced by tenant, locale, and query type. Aggregate quality can look flat while one segment collapses, which is exactly how these failures reach customers before they reach your graphs.

Aggregate satisfaction is flat, yet enterprise customers in one region are escalating heavily. What is the most likely gap?
Averages hide segment collapse. Slicing by tenant, locale, language, and query type is what surfaces a regression affecting five percent of traffic and a hundred percent of your most valuable customers.

5. Interview drill

  • "How do you know the system is any good?" The trap is naming a benchmark. Golden dataset, offline gate, online signals, human calibration, sliced monitoring.
  • "How do you ship a prompt change?" Offline evaluation, then a small online experiment, then rollout, with an explicit rollback trigger.
  • "The vendor silently updated the model. What happens?" Drift alerts fire, the regression suite catches the behaviour change, and pinned model versions limit exposure in the first place.
  • "How do you measure hallucination?" Decompose the answer into claims, check each against retrieved context, report faithfulness, and validate the automated checker against human labels.
  • "When does a human get involved?" Give thresholds and a queue with an SLA, not the phrase "human in the loop".
Your teacher. If you take one habit from this chapter, make it this: when asked about quality, always answer with a measurement and a gate, never with a technique. "We would use a better model" is a Senior answer. "We would measure faithfulness on a golden set and block the release on regression" is the Staff answer. Ask me to drill you on it.
