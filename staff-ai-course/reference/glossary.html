Glossary

40 termsprint-friendly Every term this course introduces, linked to the decision that develops it. The definitions are narrower than the field's on purpose: they are what the term means here. Print it and cover the right-hand column.

The spine

TermWhat it means here
The callThe first of the five fields: the decision in one sentence, phrased so a reasonable person could choose either way.
The billThe third field: money, latency and team attention, including the recurring part and whose budget it lands in.
The reversalThe fifth field: what undoing the decision costs, and the point after which you cannot.
One-way doorA decision that is expensive or impossible to reverse. In AI systems these are almost entirely about what the system is permitted to do.
Two-way doorA decision reversible in a deploy. Decide it at about 70 percent of the information and stop arguing about it.

Reading evidence

TermWhat it means here
HarnessEverything around the model that produced a published number: scaffolding, tools, retries, and what each arm was given.
Selection effectThe process that decided which results became public, which is a property of the instrument rather than of the model.
Benchmark exploitationSatisfying a scorer without the capability it claims to measure. Tested at the extreme on eight agent benchmarks.
TransferWhether a ranking measured elsewhere holds on your corpus. Measured not to, across seven embedding models.
Result expiryThe point at which a published result stops being usable the way you are using it, usually because its authors revisited it.
The claim auditThe nine questions, asked in order, with the cheap invalidating ones before the expensive confirming one.

Deciding to build

TermWhat it means here
The ladderRules, then a learned model, then a foundation model, with maintenance cost rather than difficulty as the trigger on every rung.
Quality barThe error rate agreed in writing before a prototype exists. After launch it becomes whatever users were shown.
VerifierWhatever decides an output is wrong without a person reading it. Its presence or absence predicts whether the last stretch is cheap.
Stop conditionA specific observation, agreed in advance, that would end the project. Invented afterwards it is a post mortem.
Restraint caseA published account of a team choosing not to build. Rare enough that the ones that exist are worth more than their number.

Shape and knowledge

TermWhat it means here
Step budgetHow many model-mediated hops a task may take before end-to-end success drops below the quality bar.
Compounding errorPer-step reliability raised to the number of steps. Five points of per-step reliability buy about five more hops.
Approval as a stateWaiting for a person modelled as a first-class task state rather than an error, so retries and timeouts do not fire on it.
Context problemA failure caused by missing, outdated or proprietary knowledge. Retrieval, a longer window or a tool call is the instrument.
Behaviour problemA failure of format, tone, style or reasoning. Fine-tuning is the instrument, and retrieval can make it worse.
Hybrid retrievalRunning lexical and dense arms together. Measured ahead of either component on two different corpora.
Domain eval setA few hundred query-to-correct-chunk pairs from your own corpus. The only instrument that settles a retrieval argument.
Sensemaking questionA question about a property of the whole corpus rather than a passage in it. No top-k contains the answer.
Patch gridThe token cost of an image: one token per patch, so the count is the grid squared and resolution multiplies every call.
Permission-aware retrievalFiltering inside the search with the asking user's grants, rather than filtering the results after the model has seen them.

Proof

TermWhat it means here
Red evalA failing gating case. The design decision is which category nobody may override and who signs a waiver on the rest.
Criteria driftThe peer-reviewed finding that some evaluation criteria only become definable after you have seen the outputs.
Clustered standard errorThe correction for questions drawn in groups. Reported to widen a naive interval up to threefold.
Paired comparisonComparing per-question differences rather than two averages. Cuts estimator variance by about a third for free.
Power analysisAsking, before building the suite, what effect size it will be able to detect at all.
Reliability against validityA judge that scores identically every time is reliable. Whether it agrees with the humans it stands in for is a separate, expensive property.
Position biasA judge preferring whichever answer it saw first. The one judge bias an engineer removes at no cost.

Money, authority and supply

TermWhat it means here
The denominatorWhat your cost number is per. Cost per resolved user problem, because cost per call rewards a system that is asked again.
Silent cache missA prompt below the model's minimum cacheable length. Nothing errors, nothing alerts, and the bill does not fall.
Agent multiplierHow many times a chat turn an agent task costs. Driven almost entirely by how much context each step carries.
Spend ceilingThe component that refuses the next call, with no human in the path, and the degraded state it enters before halting.
Lethal trifectaPrivate data, untrusted content and outward communication in one system. Any two are safe; the removal is architectural.
Tool poisoningMalicious instructions in tool metadata. Alignment does not fire, because the agent is using a legitimate tool.
Data voidA query for which no good source exists. The system's behaviour there is undefined unless you defined it.
Abstention pathWhat the system does when it should not answer. Every published fix to a scaled answering failure is one of these.
Retirement calendarEvery model identifier in production with its retirement date beside it, and a named owner reviewing it on a cadence.
Model driftBehaviour changing under a version string that did not. Detectable only against scores you kept from before.
Protocol commitmentBuilding on an interoperability protocol. What moved is its governance, not its feature set, and that is what you are deciding about.

The organisation

TermWhat it means here
Decision recordA document containing a trade-off and a rejected alternative. Without both, its own author's test says write the program instead.
ExhaustionThe published name for what settles an unescalated technical dispute: whoever has more stamina carries the decision.
Glue workNoticing blockers, spotting what a document handwaves, onboarding, improving processes. Almost everything this course calls necessary.
