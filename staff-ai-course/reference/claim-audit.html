The Claim Audit

9 questionsprint-friendly Chapter 1 as a one-page audit for reading somebody else's number. Keep it beside you the next time one arrives in a slide.

The one-minute version

  • Ask them in order. The first five can invalidate the number outright and cost almost nothing; the last four only tell you whether a valid number applies to you.
  • An audit that starts at transfer has already conceded that the number is about what it says it is about.
  • Every question below has a published case behind it, so none of them is a general suspicion of numbers.
  • The last question is the one nobody asks: have the authors revisited it?

The nine questions

#AskWhat it catchesWhere it caught something
1What was actually varied: the model, or the harness around it?A pipeline result read as a component result, which leads to the opposite procurement decision.A vendor found its largest performance gap was between harness configurations rather than between models.
2What was each arm given?An unequal comparison presented as a like-for-like one.The open-weight arms got "nothing but a prompt" while the publisher's own pipeline ran inside a purpose-built harness.
3How many items, and how were they drawn?A difference smaller than the standard error, and questions drawn in groups that are not independent.A 164-question evaluation carries a standard error of about 3.2 percent, and clustering has been reported to widen intervals up to threefold.
4How many runs, and which of them were reported?Non-determinism reported as a result, and the best run presented as the typical one."One task, one dataset, one run", said by the publisher of the headline itself.
5Who chose what became public?Selective disclosure, and a measurement instrument with its own sampling process.One provider tested 27 private variants before a release, with selective disclosure of scores.
6Could the scorer be satisfied without the capability?A benchmark that measures whether the pipeline returned a well-formed answer.Eight agent benchmarks driven to near-perfect scores without solving a task; one validator checked only whether the last message came from the assistant.
7Does the ranking hold on my corpus?A general-benchmark ordering assumed to transfer to your data.Across seven models, performance on a general embedding benchmark was not correlated with performance on a financial-domain one.
8What do the authors say it does not show?A conclusion the authors themselves declined to draw.A widely quoted productivity study carries an explicit list of what its result is not evidence for.
9Have the authors revisited it?A citation that is still live, still correct about what it measured, and no longer usable the way you are using it.The same team later changed its experiment design and said its newer data is "only very weak evidence for the size of this increase".
The reframing None of these questions asks whether the number is true. They ask what the number is about, and that is the difference between scepticism and an audit. A number can be entirely correct and still be evidence about a harness you do not have, on a corpus you do not own, from a run somebody chose to publish.

Using it on your own numbers

The audit is harder and more valuable applied inward, because your own suite is the one nobody reads the scorer of.

  • Question 3 is the one that bites. Golden datasets are almost always built several questions per source document, so most internal suites violate the independence assumption their intervals rest on. See chapter 5.
  • Question 6 has an internal form. A suite nothing has failed in two months is not a suite you are passing. Ask when it last failed something and what changed in it since.
  • Question 8 has no internal form, and that is the finding. Nobody writes down what their own number does not show, which is why the sentence wrapped around an internal number is usually the thing that is wrong rather than the number.
Your teacher. Run the nine on the last AI number you repeated out loud to somebody more senior than you. Most people find the number survives and the sentence they wrapped around it does not, and the useful output is the sentence.
