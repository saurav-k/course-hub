Module 07 · Authority · Chapter 7

The classical injection family has real mitigations. This one does not.

working 15 min Six decisions about what the system may do, and what bounds it. · Willison, The lethal trifecta

The one-minute version

  • Eight published defences against indirect prompt injection were tested against adaptive attacks and all eight fell, above 50 percent attack success.
  • A real guarantee exists and it is architectural: separating control flow from untrusted data solved 77 percent of a benchmark against 84 percent undefended.
  • Alignment does not fire on tool poisoning, because nothing looks unsafe. Across 45 real servers and 20 agents, the best refusal rate was under 3 percent.
  • A tribunal has ruled that a chatbot's answer is your representation, and a correct page elsewhere on your site does not cure it.

1. Authority, and the leg you cut

What may this system do without a human, and what contains it when it is wrong?

The call. The actions the system may take unaided, written as an allow-list rather than inferred from the tools you happened to attach.

What you must know first. What happens when a model's uncertainty becomes an irreversible commitment. Zillow wound down its home-buying programme after a $304 million write-down, the chief executive explaining that "the unpredictability in forecasting home prices far exceeds what we anticipated", with a further $240 to $265 million expected. Source: Zillow Group Form 8-K, SEC EDGAR (2 November 2021). Not an LLM incident, and the clearest published case of the risk class agents are moving into.

The bill. Every action moved behind confirmation costs throughput. The vocabulary is published: OWASP's 2025 list names excessive agency, and its 2026 agentic list adds tool misuse, identity abuse and rogue agents. Source: OWASP (12 March 2025).

How you find out you were wrong. The signal is an action taken at scale that nobody authorised individually, and the lag is however long somebody downstream takes to see a pattern. Latency is fine and errors are zero.

The reversal. Undoing the action is the whole question, and it is a property of the action rather than the system: a message sent, a payment made, a record deleted and a deploy are four different reversals. Sort the tool list by that.

Does this system hold the lethal trifecta, and which leg do we cut?

The call. Which of the three legs your design gives up: the private data, the untrusted content, or the ability to send anything outward.

What you must know first. That filtering is not an option and architecture is. Eight defences against indirect prompt injection were all bypassed by adaptive attacks, "consistently achieving an attack success rate of over 50%". A capability layer separating control flow from untrusted data solved 77% of AgentDojo with provable security against 84% undefended. Sources: Zhan et al. and Debenedetti et al.

The bill. Whichever leg you cut costs product. The cheapest is usually egress, and it is a rendering and content-security-policy problem, which puts it in the hands of an engineer who has never touched a model.

How you find out you were wrong. From an external researcher, or not at all. CVE-2025-32711 in Microsoft 365 Copilot scores CVSS 3.1 base 9.3, vector AV:N/AC:L/PR:N/UI:N/S:C/C:H/I:L/A:N: network, low complexity, no privileges, no user interaction, scope changed. Source: NIST NVD, 11 June 2025.

Read the benchmark, not the reassurance. One 2026 source reports near-zero attack success on AgentDojo against the newest frontier models with no defence. The research could not confirm it against a primary source, and the point survives either way: a benchmark that stops discriminating is not evidence of safety. Willison is blunter - a filter catching 99 percent of attacks is effectively worthless, and "The LLM vendors are not going to save us!"

The reversal. None worth the name. Adding a leg back later is a design change through every layer that touched it, and every product decision since assumed it was gone. The most one-way door in the course.

2. What you trust, and what you recorded

Is a tool description trusted input?

The call. Whether tool metadata from a server you do not operate is untrusted content, given the suspicion you would give an npm package.

What you must know first. That alignment does not save you. MCPTox tested 45 real servers holding 353 tools, generating 1,312 malicious cases against 20 agents. One model reached 72.8% attack success, more capable models were often more susceptible, and the best refusal rate was under 3 percent: the agent is using a legitimate tool for an attacker's purpose. Source: MCPTox.

The bill. The protocol's own security document is normative, and following it is ordinary work: no token passthrough, per-client consent stored server-side, exact-match redirect URIs, link-local ranges blocked, state handles never treated as authentication, minimal scopes. Source: MCP security best practices, a draft. Mechanism: the harness course.

How you find out you were wrong. The signal is a tool call whose arguments make sense to the model and none to you, in a trace nobody read. No lag and no alert, because the request succeeded.

The reversal. Removing a server is instant. Undoing what it did with your credentials is not, and the confused-deputy shape means your audit trail records your own service doing it.

What is recorded, before the incident rather than after?

The call. What your traces carry, decided before you need them, and where they are allowed to live given what they contain.

What you must know first. That the standard is unfinished and says so. The OpenTelemetry generative-AI conventions have moved to their own repository, their spans cover inference, embeddings, retrievals, memory and tool execution, every one is marked Development, and the README's schema-URL section reads TODO. Source: OpenTelemetry. With no schema URL to pin against, instrument through a library that owns the mapping.

The bill. Three unsolved problems. Cardinality: an agent trace can be a hundred spans deep, so you sample, and the trace you wanted is the rare one you dropped. Privacy: a useful trace holds the prompt and the completion, making the trace store a copy of your most sensitive data under a different retention policy. And quality is not scrapable.

How you find out you were wrong. During the first incident, when somebody asks what the model was actually sent and nobody knows. Everyone sees it at once, which is the most expensive moment to learn it. The substrate is one you already run.

The reversal. Instrumentation added later covers only traffic from that day on, so the incident you are investigating stays uninvestigable. Attribute names will rename while the convention is in Development: a rename, not a redesign.

3. Whose words they are

Is a generated claim about your own policy your representation?

The call. Whether generated statements about your own policies are produced from, or checked against, the source of truth that governs the policy.

What you must know first. That a tribunal has answered it. In Moffatt v. Air Canada the airline argued it was not liable for what its chatbot said. Paragraph 27: "This is a remarkable submission... It makes no difference whether the information comes from a static page or a chatbot." Paragraph 28 disposes of the disclaimer defence: the airline "does not explain why customers should have to double-check information found in one part of its website on another part". The award was $812.02. Source: Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149.

The bill. Retrieval over a versioned policy store, plus a refusal path when retrieval misses. Google's own post-mortem names data voids - "Limited high-quality content exists for unusual queries" - and every fix they published is a decision about when not to answer. Source: Elizabeth Reid, Google (30 May 2024).

How you find out you were wrong. From a screenshot. Cursor's support agent invented a one-device policy that did not exist and was not labelled as AI; users posted it and some cancelled. Source: The Register (18 April 2025), secondary. Air Canada's lag was longer: wrong in 2022, acknowledged in 2023, ruled in 2024.

The reversal. You cannot un-say a policy a user relied on, and the tribunal record shows the remedy is paid rather than argued. Cheaply reversible: the labelling and the abstention path, both usually added after the incident.

What does the incident review look like when the cause is a model, not a commit?

The call. Which layer owns the fix when there is no commit to revert: the model, the harness, the renderer, or the product's decision about what to answer.

What you must know first. That the published fixes cluster in one place. Every markdown-image exfiltration fix Willison catalogues - Mistral LeChat, Microsoft 365 Copilot, Salesforce AgentForce, Superhuman AI, the Claude iOS app - is a rendering-layer fix. Neither that list nor Google's contains a better model. For a shared vocabulary with a security team, bring NIST's adversarial machine learning taxonomy. Source: NIST AI 100-2e2025.

The bill. The review needs the trace, the retrieved evidence, the prompt version and the model identifier together, or it produces a story rather than a cause. The same capture the build track asks for.

How you find out you were wrong. The signal is a review that ends in "we improved the prompt" with no regression case attached. Ask what now fails without the fix: that question is the whole test.

The reversal. A prompt change is reversible and usually untested. A rendering-layer fix is durable and boring. Prefer the boring one: it holds against phrasings nobody has written yet.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

A vendor offers a guardrail that blocks 99 percent of prompt injection attempts. How should a staff engineer read that number?
An adversary is not sampling randomly, so a filter with a pass rate is a rate limiter rather than a boundary, and eight tested defences all fell above 50 percent attack success. The first option applies a reliability intuition to an adversarial setting. The second is true of controls that are boundaries and does not turn a non-boundary into one. The fourth is factually wrong: AgentDojo and MCPTox both exist and both were used to produce the numbers on this page.
Your agent reads customer emails, holds a database connection, and can send messages. Which change makes it safe by construction?
All three legs present is the exploitable condition and any two are safe, so removal is the only structural move. The second is a filter, and filters were the eight defences that fell. The third is the intuition tool poisoning kills, where more capable models were often more susceptible. The fourth is genuinely worth doing and it is detection rather than containment: it tells you what left, after it left.
Your support assistant states a policy your company does not have. The correct policy is published on your own site. What does the tribunal record say?
The tribunal asked why one part of a website should be inherently more trustworthy than another, and why a customer should have to double-check. The first option is the defence that was tried and rejected. The third adds a mitigation the ruling does not discuss and would not change who made the representation. The fourth reaches the right conclusion by exactly the argument the tribunal called a remarkable submission.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1frontier

Take an agent you have built or reviewed. List its tools, and for each one mark which of the three legs it supplies: private data, untrusted content, or outward communication.

Produce the audit, then name the cheapest leg to remove and state what capability that costs the product.

Hint

Untrusted content arrives through anything the agent reads that a stranger can write: a web page, an email, a ticket, a pull request, a file a user uploaded. Outward communication includes anything that causes a network request, which includes rendering an image.

Show the worked solution
  1. Classify every tool, not every risk. A search tool and a document reader are almost always untrusted content. A database or a mailbox is private data. Sending mail, posting to a webhook and rendering markdown are all outward communication.
  2. Check the renderer, which is not in the tool list. If your client renders images from arbitrary URLs, you have an egress leg nobody attached deliberately, and it is the one the published incidents used.
  3. Price each removal. Removing private data usually kills the feature. Removing untrusted content means the agent stops reading the world. Removing egress usually costs only convenience, which is why it is normally the cheapest.
  4. State the capability lost. Write it in the product's own words - "the assistant can no longer email a summary" - because that is the sentence somebody will try to reverse in three months.

Sanity check. If your audit finds no untrusted content, look again at what the agent reads: retrieved documents are untrusted whenever any user can put text into the corpus, and that includes your own ticketing system. An audit that finds only two legs on the first pass is usually an audit that missed the renderer.

Decision drill

Questions a reviewer puts to an authority boundary, and the trap in each one.

  • "What can it do on its own?" The trap is listing tools. List actions by how they are undone.
  • "Do we have the trifecta?" The trap is answering on the tool list. Include the renderer, which supplies egress nobody attached.
  • "Is that MCP server safe?" The trap is trusting the description. The specification says treat it as untrusted.
  • "What will the trace tell us?" The trap is naming a platform. Say what the span carries, and where that data may live.
  • "It only said it once." The trap is calling an output an accident. A tribunal already called it a representation.
Your teacher. Do the trifecta audit on a system you already shipped, not one you are designing. Almost everybody finds the third leg somewhere they did not put it: an image renderer, a link preview, a webhook inside a library.

Primary source to go deeper

Read "The lethal trifecta for AI agents" first, then "Defeating Prompt Injections by Design" for what a real guarantee costs. Keep the MCP security specification open when you review a tool integration: it is the only normative document here.
