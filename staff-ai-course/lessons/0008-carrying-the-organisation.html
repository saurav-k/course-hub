Module 09 · Organisation · Chapter 9

Being right is not where your contribution stops.

frontier 14 min Six decisions about the people who carry the other eight chapters. · Bezos, 2016 letter to shareholders

The one-minute version

  • Most unescalated technical disputes are not settled on merit. The published name for the mechanism is exhaustion: whoever has more stamina carries the decision.
  • A design document without a trade-off is not a design document. Its author's own test: if it only says how you will implement something, writing the program would have been better.
  • Almost everything this course calls necessary - looking at data, keeping the judge aligned, maintaining the golden set - is glue work, the least legible work there is.
  • Sixteen developers forecast a 24 percent speedup, measured 19 percent slower, and afterwards believed they were 20 percent faster. Your job is largely to be the person who checks.

1. The document, and how it moves

What goes in the written decision record, and where does it live?

The call. Whether this decision gets a document at all, and whether it seeks consensus inside the affected group or is broadcast.

What you must know first. The test for whether to write one. Malte Ubl: "if a doc basically says 'This is how we are going to implement it' without going into trade-offs, alternatives, and explaining decision making... it would probably have been a better idea to write the actual program right away." Two of his six stated functions matter here: scaling senior engineer knowledge, and organisational memory. Source: Ubl (6 July 2020).

The bill. Consensus documents cost the affected group's attention; broadcast RFCs cost everybody's and buy discovery of duplicate work. Source: Orosz (2018). Which you need depends on whether your failure mode is bad decisions or duplicated ones.

How you find out you were wrong. The signal is somebody re-deciding this in eighteen months without knowing it was decided. It arrives when whoever made the call has left, and is invisible until then.

The reversal. A document is cheap to revise and impossible to un-send, so the 1:1s come first. What does not reverse is a decision made with no record: the successor re-runs the argument with less information than you had.

Which battles are worth fighting?

The call. Which disagreements you spend credibility on, and which you concede quickly and in writing so the concession is recorded.

What you must know first. The four rules, especially the last. No one-size-fits-all process, because many decisions are two-way doors. Decide at about 70 percent of the information, because "being slow is going to be expensive for sure". Disagree and commit, upward included. And escalate early, because otherwise "the default dispute resolution mechanism for this scenario is exhaustion. Whoever has more stamina carries the decision." Source: Amazon, 2016 shareholder letter (SEC).

The bill. Credibility is the currency and it does not refill quickly. Every argument won on a two-way door is one you cannot have on a one-way door the same quarter, which is why you sort the list before the meeting.

How you find out you were wrong. The signal is a one-way door that closed while you argued about a model choice. Nobody announces it. You find out during chapter 7's audit, when the third leg turns out to have been attached in a sprint you spent elsewhere.

The reversal. Conceding is instantly reversible and free. Winning is not: a decision you carried is yours to defend as long as it is in production, which is the underestimated part of being right.

2. The review, and the team

How do you review an AI system, and what does the reviewer ask that a code review does not?

The call. What a design review of an AI system must establish before approving anything, given that the artefact under review is not only code.

What you must know first. That the craft standard covers it. Dropbox's staff ladder says "I design software components that are difficult to misuse", which transfers unusually well: a prompt, a tool schema, an eval harness and a retrieval index are all easy to misuse, silently. Their ML ladder adds two rows a generic one lacks: owning the quality strategy, and the soundness of the experiment design. Source: Dropbox, undated.

The bill. A longer review. Five questions have no analogue in a code review, each is a chapter of this course, and none appears in a diff.

How you find out you were wrong. The signal is an incident whose cause was visible in the design and nobody asked. It lands much later than a missed code review, because these defects surface through users rather than exceptions.

The reversal. Adding a question to a review template is free and takes effect immediately. What does not reverse is everything approved before the question existed, which is why the template is worth writing before the second system.

Who is on the team, in what order, at what stage?

The call. Which role you hire next, and whether it is a machine-learning engineer at all.

What you must know first. The published warning, about order rather than skill: "Don't fall into the trap of 'AI Engineering is all I need'", and hiring at the wrong time - an ML engineer too early - "is a waste of time and money, and causes churn". Source: applied-llms.org (8 June 2024). The research found no published ladder with AI-specific staff rows beyond one company's ML ladder.

The bill. The vocabulary is Larson's four archetypes - Tech Lead, Architect, Solver, Right Hand - with his framing that the level is "the intersection of the role, your behaviors, your impact, and the organization's recognition of all those things". Source: Larson, StaffEng, undated.

A proposition rather than a finding. One research scout behind this course argues that AI engineering over-produces Solvers and under-produces Architects: the work generates hotspots constantly, and whoever is good at them gets pulled from fire to fire, while almost every problem in chapters 5 to 7 is an Architect problem. That is the scout's own proposition, offered to be argued with, and not a published finding.

How you find out you were wrong. The signal is a specialist with nothing to do that only they can do, and it takes two quarters to be said out loud. Whoever wrote the hiring plan sees it last, because the plan reads as ambition.

The reversal. Re-sequencing a hiring plan is easy on paper and involves a person, the least reversible thing here. Being late is recoverable; being early is a decision somebody else lives inside.

3. Saying no, and being seen

How do you say no to an AI feature without becoming the person who says no?

The call. Whether your objection is a refusal or a strategy, a distinction with a published definition rather than a matter of tone.

What you must know first. That strategy has a structure most documents claiming to be one fail. Larson uses Rumelt's three parts: a diagnosis of the root cause, guiding policies addressing it through trade-offs, and coherent actions. His test: "A strategy is only meaningful if it leads to aligned action". Source: Larson (13 February 2023).

The bill. His procedure is the tractable part and has a prerequisite: "To write an engineering strategy, write five design documents, and pull the similarities out." You earn the strategy by making five concrete decisions and noticing what they had in common, so the first four nos are individual.

How you find out you were wrong. The signal is being routed around: proposals that used to come to you stop coming. It is quiet and takes a quarter, and by then the policy has been replaced by a workaround everybody knows about except you.

The reversal. A single no is easy to reverse and worth reversing publicly when the evidence changes, which is what buys the next one. A reputation is not reversible on that timescale, so the objection needs a diagnosis rather than a preference.

How do you make the highest-leverage work legible?

The call. Whether the work this course calls necessary is visible to whatever decides promotions and headcount, and what you do if not.

What you must know first. That it is glue work, and glue work has a documented failure. Reilly's definition covers noticing when people are blocked, spotting what a document handwaves, onboarding, and improving processes. Her advice should not be softened: "If you're not getting promoted for glue work…stop doing glue work." Source: Reilly, "Being Glue", undated. Almost everything chapter 5 called necessary is on her list.

The bill. Making it legible costs an owner, a named metric, a dashboard and a slot in the review. Not making it legible costs the person doing it, and they leave.

How you find out you were wrong. The signal is the promotion committee scene Reilly narrates: credit for whoever wrote the code, credit for whoever did the design, and "Your project isn't finished. You're not producing much code." It arrives at a cycle boundary, far too late to fix that cycle.

The reversal. You can stop doing glue work immediately, and Reilly's advice is that you should if the ladder does not reward it. What you cannot reverse is a year spent invisibly, which is why this is the last decision in the course.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

You and a peer have disagreed for six weeks about a design and neither has escalated. What decides it, according to the published account?
The letter names exhaustion as the default mechanism precisely because it is not merit, which is the argument for escalating genuine misalignment early. The first option describes what everybody assumes is happening. The second assumes an observer who is watching, and an unescalated dispute is by definition one nobody escalated. The fourth is a real thing that sometimes happens and it is not what the source says decides these.
A design document explains in detail how the team will implement a retrieval pipeline. It names no alternatives. What does its author's own test say?
The stated test is about trade-offs and alternatives, and a document without them is an implementation plan in a costume. The second option is the common defence and it confuses two artefacts. The third adds process to a document that has no decision in it. The fourth is good advice about a different problem: a diagram cannot supply a trade-off that was never considered.
Sixteen developers forecast a 24 percent speedup, were measured 19 percent slower, and afterwards believed they had been 20 percent faster. What should a staff engineer take from it?
The durable finding is the gap between belief and measurement, which is what makes the checking role necessary. The first option is the headline the measuring team themselves have since qualified, and the course carries their update rather than the number alone. The third rewords the problem without giving an instrument. The fourth invents a critique the source does not make, and the source is unusually explicit about its own limits.

Practice about 20 minutes, with paper

Practice 1frontier

Take a real AI feature your organisation is considering and write the two-page decision record for it, using the reference sheet.

Produce a document containing a diagnosis, one stated trade-off, one alternative considered and rejected with reasons, a named verifier, the abstention behaviour, the one-way doors identified, and an evaluation plan with a sample size and a power justification.

Hint

Every element in that list is a chapter of this course. If one of them is hard to fill, the chapter it comes from is the one to re-read, and the difficulty is the finding rather than the obstacle.

Show the worked solution
  1. Diagnose before you propose. Rumelt's first part is a theory of the root cause, and it is where an AI proposal most often skips straight to a solution. Chapter 2 supplies the ladder that tests whether a model belongs at all.
  2. State one trade-off in the author's own words. Not a risk register: a thing you gave up, and what you got. A document without this is the one Ubl says should have been a program.
  3. Name the verifier and the abstention. Chapter 2 gives the verifier question and chapter 7 gives the abstention path, and the two together decide what the schedule actually is.
  4. List the one-way doors. From the quadrant above: egress beside private data, output shown as authoritative, model output triggering irreversible actions, what you retain and send onward, and whether the harness exists before launch.
  5. Attach the evaluation plan with its arithmetic. A sample size and the effect it can detect, from chapter 5. Without the power justification the plan is a promise to measure rather than a measurement.

Sanity check. A successor picking this document up in eighteen months should be able to act on it without finding you. If any section reads as a summary of what you decided rather than why the alternative lost, that section is organisational memory that will not survive your departure.

Decision drill

Questions a reviewer puts to you rather than the system, and the trap in each.

  • "Is this written down?" The trap is pointing at a ticket. Point at a document with a trade-off and a rejected alternative in it.
  • "Can you let this one go?" The trap is treating every disagreement alike. Say which door it is, and concede the two-way ones fast.
  • "What would you ask in the review?" The trap is listing code-review questions. Name the five with no analogue in a diff.
  • "Should we hire an ML engineer?" The trap is answering on skills. Answer on order, and on what they would own on day one.
  • "Why does eval work need an owner?" The trap is arguing quality. Argue legibility: unowned work is invisible, and invisible work stops.
Your teacher. Write the decision record for something real, then do the hardest part: take it to the one person most likely to disagree, before anyone else sees it. Most people find the objection is not the one they prepared for, and that objection is worth more than the document.

Primary source to go deeper

Read "Design Docs at Google" and "Writing an engineering strategy" together: one says what a document must contain, the other that you cannot write the strategy until five of them exist. Then read "Being Glue", and do not soften it.
