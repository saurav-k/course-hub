Module 04 · From basic to agentic RAG · Lesson 0450

The loop is cheap to describe and the bill is the design

working 12 min Token multipliers and the citation mechanism are Anthropic's own account of a system they run, measured on their internal evaluation. · How we built our multi-agent research system

The one-minute version

  • A research agent is not a better retriever. It is a loop that keeps searching until a written plan is satisfied, and the plan is the thing that terminates it.
  • Four moves: plan, search, read, note. The notes live outside the context window, which is what stops the loop drowning in its own findings.
  • The citation contract is what makes the output usable. Anthropic runs a dedicated pass that locates where each claim's citation belongs.
  • The bill is the design constraint, and it is published: agents at roughly 4x the tokens of a chat, multi-agent at roughly 15x, with token usage explaining 80% of the variance in their results.
  • Evaluation is module 1's harness with two new columns, one of which is citation accuracy.

1. Four moves, and the one that is not obvious

Plan, search, read, note. Three of those you have. The fourth is what makes the loop possible.

A loop that keeps its findings in the context window fills it, and lesson 0240 already established what happens to a model's recall as its window grows. So the notes go outside: a store the loop writes to and reads back from, deliberately, rather than a transcript it accumulates. Anthropic names this structured note-taking in its own account of context engineering, alongside compaction and sub-agents, and describes it as the agent persisting notes outside the context window and retrieving them later. Module 3's Memory is four stores with four lifetimes is where that store is designed properly.

The plan is the other half. A single-shot system terminates because it has one query. A loop terminates because a written plan says what "done" is, and the honest version of this is that a vague plan is an unbounded bill rather than a poor answer.

2. The citation contract

This is the part that separates a research agent from a long answer, and it is where most implementations stop short.

After many searches, the synthesis is written from notes rather than from passages that are still in the window. That is exactly the condition under which a model will produce fluent text whose provenance nobody can check, and the whole value of the output depends on being able to check it.

Anthropic's system runs a dedicated pass for it: an agent that processes the documents to identify the specific locations where citations belong, so that all claims are attributed to their sources. Whether you run it as a separate pass or as part of synthesis matters less than the contract itself: a claim without a source is a defect, not a stylistic choice, and it is checkable by machine.

3. The bill, stated honestly

Anthropic publishes the cost shape, and it is the most useful number in this lesson because it decides whether the technique is appropriate at all.

In their account, agents use about four times the tokens of a chat interaction, and multi-agent systems about fifteen times. They also report that token usage alone explains about 80% of the variance in their evaluation results, and they say plainly that the approach is viable only where the value of the task is high enough to pay for the increased performance.

Every one of those figures is Anthropic's measurement of Anthropic's system on Anthropic's internal evaluation, which is not public. They are evidence that the cost multiple is real and large. They are not a prediction about your loop, and none of them is a target.

4. Evaluating it is your harness plus two columns

You do not need a new evaluation system for this. Anthropic describes judging their research output on a rubric covering factual accuracy, citation accuracy, completeness, source quality and tool efficiency, and three of those five are already in your harness from module 1.

The two that are new are worth adding carefully. Citation accuracy is the one you can check without a judge at all: take every claim's cited location, go to the document, and confirm the quoted text is there. That is a script, it is deterministic, and it is the cheapest quality gate in this module. Tool efficiency is searches per question from lesson 0400, which you are already recording, read against whether the extra searches changed the answer, and module 3's Grade the path, not only the answer is the page that makes it a metric.

And the caution from the same source applies here more than anywhere: they keep human evaluation in the loop because it catches what automation misses. A research report is long, and a judge scoring long text is exactly where lesson 0170's verbosity bias lives.

Multi-agent orchestration, and the arithmetic for refusing a second agent, belong to module 5. This page uses one fact from that territory - the published token multiple - as evidence that a loop's cost grows fast, and stops there. A research loop is a single agent unless module 5 convinces you otherwise, and its own account says the approach pays only where the task's value is high enough.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

Why do a research loop's notes live outside the context window?
Accumulating every passage in the window fills it and degrades recall of what is already there, so the loop writes compact notes to a store and discards the passages, which is what lets it run more searches than the window could hold. Caching is a separate optimisation that module 6 covers. Providers accept long transcripts up to their limit; the problem is that the model uses them poorly. Parallel readers are a multi-agent concern from module 5.
Which part of the research rubric can be checked without a judge?
A citation naming a document and an offset is verifiable by a script: go there and confirm the quoted text exists, deterministically and for free. Completeness is a judgement against a plan written in prose. Source quality is an editorial assessment. Factual accuracy needs either a reference answer or a judge, which is the expensive path this one avoids.

Practice about 60 minutes, with a terminal

Practice 1working

Build the smallest honest research loop over your own corpus, and price it.

Take one question that genuinely needs several lookups. Have the model write a plan of three or four sub-questions with a stated test for done. Loop: search, read, write a note of under 100 tokens to a store outside the window, repeat until the plan is satisfied or a hard cap you set is reached. Then synthesise from the notes only.

Require every claim to carry a source id and an offset, and write the script that checks them: for each citation, open the document and confirm the quoted text is there. Report tokens spent, searches made, and the fraction of claims whose citation resolves.

Hint

Set the hard cap before you run it once. A loop with no cap and a vague plan is how a single question costs more than a day of normal traffic, and you will not be watching when it happens.

Make the notes short on purpose. If a note is a paragraph, you have moved the context problem into the store rather than solving it. Under a hundred tokens forces the model to decide what mattered, which is the work.

Show what the three numbers mean together
  1. Expect roughly four times a single-shot request, and check that against the published multiple. If yours is much lower, the plan is probably terminating early and the answer will be thin. If it is much higher, the plan has no real exit test and the cap is what stopped it, which is a plan defect rather than a loop defect.
  2. The citation check will fail more often than you expect on the first run, usually because the model paraphrased a passage and cited it as a quotation. That is the useful finding: it is exactly the failure the contract exists to catch, and it is invisible to a reader who trusts the footnote.
  3. A claim with no citation at all is the other common outcome, and it is worse. Make the synthesis prompt refuse to emit an uncited claim, and count how many claims disappear. If most of the report vanishes, the notes were too thin to support it.
  4. Compare against single-shot on the same question. Sometimes the loop wins clearly. Sometimes one good retrieval answered it and you paid four times as much for the same paragraph, and knowing which is the point of running both.
  5. Put the citation check in continuous integration. It needs no model call, it is deterministic, and it is the only gate in this module that costs nothing to run on every change.

Sanity check. The fraction of resolving citations should be high but not one on the first run; a perfect score usually means the checker is matching too loosely, so try it against a citation you have deliberately corrupted and confirm it fails. A checker that never fails is not a gate.

Your teacher. Ask your agent to take your loop's final report and, for each claim, say whether it is supported by the notes it was written from, using only the notes. The claims it cannot support from the notes are the ones the model invented during synthesis, and that count is a cleaner measure of the loop's honesty than any judge score. Then ask what note would have been needed, and compare it with what the loop actually wrote.
