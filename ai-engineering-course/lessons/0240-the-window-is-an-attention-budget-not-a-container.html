Module 02 · Retrieval quality and context engineering · Lesson 0240

Order what you send, and send less of it

working 8 min Grounded in a measurement of position sensitivity in long contexts, read at arXiv:2307.03172v3, and in Anthropic's own statement of the attention budget. · arXiv:2307.03172

The one-minute version

  • The window is a budget rather than a container. Context engineering is curating the tokens that go in, not writing a better instruction.
  • Two independent effects work against filling it: position - the middle is where things are missed - and volume - recall of the context falls as the context grows.
  • Raising top-k raises the chance the answer is present and lowers the chance it is attended to. Both published findings are real and they point opposite ways.
  • Top-k is the only term in your request that grows without anybody deciding to grow it, and it is linear in your bill.
  • The cheapest experiment in the module: reverse the order of the retrieved set and re-run the same thirty questions.

1. Curating, not phrasing

Anthropic define context engineering as "the set of strategies for curating and maintaining the optimal set of tokens (information) during LLM inference". The word doing the work is curating. A prompt-engineering change edits the words you wrote; a context-engineering change edits which of somebody else's words are present at all, and the previous figure shows which of the two has more material to work on.

Their framing for why it matters is an attention budget: a model has a finite capacity to attend across its context, and every token spends some of it. The target that follows is uncomfortable if your instinct is to retrieve more - "the smallest possible set of high-signal tokens that maximize the likelihood of some desired outcome".

2. Position: the middle is where things are missed

Liu and colleagues measured what happens when the passage that answers a question is moved around inside a long input. They report that "performance is often highest when relevant information occurs at the beginning or end of the input context, and significantly degrades when models must access relevant information in the middle of long contexts, even for explicitly long-context models".

That last clause is the one to keep. A larger window is not a fix for this, because the effect is about where the answer sits and not about whether it fits.

The consequence for your pipeline is small and free. Your retriever returns passages sorted by score and your packing code almost certainly writes them out in that order, so the best passage lands at the head and the worst at the tail, and everything in between is in the region the finding warns about. Nobody chose that; it is the default of two components meeting. Putting the strongest passage last instead is a one-line change, and lesson 0000's rule applies: measure it, do not assume it.

3. Volume: the term that grows unnoticed

The second effect is about how much, not where. Anthropic name it context rot: "as the number of tokens in the context window increases, the model's ability to accurately recall information from that context decreases".

Set that beside the finding this module has been walking towards, from the same organisation's retrieval work: "passing the top-20 chunks to the model is more effective than just the top-10 or top-5". Both are that organisation's own measurements and both are plausible, because they are about different things. More passages raise the probability the answer is present; more tokens lower the probability it is attended to. The optimum is somewhere and nobody has measured where it is on your corpus.

What is not in dispute is the bill. Retrieved tokens are linear in top-k, they are input tokens on every single request, and they dominate everything else in the window.

4. Recording that you did it

When you start compacting or dropping context, the trace has to say so, or a later week's numbers will disagree with an earlier week's for a reason nobody can find. The OpenTelemetry generative-AI semantic conventions carry gen_ai.conversation.compacted for exactly this, a flag saying the context represents a compacted view of what came before. As with every attribute this course quotes, that convention is marked Status: Development and lives in a repository with no releases and no tags, so what you are committing to is the shape - record that you compacted - and a rename costs you one file of constants.

The same write-up names three techniques for when a task outgrows one window - compaction, structured note-taking and sub-agent architectures - and all three belong to later modules. Name them here so that when module 3 hands you an agent loop you already know why its context needs managing at all.

5. What this changed

Two experiments, both cheap, both on the same thirty questions.

Reverse the order. Pack the retrieved set worst-first so the highest-scoring passage is adjacent to the question, and re-run. On many corpora this moves nothing, which is a perfectly good row: it says your contexts are short enough that position is not yet your problem, and you have learned that for the price of one run.

Move k. Run at 5, 10 and 20 with everything else fixed, and record the score and the tokens per request together. A quality gain bought with a doubled bill is a decision, not a win, and this is the module's first place where the right answer might be to keep the cheaper setting.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

Top-20 beats top-5 in one published result, and recall of a long context degrades in another. How should a page treat that?
The two findings are about different quantities - whether the answer is present, and whether it is attended to - so both can hold and the balance between them is a property of a corpus. Dating them against each other decides nothing, since neither supersedes the other. Calling them both wrong throws away two measurements. Preferring one because of its subject is the move this module is written against: the resolution is your own thirty questions.
Your packing code writes retrieved passages in the retriever's score order. What has that decided?
Score order puts rank one first and the question last, so the strongest passage ends up as far from the question as it can get - a placement nobody chose. What is retrieved at all was decided by top-k and the ranking, before packing. Context length is decided by k and passage size, not by order. The last option is the belief this section exists to remove: the measured position effect is exactly the evidence that order is not invisible.

Practice about 30 minutes, with a terminal

Practice 1working

This is the cheapest increment in the module and the one most likely to return nothing, which is why it is worth doing properly.

Instrument the window first. For one request, print the token count of each slot: system, tools, history, retrieved, question. Most readers find the retrieved slot is over 80% of the request and have never seen the number.

Reverse the packing order so the highest-scoring passage sits closest to the question, and re-run the golden set. Record the delta whatever it is.

Sweep k at 5, 10 and 20, recording the score and the tokens per request for each.

Pick a retrieval budget in tokens and write it into the repository beside the baseline, so that a later change to chunk size cannot silently spend it.

Hint

Reversing the order is one change, and sweeping k is another. Run them separately or you will have two variables and one number.

A budget stated in tokens survives a change of chunk size and a change of k; a budget stated as "top-10" survives neither.

Show what a good result looks like
  1. The slot table is the finding, before any experiment runs. Four narrow slots and one wide one, with the wide one at four thousand tokens against a few hundred for everything else, is the arithmetic of lesson 0000 seen in your own request rather than in a worked example.
  2. Expect the reversal to be small. The position effect was measured on long contexts; ten passages is not long. A move inside the noise of thirty questions means keep the simpler code, and write "no change" in the table rather than nothing.
  3. Read the k sweep as two columns, not one. If recall@10 rises from 0.71 to 0.74 between k of 10 and 20 while retrieved tokens go from 4,000 to 8,000, you have bought three points for double the serving cost, forever. Whether that is worth it is your product's question and not this page's.
  4. Write the budget down as a number with a unit. "8,000 retrieved tokens per request" is checkable in code and in a trace. "Keep the context small" is not, and lesson 0220's next parser change will quietly break it.

Sanity check. Your retrieved slot should be close to k times your chunk size. If it is much larger, something is adding the document title or the whole section to each passage at pack time, and the effective chunk size you swept in lesson 0220 was never the size you actually send.

Your teacher. Ask your agent to add a per-slot token breakdown to your trace so the five numbers are on every request rather than on one you printed by hand. Then ask it to run the reversal and the k sweep as separate scripts, each writing one row, and to refuse to report a delta smaller than the run-to-run variation it measures by running the same configuration twice.
