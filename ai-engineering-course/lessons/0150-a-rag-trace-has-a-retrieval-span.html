Module 01 · Foundations · Lesson 0150

The default retrieval span records no query and no scores

foundation 11 min Attribute names, tiers and counts read in the GenAI semantic conventions at commit fee465db, where every document is marked Development. · GenAI spans

The one-minute version

  • Print statements stop working at the second model call. The vocabulary for writing a request down already exists, so do not invent one.
  • The conventions name six span kinds: Inference, Embeddings, Retrievals, Fetch response, Memory and Execute tool. You will emit three today and all six by module 4.
  • The Retrievals span carries ten attributes. Nine of them describe the call. Two describe the search, and both are Opt-In.
  • So an off-the-shelf instrumentation gives you a retrieval span with no query text and no document scores, which is a latency number wearing the name of a diagnosis.
  • Turning them on is a deliberate act with a privacy consequence, which is the decision lesson 0110 asked you to make before there was anything to leak.

1. Why print statements stop

One question through your pipeline is three operations, and by module 4 there is a loop around them that runs a variable number of times. A print statement records that something happened. What you need is a record that says this retrieval belonged to that question and returned these documents, and that survives being read tomorrow by somebody who was not there.

That is a trace: a tree of spans sharing an identifier, each with a start, a duration and attributes. The general mechanism is not this course's to teach and Observability and Operations owns it. What is this course's is narrower and more useful: which spans, with which names, carrying which attributes, so that two systems built by two teams can be read by the same query.

2. Six kinds, and the three you emit today

The OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions define six span kinds. Inference is a model call. Embeddings is a vectorisation. Retrievals is a search against a store. Fetch response covers retrieving a previously generated response, Memory covers reading and writing an agent's memory, and Execute tool covers running a tool. The last three arrive in modules 3 and 4; you emit the first three this week.

The Retrievals span has its own shape rules worth copying rather than improvising. Its gen_ai.operation.name should be retrieval. Its span name should be the operation name followed by the data source id. Its kind should be CLIENT, because from your process the store is a remote dependency even when it is a table in your own database.

3. The tiers, and the surprise in them

Every attribute in the conventions carries a requirement level, and the levels are the interesting part of this page. Count them on the Retrievals span and there are ten: one Required (gen_ai.operation.name), five Conditionally Required (error.type, gen_ai.data_source.id, gen_ai.provider.name, gen_ai.request.model and server.port), two Recommended (gen_ai.retrieval.top_k and server.address), and two Opt-In.

The two Opt-In attributes are gen_ai.retrieval.query.text and gen_ai.retrieval.documents, and the second is the one carrying each returned document's id and its score. So the two attributes that let you answer "was the right passage even a candidate, and where did it rank" are the two the convention leaves off by default.

This is not an oversight and it is worth understanding rather than working around. Both attributes carry content: the query is whatever a user typed, and the documents are whatever your corpus holds. Shipping them to a collector means storing user text and corpus text in a second system with its own retention and its own access control. The convention makes that a decision rather than a default, which is correct, and it means the useful configuration is one you write down on purpose.

4. Four metrics, and the one users complain about

Beside the spans the conventions define client metrics, and four are worth emitting from the first week. gen_ai.client.token.usage is your bill. gen_ai.client.operation.duration is the whole call. gen_ai.client.operation.time_to_first_chunk and gen_ai.client.operation.time_per_output_chunk split a streaming response into the wait and the flow.

The split matters because users complain about the first and engineers optimise the second. A response that starts in 300 milliseconds and takes six seconds to finish feels fast; one that starts in three seconds and finishes in four feels broken. One duration number cannot tell those apart, and two can.

5. What must not reach a span

Turning the two Opt-In attributes on is the right decision for most teams and it comes with an obligation. Put the redaction in one function that every span passes through, rather than at each call site, so that the rule is enforceable rather than remembered. Strip credentials from anything that could contain them, and decide explicitly whether a document id is itself sensitive, because in some corpora a filename is the disclosure.

The cheap version that works: record the query text and the document ids and scores, and never the document bodies. Ids and scores answer both diagnostic questions. Bodies answer neither and are the part that turns a trace store into a second copy of your corpus.

Every attribute name on this page is quoted from a convention marked Status: Development, in a repository that has published no release and no tag. Names can change. What is being committed to is the shape: one span per stage, the query and the document scores recorded on the retrieval one, and content-bearing fields switched on deliberately. A rename costs one file of constants. Not recording the scores costs you every diagnosis in the next seven modules.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

You enable a stock instrumentation and get Retrievals spans. What can you still not diagnose?
The document ids and scores live in gen_ai.retrieval.documents, which is Opt-In, so a default configuration has no ranking information at all. Duration is intrinsic to a span. The data source id is Conditionally Required and arrives when applicable. The error type is Conditionally Required and arrives whenever the operation failed. Only the ranking question needs an attribute you must switch on.
Why does the convention put query text and returned documents in the Opt-In tier?
Both attributes ship content into a second system with its own retention and access control, so the convention makes recording them a deliberate act rather than a default. Neither is expensive: both are already in memory when the span is created. High cardinality is a real cost concern and is not what separates this tier. The whole convention is at Development status, so instability applies equally to all ten attributes.

Practice about 40 minutes, with a terminal

Practice 1foundation

Instrument the pipeline so that one question produces one trace you can read, and then read one.

Emit three spans per request using the names from section 2. On the Retrievals span set the operation name, the data source id, the top-k, and both Opt-In attributes: the query text and every returned document with its id and score. Use the exporter you stood up in lesson 0110.

Then find a question your system gets wrong and open its trace. Read the document list. Decide, from the scores alone, which of the three failure modes from lesson 0120 you are looking at, and write the answer down.

Hint

Record the scores for every returned document, not just the top one. The diagnosis is usually in the gap between the first and the fifth: a flat set of scores means the retriever could not distinguish them and is guessing, and a sharp drop after the first means it was confident about one thing that turned out to be wrong.

Put the redaction function in place before you turn the Opt-In attributes on, not after. Traces are kept, so a leak you fix next week is a leak that is already stored.

Show what one trace should let you conclude
  1. The answering passage is absent from the document list. That is a recall failure, it belongs to modules 1, 2 and 4, and no prompt change can touch it. This is the most common of the three and the one people misdiagnose as hallucination.
  2. The answering passage is in the list, ranked low, with a score close to its neighbours. That is a ranking failure and it is what lesson 0260's fusion and lesson 0270's reranker exist for. The tell is the flat score distribution: the retriever had no basis for preferring the right one.
  3. The answering passage is ranked first and the answer is still wrong. Now, and only now, is it a generation problem. Check whether the passage survived into the packed prompt at all before blaming the wording, because lesson 0240 is about the ways it might not have.
  4. The span exists and the documents array is empty. You did not enable the Opt-In attribute. This is the single most common outcome of this exercise and it is why the page is written around the tiers.
  5. Write the conclusion into the trace's own record, not into your memory. Lesson 0430 turns these judgements into rows in the golden set, and it can only do that if the judgement was written down beside the trace id.

Sanity check. Your three spans should share one trace id and nest, with the retrieval span's duration inside the request's and the inference span starting after the retrieval span ends. If the spans are siblings at the root rather than nested, your context is not propagating, and every multi-step diagnosis in modules 3 to 5 will be impossible for the same reason.

Your teacher. Give your agent one trace as JSON and the question it came from, and ask it to state which of the three failure modes applies and what evidence in the span rules out the other two. Then ask what attribute it wished the span carried. Whatever it names is a candidate for your own instrumentation, and the convention has a slot for most of them.
