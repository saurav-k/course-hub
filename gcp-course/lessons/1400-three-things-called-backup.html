Three things are called backup, and each protects a different object

working 8 min Capabilities backup-service and dr-orchestration. Sourced from Backup and DR Service.

The one-minute version

  • Three products in this estate protect three different objects. Persistent Disk snapshots protect a volume, Backup for GKE protects a cluster's workloads and configuration, and Backup and DR Service protects estates of machines and databases.
  • Backup and DR Service is built around a vault, with plans that decide what is protected and how often, and appliances for what sits outside.
  • Its vaults can be immutable, which is the ransomware property: a backup nobody can shorten or delete is the one that survives an attacker with credentials.
  • The same product provides disaster recovery orchestration - failover workflows, replication targets and recovery plans - which is what makes it more than a copy.
  • Compute and database failover often need neither: regional managed instance groups and Spanner failover cover those patterns on their own.

1. Three products, three objects

2. The vault, and the property that matters most

This is the same shape as the retention lock from lesson 0401: a guarantee moves from being a policy somebody could change into a property of the platform. The question an auditor or an incident asks is not whether copies exist, but whether anyone could have removed them.

3. A copy is not a recovery

4. What often needs none of this

The reframing Three questions, in order. What object must come back - a volume, a cluster or an estate - which picks the product. Could anyone delete the copies - which decides whether the vault must be immutable. In what order must things return - which is the recovery plan, and it is the part no backup schedule contains.

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

What does an immutable vault protect against that an ordinary backup schedule does not?
Immutability means nobody can shorten or delete the backups, which is what makes them survive an attacker who has your credentials. The first names a placement property. The second names the recovery plan's job. The fourth invents verification.
A team has daily Persistent Disk snapshots of every production volume and calls this its backup strategy. What is missing?
A snapshot protects a volume, and the machine, the configuration and the order of recovery are not in it. The second collapses three products into one. The third makes it a scheduling question. The fourth invents an encryption gap.
Which failure does the inventory record as already covered without a recovery plan?
Regional managed instance groups and Spanner failover cover the compute and database failure patterns on their own. The first invents automatic restoration. The second and third both describe events that need a restore - which is the opposite of needing no plan.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A company runs a fleet of virtual machines behind a regional managed instance group, a GKE cluster, a Spanner database in a multi-region configuration, and a VMware estate inside a Google region. The board asks two questions: what happens if a zone is lost, and what happens if an attacker with an administrator's credentials deletes everything.

Answer both questions component by component, name what protects each part in the second scenario, and say what the company still needs beyond copies.

Hint

The two questions have very different answers for the same components. In the first, most of the estate handles itself; in the second, none of it does.

Show the worked solution
  1. Zone loss, the machine fleet. A regional managed instance group already replaces members across zones, so this is handled without a recovery plan being invoked.
  2. Zone loss, the database. A Spanner multi-region configuration already survives it, with the failover pattern the inventory records.
  3. Zone loss, the cluster and the VMware estate. These need their own placement decisions rather than a recovery product, and the module 03 and module 04 pages cover what each protects.
  4. The attacker, everywhere. Nothing above helps. Replication faithfully copies a deletion, exactly as lesson 0402 said of synchronous replication - so the answer is backups the attacker cannot reach. Backup and DR Service with an immutable vault covers the machines, the databases and the VMware estate; Backup for GKE covers the cluster's workloads and configuration.
  5. Name what is still missing. The order. Copies do not describe how to bring the estate back as a working system, and that sequence is the recovery plan the same product holds. Without it the answer to the board's second question is "we have the data" rather than "we can be running again".

Sanity check. Your first answer should invoke no recovery product for two of the four components, and your second should invoke one for all of them. If replication appeared as an answer to the attacker question, note what replication does with a deletion.

Your teacher. Ask an agent one question of any backup arrangement: could an administrator delete these copies today. If the answer is yes, the arrangement protects against accident and not against an attacker, and those are the two scenarios boards ask about in the same sentence.
