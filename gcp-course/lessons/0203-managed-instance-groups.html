Module 02 · Compute · Lesson 0203

You declare a group and a controller holds it, or you declare a job that ends

working 10 min Capabilities autoscaling-group and batch-compute. Sourced from Google's instance groups documentation.

The one-minute version

  • A managed instance group stamps members from an instance template and then keeps its promises: a target size, health-based replacement, and rolling updates.
  • Autohealing is the part that earns its keep. Scaling is what people buy it for and replacement is what saves them, and Google records those replacements in the System Event audit stream.
  • A regional group distributes members across the zones of a region, so a lost zone costs a share of the fleet instead of all of it.
  • A stateful group preserves disks and addresses across a replacement, which buys back identity at the cost of the disposability that made the group simple.
  • A group holds a size. When the work ends rather than persists, Batch is the right shape: a managed scheduler that provisions Compute Engine resources for jobs with no cluster to manage.

1. The loop that is the actual product

Managed instance groups carry autoscaling, autohealing, rolling updates and distribution across zones, and predictive autoscaling is included rather than sold beside it. Because a template is immutable, a rolling update is the controller reading a different template rather than an edit being applied to running machines, which is the mechanism the previous page set up.

One operational detail is worth carrying into module 10 now: a replacement performed by the controller is a Google-side action on your resources, so it is recorded in the System Event audit log, which is always on and free. That is where the evidence lives when a fleet quietly replaced a member overnight and nobody knows why the graph has a step in it.

2. What a regional group actually buys

That last sentence is the trade, and it is the one most often skipped. A regional group spread over three zones means the surviving fraction after a zone loss is two of three, so a fleet sized exactly for its peak is under-provisioned the moment a zone goes. Sizing for the survivable case means running more members than the load needs, every day, in exchange for not being paged on the day a zone is lost.

3. Stateful members, and what they cost

A stateful managed instance group preserves disks and addresses across a replacement, so a member keeps its identity rather than being an interchangeable copy. That is what makes a group usable for workloads that are not fully disposable. The cost is the thing that made the stateless group easy: once members are distinguishable, a replacement is no longer free, the controller has more to preserve, and the fleet stops being cattle.

The reframing Ask of any fleet: are its members interchangeable? If yes, the stateless group is doing exactly what it was designed for and every property of it - stamping, replacing, rolling forward - is cheap. If no, a stateful group will hold the identity for you, and you have chosen a harder object to operate. The wrong answer here is not choosing the wrong group, it is not noticing that the question was asked.

4. When the work ends

Batch is the fully managed scheduler for batch jobs on Compute Engine resources with no cluster to manage. It sits beside two other job-shaped services rather than replacing them: Dataflow runs Apache Beam pipelines and Dataproc runs Spark and Hadoop, both of which module 06 covers. Batch is the general case - work that is a program to run rather than a pipeline to express.

What this certification tests Fleet operations are examined as tasks rather than as theory. The Associate Cloud Engineer guide puts Planning and implementing a cloud solution at about 30 per cent and Ensuring the successful operation of a cloud solution at about 30 per cent, and managed instance groups appear in both - built in the first, healed and scaled in the second.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

A nine-member regional group is spread across three zones and one zone is lost. What is left, and what does that imply?
Three per zone means a zone loss removes three and leaves six, so surviving peak load means running more than peak needs. The first option assumes instant replacement into zones that may have no capacity. The third inverts the arithmetic. The fourth pairs the right number with a false claim about the controller.
Which property of a managed instance group is the one that saves you on a day nothing is scaling?
Autohealing runs whether or not the size is changing, which is why it is the property that pays on an ordinary day. The other three are real and each one only acts when something else happens: a load change, a template change, or a zone loss.
A team has a queue of independent jobs that each run once and finish. Which construct fits?
Work that finishes wants a job scheduler, and Batch provisions and tears down for exactly that. The second option can be made to work by writing the queue, the completion detection and the teardown by hand, which is the machinery Batch already is. The third and fourth answer different questions entirely.

Practice about 20 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A stateless web tier must serve its peak load even while one zone of its region is unavailable. Peak load needs eight members. The team plans a regional managed instance group spread across three zones.

Work out the smallest fleet size that meets the requirement, show the arithmetic, and say what the design is paying for it on an ordinary day.

Hint

A zone loss removes one third of the members. Work backwards from the eight that must survive, and remember that members come in whole numbers.

Show the worked solution
  1. State the surviving fraction. Three zones, evenly spread, so a zone loss leaves two thirds of the fleet running.
  2. Solve for the total. Two thirds of the total must be at least 8, so the total must be at least 8 multiplied by 3 and divided by 2, which is 12.
  3. Check that it divides. Twelve members over three zones is four per zone. Losing one zone removes four and leaves eight, which is exactly peak - so twelve is the smallest size that works and also happens to spread evenly.
  4. Name the ordinary-day cost. On every day when no zone is lost, twelve members serve a load that needs eight, so the design runs 50 per cent more compute than the load requires. That surplus is the price of the requirement, and it is paid continuously.

Sanity check. Twelve is above eight and divides by three, which are the two conditions. If you arrived at ten, you solved for surviving the loss of some members rather than of a whole zone - ten over three zones does not spread evenly, and the largest zone would take four members with it.

Your teacher. If the regional group still feels like free insurance, ask an agent to price the same fleet at eight members and at twelve. The difference is the actual cost of the availability requirement, and it is a number a business can decide about.
