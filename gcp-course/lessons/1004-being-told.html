Everything so far was collection - this is the part that reaches a person

working 9 min Capabilities alerting, dashboards, synthetic-monitoring, distributed-tracing, profiler, network-diagnostics and service-health-dashboard. Sourced from alerting policies and Cloud Trace.

The one-minute version

  • An alerting policy can be built on a metric, a log query or a service level objective, and it carries a condition and a duration before it fires.
  • Notification channels fan an alert out to email, messaging, paging tools, a webhook or Pub/Sub, and the console holds the incident lifecycle.
  • Uptime checks probe from several geographies over HTTP, HTTPS, TCP and gRPC. Heavier scripted synthetics run as functions.
  • Cloud Trace keeps spans for 30 days and its sampling decision is made in your client. Cloud Profiler keeps continuous production profiles for 30 days as well.
  • Personalized Service Health is an incident feed scoped to your affected resources rather than a generic status page, and Connectivity Tests answer reachability questions that no log stream can.

1. Being told

The channels are the other half. An alert can reach email, messaging and paging tools, a webhook, or Pub/Sub - and the Pub/Sub channel is what turns a notification into an automation rather than a message somebody has to read.

Cloud Trace's native BigQuery sink is deprecated and has a scheduled shutdown. Do not build new exporters on the retiring sink; check the current status on the Cloud Trace documentation.

4. The question no stream answers

The reframing Four questions, four different tools, and none of them is a log. Should somebody be woken is an alerting policy with a duration. Can the outside reach us is an uptime check. Why is this request slow is a trace, and only if your client sampled it. Is it even us is Personalized Service Health, and it is the first thing to check rather than the last.

What does the duration on an alerting policy's condition do?
The condition has to hold for the duration, which is what stops a transient spike becoming a page. The first moves the delay after firing. The third describes the resolution behaviour. The fourth describes a lookback window, which is a different setting.
Users report they cannot reach a service. Network logging was never enabled. What answers the reachability question?
Connectivity Tests reason about the configuration, so they work with no telemetry at all. The first records only the future. The second needs an instrumented client and a sampled request. The third answers whether the platform has an incident, which is a different question.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

An on-call team is paged forty times a week, mostly for spikes that clear within a minute. They also learn about regional problems from customers rather than from their own tooling, and when they do investigate a slow request they usually cannot find its trace. Their monitoring consists of one dashboard and a set of alerts with no durations.

Fix each of the three complaints with a mechanism from this page, and say which fix requires a change in the application rather than in the monitoring configuration.

Hint

Three complaints, three mechanisms. Two are configuration and one is not, and the one that is not was named on this page as a client-side decision.

Show the worked solution
  1. The forty pages. Add a duration to the conditions. A policy with no duration fires on the first sample that crosses the threshold, so a spike that clears in a minute is indistinguishable from an outage. The duration is the setting that separates them.
  2. Learning from customers. Uptime checks, probing over HTTP or HTTPS from several geographies with an alerting policy attached. Probing from more than one geography is what lets the team tell a regional network problem from a genuine outage - and Personalized Service Health should be checked alongside, so a platform incident affecting their own resources is not investigated as their bug.
  3. The missing traces. The sampling decision is made in the client, so the fix is in the application's OpenTelemetry configuration - raising the sampling rate, or sampling failed requests deliberately. No monitoring setting recovers a span the client chose not to send.
  4. Answer the last part. The third. The first two are configuration in Cloud Monitoring; the third is a change to the instrumented code and has to be released like any other change.

Sanity check. Exactly one of your three fixes should require a deployment. If the trace fix was a retention or a console setting, re-read the sequence diagram - the decision happens before anything is sent.

Your teacher. When an alert wakes somebody, ask an agent what the condition was and how long it had to hold. A large share of unnecessary pages are policies with a threshold and no duration, and fixing them is a configuration change rather than a project.
