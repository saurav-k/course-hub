Three orchestrators, and each is built to call something different

working 7 min Capabilities workflow-orchestration, scheduler-jobs and managed-integration. Sourced from Workflows, Cloud Scheduler and Application Integration.

The one-minute version

  • Workflows orchestrates APIs and services. Its definition is YAML state, with retries, subworkflows, callbacks and step-level error handling.
  • Cloud Composer, from module 06, orchestrates data pipeline directed acyclic graphs. The two share a capability row and are not interchangeable.
  • Application Integration is the third shape: low-code flows with a large library of managed connectors to enterprise applications and databases, and it is distinct from the code-shaped orchestration of the other two.
  • Cloud Scheduler is not an orchestrator. It is cron as a service, firing HTTP, Pub/Sub and App Engine targets on a schedule, and it pairs with Workflows to start a chain.
  • A callback is the feature that distinguishes an orchestrator from a script: it lets a workflow wait for something outside it, including a person.

The reframing Two questions settle this row. What is being called - services, data tasks, or somebody else's enterprise system - which picks the product. Then what starts it - an event from lesson 1202, a schedule from Cloud Scheduler, or a caller - which is a separate decision people fold into the first one and should not.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

A process must call three APIs in order and pause until a manager approves. What fits?
Calling APIs in order with a wait for an external event is exactly what Workflows and its callbacks are for. The first replaces a dependency with a guess about timing. The second brings an Airflow environment to an API sequence. The third names a plausible connector story rather than the orchestration property being asked for.
What is Cloud Scheduler, in relation to the orchestrators?
It fires HTTP, Pub/Sub and App Engine targets on a schedule and knows nothing about what happens after. The first makes it an orchestrator. The third invents a relationship with the event bus. The fourth describes Cloud Tasks from the previous lesson.
Why choose Application Integration over Workflows for a piece of enterprise integration?
The connector library is the documented reason - it is the work being avoided. The second describes Workflows' shape rather than this one's. The third and fourth both claim capabilities that Workflows demonstrably has.

Practice about 12 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

An insurer has three automations to build. A nightly reconciliation moves data between tables and depends on an earlier extract finishing. A claim submission calls four internal services in order and pauses for an underwriter's decision. And a customer record has to stay synchronised with a third-party customer relationship system and an on-premises database.

Choose a product for each, say what starts each one, and name the one where choosing wrongly costs the most and why.

Hint

Three requirements and three different orchestrators, one each. What starts each one is a separate question with three different answers as well.

Show the worked solution
  1. The nightly reconciliation. Cloud Composer. It is a data pipeline with a dependency between tasks, which is what a directed acyclic graph expresses and what Airflow's ecosystem is built for. Started by its own schedule inside the orchestrator.
  2. The claim submission. Workflows. Four service calls in order with a pause for a human is the callback pattern, and step-level error handling means one failing service does not collapse the run. Started by a caller - the submission itself - rather than by a clock.
  3. The customer synchronisation. Application Integration. Two external systems, one of them somebody else's product and one on premises, is the connector case. Started by an event or a schedule depending on how fresh the copy must be.
  4. Name the expensive mistake. The third. Choosing a code-shaped orchestrator there means writing and maintaining an authenticated, paginated, rate-limited client for each system, and then owning those clients as the vendors change their APIs. The other two mistakes cost an unnecessary environment or an awkward definition; this one costs an ongoing engineering commitment.
  5. Note what none of them is. Cloud Scheduler. It could start the first or the third and it sequences nothing, so it is never the answer to "which orchestrator".

Sanity check. Your three products should be three different ones, and the starter should have been named separately from the orchestrator in each case. If the scheduler appeared as one of the three products, re-read the first figure.

Your teacher. When somebody proposes an orchestrator, ask an agent what the steps call. A list of internal APIs, a list of data tasks and a list of third-party systems select three different products, and the list is usually easier to produce than the argument about tooling.
