Spanner is one database that a region no longer bounds

working 7 min Capabilities globally-distributed-sql, graph-database and ledger. Sourced from Spanner and Spanner Graph.

The one-minute version

  • Spanner is a relational database with global transactional consistency, ordered by the platform's TrueTime mechanism and deployed through multi-region configurations.
  • It is billed in processing units, so capacity is a dial rather than an instance size - elastic, and still provisioned.
  • Spanner Graph puts a property graph with GQL queries on the same infrastructure. Google Cloud ships no standalone graph database, and this is where the capability genuinely lives.
  • There is no managed ledger product. The documented substitutes are object retention lock for immutability and Spanner for transactional history.
  • Read this page as one service answering three questions, and note that two of them have no product of their own anywhere on the platform.

1. What "globally consistent" is a claim about

The mechanism behind the ordering is Google's TrueTime, and the Spanner overview is where its behaviour is documented. What matters at design time is the shape of the promise rather than the clockwork: a workload that genuinely needs one authoritative answer in several regions at once has exactly one relational option here, and workloads that do not need it are paying for a guarantee they will not use.

2. Processing units, and why the billing unit is a design fact

The practical consequence is a planning one. Capacity is a number you own and adjust, so a workload with a sharp daily peak either carries the peak all day or has something that moves the dial. That is a different exercise from sizing a Cloud SQL instance once, and it is the reason a Spanner design usually starts from a traffic shape rather than from a schema.

3. The two rows filled from inside

Spanner Graph is the honest case for the first reading: it is a property graph with GQL over the same infrastructure, so a graph workload gets a real graph engine. The trade-off it carries is inherited rather than its own - the graph arrives with Spanner's capacity model and Spanner's cost floor attached, whether or not the graph needed either.

The ledger row is the harder one, because a ledger requirement usually has two halves. The immutability half is answered by object retention lock, which module 04 introduced and which the Cloud Storage introduction documents. The append-only transactional-history half is answered by modelling it in Spanner. What is missing is the single product that would have done both with the verification built in, and a design that needs it assembles it.

The reframing Three questions reach Spanner and only one of them is a Spanner question. Global consistency is what the service is for. Graph is a capability it genuinely provides because nothing else here does. Ledger is a requirement it half-answers, and the other half comes from object storage. Knowing which of the three you are in decides whether Spanner's cost model is a price worth paying.

Answer from memory before revealing.

What does a Spanner multi-region configuration promise that a read replica in a second region does not?
The guarantee is about what a reader is allowed to see, and that is exactly what an asynchronous replica cannot promise. The first and third are true of ordinary replication too, so neither separates the two. The fourth invents a capability nobody offers and would defeat the point of one database.
A graph workload needs a property graph on Google Cloud. What does the verified inventory record?
There is no standalone graph product, and the capability is genuinely delivered by Spanner Graph. The first option is the over-reading this course warns about - no product is not the same as no capability. The second and third name real services that the inventory does not record as answering this row.
Why does Spanner appear under the empty serverless relational row as a near answer rather than as the product that fills it?
Elastic capacity that never reaches zero is the whole distinction. The second and third invent constraints the inventory does not record. The fourth reverses the billing model - processing units buy capacity, not queries.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A payments team states three requirements. Balances must read the same on two continents with no window in which an old value is served. Every balance change must be retained in an order that cannot be rewritten. And the fraud team wants to traverse relationships between accounts, devices and payees.

Assign each requirement to what serves it, name the requirement that no single product covers, and say what the whole design inherits from the choice.

Hint

Two of the three are served by the same service. The third is the one where the inventory records an absence, and its answer has two halves in two different modules.

Show the worked solution
  1. Assign the consistency requirement. One authoritative answer across continents with no stale window is the definition of Spanner's multi-region guarantee. Nothing else on the relational row offers it.
  2. Assign the traversal requirement. Accounts, devices and payees with relationships between them is a property graph, and Spanner Graph provides it in GQL on the same infrastructure - so it needs no second database and no export.
  3. Name the uncovered requirement. The immutable ordered history is the ledger row, and there is no managed ledger product. It is assembled: the transactional history is modelled in Spanner, and immutability of the retained records comes from object retention lock in Cloud Storage.
  4. State what the design inherits. All three answers now sit on Spanner's capacity model. Capacity is provisioned in processing units and never falls to zero, so the fraud graph and the balance store share one bill and one scaling dial - a benefit while the two traffic shapes agree, and a constraint when they diverge.

Sanity check. Two requirements should land on one service and the third should need two services from two modules. If your answer put the graph on a separate graph database, look again at what the inventory records - there is not one to put it on.

Your teacher. If you are unsure whether a workload really needs global consistency, ask an agent what breaks if a reader in the second region sees a value one second old. A design that shrugs at that answer does not need Spanner's guarantee and should not be paying for its capacity model.

Primary source to go deeper

The Spanner overview documents TrueTime, the multi-region configurations and the processing-unit capacity model, and it is the page to read before committing a design to any of the three. Spanner Graph is the reference for the graph half.
