What exists, what may exist, and what has drifted

working 8 min Capabilities resource-graph-query, policy-as-code, config-drift-assessment and compliance-pack. Sourced from Cloud Asset Inventory and Security Command Center.

The one-minute version

  • Cloud Asset Inventory answers what exists: organisation-wide asset metadata with a search API, and snapshots and feeds exported to BigQuery, Cloud Storage and Pub/Sub.
  • Custom organisation policy constraints answer what may exist. They are written in CEL and enforced at resource creation, and they are this platform's policy-as-code plane.
  • There is no standalone policy product of the kind other clouds sell. The capability lives inside the organisation policy service that lesson 0102 introduced.
  • The Security Command Center security posture service answers what has drifted: benchmark postures, with findings raised when configuration moves outside them. It is enabled automatically when Security Command Center is activated at the organisation level.
  • Security Command Center also produces compliance reports against CIS, ISO and NIST benchmarks, with continuous export to Pub/Sub and BigQuery.

2. What may exist

The verified inventory is explicit that no standalone policy-as-code product exists here of the kind some other clouds sell. The capability is delivered by the organisation policy service, and custom constraints are the mechanism. That is a packaging difference rather than a missing capability, and lesson 0102's warning still applies: a descendant can override unless the policy admin role is withheld below the organisation node.

3. What has drifted

This row was wrongly recorded as an absence during the research behind this course and corrected on review: drift detection does exist here, as a named first-party service. A related claim about Terraform drift detection in Infrastructure Manager was checked, could not be verified, and was removed rather than softened. Nothing about it is stated in this course.

4. Proving it to somebody else

The reframing Three questions and three products, and they are not substitutes. What exists is Cloud Asset Inventory, and no other answer is complete. What may exist is a custom constraint, and it is the only one of the three that prevents rather than reports. What has drifted is the posture service, and its value is in the finding you accept as much as in the one you fix.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

An automation must react within seconds whenever a resource of a certain kind is created anywhere in the organisation. What supplies that?
A feed is the near-real-time path and Pub/Sub is what an automation subscribes to. The first is the history mechanism and is a night late. The third is a report. The fourth would prevent the creation rather than react to it, which is a different requirement.
What does the inventory record about a standalone policy-as-code product on Google Cloud?
The capability lives inside the organisation policy service as CEL custom constraints. The second names a detection product. The third names a claim the research could not verify and removed. The fourth adds an absence to the packaging difference and is the over-reading this course warns about.
What is the difference in outcome between a custom constraint and a drift finding?
Prevention against detection is the whole difference, and it decides whether a bad resource ever existed. The first treats both as reporting. The second invents a scope difference. The third reverses which one is evaluated at creation.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

An auditor asks three questions. How many storage buckets exist across the organisation and who owns each. How the company prevents a bucket being created without a required configuration. And how the company would know if somebody changed one afterwards. The team currently answers all three with a spreadsheet updated quarterly.

Replace the spreadsheet with a product for each question, say which answer is prevention rather than reporting, and state what the team should keep from the spreadsheet.

Hint

The three questions are the three sections of this page, in order. The last part of the question is about something none of the three products holds for you.

Show the worked solution
  1. What exists. Cloud Asset Inventory. The search API answers the auditor in the room, and a snapshot exported to BigQuery gives the history the spreadsheet was pretending to be.
  2. What may exist. A custom organisation policy constraint, written in CEL against the bucket resource, enforced at creation. A bucket without the required configuration is refused rather than created and later reported.
  3. What has drifted. The Security Command Center security posture service, with a posture defining the benchmark, raising a finding when a bucket moves outside it. It is on automatically once Security Command Center is activated at the organisation level.
  4. Name the prevention. Only the second. The other two tell you about resources that already exist, which is exactly why the second is worth the effort of writing an expression.
  5. Keep the human part. Ownership. Asset Inventory records what exists and where, and who is accountable for it is a label your organisation applies - which is the metadata standard from lesson 0901 doing work here. That is the column of the spreadsheet no product hands you.

Sanity check. Exactly one of your three answers should prevent, and your last answer should be metadata rather than a product. If ownership came out of Asset Inventory automatically, ask which field records that a person is accountable - the answer is the one you put there.

Your teacher. When a governance requirement arrives, ask an agent whether it wants prevention or evidence. Prevention is a custom constraint and costs an expression; evidence is a feed or a posture and costs a place to keep the output. Requirements that ask for both are two pieces of work, not one.
