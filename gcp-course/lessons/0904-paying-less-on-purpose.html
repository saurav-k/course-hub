One commitment bets on a machine, the other bets on a number

working 8 min Capabilities commitment-discounts and cost-advisory. Sourced from committed use discounts and Recommender.

The one-minute version

  • Sustained use discounts apply automatically for some machine families and need no commitment at all. They are the discount you never have to ask for.
  • A committed use discount trades a one-year or three-year commitment for a lower rate, and it comes in two shapes.
  • A resource-based commitment is against a machine family in a region, and it only pays off if the committed processor and memory keep running.
  • A spend-based commitment is against an hourly amount and flexes across the services it covers, so it survives a workload changing shape.
  • Active Assist is the recommendation family and Recommender is its API. Recommendations carry an estimated impact and an apply path, and can be listed, applied and exported programmatically.

1. The discount nobody applies for

2. What each commitment is a bet on

The term is one or three years either way, so both shapes are decisions made against a forecast. The honest question before signing either is not "how much do we spend now" but "what will still be true in three years" - and the two shapes answer that question with different amounts of tolerance for being wrong.

3. The machine that reads the past for you

The reframing Work the three in order and the answers stop being guesses. What is already discounted automatically sets the baseline. What is running and should not be is Active Assist, and removing it is free. What will still be running in a year or three is the only part that justifies a commitment - and a commitment bought before the second step is a discount on waste.

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

A team commits to a machine family in one region, then re-architects onto a different family. What happens?
A resource-based commitment is against a machine family in a region and pays off only while the committed processor and memory keep running. The first three all describe flexibility, which is exactly what the other shape is for and this one is not.
Which discount needs no commitment and no request?
Sustained use is automatic for the machine families it covers and sits underneath the commitments. The first and fourth are commitments by definition. The second is advice about buying one, which is the opposite of needing no commitment.
What makes a recommendation from Active Assist actionable rather than merely informative?
The estimated impact makes it sortable and the apply path makes it executable, and both are available programmatically. The first invents automatic application. The third confuses advice with a guardrail. The fourth narrows the family, which spans security, performance, reliability and manageability too.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A company spends steadily on virtual machines and is under pressure to cut cloud cost this quarter. A vendor conversation has produced a proposal for a three-year resource-based commitment covering the current fleet. The platform team knows a container migration is planned for next year, which will move much of that fleet onto a different runtime.

Assess the proposal against what is planned, propose an order of work, and say which single step should happen before any commitment is signed and why.

Hint

The proposal names one shape of commitment and the plan describes exactly the event that shape is worst at. One of the three mechanisms on this page costs nothing and changes the number the commitment should be sized against.

Show the worked solution
  1. Assess the fit. A resource-based commitment is against a machine family in a region and pays off only while that processor and memory keep running. The container migration is a plan to stop running exactly that, so a three-year term would keep billing for two years after the workload it covers has gone.
  2. Name the better shape, if any. A spend-based commitment is against an hourly amount and flexes across the services it covers, so it tolerates the workload changing shape. Whether it covers the destination runtime is the question to check on the vendor page before signing, and it is the question that decides this.
  3. Do the free work first. Active Assist, through Recommender: idle resources and machine-type rightsizing. Both reduce the fleet without any commitment, and both are recommendations with an estimated impact so they can be sorted and applied programmatically.
  4. Check the baseline. Sustained use discounts already apply automatically to some machine families. The saving a commitment offers has to be measured against the rate after that, not against list.
  5. Name the step that must come first. The rightsizing and idle cleanup. A commitment is sized against a forecast, and buying one before removing waste commits three years of spending to resources the team already knows it does not need.

Sanity check. Your ordering should put the free work before the bet, and your assessment should reject the term rather than the idea of committing. If the three-year resource-based commitment survived your analysis, re-read what the migration does to the committed family.

Your teacher. Before any commitment discussion, ask an agent for the current Active Assist idle-resource and rightsizing recommendations with their estimated impacts. The total is the amount of the fleet that should not be in the forecast at all, and it is usually larger than the discount being negotiated.
