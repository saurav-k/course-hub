An HA VPN tunnel is quoted in packets, not in bits

frontier 9 min Capabilities site-to-site-vpn, dedicated-interconnect, cross-cloud-interconnect, on-prem-extension, vmware-stack-hosting and client-vpn. Sourced from HA VPN and Dedicated Interconnect.

The one-minute version

  • HA VPN reaches a data centre over IPsec, and its 99.99% availability SLA depends on being built with two interfaces and two tunnels as documented. Classic VPN is single-tunnel legacy and should be retired.
  • The tunnel's capacity is a packet quota, not a bandwidth figure: 250,000 packets per second combined in and out, throttled at the limit.
  • Dedicated Interconnect is physical circuits of 10, 100 or 400 Gbps in a colocation facility, and the link type cannot be changed after creation. Partner Interconnect covers 50 Mbps to 50 Gbps where colocation is impractical.
  • Cross-Cloud Interconnect links directly to another cloud provider's fabric on 10G or 100G ports.
  • There is no managed client VPN. The nearest paths are Identity-Aware Proxy TCP forwarding for SSH and RDP, and third-party VPN servers you run yourself.

1. Two ways down, both speaking BGP

2. The quota that is not a bandwidth

The quota is combined ingress and egress, and traffic is throttled at the limit rather than being refused. Capacity planning therefore starts from the packet rate, and a chatty protocol of small messages exhausts a tunnel long before a file transfer would.

3. The interconnect family, and the decision that is permanent

The verified inventory records availability SLAs that differ by interconnect topology and flags the exact percentages as needing to be read from the live pages, so none is printed here. HA VPN's 99.99% figure is recorded as verified and applies only to the documented two-interface, two-tunnel design - a single tunnel does not carry it. Read the current SLA on the interconnect documentation before a design depends on a number.

4. What sits on the ground, and the two rows with nothing in them

VMware Engine is the odd one out because it runs inside a Google region rather than on your premises: it is the full VMware Cloud Foundation stack on bare metal, with HCX migration waves and stretched clusters across zones. It belongs on this page because it is the answer to "we are not rewriting these workloads", which is the same question the hybrid links usually arrive with. The router appliance spoke is how a third-party SD-WAN appliance joins the hub from the previous lesson - it runs as a Compute Engine instance and peers with Cloud Router.

The reframing Three questions in order, and only the last one is about the cloud. Where is your equipment decides VPN against Partner against Dedicated. What shape is your traffic decides whether a tunnel's packet budget is enough. What are you unwilling to rewrite decides whether the answer is a link at all, or a VMware estate inside the region.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

Two workloads share an HA VPN tunnel and get very different throughput. What explains it?
A fixed packet rate multiplied by different packet sizes gives different bandwidths, which is the whole point of the quota being stated in packets. The first invents a scheduling rule. The second describes an overhead that would not produce a threefold difference. The third confuses distance with capacity.
Which Dedicated Interconnect decision cannot be revisited after the link exists?
The link type is fixed at creation and everything carved on top of it is adjustable. The first is exactly the adjustable part. The third is a configuration object. The fourth is a real commitment, and it is not the one the documentation flags as unchangeable.
Staff need to reach private machines for SSH from their laptops. What does the inventory record?
The row is an absence with a documented substitute, and Identity-Aware Proxy TCP forwarding is that substitute. The first contradicts the recorded lack of client-to-gateway support. The second invents the product. The fourth is the over-reading this course warns about - the need is met, by a different model.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1frontier

A retailer must connect a data centre to Google Cloud. The traffic is a message bus with an average packet size near 300 bytes, sustained at about 400,000 packets per second in each direction. The organisation has no colocation presence and its network provider does. A separate requirement is that staff reach private machines for SSH from home.

Test whether one HA VPN tunnel carries the traffic, show the arithmetic, choose a link, and answer the staff-access requirement.

Hint

The quota is combined in both directions, so add the two directions before comparing. The last part is answered by an absence and its substitute rather than by a product.

Show the worked solution
  1. Add the two directions. The quota of 250,000 packets per second is combined ingress and egress. The workload is 400,000 each way, so the demand is 800,000 packets per second.
  2. Compare against one tunnel. 800,000 ÷ 250,000 = 3.2, so a single tunnel is short by a factor of more than three and would be throttled. Note that the bandwidth looks modest - 800,000 × 300 × 8 is about 1.9 Gbps - which is exactly the trap: the bit rate looks easy and the packet rate does not.
  3. Choose the link. No colocation presence and a provider who has one points at Partner Interconnect, whose attachments run from 50 Mbps to 50 Gbps. That removes the packet-rate ceiling from the design rather than working around it with more tunnels.
  4. Answer the staff requirement. There is no managed client VPN. Identity-Aware Proxy TCP forwarding reaches SSH on machines with no external address, which is the documented substitute and is also the answer to the bastion row - module 11 covers it. A third-party VPN server run on your own instances is the other option and it is yours to operate.

Sanity check. Your packet arithmetic should have failed the tunnel while the bandwidth arithmetic passed it comfortably - if both agreed, check that you doubled the rate for the combined quota. And the staff requirement should not have been solved with the site-to-site link, which carries no client support at all.

Your teacher. When someone sizes a tunnel, ask an agent for the average packet size of the traffic, not just its throughput. A number in bits per second is not enough information to answer the question this quota actually asks.
