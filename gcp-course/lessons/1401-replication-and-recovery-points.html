Every replication has a recovery point, and they are not close together

working 7 min Capability cross-region-replication. Sourced from Cloud Storage availability and durability.

The one-minute version

  • No single product owns cross-region replication here. The capability is composite, delivered by three different mechanisms in three different services.
  • Those three are dual-region object storage with turbo replication, database multi-region configurations, and asynchronous disk replication.
  • They do not give the same guarantee. A synchronous mechanism has effectively no window; an asynchronous one always has one, and the size of it is the recovery point.
  • The only published figure in this area that the research behind this course records is turbo replication's recovery point of roughly fifteen minutes for a dual-region bucket.
  • So a resilience requirement has to be stated as a number of minutes of acceptable loss, because "replicated to another region" describes several very different promises.

1. Three mechanisms, one row

2. The one distinction that matters

Lesson 0402 made the same point at the block layer and lesson 0501 at the database layer: synchronous means the second copy is not behind, and asynchronous means it is, by an amount that is the whole conversation. The storage availability page is where the object-storage figure lives.

3. Writing the requirement so it can be met

The reframing Replication is not a property an estate has or lacks. It is a window per mechanism, and a design has as many windows as it has mechanisms. The number that matters is the largest one in the path of a business process, and finding it means reading each service's mechanism rather than a diagram with arrows between two regions.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

Why does the inventory record cross-region replication as one composite row?
Three mechanisms in three services provide it, and no product owns the capability. The first invents a central product. The third turns a packaging observation into an absence. The fourth over-generalises from one of the three.
A requirement says no writes at all may be lost when a region fails. What follows?
An asynchronous window shrinks and does not disappear, so no amount of tuning reaches zero. The first accepts fifteen minutes of loss against a requirement of none. The second treats the row as one guarantee. The third is the tuning mistake this page exists to prevent.
What is the first thing to ask when somebody says a system must survive losing a region?
Without that number every mechanism satisfies the sentence as spoken. The second and third are real questions that come after it. The fourth chooses the mechanism before the requirement that would select one exists.

Practice about 12 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

An order process writes to three places: order records in a globally consistent database, uploaded documents in a dual-region bucket with turbo replication enabled, and a working volume on a machine using asynchronous disk replication to a second region. The business states that losing a single confirmed order is unacceptable.

Give the recovery point of each of the three, identify which one decides what the business actually loses, and say what must change.

Hint

Three mechanisms and three different answers. The one that decides is not the one with the best guarantee.

Show the worked solution
  1. The order records. A globally consistent database with a multi-region configuration is synchronous, so a committed order is not behind in the second region. Effectively no window.
  2. The documents. A dual-region bucket with turbo replication has a recovery point of about fifteen minutes, which is the one figure the research records. Recent uploads may not be in the second region.
  3. The working volume. Asynchronous disk replication has a real window, and no single figure for it is printed here - it has to be read from the mechanism's own documentation.
  4. Identify what decides. Whichever of the three the confirmed order depends on, and it is the largest window in that path. If the order is confirmed only once its document is stored, the fifteen-minute window is the business's exposure, however good the database's guarantee is. The strongest mechanism does not raise the weakest.
  5. Say what must change. Either the process, so that a confirmation depends only on the synchronous write, or the mechanism, so that the document is not on an asynchronous path before the order is confirmed. "Unacceptable to lose one" is a requirement of zero, and only the synchronous mechanism offers that.

Sanity check. Your answer should identify a weakest link rather than an average, and the fix should change either the process or the mechanism rather than the tuning. If the conclusion was that the estate is replicated and therefore safe, re-read which write the confirmation depends on.

Your teacher. For any business process, ask an agent to list every place it writes and the replication mechanism of each. The largest window in that list is the process's real recovery point, and it is almost never the one the architecture diagram suggests.
