An instance is stamped from an image, and a fleet is stamped from a template

working 9 min Capabilities vm-images and instance-template. Sourced from Google's images documentation.

The one-minute version

  • An image is a boot disk you can stamp copies of. Google publishes public ones and you capture custom ones.
  • An image family is a versioned name over a series of images, so a build can refer to a family and keep receiving newer versions.
  • A machine image is a different object from a disk image: it bundles the whole instance configuration together with its data.
  • An instance template is a reusable blueprint that fleets and bulk creation stamp from, and it is immutable once created.
  • Those last two facts pull against each other. A family is a moving name and a deterministic template pins exact resources, so you choose between drift you did not ask for and staleness you must manage.

1. The stamping chain

Compute Engine images come in two supplies. Google publishes and maintains public images, and you capture custom ones from a disk you have prepared. Both are organised into image families, which are versioned names rather than single artefacts, and the platform provides operating-system image lifecycle management around them. Google also offers an image-building pipeline, so hardened images can be produced as a repeatable job rather than by hand.

2. What a family is actually for

That double edge is the point of this page. Naming a family in a build is a maintenance decision: you have chosen to receive whatever the family holds when the build next runs. Naming one exact image version is the opposite decision: the build is reproducible and it stops receiving patches until somebody moves it.

3. The template, and why immutability is the feature

Instance templates are the reusable blueprints that managed instance groups and bulk creation stamp from, and they are immutable once created. A change is therefore always a new template. That is what the fleet page builds on, and it is also the reason a Google Cloud fleet update reads as a series of small immutable artefacts rather than as an edit history.

The reframing A template can be written two ways and the difference is not cosmetic. A template that names an image family stamps whatever the family holds at stamp time, so two members created a month apart may differ. A deterministic template pins exact resources, so every member it ever stamps is identical. Pick by which failure you would rather debug: an unexplained difference between two members, or a fleet that quietly stopped receiving operating-system patches.

4. Choosing between them without hand-waving

The two failures are not equally likely in every setting. A fleet that is rebuilt often - a stateless web tier rolled forward every week - has little exposure to staleness and a lot of exposure to drift, because members are created constantly and any family movement lands between two of them. A fleet that is rebuilt rarely has the opposite exposure. The honest rule is that whichever risk you leave open must be managed somewhere else: drift by pinning and rebuilding on a schedule you own, staleness by a job that moves the pin.

Custom images are not free of the same question one level down. An image you captured is a point in time, so a fleet pinned to a custom image is pinned to whatever was patched into it on the day it was captured. Building images through a pipeline rather than by hand is what keeps that date from drifting quietly into the past, because a pipeline can be re-run and a hand-built image usually is not.

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

An instance template needs a change to its machine type. What does the platform require?
Instance templates are immutable, so every change is a new template. The first two options assume an editable blueprint, which is the assumption that makes rollback impossible. The fourth is wrong for a different reason: one template can feed several groups and bulk creation as well.
Two members of one fleet were created a month apart and differ in their operating-system patch level. What most likely explains it?
A family is a versioned name, so a template pointing at one stamps whatever the family holds at stamp time. The second option describes something the platform does not allow. The third confuses placement with content. The fourth attributes patching to a fleet controller, which stamps members rather than maintaining them.
A team wants a particular configured instance back exactly as it was, data included. Which object is that?
Machine images bundle the instance configuration with its data, which is exactly the whole-instance recovery case. A custom disk image captures a disk rather than the instance around it. A template describes a shape and carries no data at all. The fourth option describes a family, which organises images and does not hold instances.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

Two fleets exist. Fleet A is a stateless web tier rebuilt weekly. Fleet B is a licensed appliance rebuilt roughly twice a year.

Decide for each fleet whether its template should name an image family or pin an exact image, and name the specific operational job each choice creates.

Hint

Ask which of the two risks each fleet is naturally exposed to, then remember that the risk you do not take is the one you have to manage by hand.

Show the worked solution
  1. Name the two risks. A family exposes you to drift - members created at different moments differ. A pin exposes you to staleness - members stop receiving newer images until somebody moves the pin.
  2. Place fleet A. Weekly rebuilds mean members are created constantly, so any family movement lands inside the fleet. Pin the image, so a week's members are identical and a regression can be traced to one artefact.
  3. Name fleet A's job. Pinning creates the job of moving the pin - a scheduled bump of the template to a newer image, which is a small task and must actually exist, or the weekly fleet is running last quarter's image.
  4. Place fleet B and name its job. Twice-yearly rebuilds give little drift exposure and a lot of staleness exposure, so naming a family is reasonable. The job that creates is verification: because the image can change between rebuilds, the rebuild is the moment to test rather than to assume, and a licensed appliance is exactly the kind of thing an unannounced image change disturbs.

Sanity check. Each fleet should have ended with a job attached, and the two jobs should be different. If either fleet came out with no follow-up work, the choice was read as a preference rather than as a trade - both options leave one risk open, and the open risk is the job.

Your teacher. If the family-against-pin choice still feels like a coin toss, ask an agent to describe what a rollback looks like under each one. The answers are noticeably different lengths, and that difference is the decision.
