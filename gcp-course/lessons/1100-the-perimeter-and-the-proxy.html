A valid credential is not enough to get the data out

frontier 8 min Capabilities service-perimeter, zero-trust-app-access, bastion and conditional-access. Sourced from VPC Service Controls and Identity-Aware Proxy.

The one-minute version

  • VPC Service Controls draws a service perimeter around managed APIs and blocks data leaving it even when the credential is valid. Google documents it as a layer independent of IAM.
  • Perimeters support a dry-run mode, so the blocking can be observed before it is enforced, and every block is recorded in the Policy Denied audit log from lesson 1001.
  • Identity-Aware Proxy is the other gate: context-aware access in front of web applications and, through TCP forwarding, in front of SSH and RDP on machines with no public address.
  • That TCP forwarding is why there is no bastion product here - the capability is delivered without a jump host to run and patch.
  • There is no single conditional-access policy engine. The capability is assembled from Access Context Manager access levels applied through IAM conditions and through the proxy.

1. A perimeter around the API, not around the network

Two properties make a perimeter usable rather than terrifying. It supports a dry-run mode, so an estate can see what would be blocked before anything is, and its blocks land in the Policy Denied audit log - the stream lesson 1001 flagged as billed and thirty days by default. If a perimeter is a control you rely on, its evidence needs a sink.

2. A proxy in front of the door

The branding here has moved: the enterprise offering around this was BeyondCorp Enterprise and now appears as Chrome Enterprise Premium. The mechanism is what to remember rather than the name.

3. Conditional access, assembled

The reframing Three independent questions, and a design usually needs all three. May this principal act is IAM. May this data leave is the perimeter, and it is judged separately even for a perfectly valid credential. Should this connection reach the application at all is the proxy. Answering one of the three well and calling it secure is the shape of most of the incidents this module exists to prevent.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

A principal holds a valid IAM binding and tries to copy data out of a perimeter. What happens?
The perimeter is an independent layer and blocking a valid credential is exactly what it is for, with the block landing in the Policy Denied stream. The first denies the layer. The third invents a delegation. The fourth turns an enforcement control into a detection one.
Why does Google Cloud ship no bastion host product?
TCP forwarding through the proxy delivers the capability without a machine to operate. The first is the over-reading of an absence. The second names a product recorded as absent in lesson 0704. The fourth reverses what Cloud NAT does, which is outbound only.
Where does an access level get enforced?
An access level is written once and referenced from three enforcement points, which is why no single console shows the whole picture. The second names the engine that does not exist. The third and fourth each pick one enforcement point and drop the other two.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1frontier

A team has to satisfy three requirements. An analyst with legitimate access to a dataset must not be able to copy it to a project outside the organisation. Engineers must reach production machines for SSH without those machines having public addresses. And an internal web tool must be reachable only from managed devices on a corporate network. The team's plan is to tighten IAM roles.

Explain why the plan cannot meet any of the three, assign a mechanism to each, and say what you would turn on first and why.

Hint

Every requirement describes something that a correctly held credential could still do. One of the three mechanisms has a mode that lets you deploy it without breaking anything.

Show the worked solution
  1. Explain the plan's failure. All three requirements are about a principal who is legitimately entitled. The analyst is allowed to read the dataset; the engineers are allowed to log in; the tool's users are allowed to use it. IAM decides entitlement, and none of these is a question about entitlement.
  2. Assign the first. A VPC Service Controls perimeter around the services holding the dataset. It blocks the copy out of the perimeter even though the credential is valid, and the block is recorded in Policy Denied.
  3. Assign the second. Identity-Aware Proxy TCP forwarding. The machines keep no public address and each connection is authorised, which also removes the bastion the team would otherwise have built.
  4. Assign the third. An Access Context Manager access level describing the device and network conditions, enforced through the proxy in front of the web tool.
  5. Say what goes first. The perimeter, in dry-run mode. It is the highest-risk control to enforce because it can break legitimate integrations nobody documented, and dry run shows exactly which ones before anything is blocked. Route its Policy Denied entries to a longer-retention bucket at the same time, since thirty days is the default and this is now a control you rely on.

Sanity check. Your three mechanisms should be three different products, and your first action should be reversible. If the answer began by enforcing a perimeter, ask which integration you have just broken - you do not know yet, and dry run is how you find out.

Your teacher. When a requirement uses the word "prevent", ask an agent whether the actor would have a valid credential. If the answer is yes, IAM is not the control being asked for, and the design needs one of the two gates on this page.
