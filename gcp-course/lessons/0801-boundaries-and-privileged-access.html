Module 08 · Identity and access · Lesson 0801

There is no maximum-permissions object, so a boundary is assembled

frontier 8 min Capabilities permission-boundary and privileged-access. Sourced from Principal Access Boundary policies and Privileged Access Manager.

The one-minute version

  • Google Cloud has no boolean maximum-permissions object on a principal. The capability exists and is assembled from IAM Conditions, deny policies and Principal Access Boundary policies.
  • A Principal Access Boundary policy defines the resources a principal is eligible to access, and binds to workforce and workload identity pools, Workspace domains, whole project, folder or organisation principal sets, and agent identities.
  • It is partial by design. A boundary policy blocks only the predefined permission list fixed by its enforcement version, and permissions outside that list pass untouched.
  • Privileged Access Manager grants roles just in time, time-boxed, with approval workflows, so an elevated role is a temporary binding rather than a standing one.
  • Policy Analyzer answers "who can do X", and Access Approval and Access Transparency govern access by Google's own personnel.

1. Three layers standing in for one object

2. The boundary that leaks by design

A boundary policy attaches to principal sets rather than to individuals: workforce identity pools, workload identity pools, Workspace domains, entire project, folder or organisation principal sets, and agent identities. That is what makes it useful at estate scale, and it is also why a mistake in one is expensive.

This is the point where a mental model imported from another cloud goes wrong quietly. A boolean permission boundary elsewhere is complete - a permission outside it cannot be exercised, whatever a grant says. A Principal Access Boundary policy is partial by construction: outside its enforcement version's list, the ordinary evaluation chain from lesson 0800 decides, and an allow binding anywhere on the ancestor chain wins.

3. Elevation with an expiry

4. Reading the estate, and watching the vendor

The reframing Write the ceiling down as three sentences rather than looking for one object. Which resources is this principal eligible for is the boundary policy, and its answer is only complete inside its enforcement version. What is subtracted is the deny policies. When does a grant apply is the conditions. Then, because no object holds the answer, ask Policy Analyzer rather than reasoning it out.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

A team applies a Principal Access Boundary policy and reports that the principals now cannot exceed it. What is wrong with that claim?
The coverage is fixed by the enforcement version, so permissions outside that list are unaffected. The first reverses the binding model - principal sets are exactly what it attaches to. The second denies enforcement entirely. The fourth reverses the order: eligibility gates before allows are considered.
What does Privileged Access Manager change about an elevated role?
The grant is an ordinary IAM binding with a time box and an approval in front of it. The second and third name the wrong objects entirely. The fourth describes conditions, which narrow an existing grant rather than creating an approved temporary one.
Which tool answers "who in this organisation can delete this bucket"?
Effective access is assembled from bindings across the ancestor chain plus denies, conditions and boundaries, which is exactly what Policy Analyzer is for. The first two are about Google's own personnel. The third is the mistake this whole module exists to correct - one resource's policy is never the full answer.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1frontier

A regulated team must guarantee that a contractor identity pool can never reach resources in the finance folder, and that any emergency elevation is approved and time-limited. An architect proposes a single Principal Access Boundary policy on the pool and describes it in the control document as "a hard ceiling on what contractors can do".

Assess the proposal, correct the control document's wording, add whatever else the guarantee needs, and design the emergency path.

Hint

The proposal is the right object aimed at the right principals, and the claim written about it is stronger than the object supports. The emergency requirement names two properties that one product supplies together.

Show the worked solution
  1. Assess the object. A boundary policy is correct here: it bounds the resources a principal is eligible for, and it binds to a workload or workforce identity pool, which is exactly the shape of "contractor identity pool".
  2. Correct the wording. "Hard ceiling on what contractors can do" is wrong twice. The policy bounds which resources rather than what actions, and it blocks only the permission list of its enforcement version. The defensible sentence is: contractors are ineligible for finance-folder resources for the permissions covered by enforcement version 4.
  3. Add what the guarantee needs. Deny policies for the specific permissions that must never be exercised, so subtraction does not depend on the boundary's coverage. Then an audit of allow bindings on the ancestor chain, because a grant at the organisation node still applies to anything the boundary does not cover.
  4. Design the emergency path. Privileged Access Manager: an entitlement with an approval workflow and a time box, so an emergency elevation is requested, approved, active and then gone. That satisfies both stated properties with one product and leaves no standing grant behind.
  5. Say how it is verified. Policy Analyzer, asked "who can do X" against the finance folder, because no single object holds the effective answer.

Sanity check. Your corrected sentence should name both limits - resources rather than actions, and the enforcement version - and your added layer should be the one that subtracts. If the control document still contains the word "never" without a qualifier, it is still claiming more than the platform provides.

Your teacher. When a document claims a principal "cannot" do something, ask an agent which object enforces that and what its coverage is. On this platform the honest answer usually has a qualifier in it, and a control document without that qualifier is the finding.
