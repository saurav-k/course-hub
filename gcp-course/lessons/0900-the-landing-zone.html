A landing zone is assembled, and only its compliance half is a product

working 8 min Capabilities landing-zone, service-catalog and cross-account-resource-sharing. Sourced from Assured Workloads and Service Catalog.

The one-minute version

  • There is no single landing-zone product. What exists are the pieces: the resource hierarchy, organisation policy, Shared VPC, and reference blueprints.
  • Assured Workloads is the part that is a service: compliance-regime folders whose controls are enforced by policy rather than described in a document.
  • Fabric FAST is the reference landing zone as Terraform blueprints, so the assembly is something you deploy rather than something you invent.
  • Service Catalog is how the guardrails reach the people who build: an administrator publishes approved solutions, and consumers deploy them into their own projects without editing the template.
  • There is no general resource-sharing service. Networks are shared through Shared VPC, and everything else by granting IAM on the owning project.

1. The pieces, and which of them is bought

Assured Workloads creates folders bound to a compliance regime, and the controls that regime requires are enforced by policy inside them. That is the difference from a written standard: the folder does not merely document the requirement, it refuses configurations that break it.

2. Guardrails only work if building inside them is easier

Service Catalog accepts Deployment Manager templates among its solution types, and Deployment Manager is a retiring product - module 12 carries the detail and the supported replacement. Publish Terraform configurations for anything new, and treat an existing Deployment Manager solution in a catalogue as something to migrate rather than to extend.

3. Sharing across the estate, and the row with no product

The reframing A landing zone on this platform is a decision record expressed in Terraform, not a service you enable. Three questions decide most of it: what shape is the folder tree, which constraints are enforced and where, and what can a team deploy without asking. Only a regulated estate adds a fourth, and that fourth is the one with a product behind it.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

What does the inventory record about a landing-zone product on Google Cloud?
There is no single product, and the capability is recorded against the service that embodies its enforced-controls part. The first calls a set of Terraform blueprints a managed service. The third over-reads the absence. The fourth names the distribution mechanism rather than the zone.
What makes an Assured Workloads folder different from a written compliance standard?
Enforcement is the difference - the folder refuses configurations the regime forbids. The first invents a reporting relationship. The second invents a scoping rule. The fourth would remove the identity axis, which no guardrail does.
A team needs to use a dataset owned by another team's project. How is that shared?
There is no general sharing service, so everything but networking is shared by granting IAM on the owning project. The first names the missing product. The second stretches Shared VPC beyond networking. The third confuses publishing a deployable solution with sharing a live resource.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A company is starting on Google Cloud with twelve delivery teams. Two of those teams handle data under a regulatory regime; the other ten do not. Leadership wants central control over networking, a short list of things teams may deploy on their own, and no per-project approvals for ordinary work.

Assemble the landing zone from the pieces on this page, say which single piece is a purchased service and why only two teams need it, and name the mechanism that makes "no per-project approvals" true rather than aspirational.

Hint

Five of the six pieces in the first figure come from earlier modules. The last part of the question is about supply rather than restriction.

Show the worked solution
  1. Shape the tree. Folders per team or per environment under the organisation, with projects beneath, so that inheritance has something to follow. This is module 01's hierarchy used as a design rather than as a fact.
  2. Set the constraints. Organisation policy at the organisation node for the estate-wide rules, remembering module 01's finding that a descendant can override unless the policy admin role is withheld below the root. The guardrail is the IAM design, not the constraint.
  3. Centralise the network. Shared VPC, a host project owning the subnets and each team's project attached as a service project. That satisfies central control over networking without joining twelve networks.
  4. Add the purchased piece. Assured Workloads folders for the two regulated teams only, because the enforced controls of a compliance regime are exactly what those two need and are constraints the other ten would have to work around for no benefit.
  5. Deploy the assembly. Fabric FAST blueprints, so the above is Terraform that is reviewed and applied rather than console work that nobody can reproduce.
  6. Make the last requirement true. Service Catalog. Approved solutions are published and shared with the folders, and teams deploy them into their own projects without editing the template - which is what removes the per-project approval instead of merely forbidding it.

Sanity check. Exactly one of your six pieces should be a purchased service and it should apply to two teams out of twelve. If your answer met the last requirement with a policy change, ask what the ten teams deploy on the day the policy is applied.

Your teacher. If a landing-zone effort is stalling, ask an agent what a delivery team can deploy today without asking anyone. An empty answer means the effort has built restrictions and no supply, and that is the half that gets routed around.
