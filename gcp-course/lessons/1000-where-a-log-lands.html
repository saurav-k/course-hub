Module 10 · Observability and audit · Lesson 1000

Every log entry passes through one router, into one of two buckets

working 9 min Capabilities log-store, telemetry-export-pipeline, log-analytics and error-reporting. Sourced from Cloud Logging storage and the routing overview.

The one-minute version

  • The Log Router sees every entry and decides, through inclusion and exclusion filters, what is stored and what is exported. Both decisions are made in one place.
  • Two buckets exist by default. _Required holds the always-on audit streams for 400 days, is not configurable, and carries no storage charge. _Default holds everything else for 30 days.
  • A project's _Default retention is configurable from 1 to 3,650 days. At the folder and organisation level it is fixed at 30 days, and the documented answer is a sink into a log bucket in a project.
  • Sinks land in Cloud Storage, BigQuery, Pub/Sub, another log bucket or a third party, and they differ in delay: Pub/Sub in seconds, BigQuery in minutes, Cloud Storage in batches.
  • Observability Analytics runs SQL over upgraded log buckets and now spans logs and traces, and Error Reporting groups exceptions out of the logs already flowing.

1. One router, two decisions

A sink defined at the organisation or folder level aggregates entries from every project beneath it, which is the standard estate-wide export pattern. The Pub/Sub sink is the path to an external security information and event management system, and it is the one to reach for when something has to react rather than report.

2. The retention picture, which is not symmetrical

Cost follows the same shape rather than a price list. The required bucket carries no storage charge. Ordinary ingestion is billed per gibibyte after a free monthly allotment per project. Network logs vended by the platform are billed at a lower rate. And retaining entries past the default is itself a billed change, at a much smaller monthly rate per gibibyte. The quotas and retention page carries the current figures, which is where they belong.

3. Asking questions of what you kept

Error Reporting is the third surface and it costs nothing to adopt: it groups exceptions automatically out of logs that are already being ingested, with first and last seen tracking. It is read-only and raises no alerts of its own, so it tells you what is broken and never tells you when.

The reframing Three questions decide a logging design, and none of them is about which logs to collect. Which bucket does this land in decides whether it is free and how long it lives. Is that long enough for the investigation that will need it decides whether a sink is required. Does anything have to react decides whether the sink is Pub/Sub or something slower. Get those three right and the collection question becomes easy.

Answer from memory before revealing.

A security team wants a year of a stream that lands in the organisation-level _Default bucket. What do they do?
Folder and organisation _Default buckets are fixed at 30 days, and the documented answer is a sink into a project's bucket where retention is configurable. The first assumes it is adjustable. The second treats _Required as a destination you can choose. The fourth over-reads the limit into an absence.
Which sink destination suits an automation that must react within seconds?
Pub/Sub is the near-real-time path and is what a reacting consumer subscribes to. The second adds polling to a destination that lands in minutes. The third misstates Cloud Storage, whose files are batched. The fourth is a retention destination rather than a delivery one.
What does an exclusion filter on the Log Router change?
The router decides storage and export together, which is why a cost-saving filter also removes evidence. The first and third split a decision the router makes once. The fourth turns an ingestion control into a display preference.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

A security team is told to keep every configuration-changing action for a year, feed a third-party detection platform in near real time, and cut logging cost, which has grown with a chatty application. A junior engineer proposes one exclusion filter that drops the noisy application's entries at the organisation level.

Design the retention, design the feed, assess the proposed filter, and say which of the three requirements is already met by a default.

Hint

One requirement needs no work at all. The filter proposal is right in mechanism and dangerous in scope, and the reason is the second decision the router makes.

Show the worked solution
  1. Find the free requirement. Configuration-changing actions are the always-on audit stream, which lands in _Required and is kept 400 days at no storage charge. A year is 365, so the requirement is already met and needs no sink, no bucket and no budget.
  2. Design the feed. A sink at the organisation level, filtered to what the detection platform needs, with Pub/Sub as the destination. Organisation-level sinks aggregate every project beneath them, and Pub/Sub delivers in seconds, which is what "near real time" means here.
  3. Assess the filter. The mechanism is correct - exclusion filters are how ingestion cost is controlled. The scope is the problem: the router decides storage and export in the same place, so an organisation-wide exclusion also removes those entries from the sink feeding the detection platform.
  4. Correct it. Narrow the filter to the specific noisy log name and severity rather than the application as a whole, and check it against what the detection sink includes before applying it. Then confirm the audit streams are untouched, since they are the requirement with the longest memory and the one nobody notices losing.

Sanity check. One of the three requirements should have cost nothing, and your assessment should have rejected the filter's scope while keeping its mechanism. If the filter went in as proposed, ask what the detection platform stops seeing on the same day the bill improves.

Your teacher. Before writing an exclusion filter, ask an agent which sinks currently match the entries you are about to drop. The router applies one decision to both storage and export, so the answer to "what will this cost me in evidence" is a list you can read in advance.
