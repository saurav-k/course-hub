Module 06 · Agentic code review · Lesson 0620

No open reviewer found more than 62 of every 100 real bugs

working 10 min Recall and its caveats, recomputed from the same committed evaluation JSON as the previous page. · review-droid-benchmark

The one-minute version

  • Recall is the share of the real bugs present that a reviewer actually found. Across six reviewers on fifty pull requests it ran from 24.8% to 61.7%, and the best was still missing two of every five.
  • Recall did not rise with severity. The best-precision reviewer found 60.9% of the low-severity bugs and 47.2% of the high-severity ones.
  • The benchmark is run by a vendor that competes in it, and it touches the data at three points. One of those is that 31 golden bugs were added because that vendor's own tool found them.
  • So: a filter, never a gate. A green AI review means "nothing was found", and the honest reading of that is nothing more.

1. The ceiling, and it is low

Precision asks: of the comments it wrote, how many were right? Recall asks the harder question: of the real bugs that were there, how many did it mention at all? The second needs a golden set - somebody has to have gone through the pull requests by hand and written down every bug that was actually present - which is why almost nobody publishes it.

Notice what the two charts do together. The reviewer with the highest precision, at 96.8%, sits second on recall; the reviewer with the highest recall has the fourth-worst precision. Nobody escaped the trade, and the whole table is worth reading rather than the column a vendor quotes.

2. It is not better at the bugs that matter more

The intuitive defence of low recall is that a reviewer misses the trivia and catches the serious ones. The open data does not support it. Every golden bug carries a severity, so the miss rate can be split by it, and the split runs the wrong way.

This is not a surprise once you hold lesson 0610's anatomy. A finding is reachable when its site, trigger and consequence are all in the artefacts. Severity is a judgement about the consequence's cost, and cost is a fact about the business rather than the code. A severe bug is often severe precisely because the damage is far from the line.

3. Reading the benchmark against itself

The numbers above are the best available and they are still a vendor's benchmark. Factory publishes it, competes in it, and touches the data at three separate points. Each of those is documented in the repository rather than alleged here, and each is a question you should ask of any benchmark you are shown.

Two more caveats sit in the table above. The golden denominator differs per run - 157, 158, 167 and 171 across seven runs - so the recall column is not divided by one constant. And the run dates differ: the publisher's own runs are recorded in the review_droid_run_gpt_5p2_2026-01-28 directory while every competitor's sits in a directory dated a fortnight or more later, so the tools were not measured in the same window.

The honest limit, and it is this module's load-bearing one. The recall ceiling of 61.7% is the best figure in the only open head-to-head data there is, and that data is a vendor's. Precision across the six ran 55.3% to 96.8%, a 41.5-point spread, against a recall spread of 36.9 points - so the two spreads are comparable, and the difference that matters is not their width but that precision is settable and recall is not. A vendor precision headline is not a contradiction of any of this; it is an answer to a different question. Ask for both numbers, the golden set they were computed against, and who built it. If you get one number, you have been given a marketing figure. The Staff AI Engineer, lesson 0000 is the hub's full method for auditing a claim like this one.

4. So: a filter, never a gate

One configuration decision follows directly from the ceiling, and it is the one teams get wrong. If an AI review is a required status check, then a green tick means "the instrument that misses between two and three quarters of real bugs found nothing", and every reader of that pull request will read it as "reviewed". Lesson 0650 configures the gates so that never happens, and the rule is one line: your test suite is required, your AI review is not.

The other consequence is that recall is the number to try to move, and the levers are architectural rather than promptable. Lesson 0630 is what a reviewer is allowed to look at, which is the ceiling on what it could possibly find.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

What did splitting one reviewer's recall by bug severity show?
High-severity recall was 47.2% against 59.4% for medium and 60.9% for low, so the comforting story in the first option is the one the data refuses. The second is the same comfort in a stronger form, and the critical band held only three bugs. The fourth is simply false: every golden bug carries a severity, which is what makes the split possible.
Why should an AI review never be a required status check?
A required check publishes a claim, and this instrument's claim is only "nothing was found" at a recall between 24.8% and 61.7%. The second is untrue - the same job can both comment and report a check. The third describes a trigger ordering you configure, not a rule. The fourth invents a restriction: nothing stops you, which is exactly why the decision is yours.

Practice about 30 minutes, with your last five shipped defects

Practice 1working

You cannot compute recall on your own repository without a golden set, and you are not going to build one. Do the cheap version honestly instead.

Take the last five defects your team shipped to production. For each, reconstruct the pull request that introduced it and replay it past whichever reviewer you can run today.

Record how many of the five it mentions, and write one sentence per miss saying which of the three parts from lesson 0610 it could not have had.

Hint

Replay on a branch rather than reopening the original pull request, so you are not editing history that other people are reading. If the defect was fixed in the same file, check out the parent commit of the fix and open a pull request of the original change against it.

Show how to report the result without overclaiming
  1. Report the fraction, not a percentage. "Two of five" is a defensible sentence. "40% recall" implies a measurement, and five samples is not one - say so in the same breath.
  2. Classify each miss. Missing site means the defect was outside the diff, which is an architecture problem and lesson 0630's subject. Missing trigger means the defect needed a fact from outside the repository, and no tool fixes that. Missing consequence usually means the code was correct and the requirement was wrong, which is the intent job from 0600.
  3. Compare with the published ceiling. If your reviewer caught four of five, you probably chose easy defects - the open ceiling is 61.7% on hand-validated bugs, so a sample beating it badly is a sample, not a result.
  4. Write the decision sentence. "This reviewer catches roughly this share of defects of this kind, so it stays advisory and the suite stays required."

Sanity check. Expect between one and three of five, and expect at least one miss to be un-fixable by any configuration. If every miss looks fixable by a better prompt, you have not yet read the misses carefully - go back and ask what fact the reviewer would have needed and where that fact lives.

Your teacher. If the benchmark critique feels abstract, hand your agent the URL of any vendor's review benchmark and ask it four questions in order: who built the ground truth, who ran each tool and when, who decided a comment matched, and what the denominator was for each row. Then ask which of the four the page answers. Most answer one.
