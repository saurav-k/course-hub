The set language

EntryRead it aloud asWhere
x ∈ A Membership: x is one of the elements of the set A. The slashed form ∉ is "is not in". Part 1
A ⊆ B, and A = B by double inclusion Subset: every element of A is also in B. Two sets are equal when each contains the other, which is how every set equality in this course is proved. Part 1
A ∪ B = {x : x in A or x in B} Union, the event "at least one of the two", the whole of both discs. Part 1
A ∩ B = {x : x in A and x in B} Intersection, "both", the lens where the discs overlap. Part 1
Ac = {x ∈ U : x ∉ A} Complement, "not A", everything in the universe outside the disc. Part 1
A \ B = A ∩ Bc Difference, "A but not B", the crescent of A outside B. Part 1
Disjoint: A ∩ B = ∅ The two sets share no outcomes, drawn as discs that do not touch. The condition under which probabilities may be added. Part 1
(A ∪ B)c = Ac ∩ Bc and (A ∩ B)c = Ac ∪ Bc De Morgan's laws: a not over an or flips to an and, and a not over an and flips to an or. The same flip works over an indexed family of sets. Part 1
A × B = {(x, y) : x in A, y in B} Cartesian product, every ordered pair; sizes multiply. Repeating one set n times gives An, so three tosses are {H, T}3. Part 1
2A = all subsets of A, and |2A| = 2n when A has n elements Power set: every in-or-out choice over n elements, so the count is 2 to the n. Part 1

The sigma-field F

AxiomRead it aloud asWhere
Ω ∈ F The whole space is itself an event: "anything at all happened" is a legal question. Part 4
A ∈ F ⇒ Ac ∈ F Complement closure: the negation of a legal question is legal. Part 4
E₁, E₂, ... ∈ F ⇒ ∪ Eᵢ ∈ F Countable-union closure: the union of any sequence of events is an event, which is what the sigma in sigma-field promises. Part 4
∅ = Ωc; A ∩ B = (Ac ∪ Bc)c; A \ B = A ∩ Bc What the axioms buy: the empty set, intersections through De Morgan, and differences, all without extra axioms. Part 4

The probability measure P

AxiomRead it aloud asWhere
P : F → [0, 1] P is a function from the events to the interval from 0 to 1: the non-negativity clause and the ceiling at certainty. Part 5
P(∅) = 0 The impossible event weighs nothing. Part 5
P(Ω) = 1 Total mass: the certain event weighs exactly one, so every weight is a fraction of certainty. Part 5
A ∩ B = ∅ ⇒ P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) Additivity for disjoint events: with no overlap, weights add with nothing counted twice. The homework extends this to a countable sequence of disjoint events. Part 5
