The two descriptions

FormulaRead it aloud asWhere
X : Ω → R The definition. A random variable is a function from the sample space to the real line. Nothing in it is random: the function is deterministic and the randomness is in which outcome occurs. Part 2
pX(x) = P(X = x) The probability mass function, defined on the whole real line and zero off the support. X = x is shorthand for the event {ω : X(ω) = x}. Part 3
FX(x) = P(X ≤ x) The cumulative distribution function. Defined for every random variable, discrete or not, and always includes the threshold itself. Part 4
FX(x) = ∑i ∈ SX, i ≤ x pX(i) The discrete CDF as a running sum over the support up to the threshold. SX is the support, the range of the function. Part 4
FX(x) = ∫−∞x fX(z) dz The continuous case. X is continuous exactly when such a non-negative fX exists, and that function is the density. Part 11

The CDF theorem: four properties

PropertyWhat it forbids, and how it is proved
1. x ≤ y ⇒ FX(x) ≤ FX(y) The staircase never goes down. Proved by set containment: {X ≤ x} ⊆ {X ≤ y}, then the containment rule.
2. FX(x) → 0 as x → −∞ Proved with a decreasing sequence of thresholds, a shrinking family of events, an empty intersection, and continuity of probability.
3. FX(x) → 1 as x → ∞ The notes state it as "similarly" and give no proof. This course does not expand it further.
4. FX(x + ε) → FX(x) as ε ↓ 0 Right continuity. Same machine as property 2, but the intersection is {X ≤ x} rather than empty. This is why the filled dot at a jump sits on the upper tread.

All four are in Part 5, and none of the proofs uses discreteness anywhere, which is why they carry over to the continuous half of the lecture unchanged.

Four probabilities the CDF hands you

FormulaRead it aloud as
P(X > x) = 1 − FX(x)The upper tail. Just the complement rule.
P(X ∈ (a, b]) = FX(b) − FX(a)A half-open interval: a excluded, b included. Any other bracket shape needs a left limit.
P(X < x) = FX(x−)The left limit: the height of the tread just before the step, not the height at the step.
P(X = x) = FX(x) − FX(x−)The height of the jump. A value carries positive probability exactly when the CDF is discontinuous there.

All four are in Part 6. Every one of them is a difference of two "at most" quantities, which is the only question a CDF can answer directly.

What a density does and does not do

StatementWhat it means in practice
FX is continuous, and differentiable almost everywhere with F′ = fX Density and CDF are the same object, one integrated or differentiated. Almost everywhere matters: the uniform's CDF has two corners with no derivative.
∫−∞∞ fX(x) dx = 1 The total area is one. The notes add: the integral of the density is a probability, and the density itself is not.
P(a ≤ X ≤ b) = ∫ab fX(x) dx The working formula. Almost every question about a continuous variable is this one.
P(X = x) = 0 for every x And the notes go further: the probability of any countable set is zero. Stated without proof. Probability zero is not the same as impossible.

All four are in Part 11. A density is a probability per unit of x, so it may exceed 1 whenever the support is narrow.

Memorylessness

StatementDetail
P(X > s + t | X > t) = P(X > s) The property itself. Having already waited t buys nothing at all.
Geometric tail: P(X > s) = (1 − p)s The first s tosses were all tails. The step that discards the wait is the intersection collapsing, because X > s+t already implies X > t.
The geometric is the only memoryless discrete shape Asserted in the notes, not proved. Part 9.
The exponential is the only memoryless continuous shape Asserted in the notes with the words "prove it" beside it, and not proved there or here. Part 12.
Exponential CDF: FX(x) = 1 − e−λx For non-negative x, and 0 below. The only closed-form continuous CDF on this sheet apart from the uniform's ramp.
