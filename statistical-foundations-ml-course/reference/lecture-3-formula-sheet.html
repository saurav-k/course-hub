Rules the axioms force

FormulaRead it aloud asWhere
P(∅) = 0 The empty event has probability zero. It is disjoint from itself, so the add axiom forces it to equal twice itself. Part 1
P(Ac) = 1 − P(A) The complement rule. The probability A does not happen is one minus the probability it does. Part 1
A ⊆ B  ⇒  P(A) ≤ P(B) Containment. If every outcome of A is an outcome of B, A is never more likely than B. Part 1
P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B) The addition rule. Add the two events and subtract the overlap once, because adding counted it twice. Part 1

Many events, and what to do when the sum will not finish

FormulaRead it aloud asWhere
P(A1 ∪ ... ∪ An) = ∑ P(Ai) − ∑i<j P(Ai ∩ Aj) + ... + (−1)n+1 P(A1 ∩ ... ∩ An) Inclusion-exclusion. Add the singles, subtract the C(n, 2) pairs, add the C(n, 3) triples, alternating until the single n-way term. Every region ends up counted exactly once. Part 2
P(∪ Ai) ≤ ∑i P(Ai) The union bound, the level-1 truncation. A ceiling: a union is never more likely than the sum of its parts. Part 3
P(∪ Ai) ≥ ∑i P(Ai) − ∑i<j P(Ai ∩ Aj) The second Bonferroni inequality, levels 1 and 2. A floor: subtracting the pairwise overlaps dips below the truth. Part 3
P(∪ Ai) ≤ ∑i P(Ai) − ∑i<j P(Ai ∩ Aj) + ∑i<j<k P(Ai ∩ Aj ∩ Ak) The third Bonferroni inequality, levels 1 to 3. A ceiling again. Odd truncations are ceilings, even truncations are floors. Part 3

Conditioning, chaining, and turning a belief around

FormulaRead it aloud asWhere
P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B), for P(B) > 0 The conditional probability of A given B. The world shrinks to B; the numerator is A's footprint in it, the denominator is its size. Part 4
P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B | A) = P(B) × P(A | B) The multiplication rule, the conditional definition read backwards. Price the first stage, then the second given the first. Part 5
P(A ∩ B ∩ C) = P(A) × P(B | A) × P(C | A ∩ B) The chain rule for three events. Each factor conditions on everything before it. On a tree: multiply along the path. Part 5
P(A1 ∩ ... ∩ An) = P(A1) × P(A2 | A1) × ... × P(An | A1 ∩ ... ∩ An−1) The chain rule for n events, the notes' product from i = 2 to n of the conditional given all predecessors. Part 5
P(A) = ∑i P(Bi) × P(A | Bi), over a partition The law of total probability. The slices Bi are disjoint and cover everything; price A in each slice and add. The tree read as a sum. Part 6
P(Bi | A) = P(Bi) × P(A | Bi) / ∑j P(Bj) × P(A | Bj) Bayes rule. Posterior = prior × likelihood, divided by the evidence. The total-probability figure with the arrow reversed. Part 7
A, B independent  ⇔  P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B) Independence. The overlap equals the product of the singles; equivalently P(A | B) = P(A) when P(B) > 0. A numerical fact, invisible in any picture. Part 8
