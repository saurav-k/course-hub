Which tool, when

Every trap, one list

  1. Stopping at the convenient axiom. Problem 1's F2 passes complements perfectly and still fails. Tick every axiom or do not conclude.
  2. Proving with half a hypothesis. In problem 2, intersection membership means belonging to both fields; using one silently breaks the proof.
  3. Trusting a partial green board. Problem 3's impostor passes two axioms; additivity fails only on the right disjoint witness.
  4. Forgetting overlaps contain the triple. Every region fill starts by peeling P(A∩B∩C) off each pairwise number.
  5. Expanding carelessly. Problem 5's face four lives or dies on sign hygiene inside (1−P(A))(1−P(B)).
  6. Double-counting geometry. Problem 6's band counts corners twice if attacked directly; complement first.
  7. Losing the table. Problem 7's total of 43 must be demonstrated row by row; asserting invites silent errors.
  8. Complementing against the old universe. Inside problem 8's B-world, complements come from B, not Ω.
  9. Misreading "at least". Problem 9's B includes the all-six hands; "exactly five" alone is a different event.
  10. Refilling the urn. Without replacement, denominators shrink: 6/9, never 6/10.
  11. Assuming symmetry. Problem 11's die splits two-to-four; the even-split reflex lands on exactly the wrong 0.5.
  12. Declaring mutual too early. Three pairwise ticks never settle the triple; 1/4 against 1/8 is the standing refutation.

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

In flower language, what is "exactly two of E, F, G occur"?
Take everyone inside some circle, then delete those inside all three at once: whoever remains lies in exactly two. Shrinking a boundary removes nothing meaningful here. Unioning adds rather than deletes. And the middle cannot be doubled into the answer, since the middle itself is precisely what must go.
A question picks a branch by chance, then observes an outcome inside it. Which tool answers directly?
Chance-chosen branches followed by conditional observations is exactly the total-probability pattern of problem 11. Inclusion-exclusion prices unions, not staged experiments. Auditing belongs to claims about structures, and De Morgan rewrites expressions rather than computing them.
Where this goes wrongRevision sheets fail in one way: read passively, they feel like mastery. Cover the answer column, recite the theorem behind each row, and reopen the corresponding page wherever a line stalls. The traps list is the highest-yield minute in the session - it is twelve ways other people were wrong.
