TA Session 3 · Part 1 of 10 · The revision block

Four shapes take the same values, and only the probabilities tell them apart

foundation 10 min The block both slow sittings opened with, plus the question a student asked in the middle of it that turned out to be the point.

After this page

  • You can say what the support of a random variable is, and why it is not a subset of the sample space.
  • You can name, in one sentence each, the experiment behind Bernoulli, binomial, geometric and Poisson.
  • You can say what makes two random variables with the same support different variables.

Before you start: The support and the PMF, which is where the notation first appeared.

The one-minute version

  • A random variable is a function X : Ω → R, from the sample space to the real line. Nothing about it is random except the input.
  • Its support SX is the set of values it actually takes. Finite or countable makes it discrete.
  • The CDF is FX(x) = P(X ≤ x). The PMF is pX(k) = P(X = k). The first accumulates, the second does not.
  • Four discrete shapes, four experiments: one trial, n trials, trials until the first success, and arrivals in a fixed window.
  • The whole point of the block: the support does not identify the shape. Two variables can both live on 0, 1, 2, 3 and be nothing alike.

1. The word that caused the trouble

The tutor opened by naming SX the support, and the room stopped him. Nobody recognised the word, and one student asked whether it was a subset of the sample space. It is not, and the correction is worth the paragraph.

SX = { X(ω) : ω ∈ Ω } Ω, capital omega, is the sample space: the set of every outcome the experiment can produce. ω, small omega, is one such outcome, one sample. X is the random variable, a function that turns an outcome into a number. SX is the set of numbers that come out. So the support lives on the real line, and the sample space does not.

Take three coin tosses. The sample space is the eight strings from HHH to TTT. Define X as the number of heads, and the support is 0, 1, 2, 3, four numbers rather than eight strings. Define a different variable on the very same eight strings, say the difference between heads and tails, and the support becomes minus 3, minus 1, 1, 3. Same experiment, same outcomes, different support, because the support belongs to the function and not to the experiment.

The word support was never used in the lectureThe tutor used support as if the class had met it, and a student said plainly that the lecture had only written x ∈ {0, 1} without naming it. The tutor accepted the correction and said he would stop using the word for the rest of the session. This page keeps the word, because it is standard and every textbook uses it, but the disagreement is real and is the reason the block spent ten minutes on a definition the lecture had thought it gave.

2. The two functions, and what separates them

The CDF FX and the PMF pX both describe the same variable, and the difference is accumulation.

  • The PMF pX(k) = P(X = k) reports the mass sitting exactly at k. It is a probability, so it lives in [0, 1], and its values across the support add to 1.
  • The CDF FX(x) = P(X ≤ x) reports everything at or below x. It never decreases, and it is defined at every real x, not only on the support.

3. The four shapes, and the four experiments

The block listed four discrete shapes with their PMFs. The formulas are the ones Lecture 6 gave, and the value the tutorial adds is the sentence beside each one.

ShapeSXPMFThe experiment behind it
Bernoulli(p){0, 1}P(X = 1) = p, P(X = 0) = 1 − pRun the trial once and record success or failure.
Binomial(n, p){0, 1, …, n}P(X = k) = C(n, k) pk(1 − p)n−kRun it n times, independently, same p, and count the successes.
Geometric(p){1, 2, …}P(X = k) = (1 − p)k−1pRun it until the first success and count the trials.
Poisson(λ){0, 1, 2, …}P(X = k) = e−λλk/k!Count arrivals in a fixed window: patients in four hours, cars at a signal.

The tutor gave the first three sentences fluently and then said, honestly, that he had no equally tidy sentence for the Poisson. Pressed, he supplied the window: a hospital, four hours, how many patients. The number cannot be capped in advance, which is why the support runs to infinity rather than stopping at some n. Where λ comes from he deferred to the statistics half of the course.

4. The answer the block was really for

A student asked the sharp question: the geometric and the Poisson both live on the non-negative integers, so what makes them different variables. The tutor answered that the support is not the identity of a random variable, and then proved it later with an example rather than an argument.

The distribution function on Part 5 puts mass on 0, 1, 2 and 3, exactly the values three coin tosses produce. Its masses are 1/2, 1/6, 1/4 and 1/12, which no binomial can produce. Same support, different variable, and the only thing that separated them was the PMF.

Chapter 4 of Ross, A First Course in Probability introduces the same four discrete shapes in the same order, and defines the mass function before the distribution function exactly as the block did.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

Three coins are tossed and X is the number of heads. What exactly is the support of X?
The support is the set of values the function produces, and counting heads across three coins produces only 0, 1, 2 or 3. The first option is the sample space, which is what the room confused it with. The second is a list of probabilities, which is the mass function and not the support. The fourth is true of some variables but not this one, whose output is bounded by the three coins.
Two discrete variables have the same support. What can you conclude about them?
The support says which values carry mass and says nothing about how much, so two variables can share a support and disagree everywhere. The first option is exactly the mistake the block was set to break. The third follows only if the masses agree as well, which is the thing not assumed. The fourth is false: the tutorial's own variable lives on 0 to 3 and is no binomial.

Practice about 8 minutes, with paper

Practice 1foundation

A fair die is rolled twice. Let X be the larger of the two faces, with ties counting as that face.

Give the support of X, then the mass at the largest value in it, and say whether X is any of the four named shapes.

Hint

Count the ordered pairs out of 36. A pair has maximum 6 whenever at least one of the two faces is a 6.

Show the worked solution
  1. The support. The larger of two faces is at least 1 and at most 6, and every value is reachable, so SX = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}.
  2. Count the whole space. Two rolls, ordered, give 6 times 6 = 36 equally likely pairs.
  3. Count the pairs with maximum 6. Every pair except those avoiding 6 entirely, so 36 minus 5 times 5 = 36 minus 25 = 11.
  4. Read the mass. P(X = 6) = 11/36, about 0.306.
  5. Name the shape. None of the four. The support of six values is not Bernoulli, the masses 1/36, 3/36, 5/36, 7/36, 9/36, 11/36 are rising rather than binomial or geometric, and the support is finite so it is not Poisson.

Sanity check. The six masses are the odd numbers over 36, and 1 + 3 + 5 + 7 + 9 + 11 = 36, so they sum to 1 exactly. The mass must also increase with k, since a bigger maximum has more pairs producing it, and it does.

Carry this away

  • The support belongs to the random variable, not to the sample space, and it lives on the real line.
  • The PMF reports mass at a point, the CDF reports mass accumulated up to a point, and only the second is forced to climb.
  • Four shapes, four experiments: one trial, n trials, trials until the first success, arrivals in a window.
  • Two variables can share a support and be nothing alike, which is why the PMF is the thing to write down.

Next: The density that must be split, which is the same block's continuous half and the one integral the room could not do.

Your teacher. Learn the four shapes by their experiments, not their formulas. If you can say "n independent trials, count the successes" you can rebuild the binomial PMF from the counting argument in about twenty seconds, and you will never confuse it with the geometric, whose experiment has no n in it at all. In an exam you will be given the formula sheet and denied the sentence, which is the wrong way round, so the sentence is what to carry in.
