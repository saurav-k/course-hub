Tutorials are where the notation stops being friction

The one-minute version

  • This session tests everything the first lectures built: sigma-fields, probability measures, the probability properties, conditional probability, and independence.
  • Every solution here runs the same eight-step shape: question restated, symbols named, theorem named and justified, picture first, every step written, answer, trap, practice variants.
  • Three of the twelve are traps of the same kind: they reward checking every axiom rather than the convenient one.
  • The point is the analysis, not the answers. A page that ran long because every step is present is correct for this session.

How this session is arranged

A tutorial sheet read alone is a bad teacher. The questions are terse, the official notes compress three lines of algebra into one, and several problems are built so that the lazy answer looks right. So the session is rebuilt here as eight pages: a hub (this page) plus one page per cluster of problems, in the order the sheet sets out. Problem 8 gets a whole page because it constructs the conditional probability space from scratch in six parts, and it is the highest-value problem on the sheet.

Each problem is worked to one fixed shape, and the shape is the deliverable:

  1. The question restated in full, so you never hold it in your head while reading the answer.
  2. Every symbol named in words, on the page where it appears.
  3. The governing theorem named, and justified: why this tool and not another.
  4. The picture before the algebra. Venn circles for set problems, probability trees for conditional ones, a shaded region for geometry, a grid for counting.
  5. Every intermediate step, including the ones the notes skip.
  6. The answer stated plainly.
  7. Where this goes wrong: the specific trap that problem was built around.
  8. Practice variants with different numbers or a flipped condition, answers hidden so you try first.

How to work these pagesEvery solution ends with practice variants whose answers sit behind a collapsed block. The variants exist because reading a worked solution feels like understanding and is not. Do the variant on paper first, open the block only when you have an answer, and use its sanity-check line to catch your own arithmetic. That loop, not the reading, is the session.
Your teacher. If any step on any page does not land, ask for the step immediately before it. The honest request is never "explain Bayes" but "why is A∩B = A here", and the pages are built so that such a question always has a named step just above it.
