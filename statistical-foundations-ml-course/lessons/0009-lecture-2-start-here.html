Lecture 2: The probability space

start here 6 min 6 pages Aug 18, 2026. One lecture, split into six sittings, each one a single idea with its own pictures.

The one-minute version

  • Lecture 1 was judgement without machinery. This lecture builds the machinery: the probability space, written as the triple (Omega, F, P).
  • Ω is the sample space, the set of everything that could happen. F is the sigma-field, the collection of events. P is the measure that weighs each event from 0 to 1.
  • Every set identity gets its own Venn diagram, and the page of set vocabulary the tutorial assumes is taught here openly as supporting material.
  • One user-session space with real numbers is carried end to end, axioms checked before anything is computed.

How this lecture is arranged

Lecture 1 asked which engine a question needs. This lecture builds the engine itself. The object is small enough to write on one line, the triple (Ω, F, P), and the six pages each take one letter or one piece of equipment. The order matters: sets first, because the other five pages are written in them; then the space, the events, the collection, the weights, and finally one worked space with real numbers in it.

The six parts

  1. The language of sets (supporting material). Membership, subsets, the four operations, the translations table, De Morgan, the product and the power set - every identity with its own Venn diagram. The tutorial session assumes this page; the deck never teaches it, so it is taught here and labelled as supporting material.
  2. The sample space (the opening board). Ω, the set of all possible outcomes, through the deck's three examples: a coin, a coin's speed, and tosses until the first head. The point of the third is that a space need not be finite.
  3. Events are subsets (the events board). An event is a subset, "occurred" means the outcome landed in it, and the events are gathered into a collection with properties the deck wants.
  4. The sigma-field (the homework notes). Three axioms on the collection, each drawn, and what each buys: the empty set, intersections by De Morgan, differences, and why closure is countable.
  5. The probability measure (the measure board). P from F to [0, 1], the three axioms - empty set at zero, whole space at one, additivity for disjoint events - and the picture of why additivity fails on overlap.
  6. A discrete space worked (the user-session board). Four outcomes, point masses 0.18, 0.42, 0.12 and 0.28, the sum-to-one check, and the mobile and purchase events weighed.

Every page names each symbol in words where it appears, so nothing from an earlier page is silently assumed. The glossary carries every new term in one sentence each.
Your teacher. Before starting Part 1, hold one question in your head: what has to be true before the phrase "the probability of X" means anything? By Part 6 you will be able to answer it in one sentence - a space listed, a collection of events agreed, and a legal measure on that collection - and that sentence is the foundation the remaining ten lectures stand on.
