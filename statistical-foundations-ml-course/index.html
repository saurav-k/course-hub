Lecture 6: the random variable

1 hub + 14 parts

Eight pages of the lecturer's handwritten pre-class notes, and it follows TA Session 2 because that is where this course's own sequence has reached. The hinge of the whole course: the four lectures before it price events, everything after it prices numbers, and the random variable is the object that connects the two. The PMF and the CDF, the four properties every CDF obeys, five named discrete shapes including the binomial that Lecture 5 built by counting, then density, the Gaussian whose CDF has no closed form, and a heavy tail drawn against a light one. Note the numbering: the source document is titled as the lecturer's Lecture 4, and this course's Lecture 4 is a different session that is already on the site. Nothing is renumbered; the drift is recorded in PLOT.md and on the hub page below.

How this course reads

what to expect
Structure
Three levels deep
Course, then lecture, then page. A lecture is too much for one sitting and a slide is too little for one page, so each lecture gets a hub and the hub links parts that are each one tight idea.
Diagrams
At least three per page
Flowcharts, sequence diagrams, mindmaps, timelines, and quadrant charts for structure; hand-drawn SVG for bar charts, densities, confidence bands, and scatter plots. Every one carries a caption stating its point.
Numbers
Exactly as the deck states them
Where the deck gives a figure, this course gives the same figure. Anything derived here rather than taken from a slide is labelled as such on the page where it appears.
Honesty
Where the lecture is loose, it says so
A first lecture is deliberately informal in places. Three of those places carry a warning callout rather than a smooth explanation, because hiding them would teach the wrong habit.
