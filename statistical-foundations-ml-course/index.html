Reading alongside

the course canon

The sources the lecture course itself prescribes, from slide 21 of Lecture 1. Pick one textbook to work through and one video course for second explanations. Ross plus MIT OCW is the safe default, and the exercise book is where probability stops being something you have read.

SourceBest for
A First Course in Probability
Sheldon Ross		The standard first course, with a large exercise set. Work through it in parallel with the lectures.
Probability, Random Variables and Stochastic Processes
Papoulis and Pillai		The engineer's reference. Reach for it when you need a result rather than a course.
Probability with Engineering Applications
Bruce Hajek		Free as a PDF, so it is the easiest of the four to check a claim against right now.
Probability and Random Processes
Grimmett and Stirzaker		More rigorous than Ross. Graduate to it once the first pass has landed.
One Thousand Exercises in Probability
Grimmett and Stirzaker		The companion problem book, with solutions. This is where the subject is actually learned.
MIT OCW RES.6-012
Tsitsiklis and Jaillet		Short videos, one concept each, with problem sets and solutions.
NPTEL Probability Foundations
Krishna Jagannathan, IIT Madras		A full course on the same syllabus, in the same order and at the same depth.

How this course reads

what to expect
Structure
Three levels deep
Course, then lecture, then page. A lecture is too much for one sitting and a slide is too little for one page, so each lecture gets a hub and the hub links parts that are each one tight idea.
Diagrams
At least three per page
Flowcharts, sequence diagrams, mindmaps, timelines, and quadrant charts for structure; hand-drawn SVG for bar charts, densities, confidence bands, and scatter plots. Every one carries a caption stating its point.
Numbers
Exactly as the deck states them
Where the deck gives a figure, this course gives the same figure. Anything derived here rather than taken from a slide is labelled as such on the page where it appears.
Honesty
Where the lecture is loose, it says so
A first lecture is deliberately informal in places. Three of those places carry a warning callout rather than a smooth explanation, because hiding them would teach the wrong habit.
