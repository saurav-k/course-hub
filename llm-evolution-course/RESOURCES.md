# Resources

The sources this course trusts.
Anything cited in a lesson goes here, with enough identifying detail that the next writer can find the same document.

**These entries were placed by the scaffold as research pointers.**
They come from the scout reports the course was reconciled from.
A pointer is not a citation: open it, confirm it says what the lesson claims, and only then let it appear in prose.
If one is wrong, fix it here in the same pull request.

## Known sourcing traps

Recorded so nobody rediscovers them the hard way.
Each of these is also repeated in the "Do not overclaim" block of the lesson it affects.

- **The Jelinek quotation about firing linguists.** Wording and date are both disputed. Quote his own recollection and mention his later talk pushing back on the reputation it gave him.
- **The 1958 perceptron press coverage.** The newspaper archive is paywalled. Do not quote it from a secondary source.
- **Minsky and Papert, *Perceptrons*.** The XOR claim is uncontroversial and is in the standard textbooks. Do not quote the book unless you have opened it.
- **The 2014 acquisition price.** Never officially disclosed. Say so and stop.
- **How much was actually contributed to OpenAI, and why one founder left the board.** Both are disputed by the parties, with incompatible published accounts. Give the date and both accounts in one sentence each.
- **AlexNet's headline error rate.** Two competition tracks give two different real numbers. Always name the track.
- **GPT-2's parameter counts.** OpenAI corrected them. The older figures are still in wide circulation, including in this repository before they were fixed.
- **Who invented attention.** Content-based addressing appears in several places in late 2014. Present it as convergent, not singular.
