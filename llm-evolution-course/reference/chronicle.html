Chronicle

The append-only record of what happened after a lesson was written. Adding a new development to this course costs one entry here and no edit to any lesson, which is the mechanism that lets the lessons stay stable while the field does not.

Moving Accurate as of 21 August 2026. Next review due February 2027. Entries are never edited and never deleted. A wrong entry is corrected by a later entry that links back to it.

Why this page exists

A lesson narrates a mechanism. This page narrates events. Keeping the two apart is what makes the course cheap to maintain: a lesson about how a router works does not need editing when another model ships, only when routing itself changes. Everything that is news rather than mechanism belongs here.

The three zones

Every page in this course carries a zone badge and an as-of date. The zone decides how it may be edited.

  • Settled Mechanisms and events whose interpretation is stable. Corrected by appending, never rewritten to accommodate a new event.
  • Moving Roughly the last eighteen months. Rewritten freely and explicitly provisional. Carries a review date.
  • Open Named questions with no answer. A question is retired with its answer and the date, never silently deleted.

The boundary between settled and moving is one date, and moving it is a deliberate reviewed act rather than a drift. The current horizon is 1 March 2025. BUILDER-SPEC.md holds the promotion ritual.

How to append an entry

Copy the block below, put it at the top of the entries list, fill it in, and change nothing else on this page. Every claim carries one of the four labels, and every label that is not KNOWN carries a link to whatever evidence does exist.

<article class="chronicle-entry" id="YYYY-MM-DD-short-slug" data-date="YYYY-MM-DD">
  <h3>What happened</h3>
  <p class="entry-meta">
    <time datetime="YYYY-MM-DD">1 January 2027</time>
    <span class="claim claim-known">KNOWN</span>
    <a href="https://example.org/primary-source">Primary source</a>
    <span class="affects">Affects: <a href="../lessons/0056-where-we-are-now.html">Where we are now</a></span>
  </p>
  <p>Two or three sentences. What happened, and which lesson it bears on.</p>
</article>

The four labels: KNOWN stated in a document you can open, INFERRED derived from evidence with the derivation shown, MARKETING published by the seller and not independently checked, UNVERIFIABLE no public evidence exists.

Entries

The fifty-seven lessons of this course were written against a settled horizon of 1 March 2025 and read their sources on 21 August 2026. Everything below happened after that horizon and is recorded here, newest first, whether or not a lesson already covers it. An entry that corrects a lesson says so, and the lesson is corrected at the next review rather than the moment the entry lands.

Meta returns to open weights with Muse Glimmer

10 August 2026 KNOWN CNBC, 10 August 2026 Affects: Who ships open weights now

Meta published Muse Glimmer, a 30-billion-parameter dense model, under Apache 2.0, on Hugging Face under the organisation meta-models rather than the meta-llama organisation the earlier releases used. A letter from Mark Zuckerberg the same day committed the company to opening the weights of the flagship Muse Spark 1.2 in the coming weeks. The commitment is not the release, and whether those weights have shipped is tracked on the frontier watch.

ChatGPT passes one billion weekly active users

6 August 2026 KNOWN The Verge, 11 August 2026 Affects: A text box changes everything

OpenAI confirmed the crossing on 6 August 2026, folded into its announcement of unlimited text chats for free accounts, and a spokesperson quoted five days later put the crossing itself in July 2026. The weekly-active series now reads 800 million in October 2025, 900 million in February 2026 and past one billion in July 2026. Lesson 0036 stops at the one-million-users-in-five-days figure from December 2022, which is the right figure for what that lesson is about - weekly actives and monthly actives are different series and are never mixed in one sentence.

OpenAI cuts GPT-5.6 prices and replaces Priority Processing

30 July 2026 KNOWN OpenAI, 30 July 2026 Affects: Where we are now

Luna fell to $0.20 and $1.20 per million input and output tokens, from $1 and $6. Terra fell to $2 and $12, from $2.50 and $15. Sol was unchanged at $5 and $30, and a Fast mode replaced Priority Processing. Any price printed in a lesson is a price on the day it was read, which is why the lesson prints its read date beside it.

The EU AI Act's high-risk deadlines move

27 July 2026 KNOWN Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, EUR-Lex

Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 entered into force on 27 July 2026 and moved the Annex III high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027 and the Annex I embedded high-risk obligations to 2 August 2028, with some Article 50 transparency duties extended to 2 December 2026. The general application date was not deferred. No lesson in this course covers regulation, deliberately, because this timetable has moved more than once. The entry holds the fact so that a future lesson does not have to find it again.

Meta ships Muse Spark, closed

8 April 2026 KNOWN Meta, Introducing Muse Spark Affects: Who ships open weights now

Muse Spark was the first model of the Muse family from Meta Superintelligence Labs, and it launched closed, as a private API preview. It is the release that ends the company's public silence at frontier scale, four months before the open-weight Glimmer, and the two must not be conflated: one is closed and one is Apache 2.0.

The Model Context Protocol is donated to the Agentic AI Foundation

9 December 2025 KNOWN Anthropic, 9 December 2025 Affects: Tools, protocols and the agent loop

Anthropic donated the protocol to a new foundation under the Linux Foundation, alongside Block's goose and OpenAI's AGENTS.md. This entry is not a correction: lesson 0053 already tells it correctly. It is filed so that the record from the settled horizon forward is complete, and so that a later reader can see that a protocol described as one vendor's is describing 2024.
