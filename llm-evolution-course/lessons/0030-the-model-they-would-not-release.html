The model they would not release

The first time a lab withheld a trained model on the grounds that releasing it might be harmful, it set the terms of an argument the field is still having.

1. Where this lesson sits

What you need first

  • GPT-2 and the bet on scale Make the decoder much bigger and train it on much more text, and the fine-tuning step starts to become unnecessary: you describe the task in the prompt instead.
  • Where the data actually came from The dataset behind GPT-2 was scraped by its authors and filtered by a proxy for human approval, it was not a public crawl, and that filtering idea outlived the model that introduced it.

The one tight idea

The first time a lab withheld a trained model on the grounds that releasing it might be harmful, it set the terms of an argument the field is still having.

2. What this lesson must cover

To write. Full prose, complete sentences, 1,400 to 2,000 words. Every bullet below is a beat the finished lesson owes the reader, in this order.

  • The staged release: what was published when, over roughly nine months, with dates.
  • The argument made at the time, in the lab's own words.
  • The criticism made at the time, in its critics' words.
  • What happened next: replications, and the open-weights counter-movement that formed partly in reaction.
  • How to hold this without taking a side: the argument recurs in every later chapter, so name the positions rather than settling them.
Do not overclaim

Do not describe the staged release as either vindicated or discredited. Report what was argued, what was done, and what followed.
