The one tight idea

The interesting questions in this field are not predictions but open problems, and each one can be stated with what would count as an answer, so that a future reader can tell whether it has been resolved.

2. What this lesson must cover

To write. Full prose, complete sentences, 1,400 to 2,000 words. Every bullet below is a beat the finished lesson owes the reader, in this order.

  • Evaluation: what would a benchmark that does not saturate and cannot be contaminated look like.
  • Interpretability: what would count as knowing why a model produced an answer.
  • Cost: whether the price per unit of capability keeps falling, and what would stop it.
  • Reliability: whether the capability and reliability curves converge, and what would make them.
  • Data: what happens when the readily available text has been used.
  • The next binding constraint, asked rather than answered, which closes the arc the course opened with.
  • The format rule: each question carries what would count as an answer, and resolved questions stay on the page with their answer and date.
Do not overclaim

Do not predict. State the question, state what would settle it, and leave it open. A resolved question is retired with its answer and its date, never deleted.
