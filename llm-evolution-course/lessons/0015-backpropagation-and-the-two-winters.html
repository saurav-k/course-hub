Backpropagation, and the two winters

The field stalled twice for two different reasons: the first was an argument about what a single layer could represent, and the second was an obstacle in the arithmetic of training deep stacks.

Settled Accurate as of 21 August 2026. Interpretation is stable. Corrected by appending, never rewritten to fit a new event.
Where this lesson starts

What a neural network actually is describes training as nudging weights downhill. This lesson asks how a network with many layers knows which weight to nudge, and what went wrong twice when the answer was not enough.

1. Where this lesson sits

What you need first

  • What a neural network actually is A network is a stack of two operations, multiply by a table of numbers and then bend the result, and training is nudging those numbers until a measured error gets smaller.

The one tight idea

The field stalled twice for two different reasons: the first was an argument about what a single layer could represent, and the second was an obstacle in the arithmetic of training deep stacks.

2. What this lesson must cover

To write. Full prose, complete sentences, 1,400 to 2,000 words. Every bullet below is a beat the finished lesson owes the reader, in this order.

  • Credit assignment stated as the problem: which of a million numbers caused this error.
  • Backpropagation as passing blame backwards through the stack, described without derivatives.
  • The first winter: the 1969 argument about what one layer cannot represent, and why the answer took until 1986 to become common practice.
  • The second obstacle: multiply many small numbers together and the signal reaching early layers vanishes.
  • That vanishing gradients is a property of the arithmetic, not a bug in anyone's code.
Do not overclaim

Do not quote Perceptrons unless you have opened it. The XOR claim is uncontroversial and is stated in the standard textbooks, so cite those instead.
