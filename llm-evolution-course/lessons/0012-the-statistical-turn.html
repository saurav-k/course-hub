The statistical turn: guessing the next word

Language is predictable, predictability can be measured, and a machine that counts what usually follows what beats a machine that reasons about grammar.

Settled Accurate as of 21 August 2026. Interpretation is stable. Corrected by appending, never rewritten to fit a new event.
Where this lesson starts

Hand-written rules, the subject of Machines that talk without understanding, ran out of authors. This lesson introduces the idea that replaced them: do not describe language, measure it. It assumes only that you can read a table of counts.

1. Where this lesson sits

What you need first

  • Machines that talk without understanding You can produce convincing language with pattern matching and no model of meaning at all, and people will supply the understanding themselves.

The one tight idea

Language is predictable, predictability can be measured, and a machine that counts what usually follows what beats a machine that reasons about grammar.

2. What this lesson must cover

To write. Full prose, complete sentences, 1,400 to 2,000 words. Every bullet below is a beat the finished lesson owes the reader, in this order.

  • Shannon 1948 and 1951: information, entropy, and the experiment where humans guessed the next letter of English.
  • The n-gram language model stated plainly: the probability of the next word given the few words before it, estimated by counting.
  • Perplexity introduced informally as how surprised the model is, and why a lower number is better.
  • The curse of dimensionality: most word sequences a model will ever meet never appeared in its training text.
  • Smoothing and backoff in one paragraph, as the patch that kept counting alive.
  • The Jelinek quip about firing linguists, presented with its disputed wording and his own later pushback.
Do not overclaim

The Jelinek quotation's wording and date are both disputed. Quote his own recollection, mention that he later gave a talk titled Some of My Best Friends Are Linguists, and do not present any single wording as the original.
