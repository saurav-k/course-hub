Where this lesson starts

The open-weights turn left a large and growing set of models that most people still could not afford to train, tune, or serve. This chapter is about the four separate things the field did about that.

4. Where this hands off

This course owns chronology, causation, people and dates. It hands off for mechanism, maths and code rather than re-deriving them.

  • Deep dive: LoRA and QLoRA: fine-tuning for the rest of us You can adapt a large model by training a small number of extra numbers alongside the frozen originals, and if you also store the originals at lower precision the whole job fits on one ordinary machine.
  • Deep dive: Serving reality: the cache, and what it costs Generating a token requires re-reading everything the model has already seen, so serving is a memory problem before it is an arithmetic problem, and most of the engineering since 2022 is about that memory.
  • Deep dive: Mixture of experts, part one: the router Replace one large feed-forward network with many smaller ones and a small chooser that picks a couple per token, and what a model knows stops being the same number as what it costs to run.
  • Deep dive: Mixture of experts, part two: why it is hard A router that is trained only to be useful will send almost everything to a few experts, so every practical system needs something that stops the router eating itself, and three generations of that fix are the real story.
  • Deep dive: Sparsity moves to attention Once the feed-forward half of the block is cheap, attention is what is left paying the quadratic bill, so the same selective trick was pointed at attention itself.
  • Deep dive: Why a small model now beats a huge one from 2020 A model a fraction of the size now outperforms the largest models of 2020 on most tasks, and almost none of the reason is a better architecture.
  • Deep dive: Long context: capacity is not competence The advertised context length is how much text a model will accept, not how much it can use, and the two numbers are routinely different by a large factor.
