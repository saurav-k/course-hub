Why agents are a systems problem

What a model can do on a single attempt keeps improving while how often it succeeds every time keeps not improving, and that gap is where the binding constraint now sits.

Where this lesson starts

Tools, protocols, and the agent loop draws a loop that works. This lesson is about why a loop that works most of the time is not the same as a system that works. It assumes you have seen that loop.

1. Where this lesson sits

What you need first

The one tight idea

What a model can do on a single attempt keeps improving while how often it succeeds every time keeps not improving, and that gap is where the binding constraint now sits.

2. What this lesson must cover

To write. Full prose, complete sentences, 1,400 to 2,000 words. Every bullet below is a beat the finished lesson owes the reader, in this order.

  • Capability against reliability, defined so they cannot be confused: one attempt against every attempt.
  • Why compounding is brutal: a per-step success rate raised to the number of steps.
  • The measurement that makes this visible: the gap between succeeding once and succeeding on every repeat of the same task.
  • Failure modes that only appear at length: drift, looping, giving up, and unrecoverable actions.
  • Why the fix is engineering rather than a bigger model, and where the other courses in this hub take it further.
Do not overclaim

Quote trend claims as trends with their measurement method, never as a single headline number, and give the date of the measurement.
