Multimodality, and the model as an interface

Once images, audio and text all become sequences of tokens in one model, the model stops being a text tool and becomes the place where several kinds of input meet.

Moving Accurate as of 21 August 2026. Next review due February 2027. The last eighteen months. Rewritten freely, and expected to be wrong before it is old.
Where this lesson starts

This course has treated a language model as something that reads and writes text. This lesson is about what changed when the same architecture took other kinds of input. It assumes you know what a token is.

1. Where this lesson sits

What you need first

  • Tokens: how text becomes numbers A model never sees words: it sees pieces chosen by an algorithm that repeatedly merges the most frequent adjacent pair, and once you start from raw bytes nothing is ever out of vocabulary.

The one tight idea

Once images, audio and text all become sequences of tokens in one model, the model stops being a text tool and becomes the place where several kinds of input meet.

2. What this lesson must cover

To write. Full prose, complete sentences, 1,400 to 2,000 words. Every bullet below is a beat the finished lesson owes the reader, in this order.

  • The general recipe: turn the other modality into a sequence of vectors and feed it into the same stack.
  • What that buys and what it does not: shared reasoning across inputs, but not equal competence across them.
  • Where the evaluation problem gets harder, since text benchmarks do not transfer.
  • The interface consequence: screenshots, documents, voice and video become ordinary inputs.
  • The honest position: capability here is uneven and moving, so date every claim.
Do not overclaim

Do not claim parity across modalities. Give a benchmark, a date and a model name, or say that you are describing the shape of the approach rather than its current quality.
