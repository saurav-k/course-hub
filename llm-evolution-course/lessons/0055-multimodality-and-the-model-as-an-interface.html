Multimodality, and the model as an interface

Once images, audio and text all become sequences of tokens in one model, the model stops being a text tool and becomes the place where several kinds of input meet.

Moving Accurate as of 21 August 2026. Next review due February 2027. The last eighteen months. Rewritten freely, and expected to be wrong before it is old.
Where this lesson starts

This course has treated a language model as something that reads and writes text. This lesson is about what changed when the same architecture took other kinds of input. It assumes you know what a token is.

1. Where this lesson sits

What you need first

  • Tokens: how text becomes numbers A model never sees words: it sees pieces chosen by an algorithm that repeatedly merges the most frequent adjacent pair, and once you start from raw bytes nothing is ever out of vocabulary.

The one tight idea

Once images, audio and text all become sequences of tokens in one model, the model stops being a text tool and becomes the place where several kinds of input meet.

2. The recipe is one sentence long

Here is the fact that makes this whole area less mysterious than it sounds.

A Transformer block does not know it is processing language. It takes a sequence of vectors, mixes each position with the others according to learned weights, and hands on another sequence of vectors. Nothing in it refers to words. Tokens: how text becomes numbers describes the step that gets text into that form, in which a piece of text is cut into pieces from a fixed vocabulary and each piece is looked up as a list of numbers. Notice that the cutting and the looking-up are not part of the model. They are the front door.

So the recipe for another kind of input is: cut it into pieces, turn each piece into a list of numbers, and put them in the same sequence.

For images, that step was published in October 2020, before any of this was about assistants. Dosovitskiy and colleagues showed that a pure Transformer applied directly to sequences of image patches performs very well at recognising images, with no convolutional network underneath. The paper's title states the method: an image is worth sixteen by sixteen words. Cut the picture into a grid of small square patches, flatten each patch, and treat it as a token. A 224-pixel-square image cut into 16-pixel patches is a 14 by 14 grid, which is 196 tokens: about as many as a short paragraph.

For audio the equivalent is to cut the waveform into short overlapping frames and encode each one, which is what a speech model such as Whisper does before its decoder ever writes a character. Whisper is worth naming for a second reason: it was trained on 680,000 hours of transcribed audio scraped from the internet, which is the same "collect an enormous pile of naturally occurring pairs" move that made language models work.

The consequence is the point of this lesson. Multimodality did not require a new mechanism. It required a new front door, and the story of the last four years is the door moving steadily earlier in the pipeline.

3. Three generations of front door

Bolted on

The first working systems kept two separately trained models and learned only the join. In April 2022, Alayrac and colleagues published Flamingo, whose stated contribution is to bridge powerful pretrained vision-only and language-only models and to handle sequences of arbitrarily interleaved images and text. In April 2023, Liu and colleagues published LLaVA, which connects a vision encoder to a language model and tunes the pair on instruction-following data generated by a text-only model.

This is cheap, and it is why open work moved so fast. You do not retrain either half, so you need a small fraction of the compute, and you can attach a new vision encoder to whatever language model came out last month. What you get is a language model that can see, with the seam still visible.

Native

The next generation trained one model on several kinds of input from the start of its main training run rather than joining two afterwards. Google's Gemini report, published in December 2023, describes a family of models exhibiting capabilities across image, audio, video and text understanding. In May 2024, Meta published Chameleon, which is the clearest statement of the idea: a family of early-fusion token-based mixed-modal models capable of understanding and generating images and text in any arbitrary sequence. Early fusion is the technical name for moving the join to the very beginning: everything becomes tokens first, and the model has one vocabulary covering all of them.

From the first step

By 2026 the model cards claim the join has moved as early as it can go, with mixed-modality training from the first step of pre-training and video handled in the same model as text. MARKETING: those are the publishers' own descriptions of their own training procedures, and no laboratory publishes enough about its data or its schedule for anyone outside to check what "from the first step" means in practice. The direction of travel is well evidenced; the specific claims are not.

4. What that buys, and what it does not

What it buys is genuine and easy to underrate: one set of reasoning, applied to whatever arrived.

Ask why a chart contradicts the paragraph beside it, and the chart and the paragraph are positions in one sequence. There is no pipeline in which a vision system produces a description and a language system reasons over the description, which is how such things were built before and which loses everything the describer did not think to mention.

What it does not buy is equal competence. A model that reads and writes text at an expert level is not thereby an expert at reading a diagram, and the gap has been measured. In November 2023 the MMMU benchmark collected 11,500 college-level questions across six disciplines and thirty kinds of image, from charts and maps to music scores and chemical structures, all requiring both the picture and the subject knowledge. The two strongest models available to its authors reached 56 and 59 percent, at a time when the same systems were being reported near the ceiling on text-only exams.

Treat those figures as a dated snapshot rather than a current fact: it is November 2023, on that harness, with those models. The durable claim is the shape. Feeding a new modality through the same architecture makes it possible for the model to be good at it, and how good it actually is depends on how much of that modality was in the training data and how well it was paired with text. Those are separate questions from the architecture, and they are answered separately for every modality.

5. Why the evaluation problem gets harder

There is a specific and nasty measurement failure here, and it is worth understanding because it recurs whenever a benchmark spans two kinds of input.

A question about an image can often be answered without the image. Ask what colour the sky is in a photograph and a text-only model will say blue and be right most of the time. A benchmark full of such questions will report that a system understands pictures when what it has measured is that the system knows about the world.

The sharpest published demonstration of this is recent and specific. In January 2026 a group tested a document-reading model by corrupting the text semantics in its inputs, at the sentence and the word level, in order to separate genuine optical reading from the model's language expectations. Without that linguistic support, accuracy fell from roughly 90 percent to roughly 20 percent, and traditional pipeline methods proved far more robust to the same perturbation. Whatever else that says about the model, it says the benchmark had been crediting a language prior as a perception result.

So a multimodal claim needs more scaffolding than a text claim before it means anything. The general discipline is Reading an eval table like a scientist, and this area adds a specific question to it: was the input actually necessary to answer, and how do you know?

6. The model as an interface

The practical consequence for anyone building things is the largest change of all, and it is not about capability at all.

Before, every kind of input needed its own adapter: a document parser, an optical reader, a speech transcriber, a chart extractor, each with its own failure modes and each producing a different intermediate format that something downstream had to consume. After, a screenshot, a scanned page, a photograph of a handwritten form, a voice clip and a video are all just tokens, and the thing that handles them is the same thing that answers about them. The model stops being a text tool and becomes the place where inputs meet.

One consequence of that is genuinely surprising and worth carrying away, because it inverts the direction everyone assumes. In October 2025 DeepSeek published a system that treats rendering text as an image as a form of compression: a passage is drawn as a picture, the picture becomes fewer vision tokens than the passage would have become text tokens, and the model reads the text back out of it. They report about 97 percent decoding precision while the text is within ten times the number of vision tokens, and about 60 percent at twenty times. A follow-up appeared in January 2026. If that holds up, a picture of a page is a compressed representation of the page, and the long-context problem has an optical answer nobody was looking for. Read it alongside the critique in section 5, which is about that same family of models, and hold both.

7. What is honest to say about this in 2026

Split it into two claims with very different confidence, and this page's review date applies only to the second.

The approach is settled. Turning a modality into a sequence of vectors and feeding it through the same blocks is how it is done, everywhere, in open and closed models alike, and it has not been seriously challenged since 2020.

The quality is uneven and moving. Any statement of the form "models can now do X with images or audio or video" needs a named model, a named benchmark and a date, or it needs to be phrased as a description of the approach rather than of its current quality. This course will not claim parity across modalities, because nobody can currently support that claim with a measurement that survives the check in section 5.

Where the field stands more broadly, with the same discipline applied, is Where we are now.

9. Sources to read first

  • Dosovitskiy et al., An Image is Worth 16x16 Words: Transformers for Image Recognition at Scale, arXiv, 22 October 2020. The patch-as-token recipe that section 2 describes.
  • Radford et al., Robust Speech Recognition via Large-Scale Weak Supervision, arXiv, 6 December 2022. The source for the 680,000 hours.
  • Alayrac et al., Flamingo: a Visual Language Model for Few-Shot Learning, arXiv, 29 April 2022, and Liu et al., Visual Instruction Tuning (LLaVA), arXiv, 17 April 2023. The bolted-on generation.
  • Gemini Team, Gemini: A Family of Highly Capable Multimodal Models, arXiv, 19 December 2023, and Chameleon Team, Chameleon: Mixed-Modal Early-Fusion Foundation Models, arXiv, 16 May 2024. The native generation, and the clearest statement of early fusion.
  • Yue et al., MMMU, arXiv, 27 November 2023. The source for the 11,500 questions, the six disciplines and the 56 and 59 percent figures, all as of that date.
  • DeepSeek-AI, DeepSeek-OCR: Contexts Optical Compression, arXiv, 21 October 2025, and DeepSeek-OCR 2: Visual Causal Flow, arXiv, 28 January 2026. The source for the compression ratios quoted in section 6.
  • Visual Merit or Linguistic Crutch? A Close Look at DeepSeek-OCR, arXiv, 7 January 2026. The semantic-corruption experiment quoted in section 5, and the reason section 6 is stated with a caveat attached.

Every one of these was opened and checked against what this lesson claims before it was written into the prose. The 2026 papers are preprints and are cited as measurements with a stated method rather than as settled results.

Retrieval practice

Why can the same architecture accept images as well as text?
Nothing inside the blocks refers to words, so the only work is at the front door: cut the input into pieces and encode each piece.
What does early fusion mean, compared with bolting two models together?
Moving the join earlier means more of the model's capacity is shared between modalities instead of spent on a translation layer.
Why is a multimodal benchmark easy to fool?
A system that knows about the world scores well on such items, and the benchmark then reports a language prior as a perception result.
What is surprising about treating rendered text as an image?
That inverts the usual assumption and makes a page of pixels a candidate compression format for long context, with a reported accuracy cost.
