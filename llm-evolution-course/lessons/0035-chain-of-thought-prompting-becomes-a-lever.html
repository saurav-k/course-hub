Chain of thought: prompting becomes a lever

Asking a large model to write out its working before answering improves its accuracy on multi-step problems, which means the prompt is not just a request but a control on how much computation the model spends.

Settled Accurate as of 21 August 2026. Interpretation is stable. Corrected by appending, never rewritten to fit a new event.
Where this lesson starts

This lesson is about a discovery made by changing nothing but the input. It assumes you know that a model's weights are frozen at inference time and that a prompt can contain examples.

1. Where this lesson sits

What you need first

  • GPT-3, and learning without learning A model whose weights never change gets better at a task when you put a few worked examples in the prompt, which is a kind of learning that leaves no trace in the model.

The one tight idea

Asking a large model to write out its working before answering improves its accuracy on multi-step problems, which means the prompt is not just a request but a control on how much computation the model spends.

2. What this lesson must cover

To write. Full prose, complete sentences, 1,400 to 2,000 words. Every bullet below is a beat the finished lesson owes the reader, in this order.

  • The experiment: the same model, the same questions, and examples that show the working.
  • The result and its shape: the gain appears only above a certain model size.
  • Why the working helps, stated as a hypothesis rather than a fact: more generated tokens is more computation and an external place to keep intermediate results.
  • The zero-shot variant, and why one short instruction can do most of the work.
  • The honest caveat: the written reasoning is not necessarily the reason for the answer.
  • That this is the seed of the whole reasoning chapter later in the course.
Do not overclaim

Emergence claims are contested, and part of the effect depends on how the metric is chosen. Present the observation and note the dispute.
