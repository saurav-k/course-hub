Mixture of experts, part two: why it is hard

A router that is trained only to be useful will send almost everything to a few experts, so every practical system needs something that stops the router eating itself, and three generations of that fix are the real story.

Where this lesson starts

Mixture of experts, part one: the router describes the mechanism as if it simply works. This lesson is about why it does not, and it assumes you know what a router and an expert are.

1. Where this lesson sits

The one tight idea

A router that is trained only to be useful will send almost everything to a few experts, so every practical system needs something that stops the router eating itself, and three generations of that fix are the real story.

2. What this lesson must cover

To write. Full prose, complete sentences, 1,400 to 2,000 words. Every bullet below is a beat the finished lesson owes the reader, in this order.

  • Routing collapse described as a feedback loop: a chosen expert improves, so it is chosen more.
  • Generation one, an auxiliary loss plus a capacity limit, and the cost it imposes: dropped tokens.
  • Generation two, finer-grained experts plus always-on shared experts.
  • Generation three, a bias term adjusted by a control loop, which balances selection without distorting the weights.
  • Why balancing matters for hardware as well as for quality.
Do not overclaim

Do not present the third generation as final. Say what it fixed, and that the balancing problem has not been declared solved.
