Where the data actually came from

The dataset behind GPT-2 was scraped by its authors and filtered by a proxy for human approval, it was not a public crawl, and that filtering idea outlived the model that introduced it.

Settled Accurate as of 21 August 2026. Interpretation is stable. Corrected by appending, never rewritten to fit a new event.
Where this lesson starts

This lesson exists to correct a specific and very common misconception about where early training data came from. It assumes only that you know a language model is trained on text.

1. Where this lesson sits

What you need first

  • GPT-2 and the bet on scale Make the decoder much bigger and train it on much more text, and the fine-tuning step starts to become unnecessary: you describe the task in the prompt instead.

The one tight idea

The dataset behind GPT-2 was scraped by its authors and filtered by a proxy for human approval, it was not a public crawl, and that filtering idea outlived the model that introduced it.

2. What this lesson must cover

To write. Full prose, complete sentences, 1,400 to 2,000 words. Every bullet below is a beat the finished lesson owes the reader, in this order.

  • What WebText actually was: outbound links from posts above a karma threshold, deduplicated, with Wikipedia removed.
  • What it was not: a public crawl, and not a dataset anyone could download at the time.
  • Why the authors built their own rather than using the crawl that already existed.
  • The chain forward: the same corpus later served as the positive class for a quality classifier over a public crawl.
  • The general lesson: data curation is a modelling decision, and it is usually the least documented one.
Do not overclaim

State corpus sizes only with the unit the source used, and do not convert between documents, tokens and gigabytes unless the source gives you both.
