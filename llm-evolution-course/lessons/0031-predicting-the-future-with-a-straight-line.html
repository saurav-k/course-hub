Predicting the future with a straight line

Model loss falls as a power law in size, data and compute, which means a straight line on a log-log plot, which means you can forecast the result of a run you have not paid for.

Settled Accurate as of 21 August 2026. Interpretation is stable. Corrected by appending, never rewritten to fit a new event.
Where this lesson starts

Until 2020 deciding how big to build a model was a matter of taste and budget. This lesson is about the measurement that turned it into a forecast. It teaches the log-log plot inline and assumes no statistics background.

1. Where this lesson sits

What you need first

  • GPT-2 and the bet on scale Make the decoder much bigger and train it on much more text, and the fine-tuning step starts to become unnecessary: you describe the task in the prompt instead.

The one tight idea

Model loss falls as a power law in size, data and compute, which means a straight line on a log-log plot, which means you can forecast the result of a run you have not paid for.

2. What this lesson must cover

To write. Full prose, complete sentences, 1,400 to 2,000 words. Every bullet below is a beat the finished lesson owes the reader, in this order.

  • What a power law is, taught from a plot rather than an equation.
  • Why a straight line on log-log axes is the signature of one, and how to read the slope.
  • The three quantities that were varied and the ranges over which the fits hold.
  • Why a small exponent is the entire story rather than a disappointment.
  • The practical consequence: pick a budget, read off the size, run once.
  • The honest caveat: a fit is valid over its measured range and promises nothing outside it.
Do not overclaim

A scaling law is an empirical fit, not a law of nature. Give the range it was fitted over whenever you quote it.
