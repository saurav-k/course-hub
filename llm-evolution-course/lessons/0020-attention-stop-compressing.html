Attention: stop compressing

Do not squeeze the source sentence into one vector at all: keep every position, and at each output step learn a weighting that says which positions matter now.

Settled Accurate as of 21 August 2026. Interpretation is stable. Corrected by appending, never rewritten to fit a new event.
Where this lesson starts

Sequence to sequence, and the eight thousand numbers ends with a fixed vector that cannot hold a long sentence. This lesson is the fix, and it is the single most important mechanism in the course. It assumes you know what an encoder and a decoder are.

1. Where this lesson sits

What you need first

  • Sequence to sequence, and the eight thousand numbers Read a whole sentence into a single fixed list of numbers and then write another sentence out of it, and that single list is both the elegance and the flaw.

The one tight idea

Do not squeeze the source sentence into one vector at all: keep every position, and at each output step learn a weighting that says which positions matter now.

2. What this lesson must cover

To write. Full prose, complete sentences, 1,400 to 2,000 words. Every bullet below is a beat the finished lesson owes the reader, in this order.

  • The move in one sentence: replace one summary with a summary recomputed per output step.
  • Query, keys and values named and kept for the rest of the course.
  • Turning scores into weights that sum to one, and why that normalisation matters.
  • The alignment plot as evidence: the weights line up with what a translator would align by hand.
  • That this was invented for translation, and that it is a soft lookup rather than a hard choice.
Do not overclaim

Attention weights are not an explanation of the model's decision. Say what they are: a learned weighting that happens to align well on translation.
