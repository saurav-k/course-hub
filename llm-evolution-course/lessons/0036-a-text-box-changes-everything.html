A text box changes everything

The breakthrough of late 2022 was distribution rather than research: an existing recipe, tuned for conversation, put behind a free text box that anyone could use without reading anything first.

Settled Accurate as of 21 August 2026. Interpretation is stable. Corrected by appending, never rewritten to fit a new event.
Where this lesson starts

This lesson is about a product launch. It assumes you know what instruction tuning is, from Teaching a model to answer, and it argues that very little else was new.

1. Where this lesson sits

What you need first

  • Teaching a model to answer A model trained to continue text is not trying to be helpful, so you add a stage whose only job is preference: show it demonstrations, learn a stand-in for human taste, and let it practise against that on a short leash.

The one tight idea

The breakthrough of late 2022 was distribution rather than research: an existing recipe, tuned for conversation, put behind a free text box that anyone could use without reading anything first.

2. What the announcement actually said

On Wednesday 30 November 2022 OpenAI published a post titled ChatGPT: Optimizing Language Models for Dialogue. It is a short document, and the most useful thing about it now is how modest its technical claims are.

Its second paragraph places the model in an existing family: ChatGPT is "a sibling model to InstructGPT, which is trained to follow an instruction in a prompt and provide a detailed response". InstructGPT was a paper from March 2022 - Long Ouyang and colleagues at OpenAI, Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback - and its recipe is the one Teaching a model to answer describes: collect demonstrations of good answers and fine-tune on them, collect human rankings of alternative answers and train a stand-in for human taste on them, then let the model practise against that stand-in on a short leash.

The methods section says plainly which parts were reused: "We trained this model using Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), using the same methods as InstructGPT, but with slight differences in the data collection setup."

The differences are worth naming, because they are the whole of what was new.

  • The demonstrations became conversations. "Human AI trainers provided conversations in which they played both sides - the user and an AI assistant", rather than writing one good answer to one instruction.
  • The rankings became rankings inside a conversation. The team "took conversations that AI trainers had with the chatbot", picked one model message at random, sampled alternatives for it, and had trainers rank those.
  • The base model was newer. "ChatGPT is fine-tuned from a model in the GPT-3.5 series, which finished training in early 2022."

That is the list. A different shape of training data, on the same recipe, over an existing base model.

What is not known, and what nobody outside OpenAI can check

Which checkpoint in the GPT-3.5 series it started from, how many parameters that checkpoint had, what it was trained on, and how much of the behaviour came from the base model rather than from the conversational tuning. None of this was disclosed then and none of it has been disclosed since. The announcement is the entire public record of the model's construction, and it is a blog post rather than a paper. The disclosure collapse is about how that stopped being unusual.

The announcement is also unusually frank about what was wrong with it. Its limitations section says the model "sometimes writes plausible-sounding but incorrect or nonsensical answers", that it is "sensitive to tweaks to the input phrasing", and that it is "often excessively verbose and overuses certain phrases". It even names the cause of the verbosity: the human trainers "prefer longer answers that look more comprehensive", so the taste model learned that preference and the policy learned to satisfy it.

3. What was new was that you could just use it

Now put the technical ingredients on a timeline and look at the gap.

Before 30 November 2022, using a model of this class required a decision and some work. You applied for API access, you were approved or you waited, you read documentation, you obtained a key, you wrote code that assembled a prompt, you paid per token, and you designed the prompt yourself because the model was a text continuer rather than an assistant. Every one of those steps removed people.

The launch removed all of them at once. There was a web page. It was free - the announcement says so directly: "During the research preview, usage of ChatGPT is free" - and it invited you to "Try it now at chat.openai.com". There was no waiting list. There was nothing to read first. The interface was a chat window, which everyone already knew how to operate because they had used a messaging app.

And the model met you halfway. The announcement's first paragraph advertises exactly the behaviours that make a text box usable by someone who has never thought about prompting: the dialogue format "makes it possible for ChatGPT to answer followup questions, admit its mistakes, challenge incorrect premises, and reject inappropriate requests". A user who phrased something badly could simply say so in the next message. That is the difference between a tool that requires a skill and a tool that does not.

4. How fast it moved, and what can honestly be said about that

Five days after the launch, on 5 December 2022, Sam Altman posted a single sentence: "ChatGPT launched on wednesday. today it crossed 1 million users!" That is a first-party statement of a specific number on a specific date, and it is the firmest adoption figure in this whole story.

Everything after it is softer, and this lesson is going to be annoying about that on purpose.

The figure everybody repeats - a hundred million monthly users within two months, described as the fastest-growing consumer application in history - did not come from OpenAI. It came from an investment-bank analyst note reported in the press at the beginning of February 2023, using third-party web traffic estimates. It may well be right. It is an outside estimate of a private company's usage, produced by people whose job is to inform investors, and it was never confirmed by the company it describes. Repeat it if you like, but repeat it with its owner attached.

What can be said without an estimate is structural. Within four months of the launch there were three visible consequences, each of which is documented by an artefact you can open:

  • A frontier race, in public. OpenAI submitted the GPT-4 Technical Report on 15 March 2023, Anthropic released Claude to selected users in the same month, and Google announced Gemini before the year was out.
  • A counter-movement. Meta released LLaMA to approved researchers in late February 2023, arguing in a paper submitted on the 27th that state-of-the-art models could be trained from publicly available data alone. Within a week the weights were on a torrent, which is the subject of The leak that started an ecosystem.
  • A change in what the public believed was possible. Before the launch, "a computer that writes" was a claim in a paper. After it, it was something a person had personally done on a Tuesday evening, and the argument moved from whether the capability existed to what should be done about it.

5. The flywheel the text box switched on

There is one more consequence, and it is the one with teeth.

The recipe behind the launch needs human comparisons: pairs of answers with a person saying which is better. Those are expensive. In the InstructGPT work they came from a contracted team of labellers, and the size of that team is a hard limit on how much preference data a lab can have.

A free chat interface used by a very large number of people is, among other things, a machine for producing that data. Every conversation is a prompt distribution drawn from real users rather than guessed at by researchers. Every regenerated answer, every thumbs-down, every abandoned conversation is a signal about preference. The announcement asks for exactly this, in the sentence that frames the whole thing as a research preview: "We are excited to introduce ChatGPT to get users' feedback and learn about its strengths and weaknesses."

6. Interface and price are capabilities

The habit this lesson is arguing against is the habit of measuring progress only in the weights. It is a natural habit, because the weights are what the papers are about, and this course spends most of its length on them.

November 2022 is the counter-example that keeps it honest. Nothing in the model was new enough to write a paper about, and the announcement did not pretend otherwise. What changed was that the cost of a first attempt fell from "apply, wait, read, code, pay" to "open a page", and a capability that costs nothing to try is a different capability from the same one behind a door.

That pattern repeats through the rest of this course, and it is worth carrying forward as a question rather than a slogan. When a model gets ten times cheaper to run, as Serving reality: the cache, and what it costs describes, the thing that changes is not what the model can do but who can afford to find out. When weights become downloadable rather than rented, as Four axes of open: weights, code, data, licence works through, the thing that changes is who is allowed to experiment. In each case the mechanism sat still and the access moved, and in each case the access is what the history turned on.

The immediate debt this launch left is a different one. A model behind a free text box used by millions of people is a commercial asset, and a commercial asset is not something a company explains. Four months after the launch, a document called a technical report declined to report the technique, and that is where this story goes next.

Retrieval practice

What did the announcement itself say was new about the model?
The post says the method was the same as InstructGPT with slight differences in data collection, and names the conversational demonstrations as the difference.
Why does this lesson call the launch a distribution event?
Every mechanism in it was already published; the free page with no waiting list and nothing to read is what was new.
How should the hundred-million-user figure be treated?
It came from an investment-bank note using third-party traffic estimates, so it carries its owner's name and not the company's.
Why is a popular free interface an advantage a competitor cannot simply buy?
The tuning recipe runs on human comparisons, and an interface used at scale produces those from real usage instead of a contracted team.
