What a neural network actually is

A network is a stack of two operations, multiply by a table of numbers and then bend the result, and training is nudging those numbers until a measured error gets smaller.

Settled Accurate as of 21 August 2026. Interpretation is stable. Corrected by appending, never rewritten to fit a new event.

1. Where this lesson sits

What you need first

  • The statistical turn: guessing the next word Language is predictable, predictability can be measured, and a machine that counts what usually follows what beats a machine that reasons about grammar.

The one tight idea

A network is a stack of two operations, multiply by a table of numbers and then bend the result, and training is nudging those numbers until a measured error gets smaller.

2. What this lesson must cover

To write. Full prose, complete sentences, 1,400 to 2,000 words. Every bullet below is a beat the finished lesson owes the reader, in this order.

  • A vector as a list of numbers, with a concrete example the reader can hold.
  • A matrix multiply as a set of weighted sums, drawn rather than written.
  • The bend, in one sentence: without it, a stack of layers collapses into a single layer.
  • A loss as one number that says how wrong the whole guess was.
  • Gradient descent as which way is downhill, without derivative notation.
  • The vocabulary this course will reuse: weights, forward pass, loss, update, epoch.
Do not overclaim

Do not describe a unit as a neuron doing anything a neuron does. The biological analogy is historical, and it misleads a beginner about what is being computed.
