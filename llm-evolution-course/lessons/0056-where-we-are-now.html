2. How to read this page

This is a snapshot, taken on 21 August 2026, and it is the most perishable page in the course. It is written to be replaced at its review date rather than patched, which is why it says what shape the field is in rather than what happened last week. Things that happened go in the chronicle, where they are dated by construction.

Every substantive claim below carries one of four labels, and the labels are the point of the page as much as the content is.

  • KNOWN stated in a document anyone can open, or provable from an artefact such as a licence file or a configuration.
  • INFERRED derived from evidence by a third party with a method you could re-run, and not confirmed by the organisation concerned.
  • MARKETING published by the seller. It may be true. It is not evidence, because no harness, seed or baseline comes with it.
  • UNVERIFIABLE widely repeated, never confirmed, and with no method that could settle it.

One editorial rule follows from the same logic and this course keeps it everywhere: current model names appear in tables, not in prose. A sentence naming this month's strongest model is wrong within a quarter, while a sentence about the shape of the frontier survives.

3. The frontier

The strongest systems on sale in the middle of 2026 share a description that would have sounded like a wish list three years earlier. A context of about a million tokens as the default rather than a premium tier. Deliberation always on, with the reader controlling depth through an effort setting instead of choosing a separate reasoning product. Tool use through a governed protocol. Images and video handled by the same model as text. And, at the top of the range, the raw working never returned to the caller at all.

What is not in that table is more interesting than what is. No architecture, no parameter count, no expert count, no layer count, no training compute, no training data composition. UNVERIFIABLE for every closed frontier model of this generation, and it has been that way since 2023. The disclosure collapse is where that change is told as history.

Benchmark claims from all three vendors are MARKETING by the definition above, and one episode from July 2026 shows why better than any argument. On a software-engineering benchmark, one vendor reported its flagship at 80.0 against a competitor's top model at 64.6. The day before that competitor's own launch, it published an audit of the same benchmark, with an outside estimate that roughly 30 percent of its tasks are broken. Both things can be true at once: the benchmark may genuinely be partly broken, and the audit may genuinely have been published at a convenient moment. Sitting with both is the correct response, and Reading an eval table like a scientist is the discipline for it.

4. The commodity

The more useful half of a snapshot is what has stopped being remarkable, because that is where most readers will actually be working.

Available cheaply, from many suppliers, including in open weights: a million tokens of context; an effort or thinking control; tool calling through the protocol described in Tools, protocols, and the agent loop; native handling of images and video, which Multimodality, and the model as an interface explains; a sparse expert architecture, which Mixture of experts: the router covers; quantised deployment formats published by the laboratory itself rather than produced afterwards by somebody else; and small dense models whose description would have named a frontier system in 2023, which is the subject of Why a small model beats a huge one.

Read the two lists together and a rough rule falls out: roughly two years separates a frontier capability from a commodity one. INFERRED, and stated as a pattern read off a short timeline rather than a law. It has held for context length, for chat-quality instruction following, for tool calling and for multimodal input. It has not been tested on anything that requires frontier-scale training compute to reproduce.

5. What is genuinely contested

These are the questions where informed people disagree with evidence on both sides. This course states both positions and does not adjudicate.

Whether benchmarks still measure anything. The older suites are saturated: frontier scores cluster in the nineties and no longer rank anything, and contamination is impossible to rule out because no laboratory discloses its training data. The replacements are harder but harness-dependent, so the same model with a different scaffold gives a different number, and at least one has been publicly audited by a competitor at a strategically useful moment. The most promising structural answer is a benchmark that continuously collects new problems published after any model's training cutoff, which makes contamination a dating question rather than a matter of trust.

Whether agents are reliable enough to deploy unsupervised. Capability measured on a single attempt keeps rising; success on every attempt does not rise with it, and 2026 measurements find the strongest models carry the highest catastrophic failure rates. Why agents are a systems problem is the evidence in full.

Whether the open-weight gap is closing or holding. Epoch AI reports that since January 2026 the most capable open-weight models have lagged frontier closed models by an average of about four months on its capability index, slightly wider than its earlier estimate of about three. INFERRED, and dependent entirely on how that index is built and on which closed models are publicly benchmarked at all. The complicating fact is that several of the largest open-weight releases now carry revenue-triggered commercial gates, so "open weights" and "usable at scale by a competitor" have come apart, which The open-weights turn and Four axes of open both take up.

Whether the capital expenditure has a return. Analyst projections of 2026 infrastructure spending by the largest cloud and platform companies run into the hundreds of billions of dollars, and forecasts of the multi-year build-out run into the trillions. INFERRED in every case: these are projections by named financial institutions, they disagree with each other, and nobody outside those companies can audit the assumptions. Name the analyst in-line whenever you repeat one.

Whether more pre-training scale still buys much. Nobody outside a frontier laboratory can answer this, because answering it needs the training curves that stopped being published in 2023. The observable proxy is that laboratory messaging has moved decisively from parameter counts to token efficiency, effort control and post-training. Read that as evidence about where the returns are perceived to be, and not as proof of anything.

6. The turn nobody predicted

The most significant event of this window is not a model. It is that a government switched two of them off, and it is worth recording precisely because a technical history is where this fact belongs.

The sequence, KNOWN from the affected company's own dated account:

  • 9 June 2026. Two new frontier models become available.
  • 12 June 2026. The United States government applies export controls to both, requiring access to be restricted from any foreign national, whether inside or outside the United States. The trigger reported by the company is a report from researchers at another company describing a way of prompting one model into identifying software vulnerabilities, in one case producing code demonstrating an exploit.
  • The same day. Because the order took effect immediately and there was no reliable way to verify nationality in real time, the company suspends both models for everybody. Its other models stay available.
  • 26 June 2026. Government approval restores the limited-availability model for a set of United States organisations.
  • 30 June 2026. Controls are lifted after new safeguards, including a classifier that the company reports blocks the specific published technique in over 99 percent of cases. That figure is the company's own, in the company's own incident post.
  • 1 July 2026. Both models are redeployed globally.

Whatever you think of the decision, and the company publicly disagreed with the finding, three facts were established in nineteen days. A frontier model can be treated as a controlled export. A government can order one switched off, and the order can take effect the same day. And the mechanism works only because the weights never left the company's own machines.

That last point is the sharp end of the whole open-weights argument in The open-weights turn. A permissively licensed model already copied onto thousands of machines in dozens of countries cannot be recalled by anybody, including its publisher. Two release strategies that looked like commercial preferences turn out to differ in whether a state has a lever at all.

7. This page is provisional, and here is how it ages

This course marks every page with a zone. Settled pages carry mechanisms and events whose interpretation is stable, and are corrected by appending rather than rewritten. Moving pages cover roughly the last eighteen months and are rewritten freely. Open pages are registers of questions. The boundary between the first two is a single date, currently 30 June 2025, and moving it is a deliberate reviewed act rather than a drift.

This page is moving, its as-of date is at the top, and its review is due in February 2027. At that review the correct action is to replace it: read the chronicle entries appended since, decide what has changed shape rather than merely happened, and write the section again. Patching a snapshot produces a page that is half two years old and half current, with nothing marking the seam, which is the specific failure this whole mechanism exists to prevent.

The companion page is What is still open, and what would change it, which does the same job for the questions this one could not answer.
