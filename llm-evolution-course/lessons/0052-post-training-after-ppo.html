Post-training after PPO: three things deleted

The alignment recipe got simpler three separate times, and each simplification is best remembered by which component it removed: the reward model, the human rater, or the critic.

Settled Accurate as of 21 August 2026. Interpretation is stable. Corrected by appending, never rewritten to fit a new event.
Where this lesson starts

Teaching a model to answer gives a three-stage alignment recipe with several moving parts. This lesson is what happened to those parts over the following three years. It assumes you know that recipe.

1. Where this lesson sits

What you need first

  • Teaching a model to answer A model trained to continue text is not trying to be helpful, so you add a stage whose only job is preference: show it demonstrations, learn a stand-in for human taste, and let it practise against that on a short leash.
  • Rewards a machine can check Replace the human rater with a program that can check the answer, and the ceiling on training data moves from how fast people can label to how fast you can generate problems.

The one tight idea

The alignment recipe got simpler three separate times, and each simplification is best remembered by which component it removed: the reward model, the human rater, or the critic.

2. What this lesson must cover

To write. Full prose, complete sentences, 1,400 to 2,000 words. Every bullet below is a beat the finished lesson owes the reader, in this order.

  • Direct preference optimisation: train on the comparisons directly and delete the separate reward model.
  • Learning from AI feedback against a written set of principles: delete the human rater from most of the loop.
  • Group-relative optimisation: compare a group of samples against each other and delete the critic.
  • What each simplification costs as well as what it saves.
  • Synthetic data and self-improvement, with the model-collapse concern stated precisely under its assumptions rather than as an observed property.
Do not overclaim

Model collapse is a result under stated assumptions about repeated training on generated data. It is not an observed property of currently shipped models. State the assumptions.
