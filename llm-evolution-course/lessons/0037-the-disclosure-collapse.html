The disclosure collapse

Once models became commercially valuable the technical reports stopped containing technique, so from 2023 onward a reader has to sort every claim into what is known, what is inferred, what is marketing and what is unverifiable.

The one tight idea

Once models became commercially valuable the technical reports stopped containing technique, so from 2023 onward a reader has to sort every claim into what is known, what is inferred, what is marketing and what is unverifiable.

2. What this lesson must cover

To write. Full prose, complete sentences, 1,400 to 2,000 words. Every bullet below is a beat the finished lesson owes the reader, in this order.

  • The 2023 report that states plainly that it contains no details of architecture, data, compute or training method.
  • The four labels, defined and used from here on: known, inferred, marketing, unverifiable.
  • A worked example of manufactured common knowledge: a widely repeated size figure that was never confirmed by anyone in a position to confirm it.
  • Why this is a scientific problem and not only a commercial one: claims that cannot be checked cannot be built on.
  • What is still checkable, and where to look for it.
Do not overclaim

State the absence of disclosure. Do not state a motive for it. The reasons are not public and speculating about them undermines the lesson's own point.
