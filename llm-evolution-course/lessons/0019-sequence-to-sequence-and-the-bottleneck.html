Sequence to sequence, and the eight thousand numbers

Read a whole sentence into a single fixed list of numbers and then write another sentence out of it, and that single list is both the elegance and the flaw.

Settled Accurate as of 21 August 2026. Interpretation is stable. Corrected by appending, never rewritten to fit a new event.
Where this lesson starts

Word vectors give a representation for a word. This lesson is about representing a whole sentence, and about the specific failure that appears when the sentence gets long.

1. Where this lesson sits

What you need first

  • Sequences, memory, and the LSTM A recurrent network is one small network applied again and again with a carried-over memory, and the LSTM is that memory given a protected lane and gates that decide what enters it.
  • Meaning as a direction in space Give every word a position in a few hundred dimensions, learn those positions only from the company each word keeps, and similarity in meaning becomes closeness in space.

The one tight idea

Read a whole sentence into a single fixed list of numbers and then write another sentence out of it, and that single list is both the elegance and the flaw.

2. What this lesson must cover

To write. Full prose, complete sentences, 1,400 to 2,000 words. Every bullet below is a beat the finished lesson owes the reader, in this order.

  • The encoder-decoder shape, described as read then write.
  • That the entire source sentence is compressed into one fixed-size vector regardless of its length.
  • The measured consequence: translation quality falls as the source sentence grows.
  • Evaluation, briefly: what BLEU is measuring and why the field trusted it enough to chase it.
  • The reversal trick and other patches, as evidence that people knew the bottleneck was there.
Do not overclaim

Neural encoder-decoders appear in more than one place in 2013 and 2014. Present the line as several groups converging, not as one invention.
