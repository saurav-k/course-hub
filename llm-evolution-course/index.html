01

Rules

3 lessons
The problem

Everything a machine knew about language had to be written down by a person, one rule at a time.

What closed it. Counting. Stop describing language and start measuring it.

Deep dive · 0011
Machines that talk without understanding
You can produce convincing language with pattern matching and no model of meaning at all, and people will supply the understanding themselves.
Before the neural turnsettled
Deep dive · 0012
The statistical turn: guessing the next word
Language is predictable, predictability can be measured, and a machine that counts what usually follows what beats a machine that reasons about grammar.
Before the neural turnsettled
Deep dive · 0013
Translation without a linguist
Model the source language and the translation separately, learn both by counting a parallel corpus, and search for the target sentence that scores best, with no grammar written by anyone.
Before the neural turnsettled
02

Meaning

5 lessons
The problem

Counted symbols carry no similarity: a model that has seen dog learns nothing about cat, and most word sequences are never seen at all.

What closed it. Learned vectors. A word became a position in a space where closeness means similarity.

Deep dive · 0014
What a neural network actually is
A network is a stack of two operations, multiply by a table of numbers and then bend the result, and training is nudging those numbers until a measured error gets smaller.
The neural turnsettled
Deep dive · 0015
Backpropagation, and the two winters
The field stalled twice for two different reasons: the first was an argument about what a single layer could represent, and the second was an obstacle in the arithmetic of training deep stacks.
The neural turnsettled
Deep dive · 0016
Sequences, memory, and the LSTM
A recurrent network is one small network applied again and again with a carried-over memory, and the LSTM is that memory given a protected lane and gates that decide what enters it.
The neural turnsettled
Deep dive · 0017
2012: the year the hardware caught up
The ideas that won in 2012 were roughly twenty-five years old, and what actually changed was a large labelled dataset and a graphics card that could do the arithmetic in parallel.
The neural turnsettled
Deep dive · 0018
Meaning as a direction in space
Give every word a position in a few hundred dimensions, learn those positions only from the company each word keeps, and similarity in meaning becomes closeness in space.
The neural turnsettled
03

Reach

9 lessons
The problem

A model could not connect what it read at the start of a sentence to what it needed at the end, and the fix for that was too slow to train.

What closed it. Attention, and then attention without recurrence.

Deep dive · 0019
Sequence to sequence, and the eight thousand numbers
Read a whole sentence into a single fixed list of numbers and then write another sentence out of it, and that single list is both the elegance and the flaw.
The neural turnsettled
Deep dive · 0020
Attention: stop compressing
Do not squeeze the source sentence into one vector at all: keep every position, and at each output step learn a weighting that says which positions matter now.
The neural turnsettled
Deep dive · 0021
The other problem: attention did not make it fast
Attention fixed how far a model could reach, but the recurrence underneath it still forced one step per word, so training time grew with sentence length no matter how many machines you had.
The neural turnsettled
Deep dive · 0022
Who was in the room: labs, money, and machines, 2014 to 2017
The institutions that would build large language models were founded, funded and already compute-constrained before the architecture that made them possible existed.
The neural turnsettled
Deep dive · 0023
Two papers, one day
On 12 June 2017 two unrelated papers were posted, one that made models fast and one that made them listen to people, and the rest of this course is those two threads converging.
The Transformersettled
Deep dive · 0024
The Transformer: what dropping recurrence unlocked
Delete the recurrence and keep only attention, and every position in a sentence can be computed at the same time with a constant number of operations between any two words.
The Transformersettled
Deep dive · 0025
ImageNet for words: pretrain once, specialise later
Train one model on a mountain of unlabelled text, then adapt it cheaply to each task, and the field's economics invert: the expensive step happens once instead of once per task.
The Transformersettled
Deep dive · 0026
The fork: GPT-1 and BERT
Apply the same pretraining recipe to the two halves of the Transformer and you get two incompatible models, one that understands and one that writes, and for eighteen months the understanding one wins.
The Transformersettled
Deep dive · 0027
Tokens: how text becomes numbers
A model never sees words: it sees pieces chosen by an algorithm that repeatedly merges the most frequent adjacent pair, and once you start from raw bytes nothing is ever out of vocabulary.
The Transformersettled
04

Scale

6 lessons
The problem

Nobody knew how big to build a model, how much text to feed it, or how to tell before spending the money.

What closed it. Scaling laws, and the correction that moved the constraint to data.

Deep dive · 0028
GPT-2 and the bet on scale
Make the decoder much bigger and train it on much more text, and the fine-tuning step starts to become unnecessary: you describe the task in the prompt instead.
Scale and alignmentsettled
Deep dive · 0029
Where the data actually came from
The dataset behind GPT-2 was scraped by its authors and filtered by a proxy for human approval, it was not a public crawl, and that filtering idea outlived the model that introduced it.
Scale and alignmentsettled
Deep dive · 0030
The model they would not release
The first time a lab withheld a trained model on the grounds that releasing it might be harmful, it set the terms of an argument the field is still having.
Scale and alignmentsettled
Deep dive · 0031
Predicting the future with a straight line
Model loss falls as a power law in size, data and compute, which means a straight line on a log-log plot, which means you can forecast the result of a run you have not paid for.
Scale and alignmentsettled
Deep dive · 0032
GPT-3, and learning without learning
A model whose weights never change gets better at a task when you put a few worked examples in the prompt, which is a kind of learning that leaves no trace in the model.
Scale and alignmentsettled
Deep dive · 0033
Chinchilla, the correction, and data as the new constraint
The first scaling recipe held the wrong thing fixed, so the models built on it were far larger than they needed to be and far less trained than they should have been, and once that was corrected the scarce resource became text.
Scale and alignmentsettled
05

Usefulness

5 lessons
The problem

Models were extremely capable at continuing text and almost useless at answering a question, and nobody outside a lab could reach them.

What closed it. Instruction tuning, learning from human comparisons, and a free text box. Which promptly closed the scientific record.

Deep dive · 0034
Teaching a model to answer
A model trained to continue text is not trying to be helpful, so you add a stage whose only job is preference: show it demonstrations, learn a stand-in for human taste, and let it practise against that on a short leash.
Scale and alignmentsettled
Deep dive · 0035
Chain of thought: prompting becomes a lever
Asking a large model to write out its working before answering improves its accuracy on multi-step problems, which means the prompt is not just a request but a control on how much computation the model spends.
Scale and alignmentsettled
Deep dive · 0036
A text box changes everything
The breakthrough of late 2022 was distribution rather than research: an existing recipe, tuned for conversation, put behind a free text box that anyone could use without reading anything first.
Scale and alignmentsettled
Deep dive · 0037
The disclosure collapse
Once models became commercially valuable the technical reports stopped containing technique, so from 2023 onward a reader has to sort every claim into what is known, what is inferred, what is marketing and what is unverifiable.
Open and efficientsettled
Deep dive · 0038
Reading an eval table like a scientist
A benchmark number is a measurement made by someone with a stake in it, and six questions about how it was produced usually explain most of the gap between two competing claims.
Open and efficientmoving
06

Cost

10 lessons
The problem

Frontier capability existed and cost more to train, tune and serve than almost anyone could pay.

What closed it. Open weights, adapters, sparsity, and a decade of serving engineering.

Deep dive · 0039
The leak that started an ecosystem
A research-only model release leaked within a week, and the tooling that appeared in the following month showed there had been enormous unmet demand for weights anyone could run.
Open and efficientsettled
Deep dive · 0040
Four axes of open: weights, code, data, licence
Open is not one question but four independent ones, almost nothing scores well on all four, and the word is now used for releases that would fail three of them.
Open and efficientmoving
Deep dive · 0041
Who ships open weights now
The set of organisations publishing frontier-adjacent open weights has changed completely since 2023, and the change is a fact about release artefacts rather than a story about intentions.
Open and efficientmoving
Deep dive · 0042
LoRA and QLoRA: fine-tuning for the rest of us
You can adapt a large model by training a small number of extra numbers alongside the frozen originals, and if you also store the originals at lower precision the whole job fits on one ordinary machine.
Open and efficientsettled
Deep dive · 0043
Serving reality: the cache, and what it costs
Generating a token requires re-reading everything the model has already seen, so serving is a memory problem before it is an arithmetic problem, and most of the engineering since 2022 is about that memory.
Open and efficientsettled
Deep dive · 0044
Mixture of experts, part one: the router
Replace one large feed-forward network with many smaller ones and a small chooser that picks a couple per token, and what a model knows stops being the same number as what it costs to run.
Open and efficientsettled
Deep dive · 0045
Mixture of experts, part two: why it is hard
A router that is trained only to be useful will send almost everything to a few experts, so every practical system needs something that stops the router eating itself, and three generations of that fix are the real story.
Open and efficientsettled
Deep dive · 0046
Sparsity moves to attention
Once the feed-forward half of the block is cheap, attention is what is left paying the quadratic bill, so the same selective trick was pointed at attention itself.
Open and efficientmoving
Deep dive · 0047
Why a small model now beats a huge one from 2020
A model a fraction of the size now outperforms the largest models of 2020 on most tasks, and almost none of the reason is a better architecture.
Open and efficientmoving
Deep dive · 0048
Long context: capacity is not competence
The advertised context length is how much text a model will accept, not how much it can use, and the two numbers are routinely different by a large factor.
Open and efficientmoving
07

Thinking

9 lessons
The problem

However good the model, one forward pass was all the thought a question ever got.

What closed it. Not yet closed. Test-time compute, checkable rewards and tools are the current attempt.

Deep dive · 0049
Thinking is just more tokens
A reasoning model does not think in any new sense: it writes a region of working tokens before it answers, which buys both more computation per question and an external place to keep intermediate results.
The reasoning erasettled
Deep dive · 0050
Rewards a machine can check
Replace the human rater with a program that can check the answer, and the ceiling on training data moves from how fast people can label to how fast you can generate problems.
The reasoning erasettled
Deep dive · 0051
What did not work, and why that matters
The field expected the game-playing recipe of search plus a learned value function to transfer to language, two labs reported independently that it did not, and the simple recipe worked instead.
The reasoning erasettled
Deep dive · 0052
Post-training after PPO: three things deleted
The alignment recipe got simpler three separate times, and each simplification is best remembered by which component it removed: the reward model, the human rater, or the critic.
The reasoning erasettled
Deep dive · 0053
Tools, protocols, and the agent loop
Tool use went from a prompting pattern to a trained capability to an industry protocol, and the loop it produces is simple enough to draw and hard enough to make reliable.
The reasoning eramoving
Deep dive · 0054
Why agents are a systems problem
What a model can do on a single attempt keeps improving while how often it succeeds every time keeps not improving, and that gap is where the binding constraint now sits.
The reasoning eramoving
Deep dive · 0055
Multimodality, and the model as an interface
Once images, audio and text all become sequences of tokens in one model, the model stops being a text tool and becomes the place where several kinds of input meet.
The reasoning eramoving
Deep dive · 0056
Where we are now
A dated snapshot of what is frontier, what has become a commodity, and what is genuinely contested, written to be replaced rather than edited.
The reasoning eramoving
Deep dive · 0057
What is still open, and what would change it
The interesting questions in this field are not predictions but open problems, and each one can be stated with what would count as an answer, so that a future reader can tell whether it has been resolved.
