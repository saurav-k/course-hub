# Route scope: By era

**Route id:** `era`
**Sections:** 6
**Pages:** 47 deep dives

## What this route is

Straight chronology.
Six periods, the same lessons in the order the events happened, with nothing rearranged to make an argument.

| Section | Period |
|---|---|
| 01 Before the neural turn | to about 2005 |
| 02 The neural turn | about 2006 to 2014 |
| 03 The Transformer | 2015 to 2018 |
| 04 Scale and alignment | 2018 to 2022 |
| 05 Open and efficient | 2023 to 2025 |
| 06 The reasoning era | 2024 to 2026 |

This route makes no claim beyond the order.
That is its value: it is the one route that will not quietly argue with you, and it is the one to check a date against.

## An honest caveat about "straight"

Chronology in a field like this is only approximately a line.
Two things make it approximate here, and both are stated rather than hidden:

- **Periods overlap.** Section 05 begins in 2023 and section 06 begins in 2024, because the efficiency work and the reasoning work were happening at the same time in different places. The boundary is where the centre of gravity moved, not where one period stopped.
- **Order inside a period is by the order ideas mattered, not by submission date.** The chain-of-thought paper is from January 2022 and sits after the March 2022 instruction-tuning lesson, because it reads as the second thing 2022 discovered rather than the first. Where this happens the lesson says the real date plainly in its own body.

If you want strict submission dates with nothing smoothed, that is a table, not a course, and it would make a good addition to `reference/`.

## What it deliberately leaves out

- **The ten spine chapters.** They are already ordered by era, so this route would be a longer version of the same reading.
- **Causation.** The route does not say why anything happened; the lessons do, individually. A reader who wants the argument takes `constraint`.
- **Any period before 1948.** The course begins where language was first treated as something measurable. Earlier mechanical-translation ideas are mentioned inside lesson 0011 rather than given a period.

## Who it is for

Someone who wants the timeline, and someone looking for one specific decade.
It is the best route for orientation in the middle of the course, and the best route to jump into if you already know roughly when the thing you care about happened.

It is a poor route to learn from front to back, because a period is not an argument and six periods in a row can read as a list.

## How to extend it

Place a new lesson by date.
That is the entire rule, and keeping it that simple is what makes this route worth having next to the other three.

Two things to watch:

- **Do not smooth silently.** If you place a lesson out of strict date order because the story reads better, say the real date in the lesson body and note it here. The caveat section above exists so that these are visible in one place.
- **Do not create a seventh period casually.** A period boundary is a claim that the centre of gravity moved, and moving one boundary usually means re-describing the two periods on either side. Section 06 will eventually end; when it does, the new section needs a period label that a reader can date, and the two spine chapters covering the same years need looking at in the same pull request.
