# Notes

How this course teaches, and what the authoring cost.
Read `MISSION.md` first for why it exists, then `BUILDER-SPEC.md` for what differs from the house standard.

`MISSION.md` is the contract and changes rarely.
This file is the working memory and should change often.

## Learner profile

Who is reading, in more detail than `MISSION.md` needs.
What they are fluent in, what reads as friction, and what they asked for in their own words.

A useful distinction to write down where it applies: someone can be a beginner in a notation without being a beginner in thinking, and those two take opposite tones.

## Cadence

What one page is: prose words, diagram count, quiz count.
What the orientation figure on a page of this course draws, since that is the part every page shares.
Where a page ends and the next begins.
Whether quizzes come after the idea is worked, which they should.

## Teaching preferences

The decisions that are this course's, not the house's.
What gets a diagram and what gets prose.
What may be assumed and what must be re-explained on every page.
Which kinds of example land with this learner.

## Structure decisions

Why the page shape is what it is, and what was rejected.
A decision recorded with its reason survives a rewrite; a decision recorded alone gets reversed by the next author.

## Known gotchas

The things that cost an hour, so they cost the next author nothing.
Renderer quirks, a parser that breaks on a character this subject is full of, a figure that shrinks below legibility, a source that does not recompute from its own table.

Write each one as the symptom first, then the cause, then the fix. The next author arrives with the symptom.

## Honesty notes

Where this course knowingly says less than the source claims, or more than the evidence supports, and where the page says so.
Each one should point at the `.callout.warn` that carries it.

## Open threads

What is not written, what is not decided, and what should be checked once real readers have used it.
