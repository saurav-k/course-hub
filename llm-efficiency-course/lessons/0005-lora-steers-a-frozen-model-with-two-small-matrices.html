Module 03 · Steering a Frozen Model · Lesson 05

LoRA steers a frozen model with two small matrices

working 12 min The low-rank adaptation mechanism, from its premise to its memory bill. · LoRA (Hu et al., 2021)

The one-minute version

  • Fine-tuning's naive cost is not the weights themselves but the optimizer state - several multiples of the weights, held in memory during training.
  • LoRA freezes every original weight and learns a small low-rank update instead: two thin matrices whose product approximates the change.
  • The rank is the dial: bigger rank means more capacity to change behaviour and more adapter megabytes - but always a rounding error beside the base model.
  • What it costs: expressive power is capped by the rank, and quality depends on choosing which layers get adapters.

1. Why naive fine-tuning is a memory bomb

Count what full fine-tuning of a model actually holds in memory during training. The weights themselves - one copy. The gradients - a second copy of equal size. And the optimizer state: Adam keeps a momentum estimate and a variance estimate per parameter, two more copies, usually in higher precision than the weights. Four to six copies of a 320B-parameter model is not a desk budget; it is a data-center line item. That is the wall LoRA was built against.

The LoRA premise is that the change a fine-tune makes is simpler than the model it changes. Adapting a model to your house style, your tool-calling format, your domain vocabulary - that is a low-dimensional adjustment, not a rewrite of world knowledge. So the update to each weight matrix is written as a product of two thin matrices, and only those two learn anything (LoRA paper).

2. What a rank buys

The rank r is the thin dimension of the pair. For an 8192-by-8192 weight matrix, a full fine-tune trains about 67 million parameters; LoRA at rank 16 trains two matrices of 8192-by-16 - about 262 thousand, a 256-fold reduction. Across every adapted layer, the adapter for a 320B model stays in the megabyte-to-gigabyte range depending on rank and coverage. The dial is honest in both directions: doubling the rank doubles adapter size and doubles what behaviours the update can express, and empirical practice lands most instruction-style adaptations comfortably at single-digit to double-digit ranks.

3. Choosing the layers, and what it costs

You do not have to adapt every matrix. Attention's query and value projections are the classic starting set; adding the feed-forward networks increases coverage of factual-style change. Each added target multiplies adapter size roughly linearly and adds training cost per step. The bill for LoRA is a capacity ceiling: if your task genuinely needs new knowledge rather than new behaviour, a rank-16 update cannot hold it, and the failure is quiet - the adapter interpolates instead of learning. Start small, measure on held-out examples, grow the rank or coverage only when the measurement says so.

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

Why does full fine-tuning need roughly four copies of the weights in memory while LoRA needs essentially one?
Gradients are the same size as the weights, and Adam's momentum and variance estimates add two more copies. LoRA's trainable set is thousands of times smaller, so those copies shrink with it.
Your rank-16 adapter improves style but teaches the model nothing about your private API. What does that failure tell you?
Low-rank updates reshape behaviour well and store knowledge poorly - the capacity ceiling from the lesson. More rank helps marginally; retrieval over the documents usually helps more.

Practice about 10 minutes, with paper

Practice 1working

You adapt every query and value projection of the course model: 45 layers, each with two matrices of 8192 by 8192. Rank 32, stored in 16-bit halves.

Find the adapter's total size in megabytes.

Hint

Per matrix: 2 × 8192 × 32 parameters. Two matrices per projection, two projections per layer, 45 layers.

Show the worked solution
  1. Per matrix pair. 2 × 8192 × 32 = 524,288 parameters; at 2 bytes each ≈ 1 MB.
  2. Per layer. Q and V make two pairs: ≈ 2 MB.
  3. Total. 45 layers × 2 MB = about 90 MB - for an adapter that steers a 320B model.

Sanity check. Ninety megabytes is roughly 0.03 percent of even the 306 GiB base - invisible next to it, as the lesson promised.
