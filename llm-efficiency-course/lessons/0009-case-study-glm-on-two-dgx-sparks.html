Two Sparks clear the bar: the 320B MoE fits, pipelines, and streams

frontier 13 min The worked deployment: every number derived, every lever from Lessons 01-08 doing its job. · Connect Two Sparks playbook

The one-minute version

  • Two DGX Sparks join over a 200 GbE QSFP cable into one 256 GB pool (playbook).
  • The FP8 file does not fit; an NVFP4 checkpoint at ~160 GiB derived does, leaving ~90 GB for KV cache and runtime.
  • Pipeline parallelism - not tensor parallelism - splits the 45 layers across the two nodes, because the cable is narrow.
  • Expected: roughly 20-30 tokens per second solo with MTP drafting, a budget of several concurrent 32-64K sessions - derived, not measured.

1. The fit ledger

Start from the two ledgers of Lesson 02 and the formats of Lesson 04. The 256 GB pool minus a realistic 10 GB per node for the operating system and runtime leaves about 236 GB. The FP8 file needs ~306 GiB: it does not fit, full stop. An NVFP4 checkpoint at ~160 GiB derived does, and the remainder budgets the KV cache:

KV budget ≈ 236 GB − 160 GiB − ~8 GB buffers ≈ ~68 GB Read as: KV budget: memory left for the per-token caches that make conversation possible. How much context that buys depends on the model's per-token KV cost, which its hybrid attention design keeps low - the recipe's own TP=4 figures imply a multi-million-token KV pool at data-center scale (recipe). On the desk budget, plan tens of concurrent 32-64K sessions rather than a million-token monologue.

2. Why pipeline, not tensor, parallelism

Tensor parallelism shreds each layer across all devices and synchronises them several times per layer. Over the 200 GbE cable - 25 GB/s each way - those per-layer all-reduces would dominate the step. Pipeline parallelism instead hands whole layers: Spark 1 runs layers 1-23, ships one small activation per token to Spark 2, and Spark 2 runs layers 24-45. The cable crosses once per token instead of a hundred-odd times.

The price of pipelining is the bubble: while one stage works, the other waits. With a batch of concurrent requests the stages interleave and the bubble shrinks - another reason desk deployments serve a small fleet of agents rather than one stream. Quantization interacts kindly here too: NVFP4 halves the activation bytes crossing the cable versus FP8, and pipeline splits tolerate the link's latency in a way tensor splits cannot.

3. What it costs, and what to verify first

The honest caveats. Every rate on this page is derived from bandwidth arithmetic; no published benchmark of this model on GB10 hardware existed when the course was written, and the first thing to do after racking the cluster is run vllm bench serve at your real concurrency and replace these numbers with yours. NVFP4 checkpoints for a days-old model are community work until the publisher ships one - validate on your own workload. And the bubble plus the cable put a ceiling on burst parallelism: expect the six-agent fleet of your dreams to share roughly 20-35 tokens per second of aggregate decode without drafting, and to plan sessions around it.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory before revealing.

Why does pipeline parallelism beat tensor parallelism across a 200 GbE cable for this model?
Tensor parallelism synchronises devices several times per layer - hundreds of cable crossings per token. Pipeline crosses once. MoE layers can be tensor-split; the cable just makes it prohibitively chatty.
Where does most of the 256 GB pool go after the NVFP4 weights are resident?
The MTP head is one small layer, not a copy of the network. The OS share is the 20 GB already subtracted; the rest of the pool's life is the KV budget - which is what your concurrency and context lengths spend.

Practice about 15 minutes, with paper

Practice 1frontier

You plan four concurrent agent sessions at 48K context each. Assume a KV cost of 0.3 MB per token of context for this model's hybrid attention (a planning figure, not a measurement), and everything else from the lesson's ledger.

Find the KV memory the plan needs, whether it fits the ~68 GB budget, and the largest single 1M-context session's KV cost by the same figure.

Hint

4 × 48,000 × 0.3 MB for the plan; 1,000,000 × 0.3 MB for the monologue.

Show the worked solution
  1. Fleet. 4 × 48,000 × 0.3 MB ≈ 57.6 GB - fits the ~68 GB budget, barely, with little headroom for growth.
  2. Monologue. 1,000,000 × 0.3 MB = 300 GB - does not fit, by more than the whole cluster's memory.
  3. Reading it. The million-token context is a data-center feature; desk deployments live on many modest sessions. Cap contexts at what the KV budget prices, not at what the model card declares.

Sanity check. The fleet figure must be linear in sessions and context; doubling either doubles it - and 57.6 < 68 < 300 keeps the verdicts consistent.
