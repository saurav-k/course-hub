The one-minute version

  • Every deployment decision in this course was the same six questions in a different costume.
  • The worksheet walks them in order: capacity, bandwidth, format, KV budget, parallelism shape, and the rate you should expect.
  • Worked against the course model first, then left for you to run on your own numbers.
  • The output is not a number - it is a verdict: fits or not, which format, and what to believe until you benchmark.

1. The worksheet, worked once

Here it is filled in for the course's own case - two DGX Sparks and GLM-5.3-Flash - so you can see a completed sheet before producing your own.

  1. Capacity. Pool = 2 × 128 GB, minus ~20 GB OS/runtime = ~236 GB available for weights and caches.
  2. Bandwidth. 273 GB/s per node; the walk reads only active parameters, so bandwidth prices speed while capacity prices fit.
  3. Format. Silicon is Blackwell: NVFP4 native. FP8 at ~306 GiB fails the fit; NVFP4 at ~160 GiB derived passes.
  4. KV budget. 236 − 160 − ~8 buffers ≈ ~68 GB - priced into sessions, never spent twice.
  5. Shape. Two nodes, narrow link: pipeline by layers (1-23 / 24-45), one cable crossing per token.
  6. Rate. 273 ÷ 9 ≈ 30 tok/s ideal at 4-bit, ≈10 realistic, × MTP acceptance gain - until vllm bench serve says otherwise.

3. Where worksheets go wrong

Three classic failures, all avoidable. Double-counting memory: the KV budget and the weights compete for the same pool; a sheet that grants both the full remainder is lying to itself. Forgetting the OS: unified-memory boxes reserve a real slice for the desktop, and it comes out before step 1, not after step 6. Believing step 6: the derived rate is a ceiling with assumed overheads; kernels, fragmentation, and the router all shave it. The worksheet's job is to stop you buying the wrong hardware - the benchmark's job is to tell you the truth once you have.

Practice about 20 minutes, with paper - this is the capstone

Practice 1frontier

Your machine: one 64 GB workstation, 6 GB for the OS. Your model: 90B total, 9B active, dense. You want four 16K sessions; plan 0.4 MB of KV per token.

Find the full worksheet verdict: minimum bits per weight, whether that format is likely native on generic silicon, and the expected solo rate at your chosen bits.

Hint

Usable 58 GB. KV: 4 × 16,000 × 0.4 MB = 25.6 GB. Weights get 32.4 GB → 32.4 ÷ 90B × 8 ≈ 2.9 bits per weight - which fails.

Show the worked solution
  1. Capacity + KV. KV needs 25.6 GB; weights get 58 − 25.6 ≈ 32.4 GB.
  2. Bits per weight. 32.4 ÷ 90B ≈ 0.36 bytes ≈ 2.9 bits - below any format this course trusts.
  3. Verdict. Does not fit as asked. Options: drop to two sessions, halve context, or pick a smaller or MoE model with lower active count.

Sanity check. A 90B dense model wanting four 16K sessions on 64 GB fails politely at step 4 - the worksheet's whole value is failing cheaply here instead of expensively at load time.
